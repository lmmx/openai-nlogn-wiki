## This page

Concept: Linear network exchanging two banks with restored scratch
Gloss: A fixed network of linear gates (copy, gather, scatter, side injection) on banks X and Y that sends (X,Y) to (−Y,X) in three stages while restoring every auxiliary scratch wire to its arbitrary initial value.

## This page in the argument

The step: A fixed network of copy, gather, scatter and side-injection gates on banks $X$ and $Y$ sends $(X_a,Y_a)$ to $(-Y_a,X_a)$ in three stages and restores every scratch wire to its arbitrary initial value.
How: In one forward invocation first subtract the effect of the old scratch values, then copy and gather the source, scatter and inject into the target, then undo copy and gather; use the intersection-size coefficients so that the unwanted ones cancel; compose a forward, an inverse and a forward invocation.
Why: Restoring scratch wires with arbitrary contents follows the transparent-computation pattern of subtracting the initial value, computing, adding back and reversing. (the sources say so: "This cancellation of arbitrary initial scratch values is a linear" (paper))
Striking feature: The exchange is exact for arbitrary initial scratch values: the net target change $(\mathcal J\mathcal V+\mathcal R\mathcal G)x$ does not depend on them, and the intersection-one coefficients cancel exactly against the side wires. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- The net change of the target is independent of the initial scratch values, and the central and side coefficients combine to give $y\gets y+x$.
- A forward, an inverse and a forward invocation compose to the signed exchange $(x,y)\mapsto(-y,x)$, restoring every invocation's scratch wires.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A single deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: Reduce multiplication to convolution over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ through radix digits, Chinese-remainder axes, Gaussian resampling and chirps, speed up the data movement and the butterfly arithmetic with two network-based tape procedures with a strict rank saving, recover the exact product by rounding and carries, and add up the costs. (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time](main-theorem.md))
- Two tape procedures exist for fixed exponents $\tau,\lambda'<1$: one interchanges two disjoint address chunks of $u$ bits in $O(Vu^\tau)$ time, and one applies a normalized butterfly layer on up to $d$ address chunks in $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ time, where $V$ is the logical bit volume.; how: Obtain both from one fixed linear network that exchanges two banks of values and restores every auxiliary value, whose edge frame changes are smaller calls of the same procedure; the strict inequality $s<Wm$ on their number gives a recursion with a power saving.
- There are two fixed linear networks, one over bits and one over Gaussian dyadics, that exchange two banks of values while restoring every scratch wire, and whose array versions have total frame-change rank $s$ strictly below $W\cdot m$ for $W$ wires and an $m$-dimensional label space.; how: Build the scalar exchange network first, lift it to arrays with frames, then choose nested subspace labels that make the frame changes cheap, and read off the bit and complex interfaces.

**N1.1**: The net change of the target is independent of the initial scratch values, and the central and side coefficients combine to give $y\gets y+x$.
Source: "Thus both net maps are $y\gets y+x$." (paper)
> In the bit case, the central coefficient from $x_T$ to $y_S$ is $|S\cap T|\bmod2$. The side contribution cancels exactly the intersection-one cases; distinct triples with intersection zero or two already have coefficient zero, and the self coefficient is one. In the complex case, the central coefficient is $(|S\cap T|-1)/2$. The side cancels it for distinct triples with intersection zero or two; intersection one contributes zero, and the self coefficient is one. Thus both net maps are $y\gets y+x$.

**N1.2**: A forward, an inverse and a forward invocation compose to the signed exchange $(x,y)\mapsto(-y,x)$, restoring every invocation's scratch wires.
Source: "Starting from $(x,y)$, the three stages give" (paper)
> The inverse schedule with logical source $Y$ and target $X$ effects $X\gets X-Y$. Starting from $(x,y)$, the three stages give $(x,y+x)$, then $(-y,y+x)$, then $(-y,x)$. Each invocation restores its own auxiliary wires. \end{proof}
