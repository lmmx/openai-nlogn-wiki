## This page

Concept: Complex cyclic convolution by coefficient twist
Gloss: Normalized cyclic convolution f*g/T of complex arrays on power-of-two axes, obtained by twisting the last axis into the polynomial ring and using the ring convolution.

## This page in the argument

The step: Normalized cyclic convolution $f*g/T$ of complex arrays on power-of-two axes is computed with error below $6TL\cdot2^{-p}$ by twisting the last axis into the polynomial ring and using the ring convolution.
How: Twist both operands with exact phases, convolve over the polynomial ring, and untwist the result.
Why: The ring convolution handles only the first $d-1$ axes over the ring, so the last scalar axis is made into the polynomial coefficient axis by a twist. (the sources leave this for the reader to work out: spell it out)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- Multiplying coefficient $k$ by $\zeta^k$ with $\zeta=e^{\pi i/r}$ is a ring isomorphism from $\mathbb C[x_d]/(x_d^r-1)$ to $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ whose negative wrap cancels the twist. (on [Coefficient twist to negacyclic ring](negacyclic-twist.md))
- With $\iota$ identifying the last axis with polynomial coefficients and $D_\pm$ the diagonal twists, $\mathcal M_{\mathbb C}(f,g)=D_-\iota^{-1}\mathcal M_r(\iota D_+f,\iota D_+g)=f*g/T$.
- The computed version twists with truncated phases $f'=Q_p(\widetilde\theta^+\cdot f)$, applies the ring convolution to $\iota f',\iota g'$ and untwists.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- There is one deterministic fixed-alphabet, fixed-tape Turing machine multiplying $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: Reduce the product to a digit convolution computed with Fourier-type transforms on power-of-two axes, and accelerate the transforms' address rearrangements and butterfly layers by a power of the logarithm. (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time](main-theorem.md))
- With $p=6b$, $d=\lfloor b^\epsilon\rfloor$, $K$, $r$, $T$ and the tiny exponents fixed, the multiplication algorithm is correct and runs in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time.; how: Fix compatible parameters, present the algorithm built from the transform machinery, prove the recovered coefficients round correctly, and add up the cost rows. (on [Parameter choice and cost balance](parameter-balance.md))
- The algorithm splits both inputs into radix-$2^b$ digits, applies $\Phi$, computes the source transform of each, multiplies pointwise, applies the opposite transform with scalings, rounds and propagates carries to output exactly $2n$ bits.; how: Reduce the product to a cyclic convolution of digit lists on coprime axes and evaluate it by the transform, pointwise product and inverse transform with the retained permutation cancelling. (on [The multiplication algorithm](mult-algorithm.md))
