## This page

Concept: Controlled shifts and rational scalings
Gloss: A linear-time stream operation that adds to a later address field an offset computed from earlier fields, or multiplies it by a fixed rational unit, modulo q^b, by splitting and re-merging blocks.
Also called: controlled cyclic shift

## This page in the argument

The step: An update $y\mapsto y+a(\text{controls})\bmod q^b$ of a target field that follows its control fields, and multiplication of $y$ by a fixed rational unit, cost $O(V)$.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- One deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine computes the exact $2n$-bit product of two $n$-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: build two linear-network tape procedures, fast address interchange and simultaneous butterfly layers, from fixed networks with a strict rank saving; use them in synthetic transforms; place these in the Harvey–van der Hoeven reduction through radix digits, a CRT map, Gaussian resampling with retained permutations, Bluestein chirps and a phase twist; and bound every cost term (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time](main-theorem.md))
- On a fixed number of tapes, digit moves, permutations of two digit values, splits with fixed pointwise maps, cyclic row splits, controlled shifts and fixed rational scalings of an address field each cost $O(V)$ on an array of volume $V$.; how: stream each operation in lexicographic address order, splitting the array into a fixed number of aligned streams and merging them back (on [Fixed finite-tape machine model and stream operations](fixed-tape-model.md))

## Pages that build on this one

- Address shears (H, D) ↦ (H + MD, D): Array permutations Φ_M that add a rational matrix M times one address-field vector D to another H modulo q^b; the bit network's rational matrix interface makes the endpoint operator the shear H ← H + D on every role.
- Lower triangular (Bruhat) factorization: Writing a rank-a rational matrix as E₁ Π E₂ with invertible lower triangular E₁, E₂ and a partial permutation Π with a ones, so a matrix shear costs a field interchanges plus linear-time triangular updates.
- Packed selected-bit addition: A tape operation XORing the selected bits of one address chunk into the corresponding bits of another, using a temporary chunk, whole-chunk cyclic shifts and a sorted correction of a small explicit exceptional set.
- Chinese-remainder address map: The Agarwal–Cooley isomorphism x ↦ Π x_i^{μ_i} turning one cyclic convolution of length S = Π s_i (distinct primes s_i near powers of two) into a multidimensional cyclic convolution, implemented on tape by triangular controlled shifts.

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "The next operations are linear because their target field follows their
control fields." (paper)

  > The next operations are linear because their target field follows their control fields. This order will be preserved whenever they are invoked.

- "Multiplication of $y$ by any of the specified rational units, and its
inverse, also costs $O(V)$." (paper)

  > \begin{lemma}[Controlled shifts and fixed rational scalings] \label{lem:ordered-affine-streams} Fix a prime $q$, a fixed number of control fields, and finitely many rational coefficients whose denominators are prime to $q$. Whenever a coefficient is used as a unit, also require its numerator to be prime to $q$. Put $Q=q^b$, $b\ge1$. Suppose a target field $y\in[Q]$ follows its control fields, each control used in an offset having $O(b)$ digits. An update \[ y\longmapsto y+a(\text{controls})\pmod Q \] costs $O(V)$ if the offset can be prepared in $b^{O(1)}$ time from the fixed number of control values. In particular, this holds for a fixed rational linear combination of them, specialized modulo $Q$. Multiplication of $y$ by any of the specified rational units, and its inverse, also costs $O(V)$. Arbitrarily long intervening spectator fields and suffix records are permitted. \end{lemma}
