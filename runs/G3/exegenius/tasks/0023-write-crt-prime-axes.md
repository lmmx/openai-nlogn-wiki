## This page

Concept: Chinese-remainder address map to prime axes
Gloss: A ring isomorphism sending x to ∏ xᵢ^{μᵢ} that places a length-S cyclic coefficient list on a product of cyclic axes of pairwise coprime (prime) lengths while preserving convolution; the induced address change is a sequence of triangular controlled shifts.

## This page in the argument

The step: A ring isomorphism $x\mapsto\prod_i x_i^{\mu_i}$ places a length-$S$ cyclic coefficient list on a product of cyclic axes of pairwise coprime prime lengths, preserving convolution, and the induced address change is a sequence of triangular controlled shifts.
How: Define the Chinese-remainder algebra map, prove it is a bijection on monomials, and implement its address change coordinate by coordinate with controlled shifts.
Why: The coordinate powers $\mu_i$ are chosen so that the change of order becomes a sequence of controlled cyclic shifts, which are linear-time operations. (the sources say so: "sequence of controlled cyclic shifts." (paper))
Striking feature: Coordinate $i$ of the new address depends only on the lower-index digits, because terms with $j>i$ are divisible by $s_i$ and $\mu_iP_i=1$ modulo $s_i$; this triangular shape is what makes the reordering a controlled shift. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- The assignment $x\mapsto\prod_i x_i^{\mu_i}$ with $\mu_i=P_i^{-1}\bmod s_i$ is an algebra isomorphism from $\mathbb C[x]/(x^S-1)$ onto the product of cyclic axes and preserves cyclic convolution.
- The induced address change $b_i=a_i+\mu_i\sum_{j<i}a_jP_j \bmod s_i$ is a sequence of triangular prefix-controlled cyclic shifts, so padding and reordering cost $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A single deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ worst-case time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, refuting the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture in this model.; how: Reduce the product to a cyclic convolution of radix digits, evaluate it with Fourier-type transforms whose data movement and butterfly layers are accelerated by two power-saving tape procedures, recover the exact integers by rounding and carries, and add up the costs. (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time](main-theorem.md))
- The algorithm converts the inputs to radix-$2^b$ digit lists, maps them by the Chinese-remainder address map, applies the resampled chirp source transform to each, multiplies pointwise, applies the opposite transform, rescales, rounds, undoes the address map and propagates carries, giving the exact $2n$-bit product.; how: Choose the parameters, express the product as a cyclic convolution of digit lists on prime axes, evaluate that convolution through resampling, the chirp identity and the ring convolution after a phase twist, then recover exact integers by rounding and carries. (on [The multiplication algorithm](multiplication-algorithm.md))

**S5.3.1**: The assignment $x\mapsto\prod_i x_i^{\mu_i}$ with $\mu_i=P_i^{-1}\bmod s_i$ is an algebra isomorphism from $\mathbb C[x]/(x^S-1)$ onto the product of cyclic axes and preserves cyclic convolution.
Source: "$\Phi$ is an isomorphism and preserves cyclic convolution." (paper)
> Let $s_1,\ldots,s_d$ be the pairwise coprime source lengths, let $S=\prod_i s_i$, and put \[ P_i=\prod_{j<i}s_j,\qquad \mu_i=P_i^{-1}\pmod{s_i}. \] Here $P_i$ denotes an integer prefix product, not a Fourier-coordinate permutation. The assignment \[ \Phi:\mathbb C[x]/(x^S-1)\longrightarrow \mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_d]/(x_1^{s_1}-1,\ldots,x_d^{s_d}-1), \qquad x\longmapsto\prod_i x_i^{\mu_i} \] respects $x^S=1$, so it defines an algebra map. The target monomial group has size $S$, and the image of $x$ has order $S$, because each $\mu_i$ is a unit and the $s_i$ are pairwise coprime. Its powers therefore run through all target basis monomials exactly once. Hence $\Phi$ is an isomorphism and preserves cyclic convolution.

**S5.3.2**: The induced address change $b_i=a_i+\mu_i\sum_{j<i}a_jP_j \bmod s_i$ is a sequence of triangular prefix-controlled cyclic shifts, so padding and reordering cost $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$.
Source: "Each triangular update is a prefix-controlled cyclic shift." (paper)
> Each triangular update is a prefix-controlled cyclic shift. The controls precede its target in the current physical order. For each prefix, prepare the offset, split the valid target interval into its two consecutive pieces, and merge those pieces in the opposite order. Every target position carries its full spectator suffix as a block; the invalid interval is copied unchanged. There are at most $T/t_i$ control prefixes for target $i$. For any fixed setup degree $C$, all offsets therefore cost $O(dTp^C/r)=o(Tp)$ to prepare, since $t_i\ge r/2$ and $r$ exceeds every fixed power of $p$.
