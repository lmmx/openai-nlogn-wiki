Subspace labels are attached to each [Gate and terminal of a network](network-gate-terminal.md) of a linear network. Each label is a nondegenerate subspace of a fixed space, and labels are chosen so that changing representation between neighbouring labels is cheap; the [Subset-intersection network](subset-intersection-network.md) and the [Complex phase network](complex-phase-network.md) are built on them.

Write $E$ for the fixed space, and $\xi$ and $\eta$ for vectors of $E$. Labels take their orthogonality from its [Bilinear form](bilinear-form.md) $\langle\xi,\eta\rangle$, and $\mathcal L$ denotes a label, a nondegenerate subspace of $E$. The paper states that <cite data-source="paper">A label is a nondegenerate subspace of a fixed bilinear</cite> space, and that <cite data-source="paper">We now choose its intermediate labels</cite> <cite data-source="paper">to make the changes of frame inexpensive</cite>.

Vectors $\xi$ and $\eta$ are orthogonal when $\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=0$. A subspace $\mathcal L$ is nondegenerate when $\mathcal L\cap\mathcal L^\perp=\{0\}$, where $\mathcal L^\perp$ is the set of vectors orthogonal to every vector of $\mathcal L$.

<cite data-source="paper">On every edge one label will contain the other.</cite> Write $\mathcal L$ for the larger label of an edge and $\mathcal L'\subseteq\mathcal L$ for the smaller. The residual of the edge is $\mathcal L\cap\mathcal L'^\perp$, <cite data-source="paper">the orthogonal complement of the smaller label inside the larger one</cite>.

Its dimension is $\dim\mathcal L-\dim\mathcal L'$. Each vector $y$ of $\mathcal L'$ is paired with the [Linear functional](linear-functional.md) $\langle y,\cdot\rangle$ on $\mathcal L'$. This pairing is injective, since $\langle y,\cdot\rangle=0$ on $\mathcal L'$ puts $y$ in $\mathcal L'\cap\mathcal L'^\perp=\{0\}$. Both spaces have dimension $\dim\mathcal L'$, so the pairing is bijective. Hence for $u\in\mathcal L$ there is $u_1\in\mathcal L'$ with $\langle u_1,\cdot\rangle=\langle u,\cdot\rangle$ on $\mathcal L'$, and $u-u_1$ lies in $\mathcal L\cap\mathcal L'^\perp$. The split $u=u_1+(u-u_1)$ is unique, so $\dim\mathcal L=\dim\mathcal L'+\dim(\mathcal L\cap\mathcal L'^\perp)$.

A vertex's frame is an invertible operator on arrays, and <cite data-source="paper">the stored array is Dv g when the logical array is g</cite>. <cite data-source="paper">The frame in (5) is Dv = CU at a vertex with label U</cite>, so a vertex with label $\mathcal L$ carries the frame $C_{\mathcal L}$. Across an edge from $\mathcal L'$ to $\mathcal L$ the frame changes by $C_{\mathcal L}C_{\mathcal L'}^{-1}$, and in the other direction by its inverse.

Labels compute their phase exponents with the [Projection along a subspace](projection-along-subspace.md) $\Theta_{\mathcal L}$ onto $\mathcal L$ along $\mathcal L^\perp$, which exists because $\mathcal L$ is nondegenerate. <cite data-source="paper">The projection here is over F2</cite>. For a binary vector $z$, $\mathrm{hw}(z)$ is its Hamming weight, the number of ones. Let $\mathcal H=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$ be the Hadamard matrix of order 2, so $\mathcal H^2=I$, and let $\mathcal H^{\otimes m}$ be its $m$-fold tensor power. For an address $x\in\mathbb F_2^m$ of $m$ bits, $\mathrm{diag}_x$ is the diagonal operator that multiplies the value at each address $x$ by $i^{\,\mathrm{hw}(\Theta_{\mathcal L}x)\bmod 4}$, where $i$ is the imaginary unit. Then
$$C_{\mathcal L}=\mathcal H^{\otimes m}\,\mathrm{diag}_x\,\mathcal H^{\otimes m}.$$

For example, take $m=1$ and $\mathcal L=\mathbb F_2$. Then $\Theta_{\mathcal L}$ is the identity, the diagonal is $\mathrm{diag}(1,i)$, and $C_{\mathcal L}=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1+i&1-i\\1-i&1+i\end{pmatrix}$. The frame $C_{\mathcal L}$ and its inverse have entries that are a [Gaussian integer](gaussian-integer.md) divided by $2^m$.

Let $X_v$ be the translation $(X_vf)(x)=f(x+v)$ for a binary vector $v\in\mathbb F_2^m$; here $v$ is a binary vector, not a vertex, and $X$ is not the bit flip of other pages. <cite data-source="paper">Here (Xv f )(x) = f (x + v)</cite>. A kernel $\delta I+\kappa X_v$ has coefficients $\delta,\kappa$. The paper's Proposition 8 says the frame change across an edge <cite data-source="paper">is a product of one translation kernel</cite>, or its inverse, for each <cite data-source="paper">vector in an orthonormal basis of the edge residual</cite>.

<cite data-source="paper">For the binary label space, every nonzero orthogonal residual of an edge has an orthonormal basis.</cite> Let $v_1,\dots,v_\rho$ be such a basis, so $\langle v_j,v_{j'}\rangle$ is $1$ when $j=j'$ and $0$ otherwise. A basis of the residual has $\dim\mathcal L-\dim\mathcal L'$ vectors, so $\rho$ is the absolute dimension change of the edge. An edge therefore uses $\rho$ kernels, <cite data-source="paper">including on decreasing edges</cite>.

Summed over all edges, the kernel count is $\sum_{\text{edges}}\rho$. <cite data-source="paper">The saving will come from keeping the total dimension lost on decreasing</cite> edges small relative to $N$, the number of entries of the array.

Subspace labels are used in the [Bit network](bit-network.md), where they are carried by $E=\mathbb Q^h$ with form $\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=\xi^\top(I-J/9)\eta$, $\top$ transpose and $J$ the all-ones matrix. The triple labels are drawn from a [Ground set](ground-set.md) $[h]$ of $h$ elements. <cite data-source="paper">For a triple T let tT be its indicator vector.</cite> The triple labels are built from the [Indicator vector](indicator-vector.md) $t_T$ of each triple $T$. Since $t_T^\top t_T=3$ and $t_T^\top Jt_T=9$, $\langle t_T,t_T\rangle=3-9/9=2$. The triple labels take the form of the [Line spanned by a vector](line-spanned-by-vector.md) $\operatorname{span}(t_T)$, and $\langle c\,t_T,t_T\rangle=2c\neq0$ for every nonzero $c$, so each triple line is nondegenerate. For triples $S$ and $T$, $\langle t_S,t_T\rangle=|S\cap T|-1$. Neighbours, for which <cite data-source="paper">S, T are neighbors when |S ∩ T | = 1</cite>, therefore have orthogonal triple lines.
