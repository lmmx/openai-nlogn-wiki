## This page

Concept: Rank, subspaces and orthogonal complements
Gloss: Matrix rank, subspaces, projections, bilinear forms and the orthogonal complement of a subspace inside a larger one, including over the two-element field F₂.
Also called: nondegenerate subspace, orthogonal residual

## Where the argument relies on this page

- Nested nondegenerate subspace labels, spanned by indicator vectors of 3-element subsets and attached to gates and terminals, give the networks total edge rank $s_{\rm b}<W_{\rm b}m$ and $s_{\rm c}<W_{\rm c}m$ because the total dimension $L$ lost on decreasing edges is $L_{\rm b}/N=100/539$ and $L_{\rm c}/N=101/539$, below $N/2$. (on [Subspace labels from three-element subsets](subset-intersection-labels.md))
- A rational shear $(H,D)\mapsto(H+AD,D)$ over $\mathbb Z/q^b\mathbb Z$ by a rank-$a$ matrix $A$ costs exactly $a$ interchanges of $b$-digit fields plus $O(V)$ further work. (on [Address shear and lower-triangular factorization](address-shear.md))
- Phase frames in butterfly form: $C=H\mathsf SH=aI+bX$ satisfies $H_0=b\mathsf SC\mathsf S$, $C^2=X$, $C^4=I$, $C^{-1}=-iZCZ$; an edge change is $\mathcal P_M(\prod_jC_{e_j}^{\epsilon_j})\mathcal P_M^{-1}$ with $M\in\operatorname{GL}(m,\mathbb F_2)$; on a group of $e=mf$ axes this needs $s_{\rm c}$ child layers on $f$ axes, and $H_0^{\otimes D}=b^D\mathcal S_D C^{\otimes D}\mathcal S_D$. (on [Phase frames and the two-point kernel C](phase-frame-kernel.md))

## Pages that build on this one

- Linear exchange network with restored scratch wires: A fixed network of linear gates on wires (each with a role) that, for arbitrary initial scratch values, exchanges two banks of values with prescribed signs and restores every scratch wire; run in eight rows of copy, gather, scatter and side-injection steps.
- Address shear and lower-triangular factorization: The address permutation (H,D) ↦ (H+AD, D) on two vectors of fields; a rank-a matrix factors as E₁ΠE₂ with lower-triangular E₁,E₂ so that it costs a single-pair swaps plus linear-time updates, and three shears D←D−H, H←H+D, D←H−D interchange H and D.
- Frames on wire arrays: Replacing each scalar by an array and giving every vertex an invertible linear operator D_v (its frame) lets pointwise gates commute with frames, so intermediate frames cancel and only the endpoint operator D_out·D_in⁻¹ is applied to each role while each edge pays only the change of frame D_v D_u⁻¹.
- Subspace labels from three-element subsets: Attaching nested nondegenerate subspaces of F^{⊗3} (spanned by indicator vectors of 3-element subsets, with ⟨t_S,t_T⟩ = |S∩T|−1) to gates and terminals so that gather/scatter coefficients depend on intersection size and unwanted ones are cancelled by side wires; the total rank change of all edges is less than Wm.
- Phase frames and the two-point kernel C: The complex version of the network uses frames built from Hamming-weight phases i^{wt(x) mod 4}; each edge change factors into two-point kernels C = aI+bX (a=(1+i)/2, b=(1−i)/2), so a simultaneous C-layer on e=mf axes needs s smaller layers on f axes, and H₀^{⊗D} = b^D S_D C^{⊗D} S_D.

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "A label is a nondegenerate subspace of a fixed bilinear space." (paper)

  > The scalar network already has the required signed exchange. We now choose its intermediate labels to make the changes of frame inexpensive. A label is a nondegenerate subspace of a fixed bilinear space. On every edge one label will contain the other. For such an edge, the \emph{orthogonal residual} is the orthogonal complement of the smaller label inside the larger one; its dimension is the absolute change in label dimension. The saving will come from keeping the total dimension lost on decreasing edges small relative to $N$.

- "Every rational $m\times m$ matrix $A$ of rank $a$ has a factorization" (paper)

  > \begin{lemma}[Lower triangular factorization] \label{lem:lower-lower} Every rational $m\times m$ matrix $A$ of rank $a$ has a factorization \[ A=E_1\Pi E_2, \] where $E_1,E_2$ are invertible lower triangular rational matrices and $\Pi$ has exactly $a$ entries equal to one, with at most one in each row and column, and all remaining entries zero. \end{lemma}
