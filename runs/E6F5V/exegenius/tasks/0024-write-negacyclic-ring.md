## This page

Concept: Polynomial ring modulo y^r + 1
Gloss: The ring of complex polynomials of degree below r with y^r = −1, where multiplying by y^a only permutes coefficients and flips some signs, so powers of y act as exact roots of unity.
Heading: Polynomial ring modulo $y^r + 1$

The symbols in this page's heading denote (say so on the page):

- $y$ (ring variable): The formal variable of the polynomials, which satisfies y^r = −1 in the quotient.
- $r$ (record length): The number of complex coefficients in a polynomial record, a power of two.

## Where the argument relies on this page

- A positional permutation moves selected bits of the $d-1$ axes to a common offset in $K$-bit chunks so that each transform round is one simultaneous layer, giving a forward and an opposite synthetic transform each costing $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}])$. (on [Transform layout and cost](transform-layout.md))
- With $\omega_t=y^{2r/t}$ the transforms satisfy $F_t^+F_t^-=t^{-1}I$ although the ring has zero divisors, because shifting $k$ multiplies the sum by $y^{rj_0}=-1$. (on [Synthetic polynomial transform](synthetic-transform.md))
- Normalized convolution over the negacyclic ring is computed by two forward synthetic transforms, pointwise polynomial products in the retained frequency order, and the opposite transform, with error below $\sqrt2\,M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$ and cost $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}+\log(rp)])$. (on [Convolution from the retained transform layout](ring-convolution.md))
- A product of two disk-grid polynomials is computed exactly by evaluating their integer parts at the base $2^{4p}$, multiplying the resulting integers with the $O(N\log N)$ multiplier, reading centered coefficients back out, and wrapping modulo $y^r+1$, in time $O(rp\log(rp))$. (on [Signed packing (Kronecker substitution)](signed-packing.md))
- Multiplying coefficient $k$ by $\zeta^k$ with $\zeta=e^{\pi i/r}$ maps $\mathbb C[x]/(x^r-1)$ isomorphically onto the polynomial ring modulo $y^r+1$, so cyclic convolution is computed over the ring and untwisted. (on [Phase twist from cyclic to negacyclic](coefficient-twist.md))

## Pages that build on this one

- Synthetic polynomial transform: A Fourier transform over the negacyclic ring in which the roots of unity are powers of y, ω_t = y^{2r/t}, so every twiddle multiplication is a signed shift of a coefficient list (Nussbaumer–Quandalle).
- Signed packing (Kronecker substitution): A product of two disk-grid polynomials is computed exactly by evaluating their integer parts at a large base 2^{4p}, multiplying the resulting integers with the O(N log N) multiplier, and reading centered coefficients back out, then wrapping modulo y^r + 1.
- Convolution from the retained transform layout: Normalized convolution over the negacyclic ring is computed by two forward synthetic transforms, pointwise polynomial products in the retained frequency order, and the opposite transform, with explicit error bound.
- Phase twist from cyclic to negacyclic: Multiplying coefficient k by ζ^k with ζ = e^{πi/r} maps the cyclic coordinate C[x]/(x^r − 1) isomorphically onto the polynomial ring modulo y^r + 1, so cyclic convolution is computed over the ring and untwisted.

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "\mathcal R_r=\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)," (paper)

  > For an integer $r\geq 2$ that is a power of two, put \[ \mathcal R_r=\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1),\qquad \left\|\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}z_k y^k\right\|_\infty =\max_{0\leq k<r}|z_k|. \] We always use representatives of degree less than $r$. Multiplication by $y^a$, for any integer $a$, permutes the coefficients and changes some signs; consequently it is an isometry for this norm. For arrays over $\mathcal R_r$, the same notation denotes the maximum over all coefficients in all entries. A disk array has norm at most one. A $p$-bit grid array has real and imaginary coefficient parts in $2^{-p}\mathbb Z$. For any array, let $Q_p$ truncate each real and imaginary coefficient part toward zero to this grid. Then \begin{equation} \|Q_p z-z\|_\infty<\sqrt2\,2^{-p},\qquad \|Q_p z\|_\infty\leq\|z\|_\infty. \label{eq:transform-truncate} \end{equation}
