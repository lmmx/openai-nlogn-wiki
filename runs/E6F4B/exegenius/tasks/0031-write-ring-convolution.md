## This page

Concept: Convolution over the polynomial ring
Gloss: Normalized convolution on d−1 axes of arrays with entries in C[y]/(y^r+1): two forward synthetic transforms keeping the same frequency order, pointwise polynomial products (by signed packing) divided by r, and the opposite transform, all using the fast layers and chunk swaps.
Also called: synthetic convolution

## This page in the argument

The step: The normalized convolution of arrays over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ on the axes of lengths $t_i$ is computed from two forward synthetic transforms, pointwise products, and the opposite transform, with error less than $\sqrt2\,M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$.
How: transform with synthetic Fourier transforms whose roots of unity are signed shifts, keep the common stored frequency order through the pointwise product, and multiply polynomials by signed packing
Why: In $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ powers of $y$ are roots of unity and multiplying by one is just a signed shift of coefficients, so the transforms need only butterflies and shifts. (the sources say so: "serve as roots of unity in the remaining axes, and multiplication by" (paper))

Its sub-steps, in order:

- The synthetic transform $F_t^{\pm}$ with $\omega_t=y^{2r/t}$ is computed by radix-two butterfly rounds with twiddle monomials and leaves outputs in bit-reversed order. (on [Synthetic polynomial transform](synthetic-transform.md))
- On a layout that places the selected bits of all participating axes at a common offset in consecutive $K$-bit chunks, a multidimensional synthetic transform costs $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}])$ with error less than $\sqrt2\ell2^{-p}$.
- Two polynomials of $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ on the $p$-bit disk grid are multiplied exactly, and $Q_p(fg/r)$ is returned, in time $O(rp\log(rp))$. (on [Signed packing of polynomial products](signed-packing.md))
- Both forward transforms are performed keeping the common retained order, multiplied pointwise by the packed product, and the opposite transform consumes that order.
- The errors of the three transforms and the product add to less than $\sqrt2\,M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$ after the exact scaling by $M$.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A single deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: build two faster tape procedures, address-chunk interchange and simultaneous butterfly layers, put them inside a reduction of integer multiplication to cyclic convolution through resampling and synthetic ring transforms, and account the cost (on [Integer multiplication below n log n](main-theorem.md))

Source: "For the error estimate, put" (paper)
> For the error estimate, put $\delta=\sqrt2\ell\,2^{-p}$ and $\eta=\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$. Let $u,v$ be the exact stored forward outputs and $\widetilde u,\widetilde v$ the returned ones. All four are disk arrays, and each forward error is less than $\delta$. The exact map $P$ is bilinear and satisfies $\|P(x,y)\|_\infty\leq\|x\|_\infty\|y\|_\infty$ by the coefficient bound in Lemma~\ref{lem:signed-ring-product}. Hence \[ \begin{split} P(\widetilde u,\widetilde v)-P(u,v) &=P(\widetilde u-u,\widetilde v)+P(u,\widetilde v-v),\\ \|P(\widetilde u,\widetilde v)-P(u,v)\|_\infty &<2\delta, \end{split} \] where the first term uses the disk bound on $\widetilde v$ and the second uses it on $u$. Write $\widetilde z=Q_p(P(\widetilde u,\widetilde v))$ and $z_*=P(u,v)$. The product lemma makes $\widetilde z$ a disk grid array and gives $\|\widetilde z-z_*\|_\infty<2\delta+\eta$. If $\widetilde h$ is the returned opposite output, its approximation bound applies to $\widetilde z$. Since $\mathcal T_+$ is a contraction, \[ \|\widetilde h-\mathcal T_+ z_*\|_\infty \leq\|\widetilde h-\mathcal T_+\widetilde z\|_\infty +\|\mathcal T_+(\widetilde z-z_*)\|_\infty <3\delta+\eta. \] The final exact multiplication by $M$ therefore gives error less than $M(3\delta+\eta)=\sqrt2\,M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$, as claimed. For the same reason, never stop processes by name or pattern (`pkill`, `killall`), which would stop other tasks' commands too: stop a command of your own by its process id.
