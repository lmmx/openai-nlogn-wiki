## This page

Concept: Fixed-point Gaussian dyadic records
Gloss: Complex values stored as signed two's-complement integers over a power-of-two denominator 2^{-p}; intermediate values are exact and one truncation Q_p rounds back to the p-bit grid.
Also called: Z[i,1/2]

## Where the argument relies on this page

- Complex variant: the tensor product of $H_0=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$ on one bit of each of $D\le d$ address chunks (each of $K$ bits, selected at a common offset), acting coefficientwise on polynomial records with $r$ complex $p$-bit coefficients, is computed exactly followed by one truncation $Q_p$, in $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ time with $\lambda'<1$, rather than $D$ whole-array passes. (on [Simultaneous butterfly layer](simultaneous-layer.md))
- The whole layer can be evaluated exactly: with $\Delta=\lceil C_0d^{C_1}\rceil$, reserving $p+\Delta$ fractional bits and $\Delta+3$ integer and sign bits per real component represents every intermediate coefficient exactly, and this width is $O(p)$ because $\epsilon C_1<1$.
- Synthetic transforms over $\mathcal R_r$ on $d-1$ axes of lengths $t_i\in\{2^{\ell-1},2^\ell\}$ with outputs kept in a retained layout $LB$ cost $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}])$ with error less than $\sqrt2\ell\,2^{-p}$. (on [Tape layout for synthetic transforms](transform-layout.md))
- On a layout $L$ that places the selected bits of all participating axes of an array of $M=T/r$ polynomial records at a common offset in consecutive $K$-bit chunks, a forward synthetic transform (exact counterpart $LBF_{\boldsymbol t}^-$) and its opposite (exact counterpart $F_{\boldsymbol t}^+B^{-1}L^{-1}$) have error less than $\sqrt2\ell\,2^{-p}$ and cost $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}])$.
- For disk-grid polynomials $f,g\in\mathcal R_r$ the exact Gaussian-integer numerators of $fg$, and then $Q_p(fg/r)$, are computed in $O(rp\log(rp))$ time using four integer products by the established multiplier at packing base $B_0=2^{4p}$. (on [Signed packing](signed-packing.md))
- The Fourier transforms of pairwise coprime prime lengths $s_i$ that the integer product needs are computed through power-of-two lengths $t_i$ by Gaussian resampling and Bluestein chirps, with the source and target coordinate permutations left inside the transform identities, so that none is executed as a sort. (on [Retained permutation cancellation](retained-permutations.md))
- For coprime $s<t$ with $\theta=t/s-1>p/\alpha^4$ and permutations $(P_su)_j=u_{tj}$, $(P_tv)_k=v_{-sk}$ there are linear contractions $A$, $B_0$ with $P_sF_s=2^{2\alpha^2}B_0P_tF_tA$, computed with error less than $p^2$ in time $O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$ without executing $P_s$ or $P_t$. (on [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md))
- The three ordered numerical maps $\widetilde{\mathsf S}'$, $\widetilde{\mathsf J}'$, $\widetilde{\mathsf D}'$ have errors $16p$, $3p^2/4$, $4$ and times $O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$, $O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$, $O(tp^{1+\delta})$.
- The exact $2n$-bit product of the two inputs is obtained from the radix-digit convolution by the chain: radix digits, Chinese-remainder address map to a product of prime cyclic axes, two forward source transforms (resampling and chirps), pointwise product, opposite transform, rescaling, rounding, and carry propagation, with total error below $1/2$. (on [The multiplication algorithm](multiplication-algorithm.md))
- The computed chirp transform $\widetilde{\mathscr C}$ and the source transform $\widetilde{\mathscr F}$ (apply $\mathcal A$, the chirp transform, $\mathcal B_0$, then multiply by $2^\gamma$) have exact counterparts $QF_{\boldsymbol t}$ and $\mathscr F=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A=RF_{\boldsymbol s}$.
- The algorithm: read radix digits, apply $\Phi$, compute the source transforms $\widetilde{\mathscr F}(u)$, $\widetilde{\mathscr F}(v)$, multiply pointwise, apply the opposite transform, rescale by $S$ and $2^{2b+4}S$, round, undo $\Phi$ and propagate carries to output exactly $2n$ bits.
- The error on the recovered integer coefficients is below $1/2$, so rounding gives the exact integer coefficients, and carry propagation yields the exact binary product. (on [Error tracking and exact recovery](precision-recovery.md))

A fixed-point Gaussian dyadic record is the number format in which the transform layers keep their polynomial data, chosen so that a whole layer is computed without rounding and rounded once at its end.

A Gaussian dyadic is a complex number whose real and imaginary parts are integer multiples of a negative power of two. Let $p$ be the number of fractional bits, so stored values lie on the grid $2^{-p}\mathbb Z[i]$. Each record is a polynomial in the [Polynomial ring modulo y^r + 1](ring-y.md); each of its $r$ coefficients is stored as two signed two's-complement integers, one per real and imaginary part, each read as the integer divided by $2^p$. A record is laid out on the tapes of the [Fixed finite-tape machine model](machine-model.md) as $2r$ consecutive fixed-width integers, so it is read and moved by ordinary tape scans.

Inside a layer, the operations are additions, subtractions, multiplications by $\pm1$ and $\pm i$, and halving, as in $H_0(u,v)=((u+v)/2,(u-v)/2)$. After $k$ such operations, where $k$ counts the operations applied since the layer began, a value lies in $2^{-(p+k)}\mathbb Z[i]$ and has modulus at most $2^k$. This follows by induction: addition or subtraction at most doubles the largest modulus, halving adds at most one denominator bit, and signs and multiplication by $i$ change neither. For example, with $p=2$ and $u=\tfrac34$, the value $u/2=\tfrac38$ needs one more fractional bit than the grid, and loses nothing if that bit is kept.

So the components can be given room in advance. Let $\Delta$ be the number of extra bits reserved, at least the number of operations charged to the layer. Reserving $p+\Delta$ fractional bits and $\Delta+3$ integer and sign bits per real component represents every intermediate coefficient exactly <cite data-source="paper">represents every intermediate coefficient exactly</cite>. The parameters keep $\Delta$ small compared with $p$, so each component still has $O(p)$ bits. The intermediate values are exact Gaussian dyadics <cite data-source="paper">Intermediate values are exact Gaussian dyadics, whose real and imaginary parts have power-of-two denominators.</cite>

Only after the completed layer is one truncation $Q_p$ applied <cite data-source="paper">A single truncation follows the completed contractive layer.</cite> The truncation $Q_p$ drops the bits below $2^{-p}$ in each real and imaginary part, returning the record to the $p$-bit grid, with error below $\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$ per complex coefficient. Rounding at every step would add one such error per step; here there is one per layer.
