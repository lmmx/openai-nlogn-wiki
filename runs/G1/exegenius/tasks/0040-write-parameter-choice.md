## This page

Concept: Parameter choice and time bound
Gloss: Fixed rational exponents and sizes (p = 6⌈log n⌉, d ≈ p^ε, T ≈ n/log n, κ = 2^{−182}) make every cost row of the table a power of p below 1, so the total is O(n (lg n)^{1−κ}).

## This page in the argument

The step: With fixed rational exponents and sizes ($p=6\lceil\log n\rceil$, $d\approx p^\epsilon$, $T\approx n/\log n$), every row of the cost table is a power of $p$ at most $1-2\kappa$, so the total is $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.
How: fix all exponents and sizes at once, tabulate every operation's cost divided by volume as a power of $p$, and check the margin below one for each row
Why: The cost of each procedure is a power of the width, so choosing the parameters as powers of $p$ lets every row be compared with $p^1$ and the smallest margin gives $\kappa$. (the sources leave this for the reader to work out: spell it out)
Striking feature: The final exponent gap $\kappa=2^{-182}$ is tiny but strictly positive: it is half the smallest of the seven margins below one. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- The parameters are the fixed rational numbers $\tau,\sigma,\beta,\delta,\lambda,c,\lambda',C_1,\epsilon,\kappa$, chosen conservatively without optimization.
- The seven dominant margins $g_1,\dots,g_7$ below one have minimum $2^{-181}=2\kappa$.
- The complete large-input bound is $O(Tp\,p^{1-\kappa})=O(n(\log n)^{1-\kappa})$, extended to all $n$ by schoolbook multiplication below a cutoff.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies $n$-bit integers in $T_A(n)=O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: reduce the product to a digit convolution computed by synthetic transforms, replace the address rearrangement and butterfly layers inside those transforms by two faster recursive tape procedures, then assemble, round and bound the cost (on [Integer multiplication below n log n](sub-nlogn-multiplication.md))
- The product of two $n$-bit integers is computed exactly by placing radix digits on distinct prime axes, transforming them by resampled, chirped synthetic transforms, multiplying pointwise, transforming back, rescaling, rounding and carrying, within the stated time bound.; how: reduce the digit convolution to cyclic convolution on prime axes, then to power-of-two axes by Gaussian resampling and chirps, then to the ring convolution by a phase twist, and finish with rounding, carries and a cost table (on [The multiplication algorithm](multiplication-algorithm.md))
