Subspace labels are attached to each [gate and terminal of a network](network-gate-terminal.md) of a linear network. Each label $U$ is <cite data-source="paper">a nondegenerate subspace of a fixed bilinear</cite> space $E$, and labels are chosen <cite data-source="paper">to make the changes of frame inexpensive</cite>. They are used in the [subset-intersection network](subset-intersection-network.md) and the [complex phase network](complex-phase-network.md).

Subspace labels take their orthogonality from the [bilinear form](bilinear-form.md) $\langle\xi,\eta\rangle$ of $E$: vectors $\xi,\eta$ are orthogonal when $\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=0$. A subspace $U$ is nondegenerate when no nonzero vector of $U$ is orthogonal to every vector of $U$. Its orthogonal complement $U^\perp$ is the set of vectors of $E$ orthogonal to every vector of $U$.

<cite data-source="paper">On every edge one label will contain the other.</cite> Write $U'\subseteq U$ for the smaller label $U'$ and the larger label $U$ of an edge. The residual of the edge is $U\cap U'^\perp$, <cite data-source="paper">the orthogonal complement of the smaller label inside the larger one</cite>, and <cite data-source="paper">its dimension is the absolute change in label dimension</cite>.

Write $\oplus$ for the direct sum: $U=U'\oplus(U\cap U'^\perp)$ means each vector of $U$ is uniquely a vector of $U'$ plus a vector of $U\cap U'^\perp$. First, $U'\cap U'^\perp=\{0\}$ by nondegeneracy. Second, the map $y\mapsto\langle y,\cdot\rangle$ from $U'$ to the linear functionals on $U'$ is injective by the first fact, hence onto, since both spaces have dimension $\dim U'$. For $u\in U$, write $u=u_1+u_2$ with $u_1\in U'$ and $u_2\in U'^\perp$: by surjectivity some $u_1\in U'$ has $\langle u_1,\cdot\rangle=\langle u,\cdot\rangle$ on $U'$, and then $u_2=u-u_1$ is orthogonal to $U'$, so $u_2\in U\cap U'^\perp$. Since $U'\subseteq U$, the split is unique by the first fact. Hence $\dim(U\cap U'^\perp)=\dim U-\dim U'$.

A vertex with label $U$ carries the frame $C_U$, and the frame change across an edge is $C_{U'}C_U^{-1}$. <cite data-source="paper">The frame in (5) is Dv = CU at a vertex with label U</cite>. Addresses are $x\in\mathbb F_2^m$. Binary labels compute their phase exponents with the [projection along a subspace](projection-along-subspace.md) $P_U$ onto $U$ along $U^\perp$, taken over $\mathbb F_2$. The function $\mathrm{wt}(z)$ is the Hamming weight of a binary vector $z$, its number of ones. Let $H=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$ be the Hadamard matrix of order $2$. Then
$$C_U=H^{\otimes m}\,\mathrm{diag}_x\bigl(i^{\,\mathrm{wt}(P_Ux)\bmod 4}\bigr)\,H^{\otimes m},$$
where $H^{\otimes m}$ is the $m$-fold tensor power of $H$.

Every entry of $H^{\otimes m}$ is $\pm2^{-m/2}$, so each entry of $C_U$ is $2^{-m}$ times a sum of $2^m$ powers of $i$. Since $H^2=I$, the inverse $C_U^{-1}=H^{\otimes m}\,\mathrm{diag}_x\bigl(i^{-\mathrm{wt}(P_Ux)}\bigr)\,H^{\otimes m}$ has the same form. The frame $C_U$ of each label, and its inverse, have entries that are a [Gaussian integer](gaussian-integer.md) divided by $2^m$.

Let $v\in\mathbb F_2^m$ be a shift and $f$ a function of addresses, and let $X_v$ be the translation $(X_vf)(x)=f(x+v)$; the paper writes <cite data-source="paper">Here (Xv f )(x) = f (x + v)</cite>. Let $\tau$ and $\delta$ be the Gaussian dyadic coefficients of the kernel $\tau I+\delta X_v$. <cite data-source="paper">The kernels implemented below have Gaussian dyadic coefficients.</cite> On an edge the frame change $C_{U'}C_U^{-1}$ is a product of kernels: <cite data-source="paper">Its phase-frame difference CV CU−1 is a product of one translation kernel aI + bXv , or its inverse, per</cite> <cite data-source="paper">vector in an orthonormal basis of the edge residual.</cite>

<cite data-source="paper">For the binary label space, every nonzero orthogonal residual of an edge has an orthonormal basis.</cite> Let $\varrho$ be the number of vectors in such a basis, $v_1,\dots,v_\varrho$, so that $\langle v_s,v_t\rangle=1$ if $s=t$ and $0$ otherwise for indices $s,t\in\{1,\dots,\varrho\}$, where $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the ordinary dot product. Thus $\varrho$ equals the residual dimension $\dim U-\dim U'$. So an edge uses $\varrho$ kernels, <cite data-source="paper">including on decreasing edges.</cite> Each kernel is one pass over the array, so an edge costs $\varrho$ passes.

Summed over all edges, the kernel count is the sum of the absolute dimension changes, and <cite data-source="paper">The saving will come from keeping the total dimension lost on decreasing</cite> edges small.

Here $\top$ is transpose. For the [bit network](bit-network.md), subspace labels are carried by the rational space $E=\mathbb Q^h$, with form $\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=\xi^\top(I-J/9)\eta$. The triple labels are drawn from a [ground set](ground-set.md) of $h$ elements; a triple $T$ is a three-element subset of it. The triple labels are built from the [indicator vector](indicator-vector.md) $t_T$ of each triple $T$, the 0-1 vector with entry 1 on $T$ and 0 elsewhere. The triple labels are lines spanned by vectors, namely the [line spanned by a vector](line-spanned-by-vector.md) $\operatorname{span}(t_T)$ for each triple $T$.

Since $t_T^\top t_T=3$ and $t_T^\top Jt_T=9$, we get $\langle t_T,t_T\rangle=3-9/9=2$. For a second triple $T'$, $t_{T'}^\top t_T=|T'\cap T|$ and $t_{T'}^\top Jt_T=9$, so $\langle t_{T'},t_T\rangle=|T'\cap T|-1$. Each triple line is nondegenerate: a nonzero vector of $\operatorname{span}(t_T)$ is $c\,t_T$ with $c\neq0$, and $\langle c\,t_T,t_T\rangle=2c\neq0$. <cite data-source="paper">S, T are neighbors when |S ∩ T | = 1</cite>, and for neighbors $\langle t_{T'},t_T\rangle=0$, so their lines are orthogonal. <cite data-source="paper">Thus every triple line is nondegenerate, and neighboring triple lines are orthogonal, in the label</cite>.
