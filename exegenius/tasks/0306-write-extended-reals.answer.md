The extended real numbers are the real numbers with two added values, positive infinity and negative infinity. The limit inferior of a sequence is defined with them, so that it has a value even when the sequence grows without bound.

Here $+\infty$ is positive infinity, a value larger than every real number, and $-\infty$ is negative infinity, a value smaller than every real number. Let $\mathbb{R}$ denote the set of real numbers, and let $\overline{\mathbb{R}}$ denote the set $\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}$ of extended real numbers.

The order of $\mathbb{R}$ extends to $\overline{\mathbb{R}}$ with $-\infty < \tau < +\infty$ for every real number $\tau$, where $\tau$ denotes a real number. Also $-\infty < +\infty$.

Let $C$ denote any set of extended real numbers. The infimum of $C$ is the greatest extended real number that is at most every element of $C$, and the supremum of $C$ is the least extended real number that is at least every element of $C$. Every such set has an infimum and a supremum in $\overline{\mathbb{R}}$. The infimum of $C$ is $-\infty$ when $C$ has no real lower bound, the supremum of $C$ is $+\infty$ when $C$ has no real upper bound, and the infimum of the empty set is $+\infty$.

A sequence of extended real numbers has terms $\tau_\delta$ for $\delta = 1,2,3,\dots$, where $\delta$ is the position of a term in the sequence. The sequence is nondecreasing when each term is at least the term before it. Every nondecreasing sequence in $\overline{\mathbb{R}}$ has a limit, and that limit is its supremum. This is why the limit of a sequence of infima is always defined here, even when the infima are unbounded.

For example, take $\tau_\delta = \delta$. The tail infima $\inf\{\tau_{\delta'} : \delta' \ge \delta\}$ equal $\delta$, which grows without bound, so their limit is $+\infty$. Now take $\tau_\delta = (-1)^\delta \delta$. Every tail contains odd positions with arbitrarily large negative terms, so every tail infimum is $-\infty$, and the limit of these tail infima is $-\infty$.

Sums and products extend in the obvious way: $\tau + (+\infty) = +\infty$ for every real $\tau$, and $\tau \cdot (+\infty) = +\infty$ for $\tau > 0$, while $\tau \cdot (+\infty) = -\infty$ for $\tau < 0$. The expressions $(+\infty) + (-\infty)$ and $0 \cdot (+\infty)$ are left undefined.
