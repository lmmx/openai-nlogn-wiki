Radix-digit convolution is a way to multiply integers by writing each as a polynomial in a power of two whose coefficients are its digits. Radix-digit convolution reduces [exact integer multiplication](exact-integer-multiplication.md) to a polynomial product whose coefficients are short integers.

Let $B = 2^w$ be the radix, the base in which the integers are written, with $w$ the number of bits in each radix digit. Let $e$ and $f$ be digit positions. Let $d$ be the number of radix digits of each input, and let $a = (a_0, \dots, a_{d-1})$ be the list of digits of $x$, so $x = \sum_{e=0}^{d-1} a_e B^e$ with $0 \le a_e < B$. Let $z$ be a formal variable, a placeholder for the powers of a polynomial, so that $a(B) = x$ for $a(z) = \sum_e a_e z^e$. The input $y$ has digit list $b$ in the same way.

The convolution $c = a * b$ is the list of coefficients of the product $a(z)b(z)$. Here $p$ is the index of a coefficient of $c$, so $c_p = \sum_{e+f=p} a_e b_f$. Since $a(B) = x$ and $b(B) = y$, we get $xy = \sum_p c_p B^p$, and carrying the $c_p$ in base $B$ gives the binary representation of $xy$. Each $c_p$ is a sum of at most $d$ products of digits, so $c_p < dB^2$: the coefficients stay short however long $x$ and $y$ are.

For example, take $B = 4$, $d = 2$, $x = 13$ and $y = 11$. Then $13 = 3\cdot 4 + 1$ and $11 = 2\cdot 4 + 3$, so $a = (1, 3)$, $b = (3, 2)$ and $c = (3, 11, 6)$. Indeed $3 + 11\cdot 4 + 6\cdot 16 = 143 = 13\cdot 11$.

Computing $c$ directly takes about $d^2$ digit multiplications. Radix-digit convolution is computed by [Fourier transforms](discrete-fourier-transform.md) of length $q \ge 2d-1$. Pad $a$ and $b$ with zeros to length $q$ and transform both. By the [convolution theorem](convolution-theorem.md), the transform of their [cyclic convolution](cyclic-convolution.md) is the pointwise product of the transforms, so multiplying pointwise and inverting gives that cyclic convolution. It sums $a_e b_f$ over $e+f \equiv p \pmod q$. Since $e+f \le 2d-2 < q$, no pair wraps around, so it equals $c$.

The sources start from this reduction. <cite data-source="paper">Splitting the inputs into radix digits reduces multiplication to convolution of short integer coefficients</cite>. After [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md), the convolution is computed with transforms of [axis lengths](axis-length.md) that are powers of two.
