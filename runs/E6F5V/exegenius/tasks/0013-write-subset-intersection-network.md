## This page

Concept: Three-subset gather and scatter network
Gloss: A fixed network on banks indexed by triples of 3-subsets whose gather, scatter, copy and side-injection gates have net coefficient depending on intersection size, cancelled by scratch wires, so three stages realize the signed exchange (X, Y) ↦ (−Y, X).

## This page in the argument

The step: Three stages of copy, gather, scatter and side-injection gates, whose net coefficient depends on the intersection size of two 3-subsets and is cancelled by scratch wires, realize $(X,Y)\mapsto(-Y,X)$ and restore every scratch wire.
How: Make the net coefficient from a source $x_T$ to a target $y_S$ vanish except for $T=S$ by adding side wires that cancel the central coefficient, and wrap the update in a subtract-add-undo schedule.
Why: A few central wires carry a low-rank summary that gives the wanted coordinate plus unwanted contributions from neighbouring coordinates, and the side wires subtract exactly those contributions. (the sources say so: "a low-rank linear summary gives the wanted coordinate plus contributions from known neighboring coordinates, and those contributions are subtracted." (paper))

Its sub-steps, in order:

- Subtracting the effect of the old scratch values, adding the real contribution, then undoing the scratch effect changes the target by a net map independent of the scratch values. (on [Restoring arbitrary scratch values](scratch-restoration.md))
- In the bit case the central coefficient from $x_T$ to $y_S$ is $|S\cap T|\bmod 2$, and the side contribution cancels exactly the intersection-one cases, leaving $y_S\leftarrow y_S+x_S$.
- Three stages (a forward invocation, an inverse one, a forward one) send $(x,y)$ to $(-y,x)$.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A single deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, using fast address interchange and fast simultaneous butterfly layers inside a Gaussian-resampling, chirp and synthetic-transform reduction.; how: Reduce the product to a convolution evaluated through Gaussian-resampled, chirped synthetic transforms, make the two costly tape operations (address movement and butterfly layers) cheaper by a power of the logarithm using networks with a rank saving, and then assemble the algorithm and add up its costs. (on [Integer multiplication below n log n](main-theorem.md))
- There are two fixed linear networks, a bit network over $\mathbb F_2$ and a complex network over $\mathbb Z[i,1/2]$, that exchange two banks of values with prescribed signs, restore every scratch wire, and have total edge rank $s<Wm$.; how: Build a scalar exchange network from subset-intersection gates, attach nested nondegenerate subspaces to its gates so that an edge costs only its dimension change, and count the dimension changes.

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "The construction uses three-element subsets: gather and scatter create coefficients depending on intersection size, and auxiliary wires cancel the unwanted coefficients." (paper)

  > The finite construction in Section~\ref{sec:finite-networks} attaches subspaces of a fixed $m$-dimensional space to its gates and terminals. The subspaces at adjacent vertices are nested. Their dimension changes count the smaller operations needed on an edge, with an additional source contribution in the bit construction. The construction uses three-element subsets: gather and scatter create coefficients depending on intersection size, and auxiliary wires cancel the unwanted coefficients. Orthogonality of the corresponding indicator vectors permits the subspace assignments. The decisive property is that the total number $s$ of smaller calls satisfies $s<Wm$.

- "(X_a,Y_a)\longmapsto(-Y_a,X_a)" (paper)

  > \begin{lemma}\label{lem:scalar-motifs} For arbitrary initial values of its auxiliary wires, a forward invocation changes $y_S$ to $y_S+x_S$ and leaves every other value unchanged. Performing a forward invocation from $X$ to $Y$ at stage 1, the inverse forward schedule from $Y$ to $X$ at stage 2, and a forward invocation from $X$ to $Y$ at stage 3 sends \[ (X_a,Y_a)\longmapsto(-Y_a,X_a) \] and restores every auxiliary value. In the bit construction this is the permutation exchanging $X_a$ and $Y_a$. \end{lemma}
