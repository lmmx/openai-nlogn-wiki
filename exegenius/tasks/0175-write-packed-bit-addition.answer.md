Packed selected-bit addition XORs selected bits of an address chunk $x$ into the same positions of a chunk $y$, restoring a third chunk $z$ used as scratch; the [simultaneous butterfly layer](simultaneous-layer.md) needs it for its binary changes of basis. Cyclic shifts of whole chunks are cheap on tapes but bit selection is not, so the XOR is carried out by additions acting on all selected bits at once, and the few addresses where an addition would carry are repaired separately.

Each address holds an $R$-bit record (this $R$ is not the scalar ring), and an address contains three chunks $x,y,z$ of $L=fK$ bits, where $K\ge6$ is the segment spacing and $f$ is the number of selected bits per chunk; its other coordinates are the spectators. Let $M$ be the number of addresses, $V=MR$, and $A_{\rm rect}=\lceil\log_2(2V)\rceil$ bound the length of every counter. The selected positions are $j_i=\rho+iK$ for $0\le i<f$, with offset $0\le\rho<K$, and the goal is $y_{j_i}\leftarrow y_{j_i}\oplus x_{j_i}$.

For a control bit $c$ and integers $u,w$ (not their meanings on other pages), $c[P]$ is $c$ if $P$ holds and $0$ otherwise. Perform, in order,
$$\begin{array}{llll}
(1)\ u\leftarrow u-c[w\bmod2=0] & (2)\ w\leftarrow w-c[u\bmod2=0] & (3)\ u\leftarrow u+c[w\bmod2=0] & (4)\ w\leftarrow w+c[u\bmod2=0]\\
(5)\ w\leftarrow w+c[u\bmod2=1] & (6)\ u\leftarrow u+c[w\bmod2=1] & (7)\ w\leftarrow w-c[u\bmod2=1] & (8)\ u\leftarrow u-c[w\bmod2=1]
\end{array}$$
For $c=0$ nothing happens. For $c=1$, substituting the four parity pairs of $(u,w)$ shows that lines 1 to 4 permute them by the cycle $(00\ 01\ 10)$ and lines 5 to 8 by $(01\ 10\ 11)$, which compose to $(00\ 10)(01\ 11)$. Hence the output is $(u',w')=(u+c(1-2(u\bmod2)),w)$: the parity of $u$ flips exactly when $c=1$, both quotients by two are preserved, and $w$ is restored. For $u=6$, $w=3$, $c=1$ the pair runs $(6,3),(6,3),(6,2),(7,2),(7,2),(7,3),(8,3),(8,3),(7,3)$. Each variable changes four times by at most one, so it ends within four of its start; eight will suffice below.

Omit $j_{f-1}$ for now. For $0\le i<f-1$ the bits $j_i,\dots,j_i+K-1$ of $y$ form a segment, and these segments are disjoint. Write its value as $u_i=2g_i+a_i$ and that of $z$ as $w_i=2h_i+b_i$, with bits $a_i,b_i$ and guard values $g_i,h_i\in\{0,\dots,2^{K-1}-1\}$; the control is $c_i=x_{j_i}$. Line (1) on all segments at once is
$$y\leftarrow y-\sum_{i=0}^{f-2}2^{j_i}[x_{j_i}=1][z_{j_i}=0]\pmod{2^L},$$
a cyclic shift of the whole chunk $y$. The other lines change the sign, the tested parity, or exchange the roles of $y$ and $z$. The offset never depends on the target chunk, so each line is a bijection of the addresses; let $S_0$ be the composition of the eight.

Let $\mathcal B$, the exceptional addresses, be those where some guard value $g$ (any $g_i$ or $h_i$) fails $10\le g\le2^{K-1}-11$. Off $\mathcal B$ every segment lies in $[20,2^K-21]$, so even displacement eight stays inside $[0,2^K-1]$: no segment carries into or borrows from its neighbour, and $S_0$ equals the ideal map $T_0$ that performs the XORs at $j_0,\dots,j_{f-2}$ and fixes all other coordinates.

Ten values fall at each end of the guard range ($2^{K-1}\ge32$), so there are exactly twenty excluded values per guard <cite data-source="paper">There are exactly twenty excluded values for each guard in</cite>. Fix the spectator coordinates. The chunks $y$ and $z$ then range over all $2^L$ values, so each guard, a block of $K-1$ bits of one of them, takes every value in its range equally often, and a fraction $20/2^{K-1}$ of the addresses fail it. A union bound over the $2(f-1)$ guards gives $|\mathcal B|\le\delta_{\rm bad}M$ with $\delta_{\rm bad}=\min\{1,80(f-1)2^{-K}\}$, whatever the record values.

$T_0$ keeps every guard, so it preserves $\mathcal B$. Two bijections agreeing off $\mathcal B$ give $S_0(\mathcal B^c)=T_0(\mathcal B^c)=\mathcal B^c$, hence $S_0(\mathcal B)=\mathcal B$. After $S_0$ the record at address $q$ came from $S_0^{-1}(q)$ and belongs at $q'=T_0S_0^{-1}(q)$; this map fixes $\mathcal B^c$ and permutes $\mathcal B$, so only records in $\mathcal B$ move, among themselves.

To move them, scan with a record-rank counter (the position of a record in the stream). Dividing the rank by the fixed field lengths recovers the values of $x,y,z$ and their strides, the number of records between consecutive values of that chunk. Test the guards, and extract the records of $\mathcal B$. For each, compute $q'$ by undoing the eight shifts in reverse order with negated offsets, then applying the XORs directly. The destination rank is the current rank plus, for each of the three chunks, its signed value change times its stride. Attach it as a key, padded to at most $A_{\rm rect}$ bits, and mark the vacated place, a hole. Stable binary radix sorting, least significant bit first, touches only extracted records. The keys are distinct and equal the ranks of the holes, since $q'$ permutes $\mathcal B$, so one forward scan refilling the holes in order puts every record at its destination. All integers have $O(A_{\rm rect})$ bits; a schoolbook operation costs $O(A_{\rm rect}^2)$ and up to $f\le A_{\rm rect}$ selected positions are handled, so the arithmetic is $O(A_{\rm rect}^3)$ per record. Finally $j_{f-1}$ is XORed by an [elementary stream operation](stream-ops.md) in $O(V)$.

A rotation needs its target after both control chunks. If it is not the rightmost of the three, swap it there, rotate, and undo the swap: at most two swaps per rotation, hence at most sixteen swaps and eight rotations <cite data-source="paper">There are at most sixteen swaps and eight rotations in $S_0$.</cite>. It rotates chunks by [Controlled shifts and rational scalings](controlled-shifts.md) in $O(V)$ time, an offset depending only on the two control chunks. It swaps chunks by [Fast address-chunk interchange](address-interchange.md) in $O(VL^\tau)$ time, for a fixed exponent $\tau<1$.

Movement costs $O(V(L^\tau+1))$, a fixed number of record scans $O(MA_{\rm rect}^3+V)$, and at most $A_{\rm rect}$ radix passes over $|\mathcal B|$ records of $R+A_{\rm rect}$ bits $O(\delta_{\rm bad}MA_{\rm rect}(R+A_{\rm rect}))$, so the time is $O(V(L^\tau+1)+MA_{\rm rect}^3+\delta_{\rm bad}MA_{\rm rect}(R+A_{\rm rect}))$ <cite data-source="paper">With $\delta_{\rm bad}=\min\{1,80(f-1)2^{-K}\}$, its time is</cite>. Dividing by $V$, the extra terms are $A_{\rm rect}^3/R$ and $80(f-1)2^{-K}A_{\rm rect}(1+A_{\rm rect}/R)$. With $p$ the number of fractional bits, both are $o(1)$ when $A_{\rm rect}=O(p)$, $R$ exceeds every polynomial in $p$, $f\le d\le p$ and $K/\log p\to\infty$, so the cost is $O(V(L^\tau+1))$.
