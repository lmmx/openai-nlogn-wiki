An address shear is the map that adds one of two address fields to the other. It is the costly step of the paper's chunk interchange, so its cost enters the main bound, the time $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ for multiplying $n$-bit integers, where $\lg$ is the base-2 logarithm.

Let $\mathbb{Z}$ be the set of integers. Let $m$ be the number of fields per vector, so $H=(H_1,\dots,H_m)$ and $D=(D_1,\dots,D_m)$. Each field is a block of $b$ digits in radix $q$, a fixed odd prime, <cite data-source="paper">every field ranging over [q b ]</cite> so its values lie in $\mathbb{Z}/q^b\mathbb{Z}$, the integers modulo $q^b$. Here $q$, $b$, $H$ and $D$ change meaning from earlier pages.

Let $M$ be a fixed $m\times m$ matrix with rational entries, and let $\Phi_M$ be the permutation of array entries that moves the entry at address $(H,D)$ to $(H+MD,D)$ and leaves the other fields unchanged. Since $\Phi_{M'}\Phi_M=\Phi_{M+M'}$, $\Phi_{-M}$ inverts $\Phi_M$. The shear is counted by its [rational rank](matrix-rank.md) $a$, the number of linearly independent rows of $M$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. The shear is computed with [modular inverses](modular-inverse.md) of the denominators of $M$. <cite data-source="paper">Choose an odd prime avoiding every denominator</cite> makes them invertible modulo $q$.

Every rational matrix of rank $a$ factors as $M=E_1\Pi E_2$, where $E_1$ and $E_2$ are invertible lower-triangular matrices and $\Pi$ is a $0/1$ matrix with at most one 1 in each row and column. <cite data-source="paper">Every rational m × m matrix A of rank a has a</cite> factorization of this kind, found by lower-triangular elimination, one pivot row and column at a time. <cite data-source="paper">the final partial permutation matrix has exactly a ones.</cite>

The shear runs in three stages: transform $D$ by $E_2$ and $H$ by $E_1^{-1}$. For each 1 at position $(i,j)$ of $\Pi$, update $H_i\leftarrow H_i+D_j$. Transform $H$ by $E_1$ and $D$ by $E_2^{-1}$. The middle stage gives $E_1^{-1}H+\Pi E_2D$, hence $H+MD$, with $D$ unchanged. They are linear because the tape <cite data-source="paper">updates a later field using earlier fields in linear time.</cite>

Each update $H_i\leftarrow H_i+D_j$ is one interchange: set $D_j\leftarrow D_j+H_i$, swap $H_i$ and $D_j$, then set $D_j\leftarrow H_i-D_j$. This maps $(H_i,D_j)$ to $(H_i+D_j,D_j)$; $3$ and $5$ modulo $9$ become $8$ and $5$.

A shear of rank $a$ is carried out by $a$ calls of the [address-field interchange](address-interchange.md) procedure. <cite data-source="paper">Its cost is O(V uτ ) for a fixed τ < 1.</cite> Here $u$, the field width in bits, is a constant multiple of $b$ for fixed $q$ when each digit takes about $\log_2 q$ bits, so the cost is $O(aVb^{\tau})+O(V)$.

The shear is used by the [bit procedure](bit-procedure.md), which interchanges two fields. The shear is computed by the [linear network](linear-network-frames.md) of the [bit procedure](bit-procedure.md), where each vertex $v$ carries a frame $\Phi_{M_v}$. <cite data-source="paper">Thus the intermediate frames cancel</cite>, so each route applies $\Phi_{M_{\mathrm{out}}-M_{\mathrm{in}}}$, which is $\Phi_I$ because <cite data-source="paper">Msink(ρ(w)) − Msource(w) = Im for every input role w.</cite> On every role, the shear is realised as the [endpoint operator](endpoint-operator.md) of the bit network. Composition follows the rules of [linear algebra basics](linear-algebra-basics.md).

The bit network has $W$ wires, splitting the volume into $W$ role streams of volume $V/W$; the number of smaller calls is $s$. <cite data-source="paper">that the total number s of smaller calls satisfies s < W m.</cite> Hence $s/W<m$, so some $\tau<1$ has $s/W<m^{\tau}$. Let $e=m^k$ be the number of digits in a chunk at recursion depth $k$. The time per unit volume grows by at most $s/W$ per level, so a chunk of $e$ digits takes $O(Ve^{\tau})$, below the $O(Ve)$ of digit-by-digit movement.
