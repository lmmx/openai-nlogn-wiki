A cyclic coordinate is one axis of a multidimensional [cyclic convolution](cyclic-convolution.md), with its own cyclic length. The proof uses these axes twice: radix-digit coefficients are placed on several prime axes by a [Chinese-remainder map](chinese-remainder-map.md) <cite data-source="paper">a Chinese-remainder map that preserves convolution</cite>, and a [phase twist](phase-twist.md) acts on the last axis.

Let $\sigma$ name one coordinate, running from $1$ to $d$. Let $n_\sigma$ be the cyclic length of coordinate $\sigma$, the number of positions along that axis before indices wrap around. Arithmetic in coordinate $\sigma$ is taken modulo $n_\sigma$; the lengths need not be equal.

Take $d=2$, $n_1=3$ and $n_2=4$. The positions $(2,3)$ and $(1,1)$ add to $(0,0)$, since $2+1=3\equiv 0 \pmod 3$ and $3+1=4\equiv 0 \pmod 4$. Each coordinate wraps on its own.

The lengths are set by the [padded transform box](padded-transform-box.md) of the paper's Section 9.2.1, where each length is $r/2$ or $r$. Let $\varkappa$ be the number of coordinates of length $r/2$. The box holds $T$ entries, the box size, a power of two ($T$ here, not the three-element subsets of other pages). The product of the lengths is $(r/2)^{\varkappa}r^{d-\varkappa}=r^d/2^{\varkappa}$, so $\varkappa=d\log_2 r-\log_2 T$. The paper takes $r=2^{\lceil \log_2 T/d\rceil}$, so $d\log_2 r$ lies in $[\log_2 T,\ \log_2 T+d)$ and $\varkappa$ is an integer in $[0,d)$: <cite data-source="paper">is an integer in [0, d), so at least one axis has length r; choose it as the last axis.</cite> Thus fewer than $d$ coordinates are shortened, so one has length $r$; it is taken last: $n_d=r$. The twist acts on this coordinate, which must have length $r$ to match $y^r+1$.

The last coordinate then becomes polynomial: <cite data-source="paper">A phase twist changes the last cyclic coordinate into a polynomial</cite> modulo $y^r+1$. Let $\zeta=e^{\pi i/r}$, a phase factor with $\zeta^r=-1$. Let $\upsilon$ be a position on the last axis, an integer from $0$ to $r-1$. The twist is diagonal, <cite data-source="paper">multiplying coefficient k by the nonzero number</cite> $\zeta^\upsilon$, so it multiplies the coefficient at position $\upsilon$ by $\zeta^\upsilon$. Let $\upsilon'$ be a second such position. If $\upsilon+\upsilon'\ge r$, let $\upsilon''$ be the reduced position, so $\upsilon+\upsilon'=\upsilon''+r$ with $0\le\upsilon''<r$. The product of the two twisted monomials is $\zeta^{\upsilon+\upsilon'}y^{\upsilon+\upsilon'}=(-\zeta^{\upsilon''})(-y^{\upsilon''})$, since $\zeta^r=-1$ and $y^r=-1$. The two minus signs cancel, leaving $\zeta^{\upsilon''}y^{\upsilon''}$ with no sign. <cite data-source="paper">Untwisting therefore recovers the cyclic coefficient with the correct sign.</cite>
