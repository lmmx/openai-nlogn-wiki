````markdown
# Polynomial ring modulo $y^r + 1$

The polynomial [ring](ring.md) modulo one more than a power of the variable is the set of complex polynomials in which that power is identified with $-1$, so that wrap-around flips a sign. One coordinate of the array is stored in this ring, and the [convolution over this ring](polynomial-ring-convolution.md) is built on it.

Here $y$ is the polynomial variable, an indeterminate (a symbol with no numeric value) whose powers act as roots of unity. Here $r$ is the record length, the number of complex coefficients in a polynomial record, which is a power of two. We write $R_r=\mathbb{C}[y]/(y^r+1)$ for this set, in which two polynomials are identified when they differ by a multiple of $y^r+1$.

In the argument, one coordinate of the array is stored.

Every polynomial in $y$ denotes an element of $R_r$ and reduces to a representative of that element: a polynomial of degree less than $r$. The degree is the largest exponent with a nonzero coefficient. To reduce, replace each power $y^\delta$ with $\delta\ge r$ by $-y^{\delta-r}$, which is valid because $y^\delta=y^{\delta-r}\,y^r=-y^{\delta-r}$. Repeating this gives a representative of degree less than $r$, and this representative is unique. Suppose two representatives of degree less than $r$ denote the same element. Their difference is then $\theta\,(y^r+1)$ for some polynomial $\theta$. If $\theta\neq 0$, this product has degree $\deg\theta+r\ge r$, since $y^r+1$ has leading coefficient $1$. But the difference of two polynomials of degree less than $r$ has degree less than $r$, a contradiction. So $\theta=0$, and the two representatives are equal.

Each element $P$ of $R_r$ has a representative of degree less than $r$. Here $e$ is the position of a coefficient, running from $0$ to $r-1$, and $d_e$ is the complex coefficient of $y^e$ in $P$, so the representative is $\sum_{e=0}^{r-1} d_e\,y^e$. The list $(d_0,\dots,d_{r-1})$ is the coefficient list of $P$. Addition is coefficientwise, and $R_r$ satisfies the axioms of a [ring](ring.md).

A root of unity in the ring is an element $\tau$ for which $\tau^\nu=1$ for some positive integer $\nu$, the number of factors of $\tau$ whose product is $1$. The order of $\tau$ is the least such $\nu$. Since $y^r=-1$, we get $y^{2r}=(-1)^2=1$. No smaller positive power of $y$ equals $1$. For $0<e<r$, $y^e$ is a representative of degree $e\ge 1$, while $1$ has degree $0$, so $y^e\neq 1$ by uniqueness. For $r\le e<2r$, $y^e=-y^{e-r}$ is a representative of degree $e-r<r$. It is not $1$: it equals $-1$ when $e=r$, and has degree at least $1$ otherwise. Hence $y$ has order $2r$.

Take $r=4$. Then $y^4=-1$, and $P=1+2y$ has coefficient list $(1,2,0,0)$. Multiplying by $y^3$ gives $Py^3=y^3+2y^4=y^3-2$, with coefficient list $(-2,0,0,1)$. The coefficient $1$ at position $0$ moved to position $3$. The coefficient $2$ at position $1$ would move to position $4$, which is position $0$ with its sign reversed, giving $-2$ there.

In general, let $s$ be a shift amount with $0\le s<r$. Multiplying $P$ by $y^s$ gives $\sum_{e} d_e\,y^{e+s}$. If $e+s<r$, the coefficient $d_e$ stays unchanged and moves to position $e+s$. If $e+s\ge r$, then $y^{e+s}=-y^{e+s-r}$, so $d_e$ moves to position $e+s-r$ with its sign reversed. So multiplication by a power of $y$ permutes the coefficient list and negates the coefficients that wrap around. Generally, multiplication by a power of $y$ permutes the coefficients and. This wrap-around, which reverses the sign of each coefficient passing the end, is why the ring is called negacyclic. In a [cyclic convolution](cyclic-convolution.md), a coefficient that passes the end re-enters at the start with its sign unchanged.

Why this helps: a transform multiplies its entries by roots of unity, and multiplying by a general complex root costs complex multiplications at every level. Here the roots are powers of $y$: they serve, and multiplication by such a root coefficient list. A signed shift does no complex arithmetic. Each coefficient is copied to a new position, and a sign reversal changes only the sign carried with the copied value. So the sign reversals from wrap-around cost nothing beyond the data movement that the shift already requires, and multiplying by a root of unity costs only data movement.

This ring gives the arithmetic for the [synthetic polynomial transforms](synthetic-transforms.md), whose roots of unity are powers of $y$.
````
