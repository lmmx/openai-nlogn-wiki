## This page

Concept: Retained permutation cancellation
Gloss: Leaving the coordinate permutations of resampling inside the transform identity, so the source permutation cancels across the two forward transforms and the opposite one and none is executed as a sort.

## This page in the argument

The step: The Fourier transforms of pairwise coprime prime lengths $s_i$ that the integer product needs are computed through power-of-two lengths $t_i$ by Gaussian resampling and Bluestein chirps, with the source and target coordinate permutations left inside the transform identities, so that none is executed as a sort.
How: Retain both permutations of Gaussian resampling in the transform formula: the target permutation $Q$ is absorbed by the chirp convolution, and the source permutation $R$ cancels across the two forward transforms, their pointwise product and the opposite transform.
Why: The usual implementation of Gaussian resampling sorts twice on each coordinate; keeping the permutations in the formula avoids the sorts, which would cost $\Theta(tp\log t)$ per line. (the sources say so: "connects these two shapes. Its usual implementation sorts twice on each" (paper))
Striking feature: No coordinate permutation is ever executed: the source permutation $R$ appears on both forward transforms and the opposite one and cancels, and the target permutation $Q$ is absorbed by the chirp convolution. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- For coprime $s<t$ with $\theta=t/s-1>p/\alpha^4$ and permutations $(P_su)_j=u_{tj}$, $(P_tv)_k=v_{-sk}$ there are linear contractions $A$, $B_0$ with $P_sF_s=2^{2\alpha^2}B_0P_tF_tA$, computed with error less than $p^2$ in time $O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$ without executing $P_s$ or $P_t$. (on [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md))
- Applied one coordinate line at a time to a padded box of $T=\prod_it_i$ entries, with $\gamma=2d\alpha^2$, $R F_{\boldsymbol s}=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A$, both $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B_0$ are contractions with error less than $dp^2$, computed in $O(dTp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$ plus $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$ for exposing lines.
- For even $t_i$ and the chirp $a_{\boldsymbol j}=\exp(-\pi i\sum_is_ij_i^2/t_i)$ one has $QF_{\boldsymbol t}u=\overline a\cdot\bigl(a*(\overline a\cdot u)\bigr)/T$, so the target permutation $Q$ is absorbed into a cyclic convolution of length $T$ and never executed. (on [Bluestein chirp identity](chirp.md))
- The retained source permutation cancels: $(RF_{\boldsymbol s}^+)[(RF_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(RF_{\boldsymbol s}v)]=u*v/S^2$ with $S=\prod_is_i$, so no source permutation is executed across the two forward transforms, their pointwise product and the opposite transform.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A single deterministic Turing machine with a fixed finite alphabet and a fixed finite number of one-dimensional tapes multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ worst-case time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, disproving the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture of Sch\"onhage and Strassen in this model.; how: Build two power-saving tape procedures (chunk interchange and simultaneous butterfly layer) from fixed finite linear networks; use them to accelerate the synthetic polynomial transforms and ring convolution; use Gaussian resampling with retained permutations and chirps to reduce the prime-length cyclic convolution of the radix-digit coefficients to those power-of-two transforms; assemble the exact product with a proof that the error is below $1/2$; and total the cost to show a power of $\log n$ is saved. (on [Power-saving integer multiplication](main-result.md))

Retained permutation cancellation computes prime-length Fourier transforms through power-of-two ones with no coordinate permutation executed as a sort: the permutations of Gaussian resampling stay in the transform formula, where one is absorbed by a convolution and the other cancels.

Take $d$ axes with pairwise coprime prime lengths $s_i$ and power-of-two lengths $t_i>s_i$. Let $F_{\boldsymbol q}=\bigotimes_iF_{q_i}$ be the normalized [discrete Fourier transform](dft.md) along every axis, $F_{\boldsymbol q}^+$ its opposite, $S=\prod_is_i$ and $T=\prod_it_i$. Each prime-length transform is computed through a power-of-two one by [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md). Its usual implementation sorts twice on each coordinate <cite data-source="paper">Its usual implementation sorts twice on each</cite>.

Let $p$ be the bit precision of each coordinate, $\alpha$ an integer width of the Gaussian weights, and $\delta$ a fixed exponent. For one axis with coprime $s<t$, let $(P_su)_j=u_{tj}$ and $(P_tv)_k=v_{-sk}$. The resampling page gives linear contractions $A$, $B_0$ with $P_sF_s=2^{2\alpha^2}B_0P_tF_tA$, computed with error less than $p^2$ in time $O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$ without executing $P_s$ or $P_t$.

Tensoring over the axes, put $R=\bigotimes_iP_{s_i}$, $Q=\bigotimes_iP_{t_i}$, $\mathcal A=\bigotimes_iA_i$, $\mathcal B_0=\bigotimes_iB_{0,i}$. The factors multiply, so $RF_{\boldsymbol s}=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A$, where $\gamma=2d\alpha^2$ <cite data-source="paper">=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A,</cite>. Both $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B_0$ are contractions, each applied one line (entries along one axis) at a time in a padded box of $T$ entries. Axis $i$ has $T/t_i$ lines of cost $O(t_ip^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$. Errors and times add over $d$ passes: error less than $dp^2$, time $O(dTp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$ with a constant independent of $d$. Exposing each axis as the innermost field uses the [fast address-chunk interchange](address-interchange.md), which swaps chunks of $w$ bits within $V$ bits in $O(Vw^\tau)$ time for a fixed $\tau<1$. With every $t_i\in\{t_{\max}/2,t_{\max}\}$, $t_{\max}=2^\ell$, and entries of $O(p)$ bits, exposing and restoring all lines costs $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$ <cite data-source="paper">then exposing and restoring all the lines costs</cite>. Since $t_i\ge t_{\max}/2$, at most $\sum_iT/t_i\le2dT/t_{\max}$ lines occur, so setup of $O(p^c)$ per line totals $O(dTp^c/t_{\max})=o(Tp)$ once $t_{\max}$ outgrows every power of $p$.

The target permutation $Q$ is absorbed by the [Bluestein chirp identity](chirp.md), which for even $t_i$ and $a_{\boldsymbol j}=\exp(-\pi i\sum_is_ij_i^2/t_i)$ gives $QF_{\boldsymbol t}u=\overline a\cdot\bigl(a*(\overline a\cdot u)\bigr)/T$, with $*$ the [cyclic convolution](convolution.md) and a dot coordinatewise multiplication. So $Q$ becomes part of a convolution of length $T$ and is never executed.

The source permutation $R$ cancels: $(RF_{\boldsymbol s}^+)[(RF_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(RF_{\boldsymbol s}v)]=u*v/S^2$. For a unit $c$ modulo $q$, let $(R_cz)_j=z_{cj}$. Substituting $h=ck$ in the Fourier sum gives $F_q^+R_c=R_c^{-1}F_q^+$. Each $t_i$ is a unit modulo $s_i$, so tensoring gives $RF_{\boldsymbol s}^+R=F_{\boldsymbol s}^+$. A permutation commutes with pointwise products, so the left side is $RF_{\boldsymbol s}^+R(F_{\boldsymbol s}u\cdot F_{\boldsymbol s}v)=F_{\boldsymbol s}^+(F_{\boldsymbol s}u\cdot F_{\boldsymbol s}v)$. The normalized convolution rule is $F_{\boldsymbol s}(u*v)=S(F_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(F_{\boldsymbol s}v)$ and $F_{\boldsymbol s}^+F_{\boldsymbol s}=I/S$, so this equals $\frac1SF_{\boldsymbol s}^+F_{\boldsymbol s}(u*v)=u*v/S^2$.
