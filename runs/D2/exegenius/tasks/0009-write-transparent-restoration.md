Concept: Restoring scratch values (transparent computation)
Gloss: The schedule subtracts old scratch contributions, performs the operation, and undoes it so arbitrary initial auxiliary values cancel.

## This page in the argument

The step: For arbitrary initial scratch values, the scalar network exchanges the two banks with a sign and restores every scratch value.
How: schedule the operation, then undo the unwanted scratch contributions so arbitrary initial values cancel
Why: The tape machine cannot assume scratch arrays are zero, so the net effect must be independent of their initial contents. (the sources leave this for the reader to work out: spell it out)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- A forward invocation changes $y_S$ to $y_S+x_S$ for arbitrary scratch values; three stages exchange the banks and restore scratch.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

**S1.2a**: A forward invocation changes $y_S$ to $y_S+x_S$ for arbitrary scratch values; three stages exchange the banks and restore scratch.
Source: "For arbitrary initial values of its auxiliary wires, a forward" (paper)
> \begin{lemma}\label{lem:scalar-motifs} For arbitrary initial values of its auxiliary wires, a forward invocation changes $y_S$ to $y_S+x_S$ and leaves every other value unchanged. Performing a forward invocation from $X$ to $Y$ at stage 1, the inverse forward schedule from $Y$ to $X$ at stage 2, and a forward invocation from $X$ to $Y$ at stage 3 sends \[ (X_a,Y_a)\longmapsto(-Y_a,X_a) \] and restores every auxiliary value. In the bit construction this is the permutation exchanging $X_a$ and $Y_a$. \end{lemma}
