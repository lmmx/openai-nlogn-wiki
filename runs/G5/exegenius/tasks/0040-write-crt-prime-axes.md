## This page

Concept: Chinese-remainder map onto distinct prime axes
Gloss: A ring isomorphism sends a length-S cyclic convolution to a multidimensional cyclic convolution on pairwise coprime axes s_i; on tape it is a sequence of triangular controlled shifts.
Also called: Chinese remainder address map

## This page in the argument

The step: A ring isomorphism sends the length-$S$ cyclic convolution to a cyclic convolution on the pairwise coprime axes $s_i$, and on tape it is a sequence of triangular controlled shifts costing $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$.
How: Use the Chinese remainder map x to the product of x_i^(mu_i), whose coordinates are triangular in the mixed-radix digits, and implement each triangular update as a controlled cyclic shift
Why: The coordinates of the map depend only on lower-index digits, so with the axes reordered each coordinate is a shift controlled by fields that precede it, which is the linear-time case of controlled shifts. (the sources say so: "sequence of controlled cyclic shifts." (paper))

Its sub-steps, in order:

- The assignment $x\mapsto\prod_i x_i^{\mu_i}$, with $\mu_i=P_i^{-1}\bmod s_i$, is an isomorphism that preserves cyclic convolution.
- In mixed-radix digits $a_j$ the coordinate on axis $i$ is $b_i=a_i+\mu_i\sum_{j<i}a_jP_j \pmod{s_i}$, which depends only on lower digits.
- Each triangular update is a prefix-controlled cyclic shift of the valid target interval, so padding stays zero and the map and its inverse cost $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$ with the axis reordering.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- One deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: Give the multiplication algorithm and show its output is exact, then total its cost stage by stage (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time](main-theorem.md))
- The algorithm, with digits placed on prime axes by the Chinese-remainder map, transformed by Gaussian resampling, chirp convolution and polynomial-ring convolution, multiplied pointwise, transformed back, rescaled, rounded and carry-propagated, outputs the exact $2n$-bit product.; how: Reduce the product to a cyclic convolution of digit lists and evaluate it with Fourier transforms, building from the fastest inner primitive (convolution over a polynomial ring) outward through the resampling, prime-axis and twist reductions, then bound the error so that rounding is exact (on [The multiplication algorithm](multiplication-algorithm.md))

**S1.6.1**: The assignment $x\mapsto\prod_i x_i^{\mu_i}$, with $\mu_i=P_i^{-1}\bmod s_i$, is an isomorphism that preserves cyclic convolution.
Source: "$\Phi$ is an isomorphism and preserves cyclic convolution." (paper)
> Let $s_1,\ldots,s_d$ be the pairwise coprime source lengths, let $S=\prod_i s_i$, and put \[ P_i=\prod_{j<i}s_j,\qquad \mu_i=P_i^{-1}\pmod{s_i}. \] Here $P_i$ denotes an integer prefix product, not a Fourier-coordinate permutation. The assignment \[ \Phi:\mathbb C[x]/(x^S-1)\longrightarrow \mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_d]/(x_1^{s_1}-1,\ldots,x_d^{s_d}-1), \qquad x\longmapsto\prod_i x_i^{\mu_i} \] respects $x^S=1$, so it defines an algebra map. The target monomial group has size $S$, and the image of $x$ has order $S$, because each $\mu_i$ is a unit and the $s_i$ are pairwise coprime. Its powers therefore run through all target basis monomials exactly once. Hence $\Phi$ is an isomorphism and preserves cyclic convolution.

**S1.6.2**: In mixed-radix digits $a_j$ the coordinate on axis $i$ is $b_i=a_i+\mu_i\sum_{j<i}a_jP_j \pmod{s_i}$, which depends only on lower digits.
Source: "Indeed terms with $j>i$ are divisible by $s_i$, and $\mu_iP_i=1$ modulo" (paper)
> Every ordinary coefficient index has a unique mixed-radix expression \[ k=\sum_{j=1}^d a_jP_j,\qquad 0\le a_j<s_j. \] The coordinate of $\Phi(x^k)$ in axis $i$ is \begin{equation}\label{eq:triangular-crt} b_i=a_i+\mu_i\sum_{j<i}a_jP_j\pmod{s_i}. \end{equation} Indeed terms with $j>i$ are divisible by $s_i$, and $\mu_iP_i=1$ modulo $s_i$. Numerical order of $k$ is lexicographic order of $(a_d,\ldots,a_1)$. Insert the padding in one nested scan of the full box $\prod_i[0,t_i)$ in this lexicographic order, with $a_d$ the outermost counter and $a_1$ the innermost. At an address satisfying $a_i<s_i$ for every $i$, copy the next original coefficient record; at every other address, write a zero record. The valid addresses occur in the original coefficient order, so each record is copied to its intended mixed-radix address. To delete the padding after the inverse axis reorder, use the same enumeration, copying the record at each valid address and skipping the record at each invalid address.

**S1.6.3**: Each triangular update is a prefix-controlled cyclic shift of the valid target interval, so padding stays zero and the map and its inverse cost $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$ with the axis reordering.
Source: "Each triangular update is a prefix-controlled cyclic shift." (paper)
> Each triangular update is a prefix-controlled cyclic shift. The controls precede its target in the current physical order. For each prefix, prepare the offset, split the valid target interval into its two consecutive pieces, and merge those pieces in the opposite order. Every target position carries its full spectator suffix as a block; the invalid interval is copied unchanged. There are at most $T/t_i$ control prefixes for target $i$. For any fixed setup degree $C$, all offsets therefore cost $O(dTp^C/r)=o(Tp)$ to prepare, since $t_i\ge r/2$ and $r$ exceeds every fixed power of $p$.
