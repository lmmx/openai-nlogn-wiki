## This page

Concept: Multidimensional transform and cyclic convolution on a product of axes
Gloss: The tensor product of one-dimensional DFTs along several axes of a box of coordinates, acting on arrays indexed by a product of cyclic groups.

## Where the argument relies on this page

- The normalized transforms $F_t^\pm$ over $\mathcal R_r=\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ with synthetic roots $\omega_t=y^{2r/t}$ are computed by radix-two butterflies plus monomial twiddles, and $F_t^+F_t^-=t^{-1}I$ even though the ring has zero divisors. (on [Synthetic polynomial transform](synthetic-transform.md))
- Applying the resampling identity along each of $d$ axes of a padded power-of-two box gives $R F_{\boldsymbol s}=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0 QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A$ with line-map cost $O(dTp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$ and data movement $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$. (on [Tensor interface for resampling](tensor-resampling.md))
- For the chirp $a_{\boldsymbol j}=\exp(-\pi i\sum_i s_ij_i^2/t_i)$ one has $QF_{\boldsymbol t}u=\overline a\cdot\bigl(a*(\overline a\cdot u)\bigr)/T$, so the middle transform with its frequency permutation is a normalized cyclic convolution between two phase products. (on [Bluestein chirp identity](bluestein-chirp.md))
- The retained source permutation $R$ cancels across the two forward source transforms, their pointwise product and the opposite-sign transform: $(RF_{\boldsymbol s}^+)\bigl[(RF_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(RF_{\boldsymbol s}v)\bigr]=(u*v)/S^2$. (on [Cancellation of the source permutation](source-permutation-cancel.md))
- With pairwise coprime $s_i$ the map $x\mapsto\prod_i x_i^{\mu_i}$, $\mu_i=P_i^{-1}\bmod s_i$, is an isomorphism from cyclic convolution of length $S=\prod_i s_i$ to multidimensional cyclic convolution on the prime axes, implemented by triangular controlled shifts on padded axes in $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$. (on [Chinese-remainder map to prime cyclic axes](crt-address-map.md))

## Pages that build on this one

- Synthetic polynomial transform: The normalized transforms F_t^± over the polynomial ring using synthetic roots, computed by radix-two butterflies plus monomial twiddles (Nussbaumer–Quandalle); character cancellation gives F⁺F⁻ = I/t even though the ring has zero divisors.
- Tensor interface for resampling: The resampling identity is applied along each of d axes (lines) of a padded power-of-two box, with axes exposed and restored by chunk interchanges; total cost O(dTp^{3/2+δ}α) for the line maps plus O(Tp d²(1+ℓ^τ)) for data movement.
- Cyclic convolution and the convolution theorem: Convolution of arrays indexed by cyclic groups; the DFT turns it into pointwise multiplication (up to normalization), so convolution can be computed by transform, pointwise product, inverse transform.
- Bluestein chirp identity: A DFT (here with a frequency permutation) equals pre-multiplication by a chirp phase, a cyclic convolution with the chirp, and post-multiplication by the conjugate chirp, so the middle transform becomes a normalized cyclic convolution.
- Cancellation of the source permutation: The permutation R retained from resampling cancels across the two forward source transforms, their pointwise product and the opposite-sign transform: (R F⁺)[(R F u)·(R F v)] = (u*v)/S².

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "F_{\boldsymbol t}^\pm=\bigotimes_{i=1}^D F_{t_i}^\pm," (paper)

  > For $D$ axes of lengths $t_1,\ldots,t_D$, put \[ M=\prod_{i=1}^D t_i,\qquad F_{\boldsymbol t}^\pm=\bigotimes_{i=1}^D F_{t_i}^\pm, \qquad B=\bigotimes_{i=1}^D B_{t_i}. \] A round applies one current $H_0$ on each participating axis. The twiddle for axis $i$ depends only on that axis's address bits. The two entries paired by another axis's butterfly have the same axis-$i$ address, so that twiddle commutes with the other butterfly. Consequently the individual axis steps may be grouped as a parallel butterfly $\mathcal H$, followed by one recordwise monomial operation $J_E$. At each address, $J_E$ multiplies the record by \begin{equation} y^{E},\qquad E=-\sum_{i\ \mathrm{participating}} \frac{2r}{u_i}\,b_i k_i\pmod{2r}. \label{eq:combined-twiddle} \end{equation} Here $u_i$ is that axis's current recursive length, $b_i$ its newly written branch bit, and $0\leq k_i<u_i/2$ is encoded by its still unprocessed named slots. The exponent depends on the address, not on coefficient values. In the opposite direction the grouped round is $\mathcal HJ_E^{-1}$: apply $y^{-E}$ first and the parallel butterfly second. Each $\mathcal H$ is a contraction and each $J_E$ is an isometry, so every completed round is a contraction.
