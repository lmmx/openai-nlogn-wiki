Convolution is the operation that takes two finite sequences and returns a third, in which each entry sums the products of the pairs of input entries that combine to it. Convolution is the computation behind [Exact integer multiplication](exact-integer-multiplication.md) that the method below relies on. The [discrete Fourier transform](discrete-fourier-transform.md) computes it quickly.

Let $a$ and $b$ be two input sequences, and let $n$ be the number of entries in each, so $a=(a_0,\dots,a_{n-1})$ and $b=(b_0,\dots,b_{n-1})$. On earlier pages $n$ was an input length; here it has this new meaning. Here $t$ is the position of an output entry, and $i$ and $j$ are positions of input entries, with $i$ in $a$ and $j$ in $b$. The convolution $c=a*b$ is the sequence of length $2n-1$ whose $t$-th entry is

$$c_t=\sum_{\substack{i+j=t\\ 0\le i,j\le n-1}} a_i\,b_j,\qquad 0\le t\le 2n-2.$$

Each product $a_i\,b_j$ pairs one entry of $a$ with one entry of $b$, and $c_t$ collects all pairs whose positions add to $t$.

Take $n=2$, $a=(1,2)$ and $b=(3,4)$. Then $c_0=a_0b_0=3$, $c_1=a_0b_1+a_1b_0=4+6=10$, and $c_2=a_1b_1=8$, so $c=(3,10,8)$. If the positions are instead added modulo $n$, the result is the [cyclic convolution](cyclic-convolution.md) of the same sequences, here $(11,10)$, because $c_0=a_0b_0+a_1b_1=3+8=11$.

The method of the paper "Integer multiplication below n log n" (OpenAI, 2026) uses this operation to multiply integers, after splitting each input into radix digits (the digits of the input in a fixed base): <cite data-source="paper">Splitting the inputs into radix digits reduces multiplication to convolution of short integer coefficients.</cite>
