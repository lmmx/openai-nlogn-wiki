## This page

Concept: Nested subspace labels and the rank deficit
Gloss: Gates and terminals are labeled by nested subspaces of an m-dimensional space built from tensor powers of triple indicator vectors; the total dimension change over edges is below W·m (W = number of wires), which is the strict saving that powers the recursion.

## This page in the argument

The step: Gates and terminals can be labelled by nested nondegenerate subspaces of the $m$-dimensional space $\mathcal F=F^{\otimes 3}$, built from tensor powers of triple indicator vectors, so that the total dimension change over the edges of a network with $W$ wires is below $W\cdot m$.
How: Label the data wires stage by stage with tensor products of triple lines and their orthogonal complements, order the gates so that every decrease is confined to the central wires' return, and count residual dimensions edge by edge.
Why: The cost of an edge will be its dimension change, so the labels must keep the total dimension lost on decreasing edges small relative to the number of data banks. (the sources say so: "The saving will come from keeping the total dimension lost on decreasing" (paper))
Striking feature: The total number of smaller calls is strictly below $W\cdot m$, although the network moves a full $m$-dimensional label on every wire: only the central wires ever lose dimension, and their total loss $L$ stays below half of the number $N$ of data banks. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- Every edge joins comparable nondegenerate labels, and the only decreasing edges are the central-wire edges from time 3 to time 4, each losing dimension $h$.
- The loss ratios $L_{\rm b}/N=100/539$ and $L_{\rm c}/N=101/539$ are below $1/2$, which leaves a strict saving once the extra source ranks are counted.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A single deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: Reduce multiplication to convolution over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ through radix digits, Chinese-remainder axes, Gaussian resampling and chirps, speed up the data movement and the butterfly arithmetic with two network-based tape procedures with a strict rank saving, recover the exact product by rounding and carries, and add up the costs. (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time](main-theorem.md))
- Two tape procedures exist for fixed exponents $\tau,\lambda'<1$: one interchanges two disjoint address chunks of $u$ bits in $O(Vu^\tau)$ time, and one applies a normalized butterfly layer on up to $d$ address chunks in $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ time, where $V$ is the logical bit volume.; how: Obtain both from one fixed linear network that exchanges two banks of values and restores every auxiliary value, whose edge frame changes are smaller calls of the same procedure; the strict inequality $s<Wm$ on their number gives a recursion with a power saving.
- There are two fixed linear networks, one over bits and one over Gaussian dyadics, that exchange two banks of values while restoring every scratch wire, and whose array versions have total frame-change rank $s$ strictly below $W\cdot m$ for $W$ wires and an $m$-dimensional label space.; how: Build the scalar exchange network first, lift it to arrays with frames, then choose nested subspace labels that make the frame changes cheap, and read off the bit and complex interfaces.

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "The decisive property is that the total
number $s$ of smaller calls satisfies $s<Wm$." (paper)

  > The finite construction in Section~\ref{sec:finite-networks} attaches subspaces of a fixed $m$-dimensional space to its gates and terminals. The subspaces at adjacent vertices are nested. Their dimension changes count the smaller operations needed on an edge, with an additional source contribution in the bit construction. The construction uses three-element subsets: gather and scatter create coefficients depending on intersection size, and auxiliary wires cancel the unwanted coefficients. Orthogonality of the corresponding indicator vectors permits the subspace assignments. The decisive property is that the total number $s$ of smaller calls satisfies $s<Wm$.

- "the \emph{orthogonal residual} is the orthogonal complement of the smaller label inside
the larger one" (paper)

  > The scalar network already has the required signed exchange. We now choose its intermediate labels to make the changes of frame inexpensive. A label is a nondegenerate subspace of a fixed bilinear space. On every edge one label will contain the other. For such an edge, the \emph{orthogonal residual} is the orthogonal complement of the smaller label inside the larger one; its dimension is the absolute change in label dimension. The saving will come from keeping the total dimension lost on decreasing edges small relative to $N$.
