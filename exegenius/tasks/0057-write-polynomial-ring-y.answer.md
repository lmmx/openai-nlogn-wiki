The polynomial ring modulo a power-of-two length is the ring in which the last cyclic coordinate of the transform, the one convolved cyclically, is stored, so that wrap-around becomes a sign change. Here $y$ is the variable of the ring, whose powers serve as roots of unity in the remaining axes, and $r$ is the number of coefficients in the coefficient list of one polynomial record, a power of two. The ring is the set of polynomials in $y$ in which $y^r$ is replaced by $-1$, so each element is stored as its list of $r$ complex coefficients, from degree $0$ up to degree $r-1$.

Adding two elements adds their lists entrywise. In this ring, products are computed by a signed form of [Cyclic convolution](cyclic-convolution.md): the coefficient lists are convolved, and any term that wraps past index $r-1$ returns with a minus sign.

Take $r=4$ and $1+2y+3y^2+4y^3$, stored as $(1,2,3,4)$. Multiplying by $y$ gives $y+2y^2+3y^3+4y^4$, and since $y^4=-1$ this equals $-4+y+2y^2+3y^3$, so $(1,2,3,4)$ becomes $(-4,1,2,3)$.

The step applies a phase twist, which multiplies the $j$-th entry of a coefficient list by $\theta^j$, where $\theta$ is a complex $2r$-th root of unity with $\theta^r=-1$. This makes the cyclic coordinate a polynomial modulo $y^r+1$. Cyclic convolution of length $r$ works modulo $x^r-1$, where $x$ is the formal variable and $x^r$ is replaced by $1$. Substituting $x=\theta y$ gives $(\theta y)^r-1=-(y^r+1)$, so the rule $x^r=1$ becomes $y^r=-1$. The substitution $x\mapsto\theta y$ preserves sums and products and sends multiples of $x^r-1$ to multiples of $y^r+1$, so products of twisted lists are the twists of cyclic convolutions.

Multiplying by $y$ moves each entry up one place, and the entry at degree $r-1$ wraps to degree $0$ with a minus sign, as in the example. Since $y^k=-y^{k-r}$ for $r\le k<2r$ and $y^{2r}=1$, every power of $y$ acts in this way, applied some number of times. Hence multiplication by a power of $y$ is a signed cyclic shift of the list <cite data-source="paper">multiplication by such a root is a signed shift of the coefficient list</cite>, and the twisted coordinate, multiplied by powers of $y$ in the remaining axes, needs no general multiplication.
