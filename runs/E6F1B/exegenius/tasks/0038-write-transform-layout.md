## This page

Concept: Synthetic transform layout and stored frequency order
Gloss: Places selected bits of all participating axes at a common offset in consecutive chunks so each radix-two round is one simultaneous layer, and keeps the bit-reversed frequency order through the pointwise product to the opposite transform.

## This page in the argument

The step: The normalized synthetic transform on arrays of polynomials modulo $y^r+1$ (and its opposite) runs in $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}])$ time, leaving frequencies in bit-reversed order that the opposite transform consumes.
How: arrange the selected bits of all participating axes at a common offset so that each radix-two round is one simultaneous layer
Why: The simultaneous layer applies one butterfly bit position across many chunks, so the layout must line up the same bit of every axis in consecutive chunks. (the sources say so: "we must place the selected bits of the participating axes at a common" (paper))
Striking feature: The forward transform never reorders its output: the bit-reversed order is kept through the pointwise product and consumed by the opposite transform, so no sorting pass is executed. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- Taking $\omega_t=y^{2r/t}$ as root of unity in the ring, the length-$t$ transforms are contractions and $F_t^+F_t^-=t^{-1}I$, with twiddle multiplication a signed shift of coefficients. (on [Synthetic polynomial transform](synthetic-transform.md))
- After all rounds the output entry $j$ sits in slot $\operatorname{rev}_a(j)$, and the opposite procedure traverses the recursion in reverse to consume this stored order.
- One round on $D$ axes is a parallel butterfly followed by one recordwise monomial multiplication $y^E$ whose exponent depends only on the address.
- An explicit positional layout permutation, built from single-bit moves and chunk interchanges, makes each round one call of the simultaneous layer, with cost charged to the transform.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- There is one deterministic fixed-alphabet, fixed-tape Turing machine multiplying $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, disproving the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture in this model.; how: build two fast tape procedures from linear networks with a rank saving, insert them into a Gaussian-resampling and chirp reduction of the digit convolution to a ring convolution, and balance all parameters (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time](main-theorem.md))

**S5.2**: After all rounds the output entry $j$ sits in slot $\operatorname{rev}_a(j)$, and the opposite procedure traverses the recursion in reverse to consume this stored order.
Source: "After all $a$ rounds, the named slots from high to low therefore" (paper)
> After all $a$ rounds, the named slots from high to low therefore contain the frequency bits from low to high. If $\operatorname{rev}_a(j)$ reverses the $a$ binary digits of $j$, the entry $(F_t^-z)_j$ occupies slot $\operatorname{rev}_a(j)$. Let $B_t$ denote this record permutation: it moves the entry at $j$ to $\operatorname{rev}_a(j)$. The recursion writes its outputs directly in this order; it does not move whole frequency indices.

**S5.3**: One round on $D$ axes is a parallel butterfly followed by one recordwise monomial multiplication $y^E$ whose exponent depends only on the address.
Source: "Consequently the individual axis steps may be grouped as a parallel" (paper)
> For $D$ axes of lengths $t_1,\ldots,t_D$, put \[ M=\prod_{i=1}^D t_i,\qquad F_{\boldsymbol t}^\pm=\bigotimes_{i=1}^D F_{t_i}^\pm, \qquad B=\bigotimes_{i=1}^D B_{t_i}. \] A round applies one current $H_0$ on each participating axis. The twiddle for axis $i$ depends only on that axis's address bits. The two entries paired by another axis's butterfly have the same axis-$i$ address, so that twiddle commutes with the other butterfly. Consequently the individual axis steps may be grouped as a parallel butterfly $\mathcal H$, followed by one recordwise monomial operation $J_E$. At each address, $J_E$ multiplies the record by \begin{equation} y^{E},\qquad E=-\sum_{i\ \mathrm{participating}} \frac{2r}{u_i}\,b_i k_i\pmod{2r}. \label{eq:combined-twiddle} \end{equation} Here $u_i$ is that axis's current recursive length, $b_i$ its newly written branch bit, and $0\leq k_i<u_i/2$ is encoded by its still unprocessed named slots. The exponent depends on the address, not on coefficient values. In the opposite direction the grouped round is $\mathcal HJ_E^{-1}$: apply $y^{-E}$ first and the parallel butterfly second. Each $\mathcal H$ is a contraction and each $J_E$ is an isometry, so every completed round is a contraction.
