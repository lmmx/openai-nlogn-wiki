The $n\log n$ optimality conjecture is Schönhage and Strassen's proposal that $n\log n$ is the optimal order of cost for multiplying two integers, where $n$ is the number of bits in each integer. It is the benchmark that a faster algorithm must beat.

Costs are running times on a multitape Turing machine with a fixed, finite number of tapes. A cost has order $f(n)$ when it is $O(f(n))$, that is, at most a constant times $f(n)$ for all large $n$. "Optimal" means that no algorithm in this model runs in $o(n\log n)$ time.

Schönhage and Strassen's 1971 algorithm runs in $O(n\log n\log\log n)$ time <cite data-source="paper">two $n$-bit integers can be multiplied in $O(n\log n\log\log n)$ time on a multitape Turing machine</cite>. Harvey and van der Hoeven reached $O(n\log n)$ <cite data-source="paper">Harvey and van der Hoeven reached the unconditional $O(n\log n)$ upper bound</cite>. Their algorithm also runs on a multitape machine, so $O(n\log n)$ is achievable in the model the conjecture is stated for, and the conjecture asks whether that cost can be beaten. The conjecture is posed against the chain of upper bounds traced in [Schönhage–Strassen to Harvey–van der Hoeven multiplication](ss-hh-background.md).

The conjecture fails in the fixed-tape model used here. Let $A$ be the deterministic Turing machine of the paper's main theorem, with a fixed finite alphabet and a fixed finite number of one-dimensional tapes, so $A$ is one of the machines the conjecture quantifies over. Let $T_A(n)$ be its worst-case running time on pairs of $n$-bit inputs. Let $\kappa=2^{-182}$ be a fixed positive constant. The paper proves <cite data-source="paper">T_A(n)=O\!\left(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa}\right)</cite>, which has the power $1-\kappa<1$ on the logarithm where the conjecture has power $1$. So $A$ runs in $o(n\log n)$ time and the conjecture fails in this model. <cite data-source="paper">The theorem refutes an eventual $\Omega(n\log n)$ lower bound for every fixed multiplication machine in this model</cite>.
