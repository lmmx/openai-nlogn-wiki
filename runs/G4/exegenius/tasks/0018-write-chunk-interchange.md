## This page

Concept: Fast chunk interchange
Gloss: Exchanging two equal-width address fields of u bits in an arbitrary array in O(V u^τ) time for a fixed τ < 1, even though intermediate steps XOR data together.

## This page in the argument

The step: Two equal-width address fields of $u$ bits in an arbitrary array can be exchanged in $O(Vu^\tau)$ time for a fixed $\tau<1$, even though intermediate steps XOR data together.
How: Run the bit exchange network with every edge a rational matrix shear executed by smaller interchanges, using the lower triangular factorization, then remove the width and row restrictions.
Why: An edge matrix of rank $a$ needs $a$ smaller interchanges, and the sum of these ranks is below the number of wires times $m$, so the resulting recurrence saves a power of the chunk width. (the sources say so: "An edge matrix of rank $a$ will require $a$ smaller interchanges." (paper))
Striking feature: The final effect is an exact permutation of the data, although the intermediate streams hold XOR combinations; the cost $Vu^\tau$ is still a power less than the $Vu$ of moving fields one digit at a time. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- Every rational matrix $A$ of rank $a$ factors as $A=E_1\Pi E_2$ with $E_1,E_2$ invertible lower triangular and $\Pi$ a partial permutation with exactly $a$ ones. (on [Lower triangular (Bruhat-type) factorization](triangular-factorization.md))
- The map $(H,D)\mapsto(H+AD,D)$ over $\mathbb Z/q^b\mathbb Z$ can be performed with exactly $\operatorname{rank}(A)$ interchanges of $b$-digit fields and $O(V)$ further work. (on [Cost of a rational matrix shear](matrix-shear.md))
- Two disjoint chunks of $e=m^k$ radix-$q$ digits, given enough rows, can be interchanged in time $O(Ve^\tau)$ by running the bit network with a matrix shear on every edge, each shear recursing into smaller interchanges. (on [Power-width address interchange](power-width-interchange.md))
- The power-of-two width and row-count restrictions can be removed: chunks of any width $u$ are interchanged with the rows supplied by a few high digits, at total cost $O(Vu^\tau)$.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, disproving the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture in that model.; how: Reduce the product to a cyclic convolution of radix digits evaluated by Fourier-type transforms, make the two costly ingredients of those transforms (address rearrangement and butterfly arithmetic) cost a power of the logarithm less than a full scan per level by two rank-saving tape procedures, then assemble the algorithm, prove exactness and total the cost. (on [Sub-n log n integer multiplication](sub-nlogn-multiplication.md))

**S2.4**: The power-of-two width and row-count restrictions can be removed: chunks of any width $u$ are interchanged with the rows supplied by a few high digits, at total cost $O(Vu^\tau)$.
How: Move a few high digits of each chunk to form a row field, pad its length to a multiple of $W^k$, write the remaining width in base $m$ and apply the power-width interchange to each piece, then pad binary widths up to a power of the fixed prime $q$.
- S2.4.1: Append zero rows within each prefix so the row count is divisible by $W^k$; the padded volume is less than $2V$.
  Source: "Append zero rows, separately within" (paper)
  > Write each chunk as its top $\rho$ digits followed by its remaining digits, denoted $h^+,h^-$ and $d^+,d^-$, respectively. Interchange the corresponding high digits one pair at a time. The high positions now contain $d^+$ and $h^+$; move the digits of the latter, preserving their order, into consecutive positions immediately after $d^+$. Suppressing the prefix and suffix, the field orders during the construction are \[ \begin{aligned} &(h^+,h^-,g,d^+,d^-),\\ &(d^+,h^+,h^-,g,d^-) &&\text{after exchanging and joining the high parts},\\ &(d^+,h^+,d^-,g,h^-) &&\text{after interchanging the remaining parts},\\ &(d^+,d^-,g,h^+,h^-) &&\text{after separating the high parts again}. \end{aligned} \] In the two middle orders, the first two fields form a row field of range \[ R=q^{2\rho}\ge W^k \] before both remaining chunks. The joining moves preserve the order of the remaining digits and the gap field. Exchanging and joining the high parts costs $O(V\rho)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:elementary-streams}. Append zero rows, separately within each preceding prefix, until the row count is \[ R'=W^k\left\lceil R/W^k\right\rceil<2R. \] This changes the row field's length; it need not remain a power of $q$ during the computation. The padded logical volume is less than $2V$.
- S2.4.2: Write the remaining width in base $m$ and apply the power-width interchange to each corresponding pair of pieces; the cost is a geometric sum $O(Ve^\tau)$.
  Source: "Partition each remaining chunk into corresponding consecutive pieces," (paper)
  > Write the remaining width in base $m$: \[ e-\rho=\sum_{j=0}^{k} a_jm^j,\qquad 0\le a_j<m. \] Partition each remaining chunk into corresponding consecutive pieces, with $a_j$ pieces of width $m^j$. Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:power-interchange} to each corresponding pair. All these calls use the same row field of length $R'$, which is divisible by each required $W^j$. At completion every call preserves its row address, so it neither mixes genuine rows with padded rows nor depends on the values used for padding. The padded zero rows remain zero at these completed-call boundaries. The sum of the costs is bounded by \[ O(V)\sum_j a_jm^{j\tau}=O(Ve^\tau). \] The fixed bound $a_j<m$ and the inequality $m^k<me$ make this a geometric sum of the stated order. Scan away the padded rows, then reverse only the moves that joined the high parts. This is the last transition in the field-order display: $h^+$ returns to the later high position, while $d^+$ stays in the earlier one. The final order is therefore the required interchange of the two full chunks. The remaining movement cost $O(V\log(2e))$ is $O(Ve^\tau)$. Computing the piece list and every new shape is polynomial in $\log(2V)$ per piece. There are at most $(m-1)(k+1)=O(\log(2e))$ pieces; since $V\ge q^{2e}$, their total descriptor cost is a fixed power of $\log(2V)$ and hence $O(V)$. Each power-width call uses the fixed schedule proved above and leaves its work tapes clean.
- S2.4.3: For binary chunks of $u$ bits choose the least $e$ with $q^e\ge2^u$, pad both ranges, and scan away the padding; since $e=O(u)$ the bound is $O(Vu^\tau)$.
  Source: "Finally, for binary chunks let $e$ be the least integer with" (paper)
  > Finally, for binary chunks let $e$ be the least integer with $q^e\ge2^u$. Pad each of the two chunk ranges numerically from $[2^u]$ to $[q^e]$, putting zero in an entry if either chunk coordinate is invalid. The increase in volume is less than $q^2$, a fixed factor. The padding is a nested scan through consecutive valid and invalid intervals, with their lengths prepared from the shape. It costs $O(V)$ with a constant depending on $q$. Lexicographic radix-$q$ order of $[q^e]$ is its numerical order, just as binary order of $[2^u]$ is its numerical order. Padding therefore preserves the order of the existing entries without converting the digits of every address. The completed interchange preserves the set on which both chunk coordinates lie below $2^u$. Scan away the other entries to recover the desired binary array. Since $e=O(u)$ for fixed $q$, the bound is $O(Vu^\tau)$. \end{proof}
