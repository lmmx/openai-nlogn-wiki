A butterfly on one bit takes two values $u$ and $v$ at the two addresses that differ only in that bit, and replaces them by $(u+v)/2$ and $(u-v)/2$. The simultaneous butterfly layer $H_0$ is the map $H_0(u,v)=\bigl((u+v)/2,(u-v)/2\bigr)$, applied at once to one selected coordinate bit of every address chunk of an array, where an address chunk is a block of consecutive address bits. It is the basic step of the transforms used in the multiplication algorithm, and it transforms many address bits in one pass. The layer acts on arrays stored in a [frame of a vertex](frame.md) on each wire. Intermediate values in the layer are exact [Gaussian dyadic numbers](gaussian-dyadic.md), whose real and imaginary parts have power-of-two denominators.

The layer uses $D$ chunks, the number of chunks in the layer, each with one selected bit at a common offset from the least significant end. It applies $H_0$ to all $D$ selected bits. This is the tensor product of the single-bit butterflies: the result is the same as applying $H_0$ to the selected bits one at a time, in any order, and all other address fields are unchanged.

Each address holds a polynomial record of $r$ complex coefficients, and $H_0$ acts coefficientwise: each coefficient of $u$ is combined with the matching coefficient of $v$. For single coefficients $u=1+i$ and $v=1-i$, the output pair is $(1,\,i)$, since $(u+v)/2=1$ and $(u-v)/2=i$.

Here $V$ is the logical volume, the number of stored data bits. On the layouts used in the multiplication algorithm, one selects the same bit position in each of at most $d$ equal-width address chunks. The layer costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$, where the exponent $\lambda'<1$ is fixed. A single layer avoids the $D$ whole-array passes of separate butterfly factors <cite data-source="paper">separate butterfly factors would require $D$ whole-array passes</cite>.
