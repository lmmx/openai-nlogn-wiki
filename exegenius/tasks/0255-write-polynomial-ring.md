The polynomial ring modulo a fixed polynomial is the ring of complex polynomials in $y$ in which every multiple of that polynomial counts as zero. The argument needs it because its convolution on the remaining axes is computed over this ring: <cite data-source="paper">Sections 6 and 7 compute convolution on the remaining axes over this polynomial ring.</cite>

Let $f$ be a monic polynomial with complex coefficients, that is, one whose leading coefficient is $1$. Let $m \ge 1$ denote the degree of $f$, which is also the number of coefficients in each representative below. The ring $R = \mathbb{C}[y]/(f)$ has as elements the classes of polynomials $p(y)$ with complex coefficients, where $p$ and $q$ are identified when $p - q$ is a multiple of $f$. For the case $f = y^r+1$, with $r$ a power of two, the paper writes this ring as <cite data-source="paper">Rr = C[y]/(y r + 1),</cite>

Each class contains exactly one polynomial of degree less than $m$. Existence follows from division with remainder by the monic polynomial $f$, and uniqueness holds because the difference of two such polynomials is a multiple of $f$ of degree less than $m$, hence zero. Writing this representative as $z = \sum_{k=0}^{m-1} z_k y^k$, the list $(z_0, \dots, z_{m-1})$ of complex numbers is the coefficient list of $z$, and it determines $z$.

Addition is coefficientwise. Multiplication is ordinary polynomial multiplication followed by reduction: each power $y^j$ with $j \ge m$ that appears is replaced by its remainder on division by $f$.

Take $f = y^4+1$, so $m = 4$, and let $z = 1 + 2y + 3y^2$, with coefficient list $(1, 2, 3, 0)$. Then $y^3 z = y^3 + 2y^4 + 3y^5$. In $R$ we have $y^4 = -1$ and $y^5 = -y$, so $y^3 z = -2 - 3y + y^3$, with coefficient list $(-2, -3, 0, 1)$. Each coefficient moved three places up, and a coefficient that passed the top position re-entered at the bottom with its sign reversed. This is the signed shift that the paper uses: <cite data-source="paper">multiplication by such a root is a signed shift of the coefficient list</cite>. The paper states the general form: <cite data-source="paper">Multiplication by y a , for any integer a, permutes the coefficients and changes some signs</cite>.
