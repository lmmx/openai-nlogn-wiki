# Notation

The symbols that more than one page uses, in the order they are bound. Each is bound once, on a page or in a concept's name, and used on the pages listed. A symbol used on one page only is explained on that page and is not listed here.

- $s$ — **source length**: The number of coordinates of a source vector, a count. Bound on [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md "A technique that replaces a Fourier transform of arbitrary length by transforms whose axis lengths are powers of two, using Gaussian weights to move between the two sets of frequencies."); used on [Transform identity with retained permutations](transform-identity.md "The Gaussian resampling identity in which the source and target coordinate permutations are kept as part of the formula, so that they cancel across a convolution instead of being executed as sorts.").
- $t$ — **target length**: The number of coordinates of a target vector, a count. Bound on [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md "A technique that replaces a Fourier transform of arbitrary length by transforms whose axis lengths are powers of two, using Gaussian weights to move between the two sets of frequencies."); used on [Transform identity with retained permutations](transform-identity.md "The Gaussian resampling identity in which the source and target coordinate permutations are kept as part of the formula, so that they cancel across a convolution instead of being executed as sorts.").

Bound on [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md "A technique that replaces a Fourier transform of arbitrary length by transforms whose axis lengths are powers of two, using Gaussian weights to move between the two sets of frequencies."); used on [Transform identity with retained permutations](transform-identity.md "The Gaussian resampling identity in which the source and target coordinate permutations are kept as part of the formula, so that they cancel across a convolution instead of being executed as sorts.").

Bound on [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md "A technique that replaces a Fourier transform of arbitrary length by transforms whose axis lengths are powers of two, using Gaussian weights to move between the two sets of frequencies."); used on [Cyclic convolution](cyclic-convolution.md "The product of two sequences in which indices wrap around modulo the sequence length.") and [Transform identity with retained permutations](transform-identity.md "The Gaussian resampling identity in which the source and target coordinate permutations are kept as part of the formula, so that they cancel across a convolution instead of being executed as sorts.").
