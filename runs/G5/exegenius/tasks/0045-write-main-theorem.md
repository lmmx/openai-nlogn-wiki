## This page

Concept: Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time
Gloss: One deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies n-bit integers exactly in O(n (lg n)^{1−κ}) time with κ = 2^{−182}, beating the n log n conjecture by a power of the logarithm.
Also called: main theorem
Heading: Integer multiplication in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time

The symbols in this page's heading denote (say so on the page):

- $n$ (input length): The number of bits in each of the two integers being multiplied.
- $\kappa$ (saving exponent): The fixed constant 2^{-182} by which the power of the logarithm is reduced below 1.

## This page in the argument

The step: One deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.
How: Give the multiplication algorithm and show its output is exact, then total its cost stage by stage
Why: Plain transforms already cost $\Theta(n\log n)$ because each of $\Theta(\log n)$ levels scans $\Theta(n)$ bits, so the proof needs components that save on both movement and arithmetic, and the exactness and the cost are best argued separately. (the sources say so: "We therefore need savings in both movement and arithmetic." (paper))
Striking feature: The saving exponent $\kappa=2^{-182}$ is astronomically small yet positive, so the gain is a genuine power of the logarithm. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- The algorithm, with digits placed on prime axes by the Chinese-remainder map, transformed by Gaussian resampling, chirp convolution and polynomial-ring convolution, multiplied pointwise, transformed back, rescaled, rounded and carry-propagated, outputs the exact $2n$-bit product. (on [The multiplication algorithm](multiplication-algorithm.md))
- Dividing the costs of all stages by $Tp$, the seven dominant normalized terms have exponents of $p$ at most $1-2\kappa$, which gives $O(n(\log n)^{1-\kappa})$. (on [Complete time bound and the power saving](time-bound.md))
