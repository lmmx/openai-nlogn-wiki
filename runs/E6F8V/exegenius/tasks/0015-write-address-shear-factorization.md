## This page

Concept: Address shear and triangular factorization
Gloss: The address map (H,D)↦(H+AD,D) for a rational matrix A of rank a, performed with exactly a interchanges of smaller field pairs after factoring A=E₁ΠE₂ into lower-triangular factors and a partial permutation.

## This page in the argument

The step: An address shear $(H,D)\mapsto(H+AD,D)$ for a rational matrix $A$ of rank $a$ is performed with exactly $a$ interchanges of pairs of smaller fields plus $O(V)$ other work.
How: Factor $A=E_1\Pi E_2$ with lower-triangular $E_1,E_2$ and a partial permutation $\Pi$ with $a$ ones; triangular maps are controlled shifts, and each one of $\Pi$ is one field interchange.
Why: Controlled shifts only update a later field from earlier ones, so changes of coordinates must be lower triangular, and a lower-triangular elimination leaves only a rank-sized partial permutation. (the sources say so: "earlier fields in linear time.  Lower triangular changes of coordinates" (paper))
Striking feature: The number of recursive interchanges is the rank $a$ of the shear matrix, not the dimension $m$. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- Every rational $m\times m$ matrix $A$ of rank $a$ factors as $E_1\Pi E_2$ with $E_1,E_2$ invertible lower triangular and $\Pi$ having exactly $a$ ones.
- A lower triangular transformation of $H$ or of $D$ costs $O(V)$, by controlled shifts in descending physical order.
- Each one of $\Pi$ calls for $H_i\leftarrow H_i+D_j$, done as $D_j\leftarrow D_j+H_i$, an interchange, $D_j\leftarrow H_i-D_j$, using just one interchange.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: Build two faster tape procedures (address chunk interchange and simultaneous butterfly layers) from rank-saving linear networks, use them in synthetic polynomial transforms and resampled source transforms for a convolution, reduce integer multiplication to that convolution, then prove exactness and add up costs. (on [Integer multiplication below n log n](main-theorem.md))
- Two disjoint contiguous address fields of $u$ bits of an array of volume $V$ can be interchanged in $O(Vu^\tau)$ time for a fixed $\tau<1$.; how: Run the bit exchange network of the previous section on role streams: its endpoint operator is an address shear, each edge shear is implemented by smaller interchanges, and the rank saving yields a power recurrence; then remove width and row restrictions. (on [Address chunk interchange](address-chunk-interchange.md))

**B.1.1**: Every rational $m\times m$ matrix $A$ of rank $a$ factors as $E_1\Pi E_2$ with $E_1,E_2$ invertible lower triangular and $\Pi$ having exactly $a$ ones.
Source: "Every rational $m\times m$ matrix $A$ of rank $a$ has a factorization" (paper)
> \begin{lemma}[Lower triangular factorization] \label{lem:lower-lower} Every rational $m\times m$ matrix $A$ of rank $a$ has a factorization \[ A=E_1\Pi E_2, \] where $E_1,E_2$ are invertible lower triangular rational matrices and $\Pi$ has exactly $a$ entries equal to one, with at most one in each row and column, and all remaining entries zero. \end{lemma}

**B.1.2**: A lower triangular transformation of $H$ or of $D$ costs $O(V)$, by controlled shifts in descending physical order.
Source: "A lower triangular transformation of $H$, or of $D$, costs $O(V)$:" (paper)
> A lower triangular transformation of $H$, or of $D$, costs $O(V)$: update coordinates in descending physical order. At coordinate $i$, multiply by its diagonal unit and add a fixed linear combination of earlier coordinates. These earlier coordinates still have their original values. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:ordered-affine-streams}; the number of coordinates is the fixed number $m$. Inverses are also lower triangular.

**B.1.3**: Each one of $\Pi$ calls for $H_i\leftarrow H_i+D_j$, done as $D_j\leftarrow D_j+H_i$, an interchange, $D_j\leftarrow H_i-D_j$, using just one interchange.
Source: "It uses just one interchange." (paper)
> For $A=E_1\Pi E_2$, first transform $D$ by $E_2$ and $H$ by $E_1^{-1}$, then add $\Pi D$ to $H$, and finally transform $H$ by $E_1$ and $D$ by $E_2^{-1}$. Each one in position $(i,j)$ of $\Pi$ calls for $H_i\leftarrow H_i+D_j$. Implement it by \[ D_j\leftarrow D_j+H_i,\qquad (H_i,D_j)\leftarrow(D_j,H_i),\qquad D_j\leftarrow H_i-D_j. \] The first and last updates target a later field controlled by an earlier field. On an initial pair $(h,d)$ this sequence yields $(h+d,d)$. It uses just one interchange. There are $a$ ones, which proves the claim. In particular, the number of recursive calls is the rational rank $a$; temporary tapes used for the other operations do not multiply that count. \end{proof}
