## This page

Concept: Transform layout and cost
Gloss: A positional layout permutation L places the selected bits of all axes at a common offset in consecutive K-bit chunks (prefix moves plus chunk exchanges), so each round is one simultaneous layer; the forward and opposite transforms cost O(Tp[dK + pK^{τ−1} + ℓ d^{λ'}]) with error below √2 ℓ 2^{−p}.

## This page in the argument

The step: A positional layout permutation $L$ places the selected bits of all axes at a common offset in consecutive $K$-bit chunks, so each round is one simultaneous layer, and the transforms cost $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}])$ with error below $\sqrt2\ell 2^{-p}$.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- One deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in time $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: Keep the transform-based multiplication of Harvey and van der Hoeven (Gaussian resampling, Bluestein chirp, synthetic polynomial transforms) but replace its two costly tape operations, address rearrangement and butterfly layers, by faster procedures obtained from rank-saving linear networks, then check exactness and total cost. (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time](main-theorem.md))
- On arrays over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ with $M$ records, the normalized convolution $(f*g)/T$ is computed with error below $\sqrt2 M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$ in time $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}+\log(rp)])$.; how: Compute two forward synthetic transforms on a layout where every round is one simultaneous butterfly layer, multiply pointwise in the retained frequency order with signed-packed ring products, apply the opposite transform that consumes that order, and rescale by $M$. (on [Convolution from the retained transform layout](synthetic-convolution.md))

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "The procedure constructs an explicit positional" (paper)

  > The procedure constructs an explicit positional layout permutation $L$ of record addresses, preserving their polynomial suffixes, and transforms disk grid arrays of $M$ contiguous degree-less-than-$r$ polynomial records. Its exact counterpart is $LB F_{\boldsymbol t}^-$. Its opposite procedure consumes that order and has exact counterpart $F_{\boldsymbol t}^+B^{-1}L^{-1}$. Each procedure has error less than $\sqrt2\ell\,2^{-p}$, returns disk grid coefficients in the same component format, and costs \begin{equation} O\left(Tp\left[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}\right]\right). \label{eq:synthetic-transform-cost} \end{equation} Both layout construction and final restoration are charged in this bound. - "exchanges of width-$K$ address chunks, even when the chunks are" (paper)

  > Number the axes by $i$ and their chunks, from high to low, by $j$. The remaining physical order changes from \[ (1,1),(1,2),\ldots,(1,q),(2,1),\ldots,(D,q) \quad\hbox{to}\quad (1,1),(2,1),\ldots,(D,1),(1,2),\ldots,(D,q). \] To obtain this order, scan its target positions from left to right. At each position except the last, exchange the chunk currently there with the required chunk if they differ. This uses at most $Dq-1$ exchanges of width-$K$ address chunks, even when the chunks are nonadjacent. Each costs $O(TpK^\tau)$, so all these exchanges cost \[ O\bigl(Tp\,DqK^\tau\bigr) =O\bigl(Tp\,pK^{\tau-1}\bigr). \] These prefix moves and chunk exchanges define $L$. We use the same letter for its induced record permutation: a record at address $x$ moves to address $Lx$, while its polynomial suffix stays intact. Recording the inverse operations in reverse order gives $L^{-1}$ with the same cost.
