## This page

Concept: Address-chunk interchange
Gloss: The first tape procedure: it swaps two disjoint address chunks of u bits in a complete array of volume V in time O(V u^τ) for a fixed τ < 1, internally using XOR combinations of intermediate data yet ending as an exact permutation.
Also called: chunk swap, address rearrangement

## This page in the argument

The step: Two disjoint address chunks of $u$ bits in a complete array of volume $V$ can be interchanged in time $O(Vu^\tau)$ for a fixed $\tau<1$, although intermediate steps form XOR combinations of data.
How: factor each edge matrix as lower triangular, partial permutation, lower triangular so that an edge of rank $a$ costs $a$ smaller interchanges, then run the bit network recursion and remove the width and row restrictions
Why: The source says the rank sum being below the wire count times $m$ gives a power saving in the chunk width. (the sources say so: "$m$, the resulting recurrence saves a power of the chunk width." (paper))
Striking feature: The procedure computes XOR combinations of intermediate data yet ends as an exact permutation, exact for arbitrary initial auxiliary values; the page should explain why the common frame cancels so that only the shear remains. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- Every rational matrix $A$ of rank $a$ factors as $E_1\Pi E_2$ with $E_1,E_2$ invertible lower triangular and $\Pi$ a partial permutation matrix with $a$ ones. (on [Lower triangular (Bruhat-type) factorization](lower-triangular-factorization.md))
- The address map $(H,D)\mapsto(H+AD,D)$ of rank $a$ is performed with exactly $a$ interchanges of pairs of $b$-digit fields plus $O(V)$ further work. (on [Cost of a rational matrix shear](matrix-shear-cost.md))
- For power widths $e=m^k$ with enough rows, the bit network with its shears gives an interchange of two chunks in $O(Ve^\tau)$ time.
- For arbitrary width $u$ and arbitrary shape, the interchange $(p,h,g,d,z)\mapsto(p,d,g,h,z)$ costs $O(Vu^\tau)$ steps.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies $n$-bit integers in $T_A(n)=O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: reduce the product to a digit convolution computed by synthetic transforms, replace the address rearrangement and butterfly layers inside those transforms by two faster recursive tape procedures, then assemble, round and bound the cost (on [Integer multiplication below n log n](sub-nlogn-multiplication.md))
- Two recursive tape procedures, an address-chunk interchange and a simultaneous butterfly layer, run faster than the naive costs of address rearrangement and Fourier layers by a power of the width.; how: build the elementary tape tools, derive a general recursive power saving from a fixed linear network, and instantiate it twice, once for bits and once for complex values

**S1.4.3**: For power widths $e=m^k$ with enough rows, the bit network with its shears gives an interchange of two chunks in $O(Ve^\tau)$ time.
Source: "The chunks can be interchanged in $O(Ve^\tau)$ time, preserving every" (paper)
> \begin{proposition}[Power-width interchange with rows] \label{prop:power-interchange} Assume the fixed circuit data satisfy \eqref{eq:finite-shear-contract}, and let $q$ be a prime as in Lemma~\ref{lem:matrix-shear} for its edge matrices, also avoiding the denominators of every assigned terminal and gate matrix $M$. Choose a rational number $\tau\in(0,1)$ with $s/W<m^\tau$. For every $e=m^k$, $k\ge0$, consider two disjoint contiguous address chunks of $e$ radix-$q$ digits each. Suppose a row field preceding both chunks has length divisible by $W^k$. There may be additional fields before and after the row field, between the chunks, and after them. The chunks can be interchanged in $O(Ve^\tau)$ time, preserving every other address field, on a fixed number of finite-alphabet tapes. The constant is independent of all field lengths and of the data. \end{proposition}

**S1.4.4**: For arbitrary width $u$ and arbitrary shape, the interchange $(p,h,g,d,z)\mapsto(p,d,g,h,z)$ costs $O(Vu^\tau)$ steps.
Source: "in $O(Vu^\tau)$ steps, where $V=PG B\,2^{2u}$." (paper)
> \begin{lemma}[Arbitrary-width interchange] \label{lem:chunk-swap} There exist fixed $0<\tau<1$ and one fixed finite-alphabet multitape procedure with the following property. For positive integers $P,G,B$ and $u\ge1$, it transforms an arbitrary bit array on \[ [P]\times[2^u]\times[G]\times[2^u]\times[B] \] by the address permutation \[ (p,h,g,d,z)\longmapsto(p,d,g,h,z) \] in $O(Vu^\tau)$ steps, where $V=PG B\,2^{2u}$. The integers specifying the shape are part of the input. The time includes their processing, all padding and unpadding, and fixed-tape workspace cleanup. In particular, $B=1$ is allowed. \end{lemma}
