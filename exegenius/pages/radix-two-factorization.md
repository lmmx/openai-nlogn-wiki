Radix-two factorization computes a [Discrete Fourier transform](discrete-fourier-transform.md) of length $q$, when $q$ is a power of two. The transform is built from [Butterfly](butterfly.md) maps arranged in $\lg q$ layers. It is the step that lets the Fourier transforms of a convolution be replaced by [multidimensional transforms](multidimensional-transform.md) whose axis lengths are powers of two.

Let $\omega = e^{-2\pi i/q}$, so that $\omega^{q/2} = -1$, and let $(F_q f)_\ell = \frac{1}{q}\sum_{s=0}^{q-1}\omega^{\ell s} f_s$ for $0 \le \ell < q$. Evaluated directly, this is a product with a $q \times q$ matrix, costing $q^2$ multiplications. Let $\varsigma$ be the half-length output index, the output index of a transform of length $q/2$, with $0 \le \varsigma < q/2$. Every output index $\ell$ is $2\varsigma$ or $2\varsigma+1$ for exactly one $\varsigma$.

For $0 \le s < q/2$, put $\tilde f_s = f_s + f_{s+q/2}$ and $\check f_s = \omega^s\,(f_s - f_{s+q/2})$. Split the sum over $s$ into the terms with $s < q/2$ and the terms with $s+q/2$. Since $\omega^{\ell(s+q/2)} = (-1)^\ell\,\omega^{\ell s}$, the two terms combine to $f_s + f_{s+q/2}$ for even $\ell$ and to $f_s - f_{s+q/2}$ for odd $\ell$. Since $\omega^{2\varsigma s} = (\omega^2)^{\varsigma s}$ and $\omega^{(2\varsigma+1)s} = (\omega^2)^{\varsigma s}\,\omega^s$, this gives, for $0 \le \varsigma < q/2$,

$$(F_q f)_{2\varsigma} = \tfrac12\,(F_{q/2}\,\tilde f)_\varsigma, \qquad (F_q f)_{2\varsigma+1} = \tfrac12\,(F_{q/2}\,\check f)_\varsigma,$$

where $F_{q/2}$ is the transform of length $q/2$, with $\omega^2$ in place of $\omega$. The factor $\tfrac12$ comes from $\frac1q = \tfrac12\cdot\frac{1}{q/2}$.

The butterfly $H_0(x,y) = ((x+y)/2,\,(x-y)/2)$ produces $\tilde f_s$ and $\check f_s$ with the $\tfrac12$ already included. So one layer does the following. For each $0 \le s < q/2$, apply $H_0$ to the pair $(f_s, f_{s+q/2})$, multiply the second output by $\omega^s$, and store the first output in slot $s$ and the second in slot $s+q/2$. <cite data-source="paper">The two outputs of H0 occupy these same slots</cite>. The two halves, slots $0$ to $q/2-1$ and slots $q/2$ to $q-1$, are then transformed by the same procedure at length $q/2$. The first half yields the even-indexed outputs and the second half the odd-indexed ones. A piece of length one is left alone. <cite data-source="paper">The length-one procedure is the identity.</cite> The recursion therefore stops when every piece has length one.

A layer is one pass of this step over all pieces of one length. Each layer uses $q/2$ butterflies and at most $q/2$ multiplications by powers of $\omega$. Each pass halves the piece length, so there are $\lg q$ layers, and the transform costs $O(q\lg q)$ operations.

The entries come out in bit-reversed order. Let $\mathrm{rev}(\ell)$ be the integer whose $\lg q$ binary digits are those of $\ell$ in reverse order. <cite data-source="paper">the entry (Ft− z)j occupies slot reva (j)</cite>, so after the layers $(F_q f)_\ell$ sits in array slot $\mathrm{rev}(\ell)$. This is the [Bit-reversed index order](bit-reversed-order.md).

For example, take $q = 4$ and $f = (1,2,3,4)$, so $\omega = -i$. The first layer pairs $(f_0,f_2) = (1,3)$ and $(f_1,f_3) = (2,4)$. The first outputs are $(1+3)/2 = 2$ and $(2+4)/2 = 3$, in slots $0$ and $1$. The second outputs are $(1-3)/2 = -1$ and $\omega\,(2-4)/2 = i$, in slots $2$ and $3$. The array is now $(2,3,-1,i)$. The first half $(2,3)$ transforms to $(2.5,\,-0.5)$, which are $(F_4 f)_0$ and $(F_4 f)_2$. The second half $(-1,i)$ transforms, with root $\omega^2 = -1$, to $(-0.5+0.5i,\,-0.5-0.5i)$, which are $(F_4 f)_1$ and $(F_4 f)_3$. The final array $(2.5,\,-0.5,\,-0.5+0.5i,\,-0.5-0.5i)$ holds $(F_4 f)_\ell$ in slot $\mathrm{rev}(\ell)$.
