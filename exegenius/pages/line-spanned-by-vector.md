A line spanned by a vector is the set of all scalar multiples of one nonzero vector, a one-dimensional subspace. The [Subset-intersection network](subset-intersection-network.md) labels its edges with subspaces: <cite data-source="paper">On every edge one label will contain the other.</cite> The paper chooses its intermediate labels <cite data-source="paper">to make the changes of frame inexpensive</cite>, and its triple labels are lines of this kind.

Let $E$ be a vector space over $F$. The definition rests on the vector spaces and scalar multiples of [Linear algebra basics](linear-algebra-basics.md). Let $c$ be a scalar, that is, an element of $F$. For a nonzero $x \in E$, the line spanned by $x$ is $\operatorname{span}\{x\} = \{cx : c \in F\}$.

The line is a subspace: if $d$ is also a scalar, $cx+dx=(c+d)x$ and $c(dx)=(cd)x$ lie on it. It has dimension one, since $x$ is nonzero and spans it.

Take $F=\mathbb{R}$, $E=\mathbb{R}^2$ and $x=(1,2)$. Then $\operatorname{span}\{x\}=\{(c,2c): c\in\mathbb{R}\}$ is the line through the origin with slope 2. The point $(3,6)=3x$ lies on it, but $(1,3)$ does not: its first coordinate forces $c=1$, and then the second coordinate is $2$, not $3$.

Let $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ be a [Bilinear form](bilinear-form.md) on $E$, linear in each argument. A subspace $U$ is nondegenerate when no nonzero vector of $U$ is orthogonal to every vector of $U$. For a line, nondegeneracy is decided by the [Bilinear form](bilinear-form.md) on the single vector $x$. Bilinearity gives $\langle cx,dx\rangle=cd\,\langle x,x\rangle$. If $\langle x,x\rangle=0$, then $x$ is a nonzero vector orthogonal to the whole line, so the line is degenerate. If $\langle x,x\rangle\neq0$, then $\langle cx,x\rangle=c\langle x,x\rangle\neq0$ for every nonzero $cx$ on the line, so the line is nondegenerate.

A nondegenerate line $U$ shares only zero with its [Orthogonal complement](orthogonal-complement.md) $U^\perp$: a vector in both lies in $U$ and is orthogonal to all of $U$, so it is zero. A projection along $U^\perp$ needs exactly this trivial intersection. The paper states the projection for nondegenerate $U$: <cite data-source="paper">write PU for the projection onto U along U ⊥</cite>.

A line of this kind labels the edges of the network that the construction builds from [Linear networks and frames](linear-network-frames.md). The network is linear: <cite data-source="paper">We construct two fixed linear networks that exchange two banks of values</cite>, and <cite data-source="paper">A gate is a fixed linear map on a specified set of</cite> wires.

For a triple $T$ of the ground set $[h]$, the indicator vector $t_T$ has entry 1 in each of the three positions of $T$ and 0 elsewhere. For a choice $\varsigma=(\varsigma_1,\varsigma_2,\varsigma_3)$ of three triples, put $u_\varsigma = t_{\varsigma_1}\otimes t_{\varsigma_2}\otimes t_{\varsigma_3}$ and $U_\varsigma = \operatorname{span}\{u_\varsigma\}$. Each label $U_\varsigma$ is spanned by $u_\varsigma$, which is built from the [Tensor product](tensor-product.md) of three indicator vectors. The paper writes <cite data-source="paper">ua = ta1 ⊗ ta2 ⊗ ta3</cite> and <cite data-source="paper">Ua = ⟨ua ⟩</cite>, with $\varsigma$ in place of its $a$.

The bit network uses the form on $\mathbb{Q}^h$ given by <cite data-source="paper">⟨x, y⟩ = xT (I − J/9)y</cite>, where $J$ is the all-one matrix and $I$ the identity matrix. Each entry of $Jt_T$ is 3, so $t_T^{\mathsf T}Jt_T=9$, while $t_T^{\mathsf T}t_T=3$. Hence $\langle t_T,t_T\rangle = 3-9/9 = 2$, the value the paper reports: <cite data-source="paper">⟨tT , tT ⟩ = 2.</cite>

The form on a tensor power multiplies over the three factors, so $\langle u_\varsigma,u_\varsigma\rangle = 2^3 = 8 \neq 0$, and $U_\varsigma$ is nondegenerate. The paper concludes: <cite data-source="paper">Thus every triple line is nondegenerate</cite>.
