The limit inferior of a sequence of real numbers is the smallest value that the sequence keeps approaching as its index grows. The paper uses it to state the negation of the $n\lg n$ lower bound on running time, so refuting the conjecture means exhibiting one machine whose running-time ratio has limit inferior zero.

**Definition.** Let $\xi_1, \xi_2, \xi_3, \dots$ be real numbers, where $\xi_\ell$ is the term at position $\ell$, a positive integer. For a position $\mu$, the tail from $\mu$ is the set of terms $\xi_\ell$ with $\ell \ge \mu$. Its infimum $\eta_\mu$ is the greatest lower bound of these terms: the largest real number that is at most every term of the tail, or $-\infty$ if no such number exists. The tail from $\mu+1$ is a subset of the tail from $\mu$, and a greatest lower bound over fewer terms is at least as large, so $\eta_\mu \le \eta_{\mu+1}$. The sequence $\eta_\mu$ is therefore nondecreasing. The limit inferior is the limit of this sequence:
$$\liminf_{\ell\to\infty} \xi_\ell = \lim_{\mu\to\infty} \eta_\mu.$$
The limit inferior takes its values in the [extended real numbers](extended-reals.md), because a nondecreasing sequence there has a limit, its supremum.

For example, let $\xi_\ell = 1 + (-1)^\ell$. The terms are $0, 2, 0, 2, \dots$. Every tail contains a $0$, so every $\eta_\mu = 0$ and the limit inferior is $0$, although half the terms equal $2$.

**Zero criterion.** Suppose every $\xi_\ell \ge 0$. Then $\liminf_{\ell\to\infty} \xi_\ell = 0$ if and only if, for every real tolerance $\varepsilon > 0$, infinitely many $\ell$ satisfy $\xi_\ell < \varepsilon$. Every $\eta_\mu$ is nonnegative, and a nondecreasing sequence is at most its limit, so when the limit is $0$ every $\eta_\mu = 0$. If so, and $\varepsilon > 0$, then $\eta_\mu = 0 < \varepsilon$ means $\varepsilon$ is not a lower bound of the tail from $\mu$. Some $\ell \ge \mu$ then has $\xi_\ell < \varepsilon$, and since $\mu$ is arbitrary, infinitely many $\ell$ do. Conversely, if for each $\varepsilon > 0$ infinitely many $\ell$ have $\xi_\ell < \varepsilon$, every tail contains a term below $\varepsilon$, so $0 \le \eta_\mu < \varepsilon$ for every $\mu$. The limit then lies in $[0, \varepsilon]$ for every $\varepsilon > 0$, so it is $0$.

**Use in the paper.** Here $\lg$ is the base-2 logarithm, and for a machine $A$, $T_A(n)$ is its running time cost. The paper states the $n\lg n$ optimality conjecture as <cite data-source="paper">for each fixed machine A, there are constants cA > 0 and NA such that TA (n) ≥ cA n lg n</cite>. That is, $T_A(n) \ge c_A\, n\lg n$ whenever $n \ge N_A$, where $N_A$ is the threshold from which the bound holds.

Let $\rho_n = T_A(n)/(n\lg n)$ for $n \ge 2$. This ratio is nonnegative. The conjecture for $A$ says $\rho_n \ge c_A$ for every $n \ge N_A$. The limit inferior is applied against [Schönhage–Strassen multiplication](schonhage-strassen.md), since $\rho_n$ measures a running time against the $n\lg n$ order that the authors of that method conjectured to be optimal. The negation of the conjecture for $A$ says that for every $c > 0$ and every threshold $N$, some $n \ge N$ has $\rho_n < c$. That quantifier over every threshold asks for infinitely many small ratios, which is exactly the zero criterion applied to $\rho_n$ with $n$ in place of $\ell$. The paper states that its negation requires <cite data-source="paper">one machine satisfying lim inf n→∞ TA (n)/(n lg n) = 0</cite>.

The main result supplies such a machine. Take $A$ to be the machine that runs the paper's algorithm. The paper gives its worst-case time as <cite data-source="paper">We give a deterministic algorithm that multiplies two n-bit integers in O(n(lg n)1−κ )</cite>, where $\kappa = 2^{-182}$ satisfies $0 < \kappa < 1$. So there is a constant $C$ with $T_A(n) \le C\, n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa}$ for all large $n$.

Dividing by $n\lg n$ gives $0 \le \rho_n \le C(\lg n)^{-\kappa}$ for all large $n$. Fix $\mu$. Each $\rho_n$ with $n \ge \mu$ lies in the tail from $\mu$, so $\eta_\mu \le \rho_n \le C(\lg n)^{-\kappa}$ for all large $n$. Since $\kappa > 0$, the right side tends to $0$ as $n \to \infty$. So $\eta_\mu \le 0$, and with $\eta_\mu \ge 0$ this gives $\eta_\mu = 0$ for every $\mu$. Hence $\liminf_{n\to\infty} \rho_n = 0$.

This refutes the conjecture for $A$. Suppose it held with constants $c_A$ and $N_A$, where we may take $N_A \ge 2$. Then every term of the tail from $N_A$ is at least $c_A$, so $\eta_{N_A} \ge c_A > 0$, contradicting $\eta_{N_A} = 0$. The paper says <cite data-source="paper">The algorithm is exact for every input length and disproves the n log n</cite>.
