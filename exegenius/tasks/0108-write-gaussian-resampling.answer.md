Gaussian resampling replaces the transforms of the [Discrete Fourier transform](discrete-fourier-transform.md) of arbitrary length by transforms whose axis lengths are powers of two, using Gaussian weights to move between the two sets of frequencies. In the integer product, Gaussian resampling turns the Fourier transforms into [multidimensional transforms](multidimensional-transform.md) with power-of-two axes, so the later steps can use butterfly layers and polynomial transforms.

Gaussian resampling replaces the transforms needed to compute the [radix-digit convolution](radix-digit-convolution.md) by power-of-two transforms. The lengths must change because <cite data-source="paper">The transform lengths needed for the integer product are pairwise coprime</cite>, while <cite data-source="paper">transforms use powers of two</cite> in the synthetic transforms. Gaussian resampling converts the lengths for the [synthetic transforms](synthetic-transforms.md).

Let $s$ be the number of entries in the source vector, the length of the transform being replaced. Let $t$ be the number of entries in the resampled vector, a power of two greater than $s$. For a positive integer $q$, $F_q$ is the normalized discrete Fourier transform of length $q$, so $F_s$ and $F_t$ are the transforms of lengths $s$ and $t$. Write $\theta = t/s - 1$ for the relative gap between the two lengths. The integer $\alpha$ sets the width of the Gaussian weights, and the integer $p$ is a precision parameter.

Let $P_s$ be the permutation of length-$s$ vectors defined by $(P_s w)_m = w_{tm \bmod s}$, which multiplies each position $m$ by $t$ modulo $s$. It is a permutation because $\gcd(s,t)=1$: if $tm \equiv tm' \pmod s$, then $m \equiv m' \pmod s$. Let $P_t$ be the permutation of length-$t$ vectors defined by $(P_t v)_m = v_{-sm \bmod t}$. For example, with $s=5$ and $t=8$, $P_5$ takes $(w_0,\dots,w_4)$ to $(w_0,w_3,w_1,w_4,w_2)$.

The identity is <cite data-source="paper">Lemma 19 (Resampling without executing the two permutations)</cite>. It applies when <cite data-source="paper">with gcd(s, t) = 1 and</cite> <cite data-source="paper">θ := t/s − 1 > p/α4</cite>. <cite data-source="paper">There are linear contractions</cite> $L$, from length-$s$ to length-$t$ vectors, and $M$, from length-$t$ to length-$s$ vectors, which do not increase the largest modulus of an entry. The identity is
$$P_s F_s = 2^{2\alpha^2}\, M\, P_t F_t\, L.$$

The proof uses <cite data-source="paper">The Gaussian resampling argument uses two maps</cite> $S$ and $T$, from length-$s$ to length-$t$ vectors. They satisfy $T P_s F_s = P_t F_t S$. A map $N$ built from $T$ and Gaussian corrections is within $1/2$ of the identity, <cite data-source="paper">so N is invertible by the Neumann series</cite>. Inverting $N$ and scaling then gives $L$ and $M$.

A transform of length $2^\ell$ splits into two transforms of length $2^{\ell-1}$, one on the even-indexed entries and one on the odd-indexed entries, combined by one layer of two-point butterflies. A butterfly replaces two entries by their sum and their difference. Repeating the split gives $\ell$ layers, each touching all $2^\ell$ entries once. The polynomial transforms keep this layered structure: <cite data-source="paper">as roots of unity in the remaining axes, and multiplication by such a root is a signed shift of the</cite> coefficient list.
