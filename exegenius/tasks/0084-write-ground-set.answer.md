A *ground set* is a finite set from which the subsets under consideration are chosen. It fixes the universe of elements that every subset is built from, so that the subsets can be compared, counted and indexed against one common set. The construction below takes its subsets from one ground set, and the three-element subsets of that ground set index the wires and gates of the network that the later pages build.

Here $h$ is the number of elements of the ground set, and $[h]=\{1,2,\dots,h\}$ is the set of those elements (the bracket notation is standard). A subset of $[h]$ is any collection of some of these elements, and a *three-element subset* is one that contains exactly three of them. The construction takes its subsets from the following definition: <cite data-source="paper">Let T be the set of three-element subsets of [h].</cite>

Take $h=4$. Then $[4]=\{1,2,3,4\}$, and its three-element subsets are $\{1,2,3\}$, $\{1,2,4\}$, $\{1,3,4\}$ and $\{2,3,4\}$, four in all. Each of these is itself a set, an element of the collection of subsets, and not an element of $[h]$. Two different three-element subsets can share one or two elements, and the size of their intersection is what distinguishes one pair of them from another in the later constructions.
