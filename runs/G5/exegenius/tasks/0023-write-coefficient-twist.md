## This page

Concept: Phase twist to a negacyclic wrap
Gloss: Multiplying coefficient k by ζ^k with ζ = e^{πi/r} turns convolution modulo x^r − 1 into convolution modulo y^r + 1 on the last axis, and untwisting restores the cyclic wrap.

## This page in the argument

The step: Multiplying coefficient $k$ by $\zeta^k$ with $\zeta=e^{\pi i/r}$ turns convolution modulo $x^r-1$ into convolution modulo $y^r+1$ on the last axis, so the complex cyclic convolution is the synthetic one conjugated by phase twists at cost $O(Tp^{1+\delta})$.
How: Define the twist as the diagonal ring isomorphism x_d to zeta y, check that the negacyclic wrap exactly compensates the twist, and compute the twists with grid phases
Why: The synthetic transforms need the last axis to be negacyclic, while the convolution to be computed is cyclic, so a diagonal phase change that converts one wrap into the other is needed. (the sources say so: "The power-of-two complex convolution used in the chirp identity is" (paper))

Its sub-steps, in order:

- The map $x_d\mapsto\zeta y$ is well defined because $(\zeta y)^r=(-1)(-1)=1$, and it is an isomorphism that multiplies coefficient $k$ by $\zeta^k$.
- If $k+l=j+hr$ then a product term acquires $\zeta^{k+l}(-1)^h=\zeta^j$, so untwisting recovers the cyclic coefficient with the correct sign.
- The computed complex convolution twists both operands, applies the ring convolution and untwists, with every phase product rounded by $Q_p$.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- One deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: Give the multiplication algorithm and show its output is exact, then total its cost stage by stage (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time](main-theorem.md))
- The algorithm, with digits placed on prime axes by the Chinese-remainder map, transformed by Gaussian resampling, chirp convolution and polynomial-ring convolution, multiplied pointwise, transformed back, rescaled, rounded and carry-propagated, outputs the exact $2n$-bit product.; how: Reduce the product to a cyclic convolution of digit lists and evaluate it with Fourier transforms, building from the fastest inner primitive (convolution over a polynomial ring) outward through the resampling, prime-axis and twist reductions, then bound the error so that rounding is exact (on [The multiplication algorithm](multiplication-algorithm.md))
