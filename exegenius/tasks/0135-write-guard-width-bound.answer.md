The guard-width bound is an explicit number of extra fractional and integer bits that makes every intermediate coefficient of one [simultaneous butterfly layer](simultaneous-butterfly-layer.md) exact, so that a single final truncation suffices. It lets the layer's output enter the exact multiplication algorithm, whose coefficients are recovered by rounding ([exact coefficient recovery](recovery-and-error.md)).

The two-point map sends $u, v$ to $((u+v)/2,\ (u-v)/2)$. The layer applies it on $D$ selected axes, and $d\ge D$ is a positive integer bound on $D$. The guard-width bound applies to output coefficients computed by the [tensor product](tensor-product.md) of the two-point map, so each output is $2^{-D}$ times a signed sum of input coefficients. Its entries are $\pm\tfrac12$, so each entry of the $D$-fold tensor product is $\pm2^{-D}$.

The bound must allow for operators that need not be [contractions](linear-contraction.md), meaning linear maps that can increase the largest modulus of a vector.<cite data-source="paper">Its intermediate operators need not be contractions.</cite> Rounding after each stage would be unsafe, since errors need not shrink. The layer is evaluated exactly, and only its result is truncated.<cite data-source="paper">The procedure evaluates the entire layer exactly before this final truncation.</cite>

Intermediate values are exact [Gaussian dyadics](gaussian-dyadics.md), so the bound reserves bits for them.<cite data-source="paper">Intermediate values are exact Gaussian dyadics</cite>

Here $p$ is the number of fractional bits each input carries, and $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ is the set of Gaussian integers. Inputs are disk-valued, meaning of modulus at most $1$:<cite data-source="paper">start with disk-valued coefficients in 2−p Z[i]</cite>

Let $\ell$ be the depth of a value, the number of arithmetic operations on a chain computing it from an input. A value at depth $\ell$ lies in $2^{-(p+\ell)}\mathbb{Z}[i]$ with modulus at most $2^\ell$.<cite data-source="paper">lies in 2−(p+ℓ) Z[i] and has modulus at most 2ℓ</cite> We induct on $\ell$. An operand of depth at most $\ell-1$ lies in $2^{-(p+\ell-1)}\mathbb{Z}[i]\subseteq2^{-(p+\ell)}\mathbb{Z}[i]$ with modulus at most $2^{\ell-1}$. A sum or difference has modulus at most $2\cdot2^{\ell-1}=2^\ell$. A halving lies in $2^{-(p+\ell)}\mathbb{Z}[i]$ with modulus at most $2^{\ell-1}$. Sign changes and multiplications by $i$ keep both bounds.

Write $\eta=64(W+m+1)^3$ (the paper's $E$) and $\beta=s+\eta$ (the paper's $B$), where $s$ is the number of smaller calls, and $W$ and $m$ are the wire count and label dimension of the [complex phase network](complex-phase-network.md). For a group of $e$ axes, $A(e)$ bounds the number of operations on any chain, with $A(e)\le sA(e/m)+\eta$ when the group splits into $s$ calls on groups of $e/m$ axes, and $A(e)\le8e$ otherwise.<cite data-source="paper">A(e) ≤ sA(e/m) + E</cite> Since $sx+\eta\le\beta x$ for $x\ge1$, each of at most $\lceil\log_m d\rceil$ levels multiplies the bound by $\beta$, so $A(e)\le8d\beta^{\lceil\log_m d\rceil+1}$. Let $\nu$ be the least integer $j\ge1$ with $m^j\ge\beta$. Since $\beta\le m^\nu$, $\beta^{\lceil\log_m d\rceil+1}\le\beta^2d^\nu$, so $A(e)\le8\beta^2d^{\nu+1}$.

Put $C_0=128m\beta^2$ and $C_1=\max\{20,\nu+3\}$. Including its final shift, total depth is at most $128m\beta^2d^{\nu+3}\le C_0d^{C_1}$, since $C_1\ge\nu+3$.<cite data-source="paper">128mB 2 dν+3 ≤ C0 dC1</cite> Hence $\ell\le C_0d^{C_1}$ for every value.

Let $\Delta=\lceil C_0d^{C_1}\rceil$ be the number of extra fractional bits that the layer reserves. Every intermediate value has $\ell\le\Delta$, so it lies in $2^{-(p+\Delta)}\mathbb{Z}[i]$ with modulus at most $2^\Delta$. A real component then needs $p+\Delta$ fractional bits and $\Delta+2$ integer and sign bits. The layer reserves $\Delta+3$.<cite data-source="paper">represents every intermediate coefficient exactly</cite> <cite data-source="paper">Shifts consume the reserved fractional zeros and discard no bits.</cite>

Here $\varepsilon$ is the exponent in $d=\Theta(p^{\varepsilon})$, chosen so that $\varepsilon C_1<1$; the letter changes meaning here. Then $\Delta=O(p^{\varepsilon C_1})$.<cite data-source="paper">Since d = Θ(pϵ ), ∆ = o(p)</cite> The guard-width bound states growth in the sense of [little-o notation](little-o-notation.md): $\Delta=o(p)$ means $\Delta/p\to0$ as $p$ grows. So each component has width $p+2\Delta+3=O(p)$. Scanning a component's bits takes time linear in its width, so addition, signed shifts, copying and phase changes each take $O(p)$ bit operations per coefficient.

Truncation toward zero onto $2^{-p}\mathbb{Z}[i]$ changes each component by less than $2^{-p}$, because<cite data-source="paper">truncation cannot increase the absolute value of either component, and changes each by less than</cite> $2^{-p}$.
