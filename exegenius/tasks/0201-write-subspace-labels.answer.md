Nested subspace labels are subspaces of one fixed space, attached to the gates and terminals of a network so that the dimension drop along each edge counts the smaller operations that edge needs. They give the cost bound for interchanging two equal address fields below linear in the field width.

Fix a space $E$ of dimension $m$, the common space of all labels; here $m$ is that dimension, not the numerator of a dyadic rational. The labels are built on a [Subset-intersection finite network](subset-network.md). Each label sits at a gate or at a [Source, sink and terminal of a network](network-terminal.md). A vertex $w$ of the network, a gate or such a terminal, carries a label $L(w)$, a subspace of $E$.

An edge runs from its tail $\sigma$ to its head $\tau$; these letters name vertices, not bank values. Along it the labels are nested, $L(\tau)\subseteq L(\sigma)$, and the drop of the edge is $\dim L(\sigma)-\dim L(\tau)$. <cite data-source="paper">The subspaces at adjacent vertices are nested.</cite> For example, a plane $L(\sigma)$ of dimension 2 containing a line $L(\tau)$ has drop $2-1=1$.

Each gate or sink with label $U$, a subspace of $E$, is assigned $P_U$, the projection onto $U$ along its orthogonal complement $U^\perp$. <cite data-source="paper">Assign $P_U$ to each gate or sink with label $U$</cite>. For the edge from $\sigma$ to $\tau$, the difference $P_{L(\sigma)}-P_{L(\tau)}$ is the identity on the part of $L(\sigma)$ orthogonal to $L(\tau)$ and zero on its orthogonal complement. The matrix of an edge is this difference up to sign, so the edge matrix has rank equal to the drop.

Each vertex $w$ also has a [Frame of a vertex](frame.md), written $D_w$: the stored array is $D_w g$ when the logical array is $g$. Along an edge from $\sigma$ to $\tau$ the stored array changes by $D_\tau D_\sigma^{-1}$. The labels are chosen to make cheap the changes of [Frame of a vertex](frame.md) on each edge. Along a route the intermediate frames cancel, so only the two end frames fix the operator on the route.

Let $s$ be the total number of smaller calls, the sum of the drops over all edges. <cite data-source="paper">The number of recursive calls is the sum of the edge ranks, exactly $s$.</cite> A smaller call is the same procedure run on one role stream. <cite data-source="paper">Each acts on one role stream of volume $V/W$</cite>.

The claim is that $s$ is below $Wm$. <cite data-source="paper">The decisive property is that the total number $s$ of smaller calls satisfies $s<Wm$.</cite> The recursion in [Rank-saving recursion](recursive-saving.md) then gives a cost below linear in the field width. <cite data-source="paper">Because the sum of these ranks is less than the number of wires times $m$, the resulting recurrence saves a power of the chunk width</cite>.
