## This page

Concept: Nested subspace labels and orthogonal residuals
Gloss: Attaching to every gate and terminal of the network a nondegenerate subspace of the m-dimensional space F^{⊗3}, nested along each edge, whose orthogonal residual dimension counts the smaller operations that edge needs.
Also called: label, orthogonal residual, dimension loss

## This page in the argument

The step: Every gate and terminal of either network can be labelled by a nondegenerate subspace of the $m$-dimensional space $\mathcal F=F^{\otimes3}$ so that labels are nested along every edge and the only decreasing edges are the central-wire edges from time 3 to time 4, each losing dimension $h$; hence the absolute label changes sum to $Wm-2N+2L$ with $L=Ich$.
How: Pair triple indicator vectors by a form under which neighbouring triples are orthogonal, expose one tensor factor per stage with prescribed incoming and outgoing labels, and assign common gate labels by the decomposition $A\otimes F=(B\otimes F)\perp(P\otimes F)$.
Why: Because neighbouring triple lines are orthogonal, a side wire's label can grow through $P\otimes\langle t_X\rangle\subset P\otimes t_Y^\perp$ without ever shrinking, so all loss of dimension is confined to the single return of each central wire. (the sources leave this for the reader to work out: spell it out)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- With the rational form $x^{\mathsf T}(I-J/9)y$ (bit network) or the binary dot product (complex network), every triple line is nondegenerate and neighbouring triple lines are orthogonal.
- The stage transitions expose one tensor factor at each stage, and the outgoing labels of one stage are exactly the incoming labels of the next, starting at $U_a$ on $X_a$ and ending at $U_a^\perp$ on $Y_a$.
- Every edge joins comparable nondegenerate labels, the only decreasing edges are central-wire edges from time 3 to time 4 with loss $h$, and in the binary label space every nonzero residual has an orthonormal basis.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- One deterministic fixed-alphabet, fixed-tape Turing machine computes the exact $2n$-bit product of two $n$-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ worst-case time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, disproving the $n\log n$ conjecture in this model.; how: Reduce the product to radix-digit convolution, move it by a Chinese-remainder map, Gaussian resampling, chirps and a phase twist to convolution over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ computed by synthetic transforms; speed up the transforms' address movement and butterfly layers with two network-based tape procedures; then prove exact recovery and total the costs. (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^{1−κ}) time](main-theorem.md))
- Two tape procedures save over plain scanning: address chunks of width $u$ are interchanged in $O(Vu^\tau)$ time, and a simultaneous normalized butterfly layer on $D\le d$ chunks costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$, with fixed $\tau,\lambda'<1$.; how: Build each procedure from its own fixed linear network that exchanges two banks and restores scratch wires, apply the network pointwise to row-split role streams with frames chosen so every edge is a few smaller calls, and solve the volume-normalized recurrence.
- Two fixed finite networks (a bit network over $\mathbb F_2$ and a complex network over $\mathbb Z[i,1/2]$) exchange two banks while restoring scratch wires, and their label budgets lose $L<N/2$, so the total number $s$ of smaller calls their edges need satisfies $s<Wm$.; how: Construct the three-stage bank exchange on triples of a 100-element set, label it with nested nondegenerate subspaces, and count the label-dimension changes. (on [Rank deficit s < Wm](rank-deficit.md))

Source: "Thus every triple line is nondegenerate, and neighboring triple lines

> For the bit network, let $F=\mathbb Q^h$ with bilinear form \[ \langle x,y\rangle=x^{\mathsf T}(I-J/9)y, \] where $J$ is the all-one matrix. This form has eigenvalues $1$ and $1-h/9=-91/9$, so it is nondegenerate. For a triple $T$ let $t_T$ be its indicator vector. Then \[ \langle t_S,t_T\rangle=|S\cap T|-1, \qquad \langle t_T,t_T\rangle=2. \] For the complex network, take instead $F=\mathbb F_2^h$ with the ordinary dot product; here $t_T\cdot t_T=1$. Thus every triple line is nondegenerate, and neighboring triple lines are orthogonal, in the label field belonging to either construction. The scalar domain need not equal the label field. These degree-one intersection formulas specialize the construction described by Alon~\cite[Section~3, remark after Theorem~1.1]{Alon1998} to the prime $2$, with the ground-set size changed to $h=100$.

**S1.2.2**: The stage transitions expose one tensor factor at each stage, and the outgoing labels of one stage are exactly the incoming labels of the next, starting at $U_a$ on $X_a$ and ending at $U_a^\perp$ on $Y_a$.
Source: "These transitions expose one tensor factor at each stage." (paper)
> ground field; thus $B=0$ at stage 1. Let $Q=\langle t_{a_{j+1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes t_{a_3}\rangle \subset F^{\otimes(3-j)}$, again using the empty tensor product at stage 3. Since $P$ is nondegenerate, $A=B\mathbin{\perp}P$. We prescribe the following transition for each data wire in the stage, where $t$ is the indicator of that wire's $j$th triple: \[ \begin{array}{c|c|c} &\text{incoming label}&\text{outgoing label}\\ \hline X&A\otimes\langle t\rangle\otimes Q&(A\otimes F)\otimes Q\\ Y&B\otimes\langle t\rangle\otimes Q& \bigl((B\otimes F)\mathbin{\perp}(P\otimes t^\perp)\bigr)\otimes Q. \end{array} \] The outgoing $Y$ label can also be written $(P\otimes\langle t\rangle)^\perp_{A\otimes F}\otimes Q$. These transitions expose one tensor factor at each stage. To see that they fit together, for $j<3$ write $Q=\langle t_{a_{j+1}}\rangle\otimes Q'$. The next stage has \[ A'=A\otimes F,\qquad P'=P\otimes\langle t\rangle,\qquad B'=(P')^\perp_{A'} =(B\otimes F)\mathbin{\perp}(P\otimes t^\perp). \] Thus the prescribed outgoing labels are $A'\otimes\langle t_{a_{j+1}}\rangle\otimes Q'$ on $X$ and $B'\otimes\langle t_{a_{j+1}}\rangle\otimes Q'$ on $Y$, exactly the next incoming labels. At stage 1 they start at $U_a$ on $X_a$ and zero on $Y_a$; at stage 3 they end at $\mathcal F$ on $X_a$ and $U_a^\perp$ on $Y_a$, as prescribed at the terminals.

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "The subspaces at adjacent vertices are nested." (paper)

  > The finite construction in Section~\ref{sec:finite-networks} attaches subspaces of a fixed $m$-dimensional space to its gates and terminals. The subspaces at adjacent vertices are nested. Their dimension changes count the smaller operations needed on an edge, with an additional source contribution in the bit construction. The construction uses three-element subsets: gather and scatter create coefficients depending on intersection size, and auxiliary wires cancel the unwanted coefficients. - "the \emph{orthogonal residual} is the orthogonal complement of the smaller label inside the larger one" (paper)

  > The scalar network already has the required signed exchange. We now choose its intermediate labels to make the changes of frame inexpensive. A label is a nondegenerate subspace of a fixed bilinear space. On every edge one label will contain the other. For such an edge, the \emph{orthogonal residual} is the orthogonal complement of the smaller label inside the larger one; its dimension is the absolute change in label dimension. The saving will come from keeping the total dimension lost on decreasing edges small relative to $N$.
