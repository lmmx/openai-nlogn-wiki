## This page

Concept: Bluestein chirp identity
Gloss: A DFT (here with a frequency permutation) equals pre-multiplication by a chirp phase, a cyclic convolution with the chirp, and post-multiplication by the conjugate chirp, so the middle transform becomes a normalized cyclic convolution.

## This page in the argument

The step: For the chirp $a_{\boldsymbol j}=\exp(-\pi i\sum_i s_ij_i^2/t_i)$ one has $QF_{\boldsymbol t}u=\overline a\cdot\bigl(a*(\overline a\cdot u)\bigr)/T$, so the middle transform with its frequency permutation is a normalized cyclic convolution between two phase products.
How: Compare the coefficient of $u_{\boldsymbol k}$ on both sides, using that the chirp has period $t_i$ in coordinate $i$.
Why: Completing the square $-s(j-k)^2+sj^2+sk^2$ in the exponent turns the frequency product $jk$ into a convolution, and the permutation $Q$ is absorbed by the chirp. (the sources do not say this: give the reader the reason)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- The coefficient of $u_{\boldsymbol k}$ on the right equals $T^{-1}\exp(2\pi i\sum_i s_ij_ik_i/t_i)$, which is the coefficient in $QF_{\boldsymbol t}$.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A single deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: Reduce multiplication to convolution over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ through radix digits, Chinese-remainder axes, Gaussian resampling and chirps, speed up the data movement and the butterfly arithmetic with two network-based tape procedures with a strict rank saving, recover the exact product by rounding and carries, and add up the costs. (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time](main-theorem.md))
- Fourier transforms of pairwise coprime lengths $s_i$ can be replaced by transforms of power-of-two lengths $t_i$ using Gaussian resampling, with the source and target permutations retained in the identity and the middle transform computed as a chirp convolution.; how: Apply Gaussian resampling along each axis with both coordinate permutations kept inside the transform identity, evaluate the middle transform by Bluestein's chirp as a cyclic convolution, and cancel the source permutation across the convolution.

**I3.1**: The coefficient of $u_{\boldsymbol k}$ on the right equals $T^{-1}\exp(2\pi i\sum_i s_ij_ik_i/t_i)$, which is the coefficient in $QF_{\boldsymbol t}$.
Source: "The coefficient of $u_{\boldsymbol k}$ in the right side of" (paper)
> \begin{proof} Replacing $j_i$ by $j_i+t_i$ changes the exponent defining $a$ by $-\pi i s_i(2j_i+t_i)$, an integral multiple of $2\pi i$. The coefficient of $u_{\boldsymbol k}$ in the right side of \eqref{eq:permuted-chirp} is \[ \frac1T\overline a_{\boldsymbol j} a_{\boldsymbol j-\boldsymbol k} \overline a_{\boldsymbol k} =\frac1T\exp\left(2\pi i\sum_i \frac{s_i j_i k_i}{t_i}\right). \] This is precisely the coefficient in $QF_{\boldsymbol t}$, because $Q$ reads frequency $(-s_i j_i)_i$ from the negative-sign transform.
