The limit inferior of a sequence of real numbers is the smallest value that the sequence keeps approaching as its index grows. The paper states the negation of its eventual lower bound on multiplication time with it, so the page is needed to read the claim that the main result satisfies: a limit inferior of zero for the ratio of multiplication time to the Schönhage–Strassen bound, as defined below.

Let $x_1, x_2, x_3, \dots$ be a sequence of real numbers, with $k$-th term $x_k$. For each position $m$, let $y_m = \inf_{k \ge m} x_k$, the smallest term at or after position $m$. Since the infimum is taken over fewer terms as $m$ grows, $y_m$ never decreases, and its limit exists in the extended reals. This limit is the limit inferior:
$$\liminf_{k\to\infty} x_k = \lim_{m\to\infty} y_m.$$

For example, let $x_k = (-1)^k + 1/k$. The even-indexed terms $1 + 1/k$ approach $1$, and the odd-indexed terms $-1 + 1/k$ approach $-1$ from above and never go below $-1$. So $y_m = -1$ for every $m$, and $\liminf_{k\to\infty} x_k = -1$. The sequence approaches $1$ infinitely often too, but the limit inferior picks the smaller of the values it approaches infinitely often.

**Zero limit inferior for nonnegative sequences.** Suppose every $x_k \ge 0$. Here $\varepsilon$ is a positive real tolerance. Then $\liminf_{k\to\infty} x_k = 0$ exactly when for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there are infinitely many $k$ with $x_k < \varepsilon$. Equivalently, some subsequence of $x$ tends to zero. The reason is that $0 \le y_m$ always, so $y_m \to 0$ means that for each $\varepsilon > 0$ and each $m$ some term at or after $m$ is below $\varepsilon$.

**Use in the paper.** Here $n$ is the number of bits in each input. For a fixed machine $A$, $T_A(n)$ is the worst-case running time of $A$ on inputs of $n$ bits. The conjecture of Schönhage and Strassen is an eventual lower bound: <cite data-source="paper">for each fixed machine A, there are constants cA > 0 and NA such that TA (n) ≥ cA n lg n</cite>, where $\lg$ is the base-2 logarithm. Here $c$ is a positive real constant, and $N$ is a threshold on input length. Its negation, for a given machine $A$, is that for every $c > 0$ and every $N$ there is an input length $n \ge N$ with $T_A(n) < c\, n\lg n$. The ratio $T_A(n)/(n\lg n)$ is nonnegative, so this negation says exactly that the ratio drops below every $c > 0$ infinitely often. By the criterion above, that is $\liminf_{n\to\infty} T_A(n)/(n\lg n) = 0$. The paper states the negation in this form: <cite data-source="paper">Its negation requires one machine satisfying lim inf n→∞ TA (n)/(n lg n) = 0.</cite>

The main result gives a machine whose worst-case time is at most $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ with $\kappa = 2^{-182}$. Dividing by $n\lg n$ gives a ratio of order $(\lg n)^{-\kappa}$, which tends to zero, so the limit inferior is zero. The paper's main claim is stated in its abstract: <cite data-source="paper">The algorithm is exact for every input length and disproves the n log n</cite>.
