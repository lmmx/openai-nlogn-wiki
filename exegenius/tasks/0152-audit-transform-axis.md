## Page to audit (`transform-axis`)

````markdown
# Transform axis

A transform axis is one coordinate direction of a multidimensional array or transform, and it has its own length. The multiplication algorithm works with arrays of this kind: [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md) replaces the Fourier transforms of the convolution by multidimensional transforms whose axis lengths are powers of two.

An array with $D$ axes has $D$ coordinates in each address, one per axis. The length $t_i$ of axis $i$ is the number of values its coordinate takes. Along axis $i$ an address has a coordinate $x_i$ with $0 \le x_i < t_i$. An array whose axes have lengths $t_1, \dots, t_D$ therefore has $t_1 t_2 \cdots t_D$ entries.

For example, take $D = 2$, $t_1 = 2$ and $t_2 = 4$. The array has $2 \cdot 4 = 8$ addresses $(x_1, x_2)$, with $x_1 \in \{0, 1\}$ and $x_2 \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$. An array is, so its entries are read in the order $(0,0), (0,1), (0,2), (0,3), (1,0), \dots, (1,3)$.

The power-of-two lengths are what the later construction needs. One axis is stored as the coefficient list of a polynomial, and the remaining axes take powers of $y$ as their roots of unity. Multiplying an entry by such a root therefore costs only a shift of the coefficient list, not a full multiplication.
````
