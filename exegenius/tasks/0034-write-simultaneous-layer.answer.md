On a stream of $V$ bits, with address chunks of $K$ bits and coefficients of $p$ fractional bits, the simultaneous butterfly layer applies $H_0=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$ to one bit in each of $D\le d$ chunks in time $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$, $\lambda'<1$, instead of $D$ whole-array passes.

With $X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathsf S=\operatorname{diag}(1,i)$, the [two-point kernel](phase-kernel.md) is $C=aI+bX$, $a=\frac{1+i}2$, $b=\frac{1-i}2$. Direct multiplication, using $\mathsf SI\mathsf S=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, $\mathsf SX\mathsf S=iX$, $ab=ib^2=\frac12$, gives $H_0=b\,\mathsf SC\mathsf S$, $C^2=X$, $C^4=I$ <cite data-source="paper">H_0=b\mathsf SC\mathsf S,\qquad C^2=X,\qquad C^4=I,\qquad</cite>. For selected bits $x_1,\dots,x_D$ of an address $x$, $H_0^{\otimes D}=b^D\mathcal S_DC^{\otimes D}\mathcal S_D$ and $(\mathcal S_Dz)(x)=i^{x_1+\dots+x_D}z(x)$, with phases and $b^D=(-i/2)^{\lfloor D/2\rfloor}b^{D\bmod2}$ exact Gaussian dyadic operations.

On $e=mf$ axes in $m$ slots of $f$ bits, $C^{\otimes e}$ is applied by the network of [Binary phase frames](phase-frames.md), whose edges are products of kernels $C^{\eta_h}$ along residual vectors $v_h\in\mathbb F_2^m$, $\eta_h=\pm1$. For invertible $M$ with first columns $v_h$, moving the value at $x$ to $Mx$ by $\mathcal P_M$ gives $\mathcal P_MX_v\mathcal P_M^{-1}=X_{Mv}$ <cite data-source="paper">\mathcal P_MX_v\mathcal P_M^{-1}=X_{Mv}.</cite> Gaussian elimination writes $M$ as at most $m(m-1)+3m$ row additions. So apply $\mathcal P_M^{-1}$ to each column, $(C^{\eta_h})^{\otimes f}$ to the $f$ bits of slot $h$, then $\mathcal P_M$: one child per residual vector. Negative kernels need no inverse child, as $(C^{-1})^{\otimes f}=(-i)^fZ^{\otimes f}C^{\otimes f}Z^{\otimes f}$, $Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$. Summing residual dimensions gives exactly $s$ children, each on $f=e/m$ axes.

Each row addition XORs selected bits of two slots, done by [Packed selected-bit addition](packed-bit-addition.md). So the basis change and its inverse cost $O(V((eK)^\tau+1))$ <cite data-source="paper">O(V((eK)^\tau+1))</cite> for an exponent $\tau<1$.

Let $W$ be the network's wire count and $F(e)$ the time divided by volume. Applying $C$ individually to the first $q_0=\lceil\log_2W\rceil\lceil\log_m(2d)\rceil$ chunks makes their bits a row index; row $Wg+w$ goes to wire $w$, so each child has volume $V/W$. The recurrence, solved as in [Recursive power saving](rank-recurrence.md), is $F(e)\le\tfrac sWF(e/m)+O((eK)^\tau+1)\ (e\ge d^\beta),\qquad F(e)=O(e)\ (e<d^\beta)$, with $\beta=\frac12$. For fixed $\max\{\tau,\sigma\}<\lambda<1$ the overhead is $O(e^\lambda)$, as $K\le d^c\le e^{c/\beta}$. With $\gamma=s/W\le m^\sigma<m^\lambda$, internal levels sum to $Ge^\lambda\sum_{j\ge0}(\gamma/m^\lambda)^j=O(e^\lambda)$, and leaves add $O(\gamma^ju)\le O(e^\sigma u^{1-\sigma})\le O(e^\sigma d^{\beta(1-\sigma)})$. Base-$m$ pieces of the remaining chunks give $O(\log(2d)[d^\lambda+d^{\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)}+1])=O(d^{\lambda'})$ <cite data-source="paper">=O(d^{\lambda'})</cite> for $\max\{\lambda,\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)\}<\lambda'<1$. Since $s/W<m^\sigma$, the saving is slight: $\lambda'=1-2^{-54}$ <cite data-source="paper">\lambda'=1-2^{-54},</cite>.

Arithmetic operations along a chain number at most $A(e)$, with $A(e)\le sA(e/m)+E$, $E=64(W+m+1)^3$, and $A(e)\le8e$ at leaves; since $sx+E\le Bx$ for $B=s+E$, $A(e)\le8B^2d^{\nu+1}$, $\nu=\min\{j\ge1:m^j\ge B\}$. A value after $\ell$ operations lies in $2^{-(p+\ell)}\mathbb Z[i]$ with modulus at most $2^\ell$: addition doubles modulus, halving adds a denominator bit. So with $\Delta=\lceil C_0d^{C_1}\rceil$, reserving $p+\Delta$ fractional bits and $\Delta+3$ integer and sign bits per real component, as in [Fixed-point Gaussian dyadic records](fixed-point.md), represents every intermediate coefficient exactly. For $d=\Theta(p^\epsilon)$, $\epsilon C_1<1$ gives $\Delta=o(p)$, so the width is $O(p)$.

In $\mathcal H=H_0^{\otimes D}$ every row has absolute row sum one, since rows of $H_0$ have entries $\pm\frac12$ and sums multiply under tensor products; hence $\mathcal H$ maps disk arrays (coefficients of modulus at most $1$) to disk arrays <cite data-source="paper">absolute row sum one.</cite> $Q_p$ truncates each real component toward zero, keeping the disk and changing each component by less than $2^{-p}$, so $\|Q_p(\mathcal Hz)-\mathcal Hz\|_\infty<\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$ <cite data-source="paper">truncation cannot increase the absolute value of either component</cite>.
