A transform axis is one coordinate direction of a multidimensional array, with its own length. A multidimensional transform, which is a transform over several axes, is computed by transforming along each axis in turn. [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md) replaces the Fourier transforms of the convolution by multidimensional transforms whose axis lengths are powers of two <cite data-source="paper">axis lengths are powers of two. After a further conversion to convolution, one coordinate is stored</cite>.

Let $D$ be the number of axes. Let $\mu$ be the index of one axis, $1 \le \mu \le D$. Let $\theta_\mu$ be the length of axis $\mu$, the number of values its coordinate takes. Let $\xi_\mu$ be the coordinate of an address along axis $\mu$, so $0 \le \xi_\mu < \theta_\mu$. Along each axis the array is acted on by a [Discrete Fourier transform](discrete-fourier-transform.md) of length $\theta_\mu$.

For example, take $D=2$, $\theta_1=2$ and $\theta_2=4$. The addresses are $(\xi_1,\xi_2)$ with $\xi_1\in\{0,1\}$ and $\xi_2\in\{0,1,2,3\}$. The array is <cite data-source="paper">stored in lexicographic order, with the first coordinate most significant</cite>, so the addresses run $(0,0),(0,1),(0,2),(0,3),(1,0),\dots,(1,3)$, with the first coordinate changing slowest.

The power-of-two lengths come from storage. Let $P$ be the number of complex coefficients in one stored polynomial, a power of two. Modulo $y^P+1$ means $y^P$ is replaced by $-1$, so the stored form is $\phi_0+\phi_1y+\dots+\phi_{P-1}y^{P-1}$, where $\phi_k$ is the coefficient of $y^k$. These $P$ coefficients form an axis of length $P$.

Since $y^P=-1$, multiplying by $y$ moves each coefficient one place up and negates the one that passes the top. For $P=4$, $y(\phi_0+\phi_1y+\phi_2y^2+\phi_3y^3)=-\phi_3+\phi_0y+\phi_1y^2+\phi_2y^3$. Multiplying by $y^k$ repeats this $k$ times, so <cite data-source="paper">multiplication by such a root is a signed shift of the</cite> coefficient list.

On the remaining axes the transform weights its entries by powers of $y$, each a [Root of unity](root-of-unity.md). Since $(y^P)^2=1$, every power of $y$ has order dividing $2P$. Let $\varrho \ge 2$ be a power of two dividing $2P$, the length of a transform axis. Let $\zeta=y^{2P/\varrho}$, a root of unity of order $\varrho$. Then $\zeta^\varrho=1$, while $\zeta^{\varrho/2}=y^P=-1\ne 1$, so $\zeta$ has order exactly $\varrho$. Its powers $1,\zeta,\dots,\zeta^{\varrho-1}$ are the $\varrho$ weights of a length-$\varrho$ transform, with $\zeta$ in place of $\omega$. For $P=4$ and $\varrho=8$, $\zeta=y$ itself, since $y^8=1$. So these lengths match the roots.
