## This page

Concept: Integer multiplication below n log n
Gloss: A deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplying two n-bit integers in O(n(lg n)^(1−κ)) time with κ=2^−182, disproving the n log n optimality conjecture in this model.

## This page in the argument

The step: A deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.
How: Reduce the product to a convolution of radix digits evaluated by approximate Fourier transforms over a polynomial ring, make the transform's address movement and arithmetic cheaper with two new tape procedures, then show the result is exact and add up the cost.
Why: Scanning the data once per transform level already costs $\Theta(n\log n)$, so a power saving needs savings in both address movement and arithmetic, one tape procedure for each. (the sources say so: "We therefore need savings in both movement and arithmetic." (paper))
Striking feature: The saving is a genuine power of the logarithm, but an astronomically small one, $\kappa=2^{-182}$, equal to half the smallest margin $2^{-181}$ among the cost terms; the proof must explain why such a tiny uniform saving survives all the overheads. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- Two disjoint contiguous address chunks of $u$ bits of an array of volume $V$ can be interchanged in time $O(Vu^\tau)$ for a fixed $\tau<1$, although the intermediate steps form XOR combinations of data. (on [Address chunk interchange](address-chunk-interchange.md))
- Applying $H_0(u,v)=((u+v)/2,(u-v)/2)$ to the same bit position in each of $D\le d$ address chunks of polynomial records at once costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ for a fixed $\lambda'<1$, with a single truncation at the end. (on [Simultaneous butterfly layer](simultaneous-butterfly-layer.md))
- The forward synthetic transform of $d-1$ axes over $\mathcal R_r$ and its opposite run in $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}])$ time with error below $\sqrt2\,\ell\,2^{-p}$, and the forward outputs are kept in a retained layout order. (on [Transform layout on tape](transform-tape-layout.md))
- The normalized cyclic convolution $\mathcal M_r(f,g)=(f*g)/T$ of arrays over $\mathcal R_r$ is computed from two forward synthetic transforms, a pointwise polynomial product divided by $r$ and the opposite transform, with error below $\sqrt2\,M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$. (on [Normalized convolution over the polynomial ring](normalized-ring-convolution.md))
- The normalized Fourier transform of the prime-length source can be computed, up to the exact factor $2^\gamma$, from a power-of-two transform: $RF_{\boldsymbol s}=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A$ with contractions $\mathcal A,\mathcal B_0$ and approximations of error below $dp^2$. (on [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md))
- With the chirp $a_{\boldsymbol j}=\exp(-\pi i\sum_i s_ij_i^2/t_i)$, the permuted transform is $QF_{\boldsymbol t}u=\overline a\cdot(a*(\overline a\cdot u))/T$: pointwise phases around a cyclic convolution. (on [Bluestein chirp identity](bluestein-chirp.md))
- Since $F^+_{\boldsymbol s}R=R^{-1}F^+_{\boldsymbol s}$, the source permutation $R$ cancels across two forward transforms, their pointwise product and the opposite transform: $(RF_{\boldsymbol s}^+)[(RF_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(RF_{\boldsymbol s}v)]=(u*v)/S^2$. (on [Retained permutation cancellation](permutation-cancellation.md))
- With $p=6b$, $d=\lfloor b^\epsilon\rfloor$, a power of two $T\in[4n/b,8n/b)$ split into axis lengths $t_i\in\{r/2,r\}$ and distinct primes $s_i$ just below them, $Tp=\Theta(n)$ and every hypothesis of the transform lemmas holds. (on [Parameter regime for the multiplier](parameter-regime.md))
- Writing each $n$-bit integer as a polynomial in $2^b$ with $q=\lceil n/b\rceil$ digits, the product has degree below $S$ so the cyclic convolution modulo $x^S-1$ has no wraparound, and each coefficient is below $2^{3b}$. (on [Radix-digit convolution](radix-digit-reduction.md))
- The map $x\mapsto\prod_i x_i^{\mu_i}$ is an algebra isomorphism from $\mathbb C[x]/(x^S-1)$ onto $\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_d]/(x_i^{s_i}-1)$ preserving convolution, and it is implemented with triangular controlled shifts and axis reorderings. (on [Chinese-remainder address map](crt-axis-map.md))
- The computed source transform $\widetilde{\mathscr F}$, from linewise Gaussian maps, a chirp convolution of length $T$ on the padded box, linewise $B_0$ and an exact scale $2^\gamma$, approximates $\mathscr F=RF_{\boldsymbol s}$ on the prime-length source. (on [Source transform on the padded box](tensor-source-transform.md))
- Digit conversion, $\Phi$, two forward source transforms, a pointwise product, one opposite transform, scalings by $S$ and $2^{2b+4}$, rounding, $\Phi^{-1}$ and carry propagation output the $2n$-bit product, the two scalings undoing the transform normalization and the digit scale. (on [The multiplication algorithm](multiplication-algorithm.md))
- Every call receives a disk-grid input, and the recovered convolution coefficients are within an error below $1/2$ of integers below $2^{3b}$, so rounding and carry propagation give the exact product. (on [Precision and exact recovery](precision-exact-recovery.md))
- Dividing every cost by $V=Tp$ gives normalized costs at most $p^{1-2\kappa}$, so the total time is $O(Tp\,p^{1-\kappa})=O(n(\log n)^{1-\kappa})$ with $\kappa=2^{-182}$. (on [Cost accounting and the exponent κ](cost-accounting.md))
