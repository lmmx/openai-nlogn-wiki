Signed coefficient packing (Kronecker substitution) encodes a polynomial with integer coefficients of either sign as one integer, so that a polynomial product becomes an integer product, which an ordinary $O(n\log n)$ multiplier computes.

Let $p$ be the working precision of the fixed-point page, so each coefficient of an input polynomial is an integer of absolute value at most $2^p$. Let $\Theta=2^{4p}$ be the block base. Each block is $4p$ bits wide <cite data-source="paper">Every block has $4p$ bits</cite>. Here $o$ and $\tau$ are coefficient positions, with $0\le o,\tau<r$.

Let $J=\sum_{o=0}^{r-1}J_o y^o$ denote a polynomial in $\mathbb Z[y]$ with $|J_o|\le 2^p$; its packed integer is $J(\Theta)=\sum_{o=0}^{r-1}J_o\Theta^{o}$.

Evaluation respects products. Let $\Delta=\sum_{\tau=0}^{r-1}\Delta_\tau y^\tau$ denote a second input polynomial with $|\Delta_\tau|\le 2^p$. Then $J(\Theta)\,\Delta(\Theta)=(J\Delta)(\Theta)$ exactly, where $J\Delta$ is the ordinary product in $\mathbb Z[y]$. Signed coefficient packing computes its integer products by the [Schönhage–Strassen to Harvey–van der Hoeven multiplication](ss-hh-background.md), an ordinary subroutine call that costs $O(n\log n)$.

Recovering the coefficients. Write $\Upsilon=J\Delta$, and $\Upsilon_\eta$ for its coefficient of $y^\eta$, with $\eta$ running over $0,\dots,2r-2$. Each $\Upsilon_\eta$ sums at most $r$ products $J_o\Delta_\tau$, so $|\Upsilon_\eta|\le r2^{2p}$. Recovery needs $|\Upsilon_\eta|<\Theta/2$, which holds because $r<2^p$: the ring degree is at most the padded length, $r\le T$ <cite data-source="paper">t_i\leq r\leq T<8n/b<2^{6b}=2^p</cite>, and $T<2^p$ for sufficiently large inputs by the choice of $p$ on the fixed-point page. Hence $|\Upsilon_\eta|\le r2^{2p}<2^{3p}<\Theta/2$. Since every $\Upsilon_\eta\Theta^\eta$ with $\eta\ge 1$ is a multiple of $\Theta$, $\Upsilon(\Theta)\equiv\Upsilon_0 \pmod{\Theta}$. As $|\Upsilon_0|<\Theta/2$, $\Upsilon_0$ is the unique integer in $[-\Theta/2,\Theta/2)$ congruent to $\Upsilon(\Theta)$ modulo $\Theta$. Subtracting it and dividing by $\Theta$ leaves the same form for $\Upsilon_1,\Upsilon_2,\dots$.

Example with $\Theta=12$. For $J=2-y$ and $\Delta=1+3y$, $J(12)=-10$ and $\Delta(12)=37$, so the packed product is $-370$. Recovery gives $\Upsilon_0=2$, $\Upsilon_1=5$, $\Upsilon_2=-3$, so $\Upsilon=2+5y-3y^2$.

Gaussian products. A record is a polynomial of degree less than $r$ with complex fixed-point coefficients. A record $\Xi=2^{-p}(a_r+\sqrt{-1}\,b_i)$ has real and imaginary components $a_r,b_i\in\mathbb Z[y]$. Records have norm at most one, so their parts lie in $[-1,1]$ <cite data-source="paper">A disk array has norm at most one.</cite>. Truncation puts each part on the grid $2^{-p}\mathbb Z$, so $2^p$ times each part is an integer, and its coefficients are at most $2^p$ in absolute value <cite data-source="paper">The truncated numerators have magnitude at most $2^p$</cite>. The second record $\Gamma=2^{-p}(c_r+\sqrt{-1}\,c_i)$ has components $c_r,c_i\in\mathbb Z[y]$, and the product is $(a_r+\sqrt{-1}\,b_i)(c_r+\sqrt{-1}\,c_i)=(a_rc_r-b_ic_i)+\sqrt{-1}\,(a_rc_i+b_ic_r)$.
The four ordinary products $a_rc_r$, $b_ic_i$, $a_rc_i$, $b_ic_r$ are each one packed product. Each is unpacked to exact coefficients before subtraction or addition, so the bound $|\Upsilon_\eta|<\Theta/2$ holds for each product, not for the sums.
