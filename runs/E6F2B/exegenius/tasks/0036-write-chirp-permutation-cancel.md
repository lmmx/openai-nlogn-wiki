## This page

Concept: Chirp identity and source-permutation cancellation
Gloss: A chirp-convolution formula for Q·F_t that leaves the target permutation unexecuted, together with the fact that the source permutation R cancels across the two forward transforms, the pointwise product and the opposite transform.

## This page in the argument

The step: The chirp identity $QF_{\boldsymbol t}u=\overline a\cdot(a*(\overline a\cdot u))/T$ evaluates the target-permuted transform without applying $Q$, and the source permutation cancels: $(RF^+_{\boldsymbol s})[(RF_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(RF_{\boldsymbol s}v)]=(u*v)/S^2$.
How: Check the chirp formula coefficient by coefficient, and show that $R$ commutes with the inverse transform and with pointwise products, so it cancels between the two forward transforms and the opposite transform.
Why: A chirp turns the transform with its frequency permutation into a cyclic convolution of ordinary arrays, and a permutation that appears on both sides of a pointwise product can be cancelled by commuting it through. (the sources leave this for the reader to work out: spell it out)
Striking feature: The source permutation $R$ does not appear in the final answer at all: it is applied by both forward transforms, undone by the opposite transform, and leaves only the factor $1/S^2$. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- The coefficient of $u_{\boldsymbol k}$ in $\overline a\cdot(a*(\overline a\cdot u))/T$ equals the coefficient in $QF_{\boldsymbol t}$.
- Since $RF^+_{\boldsymbol s}R=F^+_{\boldsymbol s}$ and pointwise products commute with permutations, the source permutation $R$ drops out and only the normalization $1/S^2$ remains.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A single deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ worst-case time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: Start from the Gaussian-resampling reduction of integer multiplication to convolution; replace the two costly operations of the synthetic transforms (address rearrangement and butterfly layers) by faster procedures built from one recursive linear-network exchange, assemble the exact algorithm, and account for its cost. > \begin{lemma}[Chirp and permutation cancellation] \label{lem:chirp-permutation} With $R,Q,s_i,t_i$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:tensor-resampling}, assume every $t_i$ is even. For $\boldsymbol j\in\mathbb Z^d$, define \[ a_{\boldsymbol j} =\exp\left(-\pi i\sum_{i=1}^d\frac{s_i j_i^2}{t_i}\right). \] Then $a$ has period $t_i$ in coordinate $i$, and \begin{equation} QF_{\boldsymbol t}u =\overline a\mathbin{\cdot} \frac{a*(\overline a\mathbin{\cdot}u)}{T}. \label{eq:permuted-chirp} \end{equation} If $S=\prod_i s_i$, then \begin{equation} (RF_{\boldsymbol s}^+) \bigl[(RF_{\boldsymbol s}u)\mathbin{\cdot} (RF_{\boldsymbol s}v)\bigr] =\frac{u*v}{S^2}. \label{eq:source-permutation-cancel} \end{equation} Complex conjugation of the input and output of an algorithm for $RF_{\boldsymbol s}$ gives an algorithm for $RF_{\boldsymbol s}^+$. \end{lemma}
