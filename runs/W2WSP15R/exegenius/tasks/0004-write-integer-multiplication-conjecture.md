## This page

Concept: The n log n conjecture for integer multiplication
Gloss: Schönhage and Strassen's 1971 proposal that n log n is the optimal multitape-Turing-machine time for multiplying two n-bit integers; the O(n log n) algorithm of Harvey and van der Hoeven met it from above, and this paper beats it by a power of the logarithm.
Also called: n log n optimality conjecture, Schönhage–Strassen conjecture

## This page in the argument

The step: Setting: Schönhage and Strassen proposed $n\log n$ as the optimal order of multitape-Turing-machine time for multiplying two $n$-bit integers, Harvey and van der Hoeven reached $O(n\log n)$ from above, and a strict power saving in the logarithm is obtained in the same fixed finite-tape model.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A single deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ worst-case time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: Reduce the product to a convolution of short integer coefficients, evaluate the convolution through power-of-two transforms over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$, and replace the two tape operations that each cost a factor $\log n$ (address rearrangement and butterfly layers) by procedures whose cost per bit is a power of their parameter below one; then total the costs. (on [Integer multiplication below n log n](main-theorem.md))

## Pages that build on this one

- Integer multiplication below n log n: A single deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies two n-bit integers exactly in O(n (lg n)^{1−κ}) worst-case time with κ = 2^{−182}, disproving the n log n optimality conjecture; it works by reducing to convolution, using fast address interchange and fast simultaneous butterfly layers to save a power of the logarithm.

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "proposed $n\log n$ as the optimal order of growth" (paper)

  > Sch\"onhage and Strassen proved in 1971 that two $n$-bit integers can be multiplied in $O(n\log n\log\log n)$ time on a multitape Turing machine, and proposed $n\log n$ as the optimal order of growth \cite[Section~1]{SchonhageStrassen1971}. After a sequence of improvements, Harvey and van der Hoeven reached the unconditional $O(n\log n)$ upper bound~\cite{HarveyHoeven2021}. We obtain a strict power saving in the logarithmic factor, in the same fixed finite-tape model. The algorithm uses linear combinations of intermediate data to reduce the cost of both address rearrangement and Fourier-transform layers.

- "In the multitape Turing model," (paper)

  > In the multitape Turing model, Sch\"onhage and Strassen proved the bound $O(n\log n\log\log n)$ in 1971~\cite{SchonhageStrassen1971}. F\"urer improved it to $n\log n\,2^{O(\log^* n)}$ in 2007, with a full journal account in 2009~\cite{Furer2007,Furer2009}; here $\log^* n$ counts iterated logarithms until a constant threshold is reached. De, Kurur, Saha and Saptharishi developed a modular-arithmetic route to the same scale~\cite{DeKururSahaSaptharishi2013}. Later improvements made the exponential factor $8^{\log^* n}$ and then $4^{\log^* n}$~\cite{HarveyHoevenLecerf2016,HarveyHoeven2019Lattice}. Harvey and van der Hoeven proved the unconditional $O(n\log n)$ bound~\cite{HarveyHoeven2021}.
