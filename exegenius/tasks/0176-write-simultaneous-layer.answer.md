The simultaneous butterfly layer applies $H_0$ coefficientwise to one bit of each of $D\le d$ address chunks of $K$ bits (selected at a common offset, $d$ bounding their number) in time $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ with $\lambda'<1$, instead of $D$ whole-array passes; it is the second tape primitive that speeds up the synthetic transforms.

With $\mathsf S=\operatorname{diag}(1,i)$, the [Two-point kernel C](phase-kernel.md), $C=aI+bX$, satisfies $H_0=b\,\mathsf SC\mathsf S$, $C^2=X$ and $C^4=I$ <cite data-source="paper">H_0=b\mathsf SC\mathsf S,\qquad C^2=X,\qquad C^4=I,\qquad</cite>. Let $x_1,\dots,x_D$ be the selected bits of an address $x$. Applying this on every selected bit gives $H_0^{\otimes D}=b^D\mathcal S_DC^{\otimes D}\mathcal S_D$ with $(\mathcal S_Dz)(x)=i^{x_1+\dots+x_D}z(x)$. The phases $\mathcal S_D$ and the scalar $b^D=(-i/2)^{\lfloor D/2\rfloor}b^{D\bmod2}$ (a shift, a fourth-root phase, at most one factor $b$) are exact Gaussian dyadic operations, so only $C^{\otimes D}$ remains.

## One group of axes

Take a group of $e=mf$ selected axes, cut into $m$ slots of $f$ axes each; a column holds one bit per slot, so lies in $\mathbb F_2^m$. On the group, $C^{\otimes e}$ is computed by the complex network of [Binary phase frames](phase-frames.md), which has $W$ wires and $s$ smaller calls. Each edge changes the frame on every column by $\prod_hC_{v_h}^{\eta_h}$, where $v_h$ runs over a residual basis of the edge, indexed by $h$, $\eta_h=\pm1$, $C_v=aI+bX_v$ and $X_v$ shifts a column by $v$. Let $G$ be an invertible matrix over $\mathbb F_2$ whose first columns are the $v_h$, and $\mathcal P_G$ the address permutation $x\mapsto Gx$. Then $\mathcal P_GX_v\mathcal P_G^{-1}=X_{Gv}$ <cite data-source="paper">\mathcal P_MX_v\mathcal P_M^{-1}=X_{Mv}.</cite>, which uses only that $G$ is invertible. So the edge change is $\mathcal P_G(\prod_hC_{e_h}^{\eta_h})\mathcal P_G^{-1}$ with $e_h$ the standard basis vectors: apply $\mathcal P_G^{-1}$ to each column, then $(C^{\eta_h})^{\otimes f}$ to the $f$ bits of slot $h$, then $\mathcal P_G$. That is one child on $f=e/m$ axes per residual vector, hence exactly $s$ children. A negative power needs no extra child, since $(C^{-1})^{\otimes f}=(-i)^fZ^{\otimes f}C^{\otimes f}Z^{\otimes f}$ with $Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, whose wrappers are signs and phases.

Gaussian elimination writes $G$ as at most $m(m-1)+3m$ row additions (a pivot clears at most $m-1$ entries; a row swap is three additions). A row addition XORs the selected bits of one slot into another, and [Packed selected-bit addition](packed-bit-addition.md) XORs selected bits by cyclic shifts of whole chunk ranges plus a repair of a bounded exceptional set of addresses. The network is fixed, so the number of row additions is fixed, and the complete basis change and its inverse on $e$ axis chunks cost $O(V((eK)^\tau+1))$ <cite data-source="paper">O(V((eK)^\tau+1))</cite> on a stream of volume $V$, with $\tau<1$.

## Recursion

Dealing rows round-robin, a row goes to the wire role equal to its index mod $W$, at position index div $W$ there, so each child runs on a stream of volume $V/W$. This needs the row count divisible by $W$ at every level, and there are at most $\lceil\log_md\rceil$ levels. So the first $q_0=\lceil\log_2W\rceil\lceil\log_m(2d)\rceil$ chunks ($q_0$ counts chunks) are done by individual $C$ kernels in $O(V\log d)$ (all chunks if $D\le q_0$), and their bits form a row index of range at least $W^{\lceil\log_md\rceil}$. Zero rows pad each block to a multiple of $W^{\lceil\log_md\rceil}$; fewer than that many are added to a block already that long, so the volume at most doubles, and at depth $j$ the row count is divisible by $W^{\lceil\log_md\rceil-j}$. With $\beta=\tfrac12$ and $F(e)$ the time divided by volume,
$$F(e)\le \tfrac sW F(e/m)+O((eK)^\tau+1)\ (e\ge d^\beta),\qquad F(e)=O(e)\ (e<d^\beta),$$
the same recursion as [Recursive power saving](rank-recurrence.md) and [Fast address-chunk interchange](address-interchange.md).

Write $\gamma=s/W\le m^\sigma$ with $\sigma<1$, $K\le d^c$ for fixed $c>0$, and take $\lambda$ with $\max\{\tau,\sigma\}<\lambda<1$ and $\tau(1+c/\beta)<\lambda$. For $e\ge d^\beta$, $K\le e^{c/\beta}$, so the overhead is $O(e^\lambda)$ and the levels $j\ge0$ sum to $O(e^\lambda)\sum_{j\ge0}(\gamma/m^\lambda)^j=O(e^\lambda)$. A leaf at level $j$ has $u=e/m^j<d^\beta$ chunks and $\gamma^j\le(e/u)^\sigma$, so leaves add $O(\gamma^ju)\le O(e^\sigma d^{\beta(1-\sigma)})$. Writing $D-q_0=\sum_ja_jm^j$ with $0\le a_j<m$ gives $a_j$ pieces of $m^j$ chunks, at most $(m-1)(1+\lfloor\log_md\rfloor)$ in all, so
$$O(\log(2d)[d^\lambda+d^{\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)}+1])=O(d^{\lambda'})$$
for $\max\{\lambda,\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)\}<\lambda'<1$. The paper's exponents allow $\lambda'=1-2^{-54}$ <cite data-source="paper">\lambda'=1-2^{-54},</cite>, barely below $D$ passes. The saving comes from $\gamma<m^\sigma$: per factor $m$ of $e$ the cost per volume grows by less than $m$.

## Exactness

The layer is evaluated in [Fixed-point Gaussian dyadic records](fixed-point.md), where intermediate values are exact and one truncation $Q_p$ follows. Let $A(e)$ bound the arithmetic operations on any chain from an input coefficient to an intermediate one. One invocation of the network has at most $12W$ gates (each wire is in at most twelve), each evaluated from saved inputs in at most $2W^2$ operations (an output sums at most $W$ terms, each needing a halving and an addition), the inverse-child signs and phases add at most $4s$, and source, sink and bank corrections at most $4W+4$. As $s<Wm$, this is $24W^3+4s+4W+4<E=64(W+m+1)^3$. An individual pair of $C$ kernels takes eight operations (sum, difference, times $i$, two half-sums). Chains concatenate the $s$ children, so
$$A(e)\le sA(e/m)+E,\qquad A(e)\le8e\ (\text{leaf}).$$
With $B=s+E$, $sx+E\le Bx$ for $x\ge1$, so over at most $\lceil\log_md\rceil$ levels from the leaf bound $8e\le8dB$, $A(e)\le8dB^{\lceil\log_md\rceil+1}$. With $\nu=\min\{j\ge1:m^j\ge B\}$, $B^{\lceil\log_md\rceil+1}\le B^{\log_md+2}=B^2d^{\log_mB}\le B^2d^\nu$, so $A(e)\le8B^2d^{\nu+1}$. The at most $md$ pieces add depth at most $8mB^2d^{\nu+2}$, the individual kernels at most $8d$, and the two diagonals and $b^D$ (a shift of $\lfloor D/2\rfloor$ places charged as that many halvings, a phase, possibly a factor $b$) at most $\lfloor D/2\rfloor+9$. The total is at most $8mB^2d^{\nu+2}+18d\le26mB^2d^{\nu+2}<128mB^2d^{\nu+3}\le\Delta=\lceil C_0d^{C_1}\rceil$ with $C_0=128mB^2$ and $C_1=\max\{20,\nu+3\}$.

A depth-$\ell$ value lies in $2^{-(p+\ell)}\mathbb Z[i]$ with modulus at most $2^\ell$, by induction: addition at most doubles the modulus, and halving adds a denominator bit. So $p+\Delta$ fractional bits and $\Delta+3$ integer and sign bits per real component represent every intermediate coefficient exactly <cite data-source="paper">represents every intermediate coefficient exactly and prevents overflow</cite>. With $d=\Theta(p^\epsilon)$ and $\epsilon C_1<1$, $\Delta=o(p)$, so the width is $O(p)$.

## Truncation

Each row of $H_0$ has absolute row sum one, and these multiply under tensor products, so every row of $\mathcal H=H_0^{\otimes D}$ has absolute row sum one <cite data-source="paper">absolute row sum one.</cite> Hence $\mathcal H$ maps disk arrays to disk arrays. Componentwise truncation toward zero cannot increase either component's absolute value, so $Q_p$ keeps the disk and changes each component by less than $2^{-p}$, giving $\|Q_p(\mathcal Hz)-\mathcal Hz\|_\infty<\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$ <cite data-source="paper">truncation cannot increase the absolute value of either component</cite>.
