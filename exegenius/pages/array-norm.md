The norm of an array is the largest modulus among its entries, so it measures the size of the worst entry. It is the size measure in which the [convolution over the polynomial ring](polynomial-ring-convolution.md) and the error bound of [exact coefficient recovery](recovery-and-error.md) are stated.

The norm is defined on each finite [Array](array.md) of complex values. Let $X$ be such an array, and let $u$ range over its entries. The modulus of $u$ is its distance from $0$ in the complex plane: $|u| = \sqrt{\operatorname{Re}(u)^2 + \operatorname{Im}(u)^2}$, where $\operatorname{Re}(u)$ and $\operatorname{Im}(u)$ are the real and imaginary parts of $u$. The norm of $X$, written $\|X\|_\infty$, is the largest modulus $|u|$ over all entries $u$ of $X$.

For example, let $X$ have the entries $0.6+0.8\,\mathrm{i}$, $-0.3$ and $0.5\,\mathrm{i}$, where $\mathrm{i}$ is the imaginary unit. Their moduli are $1$, $0.3$ and $0.5$, so $\|X\|_\infty=1$. Replacing $-0.3$ by $1.2$ gives norm $1.2$.

Since $\|X\|_\infty$ is the largest modulus, $\|X\|_\infty \le 1$ holds exactly when every entry has modulus at most one. The source uses the name disk array for such an array: <cite data-source="paper">A disk array has norm at most one.</cite>

The norm extends to arrays over the [polynomial ring](polynomial-ring-mod-yr-plus-1.md). Here $r$ is the number of coefficients in each representative. The ring is $\mathbb{C}[y]/(y^r+1)$, the polynomials with complex coefficients in $y$, taken modulo $y^r+1$, so $y^r$ is replaced by $-1$. Each element $h$ of the ring is written $h = \sum_{\ell=0}^{r-1} h_\ell y^\ell$, where the index $\ell$ runs from $0$ to $r-1$ and $h_\ell$ is the coefficient of $y^\ell$. <cite data-source="paper">We always use representatives of degree less than r.</cite> The norm of $h$ is $\max_\ell |h_\ell|$, the largest modulus among its coefficients. For an array over the ring, <cite data-source="paper">the same notation denotes the maximum over all coefficients in all entries.</cite>
