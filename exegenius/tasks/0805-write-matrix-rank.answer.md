Matrix rank is the number of linearly independent rows of a matrix, taken over the rational numbers $\mathbb{Q}$. The paper uses it to price matrix shears, which cost one interchange per unit of rank.

Matrix rank is defined using the vector spaces and linear maps of [Linear algebra basics](linear-algebra-basics.md). Let $A$ be an $m \times m$ matrix over $\mathbb{Q}$. The rank $\operatorname{rank} A$ is the largest number of rows of $A$ that are linearly independent, meaning that no nontrivial rational combination of them is the zero vector. Row reduction computes it: each pivot, a nonzero entry left after clearing, marks one independent row, and the pivots are counted.

The paper's Lemma 9 factors every such matrix. In that factorization, $\mathcal{L}_1$ and $\mathcal{L}_2$ are invertible lower triangular matrices (zero above the diagonal) and $\mathcal{P}$ is a partial permutation matrix (at most one 1 in each row and column, all other entries zero). <cite data-source="paper">Every rational m × m matrix A of rank a has a</cite> factorization $A = \mathcal{L}_1 \mathcal{P} \mathcal{L}_2$.

The proof is elimination. It takes the topmost nonzero row and its rightmost nonzero entry as a pivot, clears the entries to its left by lower triangular column operations and the entries below it by lower triangular row operations, and scales the pivot to 1. It then deletes that row and column and repeats. <cite data-source="paper">Each step isolates a rank-one block and leaves the remaining rank in the active submatrix.</cite> Each step therefore places one 1 in $\mathcal{P}$, and <cite data-source="paper">Thus the final partial permutation matrix has exactly a ones.</cite>

Invertible factors do not change rank, so $\operatorname{rank} A = \operatorname{rank} \mathcal{P}$. The nonzero rows of $\mathcal{P}$ each contain exactly one 1, and no two put it in the same column, so these rows are distinct vectors of the form [standard basis vectors](standard-basis-vector.md). Distinct standard basis vectors are linearly independent, and zero rows add nothing. The rank of $\mathcal{P}$ counts the rows that are [standard basis vectors](standard-basis-vector.md), which is the number of ones in $\mathcal{P}$.

Example: $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ has row 2 equal to twice row 1, so $\operatorname{rank} A = 1$. It factors as $A = \mathcal{L}_1 \mathcal{P} \mathcal{L}_2$ with $\mathcal{L}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 4 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $\mathcal{P} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathcal{L}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ \frac{1}{2} & 1 \end{pmatrix}$. Here $\mathcal{L}_1 \mathcal{P} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$, and multiplying by $\mathcal{L}_2$ gives $A$.

The factorization prices a shear. Let $H = (H_1, \ldots, H_m)$ and $J = (J_1, \ldots, J_m)$ be two ordered families of $m$ address fields, each field a block of digits; here $H$ and $J$ name these families, not the address blocks of earlier pages. An interchange swaps an $H$ field with a $J$ field. The shear for $A$ sends $(H, J)$ to $(H + AJ, J)$. The paper writes this matrix as $\Pi$. <cite data-source="paper">Each one in position (i, j) of Π calls for</cite> the update $H_i \leftarrow H_i + J_j$. Three operations perform it: $J_j \leftarrow J_j + H_i$, then a swap of $H_i$ with $J_j$, then $J_j \leftarrow H_i - J_j$. The swap is the only interchange. <cite data-source="paper">It uses just one interchange.</cite> The triangular factors cost linear work: <cite data-source="paper">A lower triangular transformation of H, or of D, costs</cite> $O(V)$, where $D$ is the paper's name for $J$ and $V$ is the stored bit volume. Hence the shear for $A$ <cite data-source="paper">can be performed with exactly a interchanges</cite>, one for each 1 in $\mathcal{P}$, so the number of interchanges is the rank.

In the network, each vertex carries a rational $m \times m$ matrix, and the edge matrix of an edge is the difference of the matrices at its two ends. The rank of an edge matrix prices each edge change in the [Linear networks and frames](linear-network-frames.md). <cite data-source="paper">implements an edge change with one smaller interchange per unit of its rational rank.</cite> <cite data-source="paper">The sum of the rational ranks of all edge differences is</cite> the total cost. The paper's Lemma 10 ends with <cite data-source="paper">the number of recursive calls is the rational rank a</cite>, which feeds the [Rank-saving recursion](rank-saving-recursion.md).
