The Frankl–Wilson construction is a result on set systems whose intersection sizes are restricted modulo a prime. Its algebraic content is a low-degree polynomial representation of an intersection graph: a polynomial in the vectors attached to two subsets whose value is zero or nonzero according to how their intersection is sized. Low degree means the polynomial has few terms, which is what makes the representation useful over a field.

The paper cites Alon's presentation of this construction. That presentation gives low-degree representations of complementary intersection graphs over different fields, so the same subsets can be used for two different algebraic tests, one over each field.

The case the paper uses has prime $p = 2$. Its subsets have size three, and the two polynomial evaluations are the intersection count modulo two and the intersection count minus one. These are the two pairing formulas the paper uses. The [subset-intersection construction](subspace-intersection-network.md) page shows how they are used.

The paper does not use the construction unchanged. It changes the size of the ground set, and it expresses the second formula through a rational bilinear form. These changes are what let the subspace labels of its network be built from the same intersection data.
