The simultaneous butterfly layer, the second tape primitive of the multiplier, applies $H_0=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$ coefficientwise to records of $r$ complex $p$-bit coefficients, on one bit of each of $D\le d$ address chunks of $K$ bits (at a common offset), then truncates once with $Q_p$. It takes $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ time, $V$ the array volume and $\lambda'<1$ fixed, while separate factors need $D$ whole-array passes. Here $D$ counts selected chunks (unlike the chunk value of [Fast address-chunk interchange](address-interchange.md) or the source group of [Matrix shear by triangular factorization](matrix-shear.md)), $e$ counts selected axes (not a chunk's digits), and $z$ is an array.

Let $a=\frac{1+i}2$, $b=\frac{1-i}2$, $X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathsf S=\operatorname{diag}(1,i)$. The [Two-point kernel C](phase-kernel.md) is $C=aI+bX$. Since $ib=a$ and $ab=\frac12$, $\mathsf SC\mathsf S=a\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$, so $H_0=b\,\mathsf SC\mathsf S$. Also $a^2=\frac i2=-b^2$, so $C^2=X$ and $C^4=I$ <cite data-source="paper">H_0=b\mathsf SC\mathsf S,\qquad C^2=X,\qquad C^4=I,\qquad</cite>. For an address $x$ with selected bits $x_1,\dots,x_D$, let $(\mathcal S_Dz)(x)=i^{x_1+\cdots+x_D}z(x)$. Applying the identity on every selected bit gives
$$H_0^{\otimes D}=b^D\mathcal S_DC^{\otimes D}\mathcal S_D.$$
The phases and $b^D$ (a shift, a fourth-root phase and at most one factor $b$) are exact Gaussian dyadic operations, leaving $C^{\otimes D}$.

A group of $e=mf$ selected axes splits into $m$ slots of $f$ axes; one bit per slot forms a column in $\mathbb F_2^m$. The layer is based on the finite network of [Binary phase frames](phase-frames.md), with $W$ wires and $s$ smaller calls. Each edge changes the frame by $\prod_\xi C_{v_\xi}^{\pm1}$, where $v_\xi$ is an orthonormal residual basis, $C_v=aI+bX_v$, and $X_v$ translates a column by $v$. Let $\Gamma$ be an invertible $\mathbb F_2$-matrix with first columns $v_\xi$, and $\mathcal P_\Gamma$ the address permutation moving the value at $x$ to $\Gamma x$. Then $\mathcal P_\Gamma X_v\mathcal P_\Gamma^{-1}=X_{\Gamma v}$ <cite data-source="paper">\mathcal P_MX_v\mathcal P_M^{-1}=X_{Mv}.</cite>, which uses only invertibility. The edge change becomes $\mathcal P_\Gamma\bigl(\prod_\xi C_{\mathbf 1_\xi}^{\pm1}\bigr)\mathcal P_\Gamma^{-1}$, with $\mathbf 1_\xi$ the $\xi$-th standard basis vector. So apply $\mathcal P_\Gamma^{-1}$ to each column, then $(C^{\pm1})^{\otimes f}$ to the $f$ bits of slot $\xi$, then $\mathcal P_\Gamma$: one child on $f=e/m$ axes per residual vector. An exponent $-1$ costs no extra call, since $(C^{-1})^{\otimes f}=(-i)^fZ^{\otimes f}C^{\otimes f}Z^{\otimes f}$ with $Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$. Summing residual dimensions gives exactly $s$ children.

Gaussian elimination writes $\Gamma$ as at most $m(m-1)+3m$ row additions (a swap is three), each XORing selected bits of one slot into another. The layer performs its row additions by [Packed selected-bit addition](packed-bit-addition.md), which XORs selected bits of two chunks by cyclic shifts of whole chunk ranges plus a repair of exceptional addresses. With a fixed number of additions, the change of basis and its inverse on $e$ axis chunks cost $O(V((eK)^\tau+1))$ <cite data-source="paper">O(V((eK)^\tau+1))</cite> for a fixed $\tau<1$.

Rows go to the $W$ wire roles by row number modulo $W$, so each child works on a stream of volume $V/W$. To keep the row count divisible by $W$ at every level, the first $q_0=\lceil\log_2W\rceil\lceil\log_m(2d)\rceil$ chunks get individual $C$ kernels, $O(V\log d)$ in all, and supply a padded row index. Let $F(e)$ be the time on $e$ axes per unit volume, and $\beta=\frac12$. A node pays $O((eK)^\tau+1)$ for basis changes, its $s$ children cost $s(V/W)F(e/m)$, and below $d^\beta$ axes kernels are applied individually. Hence
$$F(e)\le\tfrac sWF(e/m)+O((eK)^\tau+1)\ (e\ge d^\beta),\qquad F(e)=O(e)\ (e<d^\beta).$$
The time bound follows from [Recursive power saving](rank-recurrence.md). Take $\sigma<1$ with $s/W\le m^\sigma$, and $\lambda$ with $\max\{\tau,\sigma\}<\lambda<1$ and $\tau(1+c/\beta)<\lambda$, where $K\le d^c$. At internal nodes $K\le e^{c/\beta}$, so the overhead is $O(e^\lambda)$, and the internal levels sum geometrically to $O(e^\lambda)$. The leaves contribute $O(e^\sigma d^{\beta(1-\sigma)})$, since $(s/W)^j\le m^{j\sigma}$ after $j$ levels. Writing $D-q_0$ in base $m$ cuts the remaining chunks into $O(\log d)$ pieces of $m^j$ chunks, giving $O(\log(2d)[d^\lambda+d^{\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)}+1])=O(d^{\lambda'})$ for fixed $\max\{\lambda,\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)\}<\lambda'<1$. The paper takes $\sigma=1-2^{-50}$ and $\lambda'=1-2^{-54}$ <cite data-source="paper">\lambda'=1-2^{-54},</cite>: $s/W$ is only just below $m$, so the gain is a small power of $d$.

The layer is evaluated exactly in [Fixed-point Gaussian dyadic records](fixed-point.md). Let $\chi(e)$ bound the arithmetic operations on a chain from an input coefficient to an intermediate one. One invocation takes fewer than $\Xi=64(W+m+1)^3$ operations, as $s<Wm$ makes its total $24W^3+4s+4W+4$ <cite data-source="paper">\(24W^3+4s+4W+4\) is less than</cite> that. Chains concatenate the $s$ children (not $s/W$); a leaf pair takes eight operations:
$$\chi(e)\le s\chi(e/m)+\Xi,\qquad\chi(e)\le8e\ \text{at a leaf}.$$
With $\Psi=s+\Xi$, $sx+\Xi\le\Psi x$ for $x\ge1$, so each of the at most $\lceil\log_md\rceil$ internal levels multiplies the bound by at most $\Psi$: $\chi$ stays below $\Delta=\lceil C_0d^{C_1}\rceil$ for constants $C_0$ and $C_1$. A value at depth $\ell$ lies in $2^{-(p+\ell)}\mathbb Z[i]$ with modulus at most $2^\ell$: addition at most doubles the modulus, and halving adds a denominator bit. So $p+\Delta$ fractional bits and $\Delta+3$ integer and sign bits per real component represent every intermediate coefficient exactly <cite data-source="paper">represents every intermediate coefficient exactly</cite>. As $d=\Theta(p^\epsilon)$ and $\epsilon C_1<1$, $\Delta=o(p)$, so the width is $O(p)$.

Let $\mathcal H=H_0^{\otimes D}$. Rows of $H_0$ have absolute row sum $\frac12+\frac12=1$, and tensor-product rows consist of products of entries, so every row of $\mathcal H$ has absolute row sum one <cite data-source="paper">absolute row sum one.</cite> By the triangle inequality $\mathcal H$ maps disk arrays (coefficients of modulus at most $1$) to disk arrays. Truncating real and imaginary parts toward zero cannot increase either absolute value, so $Q_p$ keeps the disk, and it changes each part by less than $2^{-p}$. Hence $\|Q_p(\mathcal Hz)-\mathcal Hz\|_\infty<\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$ <cite data-source="paper">truncation cannot increase the absolute value of either component</cite>.
