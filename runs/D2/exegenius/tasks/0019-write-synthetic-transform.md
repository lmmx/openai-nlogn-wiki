## This page

Concept: Synthetic polynomial transform
Gloss: A Fourier transform over the ring R_r using ω_t = y^{2r/t} as root of unity, so twiddle multiplications are signed shifts; its inverse pairs are exact up to a factor.
Heading: Synthetic transform $F_t^{\pm}$ over $\mathcal R_r$

The symbols in this page's heading denote (say so on the page):

- $F_t^{\pm}$ (synthetic transform): The forward (+) or opposite (−) sign transform of length t over the ring.
- $t$ (transform length): The number of ring entries along an axis; a power of two dividing 2r.
- $\mathcal R_r$ (ring): The polynomial ring modulo y^r+1.
- $r$ (record length): The number of complex coefficients in a record.

## This page in the argument

The step: The synthetic Fourier transform over $\mathcal R_r=\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ is a contraction with exact inverse pair up to a factor and is computed on a tape layout with simultaneous butterfly layers.
How: use roots of unity that are signed shifts, compute butterfly levels in simultaneous layers, and lay out axes so chunk swaps bring selected bits together
Why: A transform has about log n butterfly levels, so each level must cost less than a whole-array pass; roots y^(2r/t) make twiddles cheap signed shifts. (the sources say so: "Scanning an array of $\Theta(n)$ stored bits at each of" (paper))

Its sub-steps, in order:

- A layer applying $H_0(u,v)=((u+v)/2,(u-v)/2)$ to the same bit in $d$ address chunks of complex fixed-point polynomial records costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ with $\lambda'<1$. (on [Simultaneous butterfly layer](simultaneous-butterfly-layer.md))
- With $\omega_t=y^{2r/t}$, the transforms $F_t^\pm$ are contractions and $F_t^+F_t^-=t^{-1}I$.
- On a layout that places selected bits in consecutive chunks, the synthetic transform costs a sum of prefix-move, chunk-exchange and simultaneous-round terms.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies $n$-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: reduce integer multiplication to a Fourier convolution evaluated with two fast tape procedures, then bound error and cost (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^{1−κ})](main-theorem.md))

**S2.2**: With $\omega_t=y^{2r/t}$, the transforms $F_t^\pm$ are contractions and $F_t^+F_t^-=t^{-1}I$.
Source: "If $j$ is an integer, then" (paper)
> \begin{lemma}[Synthetic character cancellation] \label{lem:synthetic-characters} If $j$ is an integer, then \[ \sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\omega_t^{jk} =\begin{cases}t,&t\mid j,\\0,&t\nmid j.\end{cases} \qquad F_t^+F_t^-=t^{-1}I. \] \end{lemma} \begin{proof} Only the second case needs proof. Write $j=2^v j_0$ with $j_0$ odd and $0\leq v<\log_2t$. Replacing $k$ by $k+t/2^{v+1}$ modulo $t$ permutes the summands and multiplies their sum by $y^{rj_0}=-1$. Hence the sum is zero, since the additive group of $\mathcal R_r$ has no two-torsion. Expanding the composite transforms and applying this identity proves the final formula. \end{proof}

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "define $\omega_t=y^{2r/t}$" (paper)

  > For a power of two $t$ dividing $2r$, define $\omega_t=y^{2r/t}$. For $z\in\mathcal R_r^t$ and $0\leq j<t$, set \[ (F_t^-z)_j=\frac1t\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\omega_t^{-jk}z_k, \qquad (F_t^+z)_j=\frac1t\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\omega_t^{jk}z_k. \] Every power of $\omega_t$ is an isometry, and each transform entry is an average of $t$ such images. Thus both transforms are contractions. The ring has zero divisors, so we verify character cancellation directly instead of dividing by $\omega_t^j-1$.
