This identity computes its transforms by [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md), which replaces a transform of arbitrary length by transforms whose axis lengths are powers of two. It keeps the two coordinate permutations of that method inside the transform formula, so that they cancel across a convolution. It is the resampling step of [Integer multiplication below n log n](main-theorem.md).

The usual implementation of Gaussian resampling <cite data-source="paper">sorts twice on each coordinate.</cite> This identity keeps both permutations in the formula, so they need not be <cite data-source="paper">executed as separate full sorts.</cite>

The two sides have different shapes. <cite data-source="paper">The transform lengths needed for the integer product are pairwise coprime, whereas the synthetic</cite> transforms use powers of two. The identity pairs a transform of length $s$ with one of length $t$, so the permutations $P_s$ and $P_t$ reorder entries to let the two sides be matched.

The letters $u$ and $w$ denote complex vectors of length $q$, with indices taken [modulo](congruence-modulo.md) $q$. A linear map $\Psi$ is a contraction when $\|\Psi u\|\le\|u\|$ for every $u$, where $\|u\|$ is the largest modulus of an entry of $u$. $F_q$ is the normalized discrete Fourier transform, $(F_q u)_k=\frac{1}{q}\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}u_j\,e^{-2\pi i jk/q}$. $F_s$ and $F_t$ are $F_q$ at $q=s$ and $q=t$.

Each permutation is defined by [congruence](congruence-modulo.md) of positions: $(P_s u)_j=u_{tj}$ and $(P_t w)_k=w_{-sk}$. Both permutations are bijections, which depends on $s$ and $t$ being [coprime](coprime.md). An integer $t$ has an inverse modulo $s$ exactly when $\gcd(s,t)=1$, so $j\mapsto tj$ is one-to-one on positions. For example, with $s=3$ and $t=5$, $(P_3 u)=(u_0,u_2,u_1)$.

The identity is built from two [linear maps](linear-algebra-basics.md): $A$ is a linear contraction from length-$s$ vectors to length-$t$ vectors, and $B_0$ one from length-$t$ to length-$s$. The source's <cite data-source="paper">Lemma 19 (Resampling without executing the two permutations).</cite> states: <cite data-source="paper">There are linear contractions A : Cs → Ct and B0 : Ct → Cs satisfying</cite>
$$P_s F_s = 2^{2\alpha^2}\, B_0\, P_t F_t\, A.$$
The source derives this from its Gaussian resampling identity, which uses two Gaussian-weighted maps $S$ and $T$ from length-$s$ to length-$t$ vectors; these letters are the source's, not those bound elsewhere. The identity reads <cite data-source="paper">TPs Fs = Pt Ft S,</cite> and the proof uses it as follows. Let $\Gamma$ select the coordinates nearest $tj/s$, let $\Lambda$ be a diagonal scaling, and let $N=\Gamma T\Lambda$, a map from length-$s$ vectors to themselves. <cite data-source="paper">Since α2 < p and θ > p/α4 , we have</cite> $\alpha^2\theta>p/\alpha^2>1$, and this is why the lemma needs $\theta>p/\alpha^4$. <cite data-source="paper">Thus ∥N − I∥ < 1/2, so N is invertible by the Neumann series.</cite> Put $L=\Lambda N^{-1}\Gamma$, a left inverse of $T$. Multiplying the Gaussian identity by $L$ gives $P_sF_s=LP_tF_tS$. With $A=S/2$ and $B_0=2^{-(2\alpha^2-1)}L$, this is the identity above.

The original proof uses two label-and-sort operations, one for $P_t$ and one for $P_s^{-1}$, <cite data-source="paper">each costing O(tp log t).</cite> No sort is run.

Permutations pass through pointwise products. $\Pi$ is a coordinate permutation, which rearranges the entries of a vector, and $\cdot$ is the entrywise product. Then $(\Pi u)\cdot(\Pi w)=\Pi(u\cdot w)$, because <cite data-source="paper">a coordinate permutation commutes with pointwise</cite> products. The retained source permutation $R$ cancels across the two forward transforms, their pointwise product, and the [opposite-sign transform](opposite-transform.md) $F_s^+$, the sum with $+$ in the exponent. The source shows this with <cite data-source="paper">hence RFs+ R = Fs+</cite>.
