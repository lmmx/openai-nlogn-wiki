A cyclic coordinate is one axis of a multidimensional [cyclic convolution](cyclic-convolution.md), with its own cyclic length. The argument uses these axes twice: the radix-digit coefficients are placed on several prime axes by a [Chinese-remainder map](chinese-remainder-map.md) <cite data-source="paper">that preserves convolution</cite>, and a [phase twist](phase-twist.md) acts on the last axis.

Let $d$, a positive integer, be the number of cyclic coordinates of an array. Let $\nu$ name one coordinate, running from $1$ to $d$, and let $\Lambda_\nu$ be the cyclic length of coordinate $\nu$, the number of positions along that axis before indices wrap around. Index arithmetic in coordinate $\nu$ is taken modulo $\Lambda_\nu$, and the lengths need not be equal.

Take $d=2$, $\Lambda_1=3$ and $\Lambda_2=4$. The positions $(2,3)$ and $(1,1)$ add to $(0,0)$, since $2+1=3\equiv 0 \pmod 3$ and $3+1=4\equiv 0 \pmod 4$. Each coordinate wraps on its own, so in a cyclic convolution the product of the entries at those two positions lands at output $(0,0)$.

The last coordinate is $\nu=d$. The construction is the padded box of the paper's Section 9.2.1, in which some axes are shortened to $r/2$ while the others keep length $r$. Fewer than $d$ axes are shortened, so at least one axis has length $r$, and that axis is taken last: <cite data-source="paper">at least one axis has length r; choose it as the last axis.</cite> Thus $\Lambda_d=r$.

The phase twist turns this coordinate into a polynomial coordinate: <cite data-source="paper">A phase twist changes the last cyclic coordinate into a polynomial</cite> modulo $y^r+1$. Let $\kappa$ and $\kappa'$, integers from $0$ to $r-1$, be two positions on this axis. The twist multiplies position $\kappa$ by $e^{\pi i\kappa/r}$. If $\kappa+\kappa'\ge r$, the sum wraps. In the polynomial ring $y^{\kappa+\kappa'}=-y^{\kappa+\kappa'-r}$, since $y^r=-1$, so the wrap carries a sign $-1$. The twist factors multiply to $e^{\pi i(\kappa+\kappa')/r}=-e^{\pi i(\kappa+\kappa'-r)/r}$, which supplies the opposite sign. The signs cancel, so the twisted product lands at position $\kappa+\kappa'-r$ with no sign, as the cyclic wrap does: <cite data-source="paper">its negative wrap gives precisely the original cyclic wrap after the twist is removed.</cite>
