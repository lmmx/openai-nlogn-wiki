A *payload* is the record data stored at one address of an array, as distinct from the address that locates that record. An array here is a collection of records, each sitting at an address; the address says where the record is, and the payload is what it carries. The concept is needed because the paper's constructions move records between addresses, change addresses, or restore addresses, and their cost bounds must hold whatever data the records carry.

Take an array with four records at the two-bit addresses $00$, $01$, $10$ and $11$, carrying the payloads $5$, $0$, $3$ and $1$. Swapping the two address bits sends the record at $01$ to $10$ and the record at $10$ to $01$. After the swap, address $01$ holds payload $3$ and address $10$ holds payload $0$: the payloads moved with their records, and the addresses are the only thing that changed.

The paper's bounds are stated with the payload left arbitrary. The packed-bit addition step has a time bound that is worst-case <cite data-source="paper">over all payloads.</cite> A payload can also be very small: <cite data-source="paper">The general address-swap subroutine also covers one-bit payloads</cite>, so the swap cost must be bounded even when each record carries a single bit. The cost of a step therefore has to come from the addresses it touches, not from the size of the data it moves.
