This page fixes every size and exponent of the multiplication algorithm so that the hypotheses of its lemmas hold, for two inputs of $n$ bits each. The padded box is an array with $d$ axes; it has $T$ positions, and its axis $i$ has length $t_i$.

Of the fixed rational constants, $\epsilon$ and $c$ set the number of axes and the chunk width, $\lambda'$ is the exponent of $d$ in the cost of a butterfly layer, $\kappa$ is the saving exponent of the final running time, and $\tau,\sigma,\beta,\delta,\lambda,C_1$ appear in the lemmas' inequalities. Take $\tau=\sigma=1-2^{-50}$, $\beta=1/2$ and $\delta=1/16$. Take $\lambda=1-2^{-52}$ and $c=2^{-56}$. Take $\lambda'=1-2^{-54}$. Take $C_1=20$, $\epsilon=2^{-75}$ and $\kappa=2^{-182}$. All are deliberately conservative<cite data-source="paper">These constants are deliberately conservative; no optimization is claimed.</cite>, so inequalities among them are exact comparisons.

The layer conditions hold with room to spare: $\tau(1+c/\beta)<1-31\cdot2^{-55}<\lambda$, because $\tau(1+c/\beta)=(1-2^{-50})(1+2^{-55})=1-31\cdot2^{-55}-2^{-105}$ and $\lambda=1-8\cdot2^{-55}$. Also $\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)=1-2^{-51}<\lambda<\lambda'<1$.

## Sizes

Let $b$ be the bit length of $n$, $K$ the width in bits of the address chunks interchanged, $\ell$ the number of address bits of the longest axis, and $p$ the precision in bits, which sets the $2^{-p}$ grid of the [Fixed-point Gaussian dyadic records](fixed-point.md). For large $n$ put $b=\lceil\log_2n\rceil$, $p=6b$, $d=\lfloor b^\epsilon\rfloor$. Put $K=\lfloor d^c\rfloor$. Take the power of two $T$ in $[4n/b,8n/b)$, so $Tp=6bT\in[24n,48n)$.

Let $r=2^{\lceil(\log_2T)/d\rceil}$ and $\ell=\log_2r$; choose $d\ell-\log_2T\in[0,d)$ of the axis lengths to be $r/2$ and the others $r$, with the last axis of length $r$. This works because $d\ell\ge\log_2T$ and $d\ell<\log_2T+d$: $m=d\ell-\log_2T$ axes of length $r/2$ give product $2^{d\ell-m}=T$, and $m<d$ leaves a full last axis. Thus $t_i\in\{r/2,r\}$, $\prod_it_i=T$ and $t_d=r$.

The layer and transform lemmas bound $d$, $r$ and $\ell$ by comparison constants $a_d$, $a_r$, $b_d$, $b_r$, $C_\ell$. These choices give $Tp=\Theta(n)$, $d=\Theta(p^\epsilon)$, $K=\Theta(p^{\epsilon c})$, $\ell=\Theta(p^{1-\epsilon})$, $r=2^{\Theta(p^{1-\epsilon})}$, so the comparison constants $a_d=a_r=1/12$, $b_d=1$, $b_r=1/3$, $C_\ell=1$ can be fixed for all lengths at once: $b/2\le\log_2T\le b$ and $d\le b$ give $\tfrac1{12}p^\epsilon\le d\le p^\epsilon$ and $\tfrac p{12d}\le\ell\le\tfrac p{3d}$.

## Primes

This page fixes the width $\alpha$ of the Gaussian weights in [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md), their total scaling $\gamma$ over the $d$ axes, and the relative gap $\eta$ between each prime and its power of two. Put $\alpha=\lceil(12d^2b)^{1/4}\rceil$. Put $\gamma=2d\alpha^2$ and $\eta=1/(4d)$. Since $\lceil A\rceil\le2A$ for $A\ge1$ and $8\sqrt{12}<28$, $\alpha\le2(12d^2b)^{1/4}$. Then $\gamma<28d^2\sqrt b\le28b^{1/2+2\epsilon}\le28b^{2/3}$, so $2\le\alpha<\sqrt p$ eventually and $\gamma=o(b)$.

Let $x$ be a real number and $q$ an integer. By the prime-interval lemma, for $0<\eta<1/4$ and $x>e^{2/\eta}$ the interval $(1-2\eta)x<q\le(1-\eta)x$ contains at least $\eta x/(2\log x)$ primes; both $x=r/2$ and $x=r$ qualify since $\log x>8d=2/\eta$, and the count $x/(8d\log x)$ exceeds $d$. <cite data-source="paper">contains at least $\eta x/(2\log x)$ primes</cite> This is due to Harvey and van der Hoeven; the conditions hold because $\log x=\Theta(p^{1-\epsilon})$ exceeds $8d=\Theta(p^\epsilon)$ and $\log(8d^2\log x)=o(\log x)$.

This page fixes the prime axis lengths $s_i$ of the [Chinese-remainder address map](crt-map.md) near the $t_i$. The two intervals are disjoint and each holds more than $d$ primes, so we can choose distinct odd primes with $(1-2\eta)t_i<s_i\le(1-\eta)t_i$; $\gcd(s_i,t_i)=1$ as $t_i$ is a power of two. Put $S=\prod_is_i$, the length of the cyclic convolution. Multiplying the lower bounds and applying strict Bernoulli gives $S/T>(1-1/(2d))^d>1/2$ for $d\ge2$. With $s_i<t_i$ this gives $T/2<S<T$.

Let $\theta_i=t_i/s_i-1$ be the relative gap of axis $i$. With $\theta_i=t_i/s_i-1\ge\eta/(1-\eta)>\eta$ we get $\alpha^4\theta_i>12d^2b/(4d)=3db\ge6b=p$, the separation hypothesis of the resampling lemma; also $t_i\le r\le T<2^p$.

Trial division over the two intervals costs $O(dr^{3/2}\operatorname{poly}(p))$, with logarithm $O(\log p+p/d)=o(p)$. The deterministic search for the primes takes $n^{o(1)}=o(n)$ time, since its logarithm is $o(p)$ and $p=\Theta(\log n)$; all primes and length parameters are generated by the machine, not given as advice.
