An indicator vector is a 0-1 vector that is indexed by the elements of a [Ground set](ground-set.md) and marks which belong to a given subset. The [subset-intersection network](subset-intersection-network.md) assigns subspaces using indicator vectors of three-element subsets, which must be [orthogonal](bilinear-form.md).

Let the ground set be $[h]=\{1,\dots,h\}$. A *triple* is a three-element subset of $[h]$. Here $S$ and $T$ name triples; $T$ changes meaning from the ground-set page. <cite data-source="paper">For a triple T let tT be its indicator vector.</cite> The indicator vector $t_T$ has entry $1$ at each position $e \in T$, where $e$ is an element of $[h]$, and entry $0$ elsewhere. For example, with $[h]=\{1,\dots,6\}$ and $T=\{1,2,4\}$, $t_T=(1,1,0,1,0,0)$.

Call $S$ and $T$ *neighbors* when the construction links them. In the bit construction, <cite data-source="paper">S, T are neighbors when |S ∩ T | = 1</cite>. In the complex construction, <cite data-source="paper">neighbors are distinct triples with</cite> an even intersection.

An indicator vector is compared with others by the [bilinear form](bilinear-form.md) of each construction. Let $J$ be the $h\times h$ matrix of all ones. In the bit construction the form lives on $\mathbb{Q}^h$, the $h$-tuples of rational numbers: <cite data-source="paper">let F = Qh with bilinear form</cite> $\langle x,y\rangle = x^{\top}(I-J/9)y$. Since $t_S^{\top}t_T=|S\cap T|$ and $t_S^{\top}Jt_T=|S|\,|T|=9$, this gives <cite data-source="paper">⟨tS , tT ⟩ = |S ∩ T | − 1,</cite> which is $0$ for neighbors.

In the complex construction the form is the dot product on $\mathbb{F}_2^h$: <cite data-source="paper">take instead F = Fh2 with the ordinary dot product</cite>. Then $t_S\cdot t_T=|S\cap T| \bmod 2$, which is $0$ for neighbors because their intersection is even.

On each edge the network takes <cite data-source="paper">the orthogonal complement of the smaller label inside the larger one</cite>. For a nondegenerate subspace $U$ of a nondegenerate space $V$, $V=U\oplus U^{\perp}$, so $\dim U^{\perp}=\dim V-\dim U$. The source checks <cite data-source="paper">every triple line is nondegenerate</cite> and that <cite data-source="paper">neighboring triple lines are orthogonal</cite>. Orthogonality <cite data-source="paper">of the corresponding indicator vectors permits the subspace assignments.</cite>
