## This page in the argument

The step: The accumulated coefficient error before rounding is below $1/2$, so rounding recovers the exact integer coefficients, and radix-$2^b$ carry propagation yields the $2n$-bit product.
How: track error and disk margins through each approximate transform, scale by the known constants, then round and carry
Why: The transforms only approximate their exact counterparts; since the true coefficients are integers, an error strictly below $1/2$ identifies each uniquely, and the capacity bound $2^{3b}$ keeps the carries bounded. (the sources leave this for the reader to work out: spell it out)
Striking feature: The error bound $448b\,2^{-3b/4}$ is tiny only because the precision $p=6b$ exceeds the $3b$ bits of coefficient capacity plus the $\gamma+O(\log)$ bits lost to the scalings; the slack is what lets truncated disk-grid arithmetic give exact integers. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- The source transform has error below $E_sh_p$ with $E_s=9\cdot2^{\gamma}TL$ and stays in the disk.
- After the first scaling by $S$ the error is below $E_wh_p$ with $E_w=S(3E_s+2)<28\cdot2^{\gamma}T^2L$.
- After the second scaling the absolute error is below $448b\,2^{-b+\gamma}\le448b\,2^{-3b/4}<1/2$.
- The nearest integer to each recovered coefficient is therefore the unique correct coefficient.
- Carries propagate by $k_0=0$, $d_j=(c_j+k_j)\bmod Q_0$, $k_{j+1}=\lfloor(c_j+k_j)/Q_0\rfloor$ with $Q_0=2^b$ and $k_j<Q_0^2$.
- The product is below $2^{2n}$, so after removing padding bits exactly $2n$ output bits remain.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A single deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, disproving the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture.; how: reduce integer multiplication to convolution over a polynomial ring, and speed up the data movement and the butterfly layers of its transforms with two new tape procedures that each save a power of the logarithm (on [Integer multiplication below n log n](main-theorem.md))
- Two $n$-bit integers are multiplied exactly by forming radix-$2^b$ digits, mapping them to prime axes, taking two source transforms, multiplying pointwise, applying the opposite transform, and rescaling, rounding and carrying.; how: chain the reductions digit convolution, prime-axis cyclic convolution, power-of-two axes via resampling and chirps, and ring convolution after a coefficient twist (on [The multiplication algorithm](multiplication-algorithm.md))
