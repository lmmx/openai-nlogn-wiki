The simultaneous butterfly layer applies the two-point butterfly $H_0=\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$ coefficientwise to one selected bit in each of $D\le d$ address chunks of $K$ bits, at a common offset, on a stream of volume $V$, in time $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ for a fixed $\lambda'<1$, instead of $D$ whole-array passes <cite data-source="paper">factors would require $D$ whole-array passes.</cite>. Here $D$ is a chunk count (not a source group), $e$ counts axes (not digits), and $z$ is an array (not a suffix).

The [Two-point kernel C](phase-kernel.md) is $C=aI+bX$, and $\mathsf S=\operatorname{diag}(1,i)$. Since $ab=\tfrac12$, $ib^2=\tfrac12$ and $a^2+b^2=0$, multiplying out gives $H_0=b\,\mathsf SC\mathsf S$, $C^2=2abX=X$ and so $C^4=I$ <cite data-source="paper">H_0=b\mathsf SC\mathsf S,\qquad C^2=X,\qquad C^4=I,\qquad</cite>. Tensoring over the selected bits $x_1,\dots,x_D$ of an address $x$ gives
$$H_0^{\otimes D}=b^D\mathcal S_DC^{\otimes D}\mathcal S_D,\qquad(\mathcal S_Dz)(x)=i^{x_1+\dots+x_D}z(x).$$
The phases and $b^D=(-i/2)^{\lfloor D/2\rfloor}b^{D\bmod2}$ are exact Gaussian dyadic operations, so only $C^{\otimes D}$ remains.

Take $e=mf$ selected axes, as $m$ slots of $f$ axes: the selected bits of an address form $f$ columns of $m$ bits. The network of [Binary phase frames](phase-frames.md), with $W$ wires and $s$ smaller calls, computes $C^{\otimes e}$. Each edge changes the frame of every column by a product of kernels $C_v^{\pm1}$, $C_v=aI+bX_v$, one per vector $v\in\mathbb F_2^m$ of a residual basis. Let $\Gamma$ be an invertible $\mathbb F_2$-matrix with these $v$ as first columns, and $\mathcal P_\Gamma$ the address permutation moving the value at $x$ to $\Gamma x$. Then $\mathcal P_\Gamma X_v\mathcal P_\Gamma^{-1}=X_{\Gamma v}$ <cite data-source="paper">\mathcal P_MX_v\mathcal P_M^{-1}=X_{Mv}.</cite> so after $\mathcal P_\Gamma^{-1}$ the residual vectors are coordinate directions. Apply $\mathcal P_\Gamma^{-1}$ to every column, then $(C^{\pm1})^{\otimes f}$ to the $f$ bits of slot $\xi$ for each residual vector $\xi$, then $\mathcal P_\Gamma$: one child on $f=e/m$ axes per residual vector, exactly $s$ in all. An inverse needs no extra child, since $C^{-1}=-iZCZ$ with $Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ gives $(C^{-1})^{\otimes f}=(-i)^fZ^{\otimes f}C^{\otimes f}Z^{\otimes f}$ <cite data-source="paper">=(-i)^f Z^{\otimes f}C^{\otimes f}Z^{\otimes f}.</cite>

Elimination writes $\Gamma$ as at most $m(m-1)+3m$ row additions <cite data-source="paper">a product of at most $m(m-1)+3m$ elementary row additions</cite>. A row addition XORs selected bits of one slot into another, which [Packed selected-bit addition](packed-bit-addition.md) does by eight cyclic shifts of whole chunk ranges plus a repair of a bounded exceptional set of addresses. So the basis change and its inverse on $e$ axis chunks cost $O(V((eK)^\tau+1))$ <cite data-source="paper">O(V((eK)^\tau+1))</cite> for a fixed $\tau<1$.

Row index $Wg+w$ goes to wire role $w$, so each child has volume exactly $V/W$ if the row count stays divisible by $W$ through $\lceil\log_md\rceil$ levels. So the first $q_0=\lceil\log_2W\rceil\lceil\log_m(2d)\rceil$ chunks get individual $C$ kernels, in $O(V\log d)$, and their bits form a row index of range $2^{q_0K}\ge W^{\lceil\log_md\rceil}$. Zero-row padding at most doubles the volume and makes the row count at depth $j$ divisible by $W^{\lceil\log_md\rceil-j}$.

Let $F(e)$ be the time divided by the volume and $\beta=\tfrac12$. For $e<d^\beta$, individual kernels give $F(e)=O(e)$. Otherwise the $s$ children cost $s(V/W)F(e/m)$, so
$$F(e)\le\tfrac sW F(e/m)+O((eK)^\tau+1),$$
the recursion of [Recursive power saving](rank-recurrence.md). As $e\ge d^\beta$ and $K\le d^c$, $K\le e^{c/\beta}$, and the overhead is $O(e^\lambda)$ for $\max\{\tau,\sigma\}<\lambda<1$ and $\tau(1+c/\beta)<\lambda$. The saving is $s/W\le m^\sigma$, $\sigma<1$, against factor $m$ per level for individual kernels. Internal levels sum to at most $Ce^\lambda\sum_j(m^{\sigma-\lambda})^j=O(e^\lambda)$. Leaves after $j$ levels hold $u=e/m^j<d^\beta$ chunks, contributing $O((s/W)^ju)\le O(m^{j\sigma}u)=O(e^\sigma u^{1-\sigma})\le O(e^\sigma d^{\beta(1-\sigma)})$.

The other $D-q_0$ chunks split by the base-$m$ digits of $D-q_0$ into at most $(m-1)(1+\lfloor\log_md\rfloor)$ pieces of $m^j$ chunks. Summing gives $O(\log(2d)[d^\lambda+d^{\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)}+1])=O(d^{\lambda'})$ for fixed $\max\{\lambda,\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)\}<\lambda'<1$. The paper takes $\lambda'=1-2^{-54}$ <cite data-source="paper">\lambda'=1-2^{-54},</cite>, barely below $D$ passes.

Let $\chi(e)$ bound the operations on any chain from an input coefficient to an intermediate value. One invocation takes $24W^3+4s+4W+4<\psi=64(W+m+1)^3$ operations, as $s<Wm$. Chains concatenate the $s$ children, and a leaf pair takes eight operations:
$$\chi(e)\le s\chi(e/m)+\psi,\qquad\chi(e)\le8e\ (e<d^\beta).$$
With $\Psi=s+\psi$, $sx+\psi\le\Psi x$ for $x\ge1$, and there are at most $\lceil\log_md\rceil$ internal levels, so $\chi(e)\le8d\Psi^{\lceil\log_md\rceil+1}\le8\Psi^2d^{\vartheta+1}$, where $\vartheta=\min\{j\ge1:m^j\ge\Psi\}$ is an exponent, not a digit count. The pieces and individual kernels keep every chain below $\Delta=\lceil C_0d^{C_1}\rceil$, $C_0=128m\Psi^2$, $C_1=\max\{20,\vartheta+3\}$.

A value after $k$ operations lies in $2^{-(p+k)}\mathbb Z[i]$ with modulus at most $2^k$: addition at most doubles the modulus, halving adds a denominator bit, and signs and multiplication by $i$ change neither. So $p+\Delta$ fractional and $\Delta+3$ integer and sign bits per real component represent every intermediate coefficient exactly <cite data-source="paper">represents every intermediate coefficient exactly and prevents overflow</cite>. As $d=\Theta(p^\epsilon)$ and $\epsilon C_1<1$, $\Delta=o(p)$, so the width is $O(p)$.

Rows of $H_0$ have absolute sum $\tfrac12+\tfrac12=1$, and these multiply under tensor products, so every row of $\mathcal H=H_0^{\otimes D}$ has absolute row sum one <cite data-source="paper">absolute row sum one. Thus $\mathcal H$ maps disk arrays to disk</cite>. So $\mathcal H$ maps arrays with coefficients in the unit disk to such arrays. Truncation $Q_p$ toward zero cannot increase either component's absolute value, so the disk is kept, and it changes each component by less than $2^{-p}$. Hence $\|Q_p(\mathcal Hz)-\mathcal Hz\|_\infty<\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$ <cite data-source="paper">error less than $\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$</cite>.
