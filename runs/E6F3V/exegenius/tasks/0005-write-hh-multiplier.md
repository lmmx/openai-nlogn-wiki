## This page

Concept: Harvey–van der Hoeven O(n log n) multiplier
Gloss: The established fixed-tape algorithm multiplying N-bit integers in O(N log N) time, used here as an ordinary subroutine for short integer products.

## Where the argument relies on this page

- For arrays on $\prod_i\mathbb Z/t_i\mathbb Z$ with entries in $\mathcal R_r$, the normalised convolution $\mathcal M_r(f,g)=(f*g)/T$ is computed with error below $\sqrt2\,M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$ by two forward synthetic transforms, pointwise polynomial products by Kronecker packing, and the opposite transform on the retained order. (on [Convolution over the polynomial ring](ring-convolution.md))
- Two disk grid polynomials $f,g\in\mathcal R_r$ with $p$-bit components are multiplied exactly, returning $Q_p(fg/r)$, in $O(rp\log(rp))$ time on a fixed number of tapes, by evaluating at $B_0=2^{4p}$, multiplying four pairs of integers with the established multiplier, and unpacking the coefficients. (on [Signed Kronecker packing](kronecker-packing.md))
- For coprime $s<t$ with $t/s-1>p/\alpha^4$ there are contractions $A,B_0$ with $P_sF_s=2^{2\alpha^2}B_0P_tF_tA$, each computed with error below $p^2$ in $O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$ time; tensoring over $d$ axes gives $RF_{\boldsymbol s}=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A$ in $O(dTp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$. (on [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md))
- Digits of $x$ and $y$ are placed on prime cyclic axes by the Chinese-remainder map, transformed by Gaussian resampling around a chirp convolution over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$, multiplied pointwise, transformed with opposite sign, rescaled, rounded and carried to give the exact $2n$-bit product. (on [The multiplication algorithm](multiplication-algorithm.md))

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "Harvey and van der Hoeven reached the unconditional $O(n\log n)$ upper" (paper)

  > Sch\"onhage and Strassen proved in 1971 that two $n$-bit integers can be multiplied in $O(n\log n\log\log n)$ time on a multitape Turing machine, and proposed $n\log n$ as the optimal order of growth \cite[Section~1]{SchonhageStrassen1971}. After a sequence of improvements, Harvey and van der Hoeven reached the unconditional $O(n\log n)$ upper bound~\cite{HarveyHoeven2021}. We obtain a strict power saving in the logarithmic factor, in the same fixed finite-tape model. The algorithm uses linear combinations of intermediate data to reduce the cost of both address rearrangement and Fourier-transform layers.

- "Calls to the established $O(n\log n)$ multiplier are ordinary subroutine" (paper)

  > More specifically, the proof adapts Harvey and van der Hoeven's Gaussian-resampling identity, contraction bounds, and short-convolution procedures~\cite[Theorem~4.2, Proposition~4.7 and Lemmas~4.8--4.12]{HarveyHoeven2021}. Their framework already permits arbitrary dimension. Here the source and target permutations remain in the transform identity, while the faster tape procedures perform the required axis movements. Bluestein's conversion to convolution~\cite{Bluestein1970} and the synthetic-root convolution algorithm are retained; the latter follows the polynomial-transform construction in~\cite[Section~2.4 and Section~3]{HarveyHoeven2021}. Signed coefficient packing is a form of Kronecker substitution, with the fixed-tape version of~\cite[Lemma~2.5]{HarveyHoeven2021} as a direct antecedent. Calls to the established $O(n\log n)$ multiplier are ordinary subroutine calls; they do not invoke the improved theorem recursively.
