Matrix rank is the number of linearly independent rows of a matrix, over the rational numbers. The paper's matrix shears cost exactly this many interchanges, and that cost saves a power of the chunk width below $n \log n$.

Matrix rank is defined using the vector spaces and linear maps of [Linear algebra basics](linear-algebra-basics.md). Over the rational numbers, the rank of $A$ is the largest number of its rows that are linearly independent. Write $a$ for this count, so $a$ is the number of independent rows of $A$. Row reduction computes it, since each pivot is one independent row. For $A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix}$ the second row is twice the first, so $a = 1$.

Lemma 9 of the paper states that <cite data-source="paper">Every rational m × m matrix A of rank a has a</cite> factorization into two invertible lower triangular matrices with a partial permutation matrix between them. Write $E_1$ and $E_2$ for the invertible lower triangular factors, which have zeros above the diagonal. Write $\Pi$ for the partial permutation matrix, which has at most one 1 in each row and column and zeros elsewhere. Then $A = E_1 \Pi E_2$. <cite data-source="paper">Π has exactly a entries equal to</cite> one, so the number of ones is the rank. Invertible factors do not change rank, and the rows of $\Pi$ holding a one are distinct standard basis vectors. For the example, $E_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 4 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, $\Pi = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $E_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0.5 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ give $E_1 \Pi E_2 = A$, and $\Pi$ has one 1.

Let $H = (H_1, H_2, \ldots, H_m)$ and $D = (D_1, D_2, \ldots, D_m)$ be vectors of $m$ address fields, each field a block of digits, the chunk width. The matrix shear for $A$ sends $(H, D)$ to $(H + AD, D)$. An interchange swaps an $H$ field with a $D$ field. A one at row $s$ and column $\ell$ of $\Pi$ means the update $H_s \leftarrow H_s + D_\ell$, which takes three steps: $D_\ell \leftarrow D_\ell + H_s$, swap $H_s$ with $D_\ell$, then $D_\ell \leftarrow H_s - D_\ell$. On a pair $(\gamma, \delta)$ these give $(\gamma + \delta, \delta)$ with one interchange. The other steps are lower triangular changes of coordinates, which cost linear work. So the shear <cite data-source="paper">can be performed with exactly a interchanges of pairs of b-digit fields</cite>, and the rank is the cost.

The paper's network has $W$ wires. Each wire carries an [edge change](linear-network-frames.md), and the rank of its matrix gives the cost of that edge change. <cite data-source="paper">The sum of the rational ranks of all edge differences is</cite> the total cost of the network. <cite data-source="paper">Because the sum of these ranks is less than the number of wires</cite> times $m$, this total is below $W m$. The paper's recursion makes <cite data-source="paper">the number of recursive calls is the rational rank a</cite>, so the [Rank-saving recursion](rank-saving-recursion.md) page turns this count into the saving.
