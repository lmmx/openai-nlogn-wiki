## This page

Concept: Chinese-remainder map to prime axes
Gloss: An algebra isomorphism from cyclic convolution of length S = s_1⋯s_d (pairwise coprime) to convolution on the product of cyclic axes of lengths s_i, implemented by triangular controlled shifts.

## This page in the argument

The step: The assignment $x\mapsto\prod_ix_i^{\mu_i}$ with $\mu_i=P_i^{-1}\bmod s_i$ is an algebra isomorphism from $\mathbb C[x]/(x^S-1)$ to $\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_d]/(x_i^{s_i}-1)$, carrying cyclic convolution of length $S=s_1\cdots s_d$ to convolution on the product of cyclic axes, and its action on padded addresses is a sequence of triangular controlled shifts costing $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$.
How: Use the Chinese-remainder (Agarwal–Cooley) map with coordinate powers $\mu_i$ chosen so that the new coordinate of axis $i$ depends only on lower-index digits, making the change of order triangular, then implement it by controlled cyclic shifts and axis exchanges.
Why: The coordinate powers are chosen so that the change of order becomes a sequence of controlled cyclic shifts. (the sources say so: "The following map uses that
algebraic split, with coordinate powers that make the change of order a
sequence of controlled cyclic shifts." (paper))

Its sub-steps, in order:

- The map respects $x^S=1$, its image of $x$ has order $S$ because each $\mu_i$ is a unit and the $s_i$ are coprime, so it is an isomorphism preserving cyclic convolution.
- For $k=\sum_ja_jP_j$ the coordinate of $\Phi(x^k)$ in axis $i$ is $b_i=a_i+\mu_i\sum_{j<i}a_jP_j \pmod{s_i}$, depending only on lower-index digits.
- After reordering the axes to $(a_1,\dots,a_d)$, update the targets in the order $i=d,d-1,\dots,1$ so each offset is computed from still-unchanged lower-index digits.
- Each triangular update is a prefix-controlled cyclic shift: prepare the offset, split the valid target interval into two pieces and merge them in the opposite order.
- Reversing the $d$ axes uses $O(d^2)$ exchanges of unequal-width chunks, so $\Phi$, its inverse and the padding cost $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- One deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, refuting the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture in this model.; how: Reduce the product to a convolution of short digits evaluated by Fourier transforms, speed up the two costly ingredients (moving data between addresses, and butterfly arithmetic) by a power of a block size using one recursive saving, and total every cost term. (on [Sub-n-log-n integer multiplication](main-theorem.md))
- Digits of $x$ and $y$ are placed on prime cyclic axes by the Chinese-remainder map, transformed by Gaussian resampling around a chirp convolution over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$, multiplied pointwise, transformed with opposite sign, rescaled, rounded and carried to give the exact $2n$-bit product.; how: Follow the chain of reductions: radix-digit convolution, then cyclic convolution on distinct prime axes, then normalised convolution on power-of-two axes, then convolution over the polynomial ring, and account for every change of representation and its error. (on [The multiplication algorithm](multiplication-algorithm.md))

**S6.3.1**: The map respects $x^S=1$, its image of $x$ has order $S$ because each $\mu_i$ is a unit and the $s_i$ are coprime, so it is an isomorphism preserving cyclic convolution.
Source: "$\Phi$ is an isomorphism and preserves cyclic convolution." (paper)
> Let $s_1,\ldots,s_d$ be the pairwise coprime source lengths, let $S=\prod_i s_i$, and put \[ P_i=\prod_{j<i}s_j,\qquad \mu_i=P_i^{-1}\pmod{s_i}. \] Here $P_i$ denotes an integer prefix product, not a Fourier-coordinate permutation. The assignment \[ \Phi:\mathbb C[x]/(x^S-1)\longrightarrow \mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_d]/(x_1^{s_1}-1,\ldots,x_d^{s_d}-1), \qquad x\longmapsto\prod_i x_i^{\mu_i} \] respects $x^S=1$, so it defines an algebra map. The target monomial group has size $S$, and the image of $x$ has order $S$, because each $\mu_i$ is a unit and the $s_i$ are pairwise coprime. Its powers therefore run through all target basis monomials exactly once. Hence $\Phi$ is an isomorphism and preserves cyclic convolution.

**S6.3.2**: For $k=\sum_ja_jP_j$ the coordinate of $\Phi(x^k)$ in axis $i$ is $b_i=a_i+\mu_i\sum_{j<i}a_jP_j \pmod{s_i}$, depending only on lower-index digits.
Source: "The coordinate of $\Phi(x^k)$ in axis $i$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triangular-crt}
 b_i=a_i+\mu_i\sum_{j<i}a_jP_j\pmod{s_i}." (paper)
> Every ordinary coefficient index has a unique mixed-radix expression \[ k=\sum_{j=1}^d a_jP_j,\qquad 0\le a_j<s_j. \] The coordinate of $\Phi(x^k)$ in axis $i$ is \begin{equation}\label{eq:triangular-crt} b_i=a_i+\mu_i\sum_{j<i}a_jP_j\pmod{s_i}. \end{equation} Indeed terms with $j>i$ are divisible by $s_i$, and $\mu_iP_i=1$ modulo $s_i$. Numerical order of $k$ is lexicographic order of $(a_d,\ldots,a_1)$. Insert the padding in one nested scan of the full box $\prod_i[0,t_i)$ in this lexicographic order, with $a_d$ the outermost counter and $a_1$ the innermost. At an address satisfying $a_i<s_i$ for every $i$, copy the next original coefficient record; at every other address, write a zero record. The valid addresses occur in the original coefficient order, so each record is copied to its intended mixed-radix address. To delete the padding after the inverse axis reorder, use the same enumeration, copying the record at each valid address and skipping the record at each invalid address.

**S6.3.3**: After reordering the axes to $(a_1,\dots,a_d)$, update the targets in the order $i=d,d-1,\dots,1$ so each offset is computed from still-unchanged lower-index digits.
Source: "Update the targets in the
order $i=d,d-1,\ldots,1$ using \eqref{eq:triangular-crt}.  When target
$i$ is updated, none of its lower-index controls has yet changed, so
the offset is computed from the original digits $a_j$ required by the
formula." (paper)
> Now reorder the complete padded axes to $(a_1,\ldots,a_d)$. In this physical order, the coordinates with indices below $i$ precede coordinate $i$. Update the targets in the order $i=d,d-1,\ldots,1$ using \eqref{eq:triangular-crt}. When target $i$ is updated, none of its lower-index controls has yet changed, so the offset is computed from the original digits $a_j$ required by the formula.

**S6.3.4**: Each triangular update is a prefix-controlled cyclic shift: prepare the offset, split the valid target interval into two pieces and merge them in the opposite order.
Source: "Each triangular update is a prefix-controlled cyclic shift.  The
controls precede its target in the current physical order.  For each
prefix, prepare the offset, split the valid target interval into its
two consecutive pieces, and merge those pieces in the opposite order." (paper)
> Each triangular update is a prefix-controlled cyclic shift. The controls precede its target in the current physical order. For each prefix, prepare the offset, split the valid target interval into its two consecutive pieces, and merge those pieces in the opposite order. Every target position carries its full spectator suffix as a block; the invalid interval is copied unchanged. There are at most $T/t_i$ control prefixes for target $i$. For any fixed setup degree $C$, all offsets therefore cost $O(dTp^C/r)=o(Tp)$ to prepare, since $t_i\ge r/2$ and $r$ exceeds every fixed power of $p$.

**S6.3.5**: Reversing the $d$ axes uses $O(d^2)$ exchanges of unequal-width chunks, so $\Phi$, its inverse and the padding cost $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$.
Source: "Therefore the map $\Phi$, its inverse, and
their padding operations cost
\[
 O\bigl(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau)\bigr)." (paper)
> The axis widths are $\ell$ or $\ell-1$. To exchange two adjacent axes of unequal width, move the longer axis's leading bit outside their equal-width pair, swap that pair by the proved chunk-swap procedure, and put the leading bit at its new position. This adds a fixed number of linear passes to the equal-width swap cost. Reversing all axes uses $O(d^2)$ such moves. Therefore the map $\Phi$, its inverse, and their padding operations cost \[ O\bigl(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau)\bigr). \] This construction describes payload movement from address $a$ to its new address. It is distinct from the operator convention $(R_cz)_j=z_{cj}$ used for Fourier dilations, which moves a payload from address $j$ to $c^{-1}j$.
