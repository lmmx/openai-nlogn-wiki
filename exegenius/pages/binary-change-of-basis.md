A binary change of basis is an invertible linear change of coordinates on the selected address bits, built over the [Binary field](binary-field.md) $\mathbb F_2$, where addition is exclusive or. It is the step that puts the directions of a [Rank-saving recursion](rank-saving-recursion.md) into coordinate positions, so that each direction becomes a single-bit change.<cite data-source="paper">The next step puts those directions into coordinate</cite>

Let $m$ be the number of selected bits and $\varsigma=(\varsigma_1,\dots,\varsigma_m)$ the column of their values, each $\varsigma_i\in\mathbb F_2$. A binary change of basis is specified by an invertible $m\times m$ matrix $M$ over $\mathbb F_2$, as defined in [Linear algebra basics](linear-algebra-basics.md). The inverse of $M$ is $M^{-1}$. The change acts on an [Address field](address-field.md), sending $\varsigma$ to $M\varsigma$.

A residual basis of directions is a basis $\mathbf w_1,\dots,\mathbf w_\varrho$ of an edge's residual, a subspace of $\mathbb F_2^m$, where $\varrho$ is the number of its vectors.<cite data-source="paper">For an edge e, write Be for its residual basis</cite> Let $\zeta_j$ be the vector with 1 in position $j$ and 0 elsewhere. Extend the $\mathbf w_j$ to a basis of $\mathbb F_2^m$ and take these vectors as the first $\varrho$ columns of $M$, so that $M\zeta_j=\mathbf w_j$ for $j\le\varrho$.<cite data-source="paper">take these vectors as the first</cite>

Let $P_M$ be the address permutation that moves the value at each address $\alpha$ to $M\alpha$, so $(P_Mf)(\alpha)=f(M^{-1}\alpha)$ for a function $f$ from addresses to values. Write $(X_{\mathbf u}f)(\alpha)=f(\alpha+\mathbf u)$ for the shift by $\mathbf u$. Direct substitution gives $P_MX_{\mathbf u}P_M^{-1}=X_{M\mathbf u}$. Taking $\mathbf u=\zeta_j$ gives $X_{\mathbf w_j}=P_MX_{\zeta_j}P_M^{-1}$: the shift by $\mathbf w_j$ is a single-bit flip wrapped in two address permutations.

Over $\mathbb F_2$ the only nonzero scalar is 1, so elimination needs no scaling. A row addition adds one row of a matrix to another, and it is its own inverse, since adding a row twice gives 0.<cite data-source="paper">Gaussian elimination expresses any M ∈ GL(m, F2 ) as a product of at most m(m − 1) + 3m</cite> row additions: at most $m-1$ clearing additions per pivot, and at most one swap per pivot, where a swap of two rows is three additions. Reversing them gives $M$ as their product.

For $m=2$, take $M=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$. Then $M\varsigma=(\varsigma_1\oplus\varsigma_2,\ \varsigma_2)$, which is one row addition, and $M^{-1}=M$ because $M^2=I$.
