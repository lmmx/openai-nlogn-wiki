Convolution over the polynomial ring is computed along the [remaining axes](transform-axis.md) after the earlier reductions. It is the normalized product of two arrays of records: it transforms both arrays, multiplies the transforms entry by entry, and transforms the product back, which reduces the exact product in [Integer multiplication below n log n](main-theorem.md) to integer products.

Convolution over the polynomial ring takes its products in the [polynomial ring modulo $y^r + 1$](polynomial-ring-mod-yr-plus-1.md), $R_r$, where $y^r = -1$, so a power $y^j$ with $j \ge r$ wraps around with a sign flip. Let $\phi$ and $\psi$ be the two input records, each a polynomial in $y$ of degree less than $r$ with complex coefficients. Their normalized product is $\phi\psi/r$: the ordinary product, reduced in $R_r$ and divided by $r$.

Convolution over the polynomial ring computes its transforms by [synthetic polynomial transforms](synthetic-transforms.md), whose roots of unity are powers of $y$. Let $\Theta$ be the forward transform along the remaining axes, $\Theta^{+}$ its opposite, and $\nu$ the number of record positions in the array, the product of the remaining axis lengths. The one-axis identity <cite data-source="paper">Synthetic character cancellation</cite> gives $\Theta^{+}\Theta = I/\nu$ over all axes.

Write $\ast$ for cyclic convolution over the remaining axes, and $\phi\star\psi=\phi\ast\psi/(r\nu)$ for the normalized convolution. <cite data-source="paper">expansion of the finite sums gives</cite> $\Theta(\phi\ast\psi)=\nu\,(\Theta\phi)(\Theta\psi)$, with the product taken in $R_r$. Here $\odot$ is the normalized entrywise product, so $\Theta\phi\odot\Theta\psi$ means $(\Theta\phi)(\Theta\psi)/r$ taken entry by entry. Then $\Theta(\phi\star\psi)=\Theta\phi\odot\Theta\psi$. Applying $\Theta^{+}$ and using $\Theta^{+}\Theta=I/\nu$ gives $\phi\star\psi=\nu\,\Theta^{+}(\Theta\phi\odot\Theta\psi)$. The division by $r$ makes this exact, with no extra factor.

The two forward transforms list their entries in the same frequency order, so equal positions in the two lists hold the same frequency. <cite data-source="paper">the forward entry with frequency address j is stored at L(Bj)</cite>, and <cite data-source="paper">forward synthetic transforms retain the same frequency order through their pointwise product</cite>; the opposite transform consumes that order. <cite data-source="paper">Pointwise multiplication commutes with applying the same record permutation</cite> to both operands, so the product is formed on the stored records, in that order.

Convolution over the polynomial ring computes its record products by [signed coefficient packing](signed-packing.md), which turns each polynomial product into an integer product. Let $p$ be the number of fractional bits of each coefficient, so each real and imaginary coefficient part of a record is a multiple of $2^{-p}$. The inputs have <cite data-source="paper">disk-valued coefficients in 2−p Z[i]</cite>: each coefficient lies in the unit disk, so its real and imaginary parts lie in $[-1,1]$, and multiplying by $2^p$ gives integers of absolute value at most $2^p$. Hence $\phi = 2^{-p}(\beta+i\gamma)$ and $\psi = 2^{-p}(\delta+i\lambda)$, where $\beta,\gamma,\delta,\lambda$ are integer polynomials of degree less than $r$ with coefficients of absolute value at most $2^p$.

Let $h=\beta\delta$ be the ordinary integer product, and let $B=2^{4p}$ be the packing base (<cite data-source="paper">B0 = 24p and, for u</cite>). Evaluation at $B$ turns the product into one integer product, $\beta(B)\,\delta(B)=h(B)$. Its base-$B$ blocks are the coefficients of $h$ if each coefficient is below $B/2$ in absolute value. Each $h_j$ is a sum of at most $r$ products of two integers bounded by $2^p$, so $|h_j|\le r\,2^{2p}$. Since $r<2^p$ (<cite data-source="paper">Let r = 2ℓ < 2p with integers</cite>), $|h_j|<2^{3p}<2^{4p-1}=B/2$. <cite data-source="paper">Every coefficient in any of these ordinary</cite> polynomial product satisfies this bound. Convolution over the polynomial ring reads each coefficient as the [centered residue](centered-residue.md) of its block modulo $B$. Convolution over the polynomial ring extracts its blocks by a [carry scan](carry-propagation.md).

Signs are restored after multiplying magnitudes: <cite data-source="paper">Multiply the four pairs of absolute values using the established multiplier</cite>, the [established O(n log n) multiplier](established-multiplier.md) run as an ordinary subroutine. <cite data-source="paper">The reduction is a signed form of Kronecker substitution</cite> (evaluation at a large power of two).

Write $\sigma=\beta\delta-\gamma\lambda$ and $\chi=\beta\lambda+\gamma\delta$ for the real and imaginary numerators, which are the real and imaginary parts of $(\beta+i\gamma)(\delta+i\lambda)$. <cite data-source="paper">The real and imaginary numerator streams are respectively</cite> these two sums. Four integer products suffice: $\beta\delta$, $\gamma\lambda$, $\beta\lambda$ and $\gamma\delta$.

The numerators have degree at most $2r-2$ and must be reduced modulo $y^r+1$. Since $y^r=-1$, the coefficient at $j+r$ wraps to position $j$ with a minus sign, so <cite data-source="paper">subtract the coefficient at j + r from the one at j</cite> for $0 \le j < r$, treating coefficients of degree above $2r-2$ as zero. Write $\sigma_j$ for the coefficient of $y^j$ in $\sigma$, and $w_j$ for the reduced coefficient of $y^j$. Then $w_j=\sigma_j-\sigma_{j+r}$, with $\sigma_{j+r}=0$ when $j+r>2r-2$.

Worked example, with $r=2$, $\beta=1+2y$, $\delta=3+y$, and the toy base $B=100$ (not a power of two). Then $\beta(100)=201$, $\delta(100)=103$, and $201\cdot103=20703$. Its base-100 blocks, from the least significant end, are $03$, $07$, $02$, so $\beta\delta=3+7y+2y^2$ with every coefficient below $B/2=50$. The reduction gives $w_0=3-2=1$ and $w_1=7$, so $1+7y$, which agrees with $(1+2y)(3+y)$ at $y^2=-1$.
