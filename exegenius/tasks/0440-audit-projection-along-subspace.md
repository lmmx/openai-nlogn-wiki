````markdown
# Projection along a subspace

A projection along a subspace is a linear map that keeps the component of a vector lying in one subspace and removes its component in a second subspace. The paper assigns such a map to each gate and sink with a label $U$, and for nested labels the rank of the difference of two such maps is the dimension change between the labels.

Let $X$ be a vector space over the field $F$. Let $U$ be a subspace of $X$, and let $C$ be a subspace of $X$ complementary to $U$, meaning $U \cap C = \{0\}$ and $U + C = X$. Every vector $x$ of $X$ then splits in exactly one way as $x = x_U + x_C$ with $x_U \in U$ and $x_C \in C$, where $x_U$ is the component of $x$ in $U$ and $x_C$ is its component in $C$. The projection onto $U$ along $C$ is the map $\Theta$ sending $x$ to $x_U$.

A projection along a subspace is built from [Linear algebra basics](linear-algebra-basics.md) alone: it uses vector spaces, subspaces and the unique splitting of a vector into two parts. The map $\Theta$ sends each vector of $U$ to itself and each vector of $C$ to $0$, so applying it twice changes nothing: $\Theta(\Theta(x)) = \Theta(x)$. The map $I - \Theta$ sends $x$ to $x - x_U = x_C$, so it is the projection onto $C$ along $U$.

Let $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ be a bilinear form on $X$. A subspace $U$ is nondegenerate when no nonzero vector of $U$ is orthogonal to every vector of $U$. For such $U$, the intersection $U \cap U^\perp$ is $\{0\}$, and when the form on all of $X$ is nondegenerate, $\dim U^\perp = \dim X - \dim U$. So $U^\perp$ is complementary to $U$, and the projection onto $U$ along $U^\perp$ is defined. Write $\Theta_U$ for the projection onto $U$ along $U^\perp$; the paper calls this map $P_U$.

Take $X = \mathbb{Q}^2$, $U = \langle (1,0) \rangle$ and $C = \langle (1,1) \rangle$. Then $U \cap C = \{0\}$, and every $(a,b)$ equals $(a-b)(1,0) + b(1,1)$, so $\Theta(a,b) = (a-b, 0)$. For instance $\Theta(3,5) = (-2,0)$, and $(3,5) - (-2,0) = (5,5)$ lies in $C$, as the splitting requires. Also $\Theta(1,1) = (0,0)$ because $(1,1)$ lies in $C$.

The paper uses this definition for its labels. It then assigns these maps to the vertices:

For nested labels $U \subset V$, with $\Theta_V$ and $\Theta_U$ the projections defined above, the paper states that A map that is the identity on a subspace and zero on a complementary subspace has rank equal to the dimension of that subspace. So the rank of $\Theta_V - \Theta_U$ is the dimension of $V \cap U^\perp$, and the paper's stated value is that dimension.

The endpoint identity uses the fact that $I - \Theta$ is the projection onto the complement:. The labels that these maps are attached to are the subspaces studied on [Subspace labels](subspace-labels.md).
````
