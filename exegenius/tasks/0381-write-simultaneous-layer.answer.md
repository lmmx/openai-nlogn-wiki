The simultaneous butterfly layer applies the two-point step $H_0(u,v)=((u+v)/2,(u-v)/2)$ to one bit of each of $D\le d$ selected address chunks of $K$ bits, coefficientwise on records of $r$ complex $p$-bit coefficients, in time $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ for a fixed $\lambda'<1$, where $V$ is the bit volume of the array. It is the second power-saving tape procedure, next to the [Fast address-chunk interchange](address-interchange.md).

A tape machine performs one butterfly factor per whole-array pass, so $D$ separate factors would cost $D$ whole-array passes <cite data-source="paper">factors would require $D$ whole-array passes.</cite> Every round of the synthetic transform has this shape, so pass-by-pass evaluation pays a factor $d$ per round. The layer rewrites $H_0^{\otimes D}$ through a simpler kernel that a fixed network computes recursively on $1/W$ of the data per call, so that $e$ axes cost about $e^{\sigma}$ per unit volume with $\sigma<1$, not $e$.

Let $\mathsf S=\operatorname{diag}(1,i)$. The [Two-point kernel C](phase-kernel.md) $C=aI+bX$, with $X$ the bit flip, satisfies $H_0=b\,\mathsf SC\mathsf S$, $C^2=X$ and $C^4=I$ <cite data-source="paper">H_0=b\mathsf SC\mathsf S,\qquad C^2=X,\qquad C^4=I,\qquad</cite>. Write $x_1,\dots,x_D$ for the selected bits of an address $x$, and $H_0^{\otimes D}$ for $H_0$ applied to each of them. Applying the identity on every selected bit gives $H_0^{\otimes D}=b^D\mathcal S_DC^{\otimes D}\mathcal S_D$, where the diagonal operator $\mathcal S_D$ acts on an array $z$ by $(\mathcal S_Dz)(x)=i^{x_1+\dots+x_D}z(x)$. The scalar $b^D=(-i/2)^{\lfloor D/2\rfloor}b^{D\bmod2}$ is a binary shift, a fourth-root phase and at most one factor $b$, so all of this is exact Gaussian dyadic arithmetic and only $C^{\otimes D}$ remains.

Take a group of $e=mf$ selected axes ($e$ counts axes here, not chunk digits as on the interchange page), split into $m$ slots of $f$ axes; the $j$th bits of the slots form a column in $\mathbb F_2^m$. The group is handled by the network of [Binary phase frames](phase-frames.md), with $W$ wires and $s$ smaller calls. Along an edge the frame of every column changes by a product, over residual basis vectors $v_\xi$, of kernels $C_{v_\xi}^{\pm1}$, where $C_v=aI+bX_v$ and $X_v$ adds $v$ to a column modulo 2. Take an invertible $\mathbb F_2$-matrix $\Gamma$ whose first columns are the $v_\xi$, and the address permutation $\mathcal P_\Gamma:x\mapsto\Gamma x$. Then $\mathcal P_\Gamma X_v\mathcal P_\Gamma^{-1}=X_{\Gamma v}$ <cite data-source="paper">\mathcal P_MX_v\mathcal P_M^{-1}=X_{Mv}.</cite>. So the edge change is $\mathcal P_\Gamma(\prod_\xi C_{\mathbf 1_\xi}^{\pm1})\mathcal P_\Gamma^{-1}$, where $\mathbf 1_\xi$ is the $\xi$th standard basis vector and $C_{\mathbf 1_\xi}$ acts on slot $\xi$ alone. Apply $\mathcal P_\Gamma^{-1}$ to every column, then $(C^{\pm1})^{\otimes f}$ to the $f$ bits of slot $\xi$, then $\mathcal P_\Gamma$: one child on $f=e/m$ axes per residual vector, exactly $s$ children in all. A negative power costs no extra child, since $(C^{-1})^{\otimes f}=(-i)^fZ^{\otimes f}C^{\otimes f}Z^{\otimes f}$ with $Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$.

Gaussian elimination writes $\Gamma$ as at most $m(m-1)+3m$ row additions (a swap is three), each XORing selected bits of one slot into another. The layer performs each row addition by [Packed selected-bit addition](packed-bit-addition.md), which XORs selected bits of two chunks, with a third chunk as restored scratch, by eight cyclic shifts of whole chunk ranges plus a repair of an explicitly bounded set of exceptional addresses. The number of row additions is fixed, so the complete binary change of basis and its inverse on $e$ axis chunks cost $O(V((eK)^\tau+1))$ <cite data-source="paper">O(V((eK)^\tau+1))</cite> for a fixed $\tau<1$.

Let $F(e)$ be the time for a group divided by the volume of its stream. The $s$ children run on streams of volume $V/W$ (rows are dealt to the $W$ wire roles by row index modulo $W$), so with $\beta=\tfrac12$ and individual kernels, at cost $O(Ve)$, below $d^\beta$ axes,
$$F(e)\le \tfrac sW F(e/m)+O((eK)^\tau+1)\ (e\ge d^\beta),\qquad F(e)=O(e)\ (e<d^\beta).$$
The cost of the layer follows the recursion of [Recursive power saving](rank-recurrence.md): since $s<Wm$, $s/W\le m^\sigma$ for a fixed $\sigma<1$, so each factor $m$ in $e$ multiplies the cost per unit volume by at most $m^\sigma$ instead of $m$. To keep the row count divisible by $W$ through $\lceil\log_md\rceil$ levels, the first $q_0=\lceil\log_2W\rceil\lceil\log_m(2d)\rceil$ chunks get individual $C$ kernels, in $O(V\log d)$, and serve as a row index, padded with zero rows to a multiple of $W^{\lceil\log_md\rceil}$; this at most doubles the volume.

At an internal node $e\ge d^\beta$, so $K\le d^c\le e^{c/\beta}$ and the overhead is $O(e^{\tau(1+c/\beta)}+1)=O(e^\lambda)$ for a fixed $\lambda<1$ above $\sigma$ and $\tau$. As $s/W<m^\lambda$, the internal costs form a geometric series summing to $O(e^\lambda)$. A leaf reached after $j$ levels holds $e/m^j<d^\beta$ chunks and has accumulated the factor $(s/W)^j\le m^{j\sigma}$, so leaves add at most $O\big(e^\sigma(e/m^j)^{1-\sigma}\big)\le O(e^\sigma d^{\beta(1-\sigma)})$. Writing $D-q_0$ in base $m$ cuts the remaining chunks into at most $(m-1)(1+\lfloor\log_md\rfloor)$ pieces of $m^j$ chunks, and adding the pieces gives $O(\log(2d)[d^\lambda+d^{\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)}+1])=O(d^{\lambda'})$ for any fixed $\lambda'<1$ above $\lambda$ and $\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)$. The paper takes $\sigma=1-2^{-50}$ and $\lambda'=1-2^{-54}$ <cite data-source="paper">\lambda'=1-2^{-54},</cite>, only barely below the $D\le d$ passes, because $s/W$ is only barely below $m$.

The layer is evaluated in [Fixed-point Gaussian dyadic records](fixed-point.md). Let $\chi(e)$ bound the operations on a chain from an input coefficient to an intermediate one. An invocation takes $24W^3+4s+4W+4$ operations for its gates and corrections, below $E=64(W+m+1)^3$ since $s<Wm$ ($E$ is an operation bound here). A chain passes through the $s$ children in turn, so the recurrence uses $s$, not $s/W$; a leaf pair takes eight operations:
$$\chi(e)\le s\chi(e/m)+E,\qquad \chi(e)\le8e\ (\text{leaf}).$$
With $\Psi=s+E$, $s\varphi+E\le\Psi\varphi$ for $\varphi\ge1$, so each of at most $\lceil\log_md\rceil$ levels multiplies the bound by at most $\Psi$, and $\chi(e)\le8d\Psi^{\lceil\log_md\rceil+1}$. With $\vartheta=\min\{j\ge1:m^j\ge\Psi\}$, $\Psi^{\lceil\log_md\rceil+1}\le\Psi^2d^{\log_m\Psi}\le\Psi^2d^\vartheta$. Adding the at most $md$ pieces, individual kernels, diagonals and $b^D$ keeps the total below $128m\Psi^2d^{\vartheta+3}\le\Delta=\lceil C_0d^{C_1}\rceil$, where $C_0=128m\Psi^2$ and $C_1=\max\{20,\vartheta+3\}$.

A value after $\ell$ operations lies in $2^{-(p+\ell)}\mathbb Z[i]$ with modulus at most $2^\ell$ (addition at most doubles the modulus, halving adds a denominator bit). So reserving $p+\Delta$ fractional bits and $\Delta+3$ integer and sign bits per real component represents every intermediate coefficient exactly <cite data-source="paper">represents every intermediate coefficient exactly and prevents overflow</cite>. As $d=\Theta(p^\epsilon)$ and $\epsilon C_1<1$, $\Delta=o(p)$, so the width is $O(p)$.

Let $\mathcal H=H_0^{\otimes D}$. A row of $\mathcal H$ consists of all products of $D$ entries of $H_0$, one per selected bit, so its absolute row sum is $(\tfrac12+\tfrac12)^D=1$. By the triangle inequality $\mathcal H$ then maps disk arrays (entries of modulus at most 1) to disk arrays <cite data-source="paper">absolute row sum one. Thus $\mathcal H$ maps disk arrays to disk</cite>. Truncation $Q_p$ toward zero cannot increase the absolute value of either component of an entry, so it keeps the disk, and it changes each component by less than $2^{-p}$ <cite data-source="paper">truncation cannot increase the absolute value of either component</cite>. Hence $\|Q_p(\mathcal Hz)-\mathcal Hz\|_\infty<\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$.
