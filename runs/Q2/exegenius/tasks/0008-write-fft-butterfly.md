## This page

Concept: Normalized butterfly H₀
Gloss: The two-point map H₀(u, v) = ((u+v)/2, (u−v)/2), the basic arithmetic step of a radix-two fast Fourier transform.
Also called: butterfly, two-point butterfly
Heading: Normalized butterfly $H_0$

The symbols in this page's heading denote (say so on the page):

- $H_0$ (normalized butterfly): The two-point linear map sending (u, v) to ((u+v)/2, (u−v)/2), i.e. half the 2×2 Hadamard matrix.

## Where the argument relies on this page

- A layer of normalized butterflies $H_0$ on the same bit position of $D\le d$ address chunks of an array of polynomial records can be applied, exactly and then truncated once, in $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ time for a fixed $\lambda'<1$. (on [Simultaneous butterfly layer](simultaneous-butterfly-layer.md))
- The kernel $C=H\mathsf SH=aI+bX$ with $a=(1+i)/2$, $b=(1-i)/2$ satisfies $H_0=b\mathsf SC\mathsf S$, $C^4=I$ and $C^{-1}=-iZCZ$, all with Gaussian dyadic coefficients. (on [Phase kernel C](phase-kernel.md))
- The decimation-in-frequency recursion with synthetic roots $\omega_t=y^{2r/t}$ computes $B_tF_t^-$ (frequency $j$ stored at slot $\operatorname{rev}(j)$), and the reversed recursion computes $F_t^+B_t^{-1}$. (on [Synthetic polynomial transform](synthetic-transform.md))

## Pages that build on this one

- Phase kernel C: The 2×2 matrix C = H·diag(1, i)·H = aI + bX with a = (1+i)/2, b = (1−i)/2, from which the butterfly is recovered as H₀ = b·S·C·S with S = diag(1, i).
- Simultaneous butterfly layer: A procedure applying H₀ on the same bit position of up to d address chunks of an array of polynomial records at once, in O(V d^{λ′}) time for a fixed λ′ < 1, evaluated exactly and truncated once.
- Decimation-in-frequency FFT and bit-reversed order: The radix-two recursion that pairs z_k with z_{k+t/2} by a butterfly, multiplies one branch by a twiddle root, and recurses, leaving frequency j stored at the bit-reversed slot rev(j).

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "the transform calculation consists of address rearrangements, signed shifts, and two-point butterfly operations." (paper)

  > In this representation, the transform calculation consists of address rearrangements, signed shifts, and two-point butterfly operations. Scanning an array of $\Theta(n)$ stored bits at each of $\Theta(\log n)$ transform levels already costs $\Theta(n\log n)$. We therefore need savings in both movement and arithmetic. Two tape procedures supply them; in both bounds, $V$ denotes the stored bit volume.

- "The two outputs of $H_0$ occupy these same slots" (paper)

  > Write \[ H_0=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}. \] The length-one procedure is the identity. For $t\geq2$, the decimation-in-frequency step pairs $z_k$ with $z_{k+t/2}$ for $0\leq k<t/2$. The two outputs of $H_0$ occupy these same slots, whose high address bit is $b=0$ or $b=1$, respectively. Multiply the $b=1$ output by $\omega_t^{-k}$, and then apply the length-$t/2$ procedure separately within the two branches. This computes $F_t^-$ because, for $b\in\{0,1\}$, \begin{equation} (F_t^-z)_{2h+b} =\frac1{t/2}\sum_{k=0}^{t/2-1} (\omega_t^2)^{-hk}\omega_t^{-bk} \frac{z_k+(-1)^b z_{k+t/2}}2. \label{eq:synthetic-dif} \end{equation} To follow the recursion, name the original address-bit slots of an axis of length $t=2^a$ by $a-1,\ldots,0$, from most to least significant. For $0\leq s\leq a$, after $s$ levels the values of the first $s$ named slots are branch bits $b_0,\ldots,b_{s-1}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:synthetic-dif} identifies these
