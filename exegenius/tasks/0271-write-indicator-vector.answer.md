An indicator vector is the 0-1 vector that marks which elements of a [Ground set](ground-set.md) belong to a given subset. The [subset-intersection network](subset-intersection-network.md) assigns its subspaces using the indicator vectors of three-element subsets, and the orthogonality of those vectors is what permits the assignments.

The ground set is $[h]=\{1,\dots,h\}$ with $h=100$. Here $S$ and $T$ denote three-element subsets of $[h]$, called triples; on earlier pages these letters denote other objects. For a triple $T$, <cite data-source="paper">For a triple T let tT be its indicator vector.</cite> Its indicator vector $t_T$ has an entry $1$ in each position $e \in T$ and $0$ in every other position. For example, in the ground set $\{1,\dots,6\}$ with $T=\{1,2,4\}$, $t_T=(1,1,0,1,0,0)$.

Call $S$ and $T$ *neighbors* when the construction links them. In the bit construction, <cite data-source="paper">S, T are neighbors when |S ∩ T | = 1</cite>. In the complex construction, <cite data-source="paper">neighbors are distinct triples with</cite> an even intersection $|S\cap T|$.

The two constructions use different label fields for these vectors. Here $\mathbb{Q}$ denotes the rational numbers, not the ring $Q$ of an earlier page. Let $J$ be the $h\times h$ matrix of all ones. The bit construction uses $\mathbb{Q}^h$, with <cite data-source="paper">For the bit network, let F = Qh with bilinear form</cite> $\langle x,y\rangle = x^{\top}(I-J/9)y$. Since $t_S^{\top}Jt_T=|S|\,|T|=9$, we get $\langle t_S,t_T\rangle=|S\cap T|-1$, which is $0$ for neighbors. The complex construction uses the binary field $\mathbb{F}_2$, the field with two elements $0$ and $1$ with $1+1=0$, so its label field is $\mathbb{F}_2^h$: <cite data-source="paper">take instead F = Fh2 with the ordinary dot product</cite>. Then $t_S\cdot t_T=|S\cap T| \bmod 2$, which is $0$ for neighbors. For $T=\{1,2,3\}$ and $S=\{4,5,6\}$, $t_S\cdot t_T=0$, while $t_T\cdot t_T=3 \bmod 2=1$.

So neighboring triples have orthogonal indicator vectors in both constructions. The paper states why this matters: Orthogonality <cite data-source="paper">of the corresponding indicator vectors permits the subspace assignments.</cite>
