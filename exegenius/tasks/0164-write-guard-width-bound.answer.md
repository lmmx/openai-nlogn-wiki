A guard-width bound is an explicit number of extra bits that bounds the intermediate coefficients of a [simultaneous butterfly layer](simultaneous-butterfly-layer.md) and makes them exact, so that one truncation at the end suffices.

Inside a layer, each value comes from complex addition and subtraction, halving, sign change, and multiplication by $i$. The depth $\ell$ of an intermediate coefficient is the number of these operations on its longest chain, where a chain runs from an input coefficient and follows an operand into each result that uses it. Let $C_0$ and $C_1$ be constants fixed by the network construction. Let $\Delta = \lceil C_0 d^{C_1} \rceil$ be the guard count, where $d$ is the chunk count bound of the layer. Every depth is at most $\Delta$, and the paper's calculation charges both denominator and magnitude growth to this count. <cite data-source="paper">The guard-width calculation above bounds both denominator and magnitude growth by ⌈C0 dC1 ⌉.</cite>

Take inputs in $2^{-p}\mathbb{Z}[i]$ with modulus at most $1$, where $p$ is the number of fractional bits in each input coefficient. A value of depth $\ell$ then lies in $2^{-(p+\ell)}\mathbb{Z}[i]$ and has modulus at most $2^{\ell}$. This holds by induction on depth: addition or subtraction at most doubles the larger modulus of its operands; halving adds one denominator bit and halves the modulus; sign changes and multiplication by $i$ change neither bound. So the guard count limits the denominators of the [Gaussian dyadics](gaussian-dyadics.md) that appear as intermediate coefficients.

For example, let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be two example inputs of depth 0, with $\alpha = 1$ and $\beta = i$. Then $\alpha+\beta = 1+i$ has depth 1, and $(\alpha+\beta)/2 = (1+i)/2$ has depth 2. Its modulus $1/\sqrt{2}$ is at most $2^2$, as the bound says.

With every depth at most $\Delta$, each intermediate coefficient has at most $p+\Delta$ fractional bits, and each real component has absolute value at most $2^{\Delta}$. That value takes $\Delta+1$ bits, and a sign bit makes $\Delta+2$. The paper reserves $\Delta+3$ integer and sign bits per real component, one more than this count. <cite data-source="paper">⌈C0 dC1 ⌉ + 3 integer and sign bits per real component internally</cite> Halving shifts the numerator into reserved zero fractional bits, so no bit is lost. The whole layer is evaluated exactly, and one truncation toward zero, which drops the extra fractional bits, is applied at the end. <cite data-source="paper">The procedure evaluates the entire layer exactly before this final truncation.</cite>
