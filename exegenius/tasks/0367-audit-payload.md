## Page to audit (`payload`)

````markdown
# Payload

A *payload* is the record data stored at one address of an array, as distinct from the address that locates that record. Here an array is a finite collection of records, each stored at an address, where an address is a string of bits (such as $01$) saying where the record sits. The payload is what the record carries. The concept is needed because the paper's constructions move records between addresses, change addresses, or restore addresses, and their cost bounds must hold whatever data the records carry.

Take an array with four records at the two-bit addresses $00$, $01$, $10$ and $11$, which are binary strings of length two, carrying the payloads $5$, $0$, $3$ and $1$, where each numeral is the integer value of that record's data. Swapping the two address bits sends the record at $01$ to $10$ and the record at $10$ to $01$. After the swap, address $01$ holds payload $3$ and address $10$ holds payload $0$: the payloads moved with their records, and the addresses are the only thing that changed.

The paper (*Integer multiplication below n log n*, OpenAI 2026) states its bounds with the payload left arbitrary. Its packed-bit addition step, which adds one selected bit position across several address chunks at once, has a time bound that is worst-case A payload can also be very small:, so the swap cost must be bounded even when each record carries a single bit. Since the bound must hold for every payload, including one bit, the cost of a step cannot grow with the size of the data it moves. It has to come from the addresses the step touches.
````
