A destination rank is the position a record takes in the final order after a move: its rank among the records in that order. It is the sort key that returns each record extracted by the correction to its hole, which repairs the exceptional addresses of the packed selected-bit addition.

Each record is a block of $R$ payload bits, where $R$ is the number of payload bits in a record. It is stored at an address $\Psi=(\Psi_1,\Psi_2,\Psi_3)$ of three chunks, named $1$, $2$ and $3$, where $\Psi_1$, $\Psi_2$, $\Psi_3$ are its chunk values. The rectangular routine runs packed additions on a stream whose addresses form a complete rectangle in these chunks. Every other address field is fixed across the rectangle; such a field is a spectator. The rank $\operatorname{rank}(\Psi)$ of the record at $\Psi$ is its position among the records in stream order, counted from $0$. The destination rank is computed by a scan of the [Stream](stream.md) in order, with a counter. The paper computes ranks this way: <cite data-source="paper">scan the stream with a record-rank counter</cite>.

Let $\sigma_1$, $\sigma_2$, $\sigma_3$ be the strides of the chunks: the stride of a chunk is the amount the rank changes when that chunk rises by one with the other two fixed. Stream order is mixed-radix with chunk $1$ varying fastest, so $\sigma_1=1$, $\sigma_2$ is the number of values of chunk $1$, and $\sigma_3$ is the product of the value counts of chunks $1$ and $2$. This is the premise of the formula below.

Let $\Psi'$ be the destination of the record at $\Psi$, as defined below. Since the rank is linear in each chunk with slope equal to its stride, changing one chunk at a time from $\Psi$ to $\Psi'$ adds one term per chunk. <cite data-source="paper">the fixed spectator coordinates give</cite>

$$\operatorname{rank}(\Psi') = \operatorname{rank}(\Psi) + (\Psi'_1-\Psi_1)\sigma_1 + (\Psi'_2-\Psi_2)\sigma_2 + (\Psi'_3-\Psi_3)\sigma_3.$$

<cite data-source="paper">The differences are signed.</cite>

For example, let chunk $1$ take $4$ values and chunk $2$ take $3$ values, so $\sigma_1=1$, $\sigma_2=4$ and $\sigma_3=12$, with the spectators at $0$. The record at $\Psi=(1,2,0)$ has rank $1\cdot1+2\cdot4=9$. Take $\Psi'=(3,0,1)$, whose rank is $3+0+12=15$. The formula gives $9+(3-1)\cdot1+(0-2)\cdot4+(1-0)\cdot12=15$.

The correction repairs the exceptional addresses. Let $\mathcal{E}$ be the set of exceptional addresses (the paper's $B$), where some guard value lies too close to the edge of its range for the packed addition to be exact. A guard value is a segment's value, kept away from that edge so no segment carries into the next. Let $T_0$ be the ideal permutation of addresses that the packed addition should perform, and let $S_0$ be the packed approximation, which agrees with $T_0$ off $\mathcal{E}$. The correction is $\Xi = T_0 S_0^{-1}$. <cite data-source="paper">Since T0 S0−1 permutes B</cite>, so $\Xi$ permutes $\mathcal{E}$, and the destinations of the exceptional records are exactly the addresses of $\mathcal{E}$. The record at $\Psi$ moves to $\Psi'=\Xi(\Psi)$, its destination.

The algorithm extracts the records at the addresses of $\mathcal{E}$, gives each one the key $\operatorname{rank}(\Psi')$, and marks its hole, the address $\Psi'$ it returns to: <cite data-source="paper">mark its hole on a fixed track of the full stream</cite>. Distinct addresses have distinct ranks, so <cite data-source="paper">these keys are distinct and are exactly the ranks of the marked</cite> holes.

Each destination rank is the key on which the extracted records are sorted by [Stable radix sort](stable-radix-sort.md). That sort makes one pass per key bit, least significant bit first, and keeps equal keys in their earlier order. <cite data-source="paper">All involved integers have O(Arect ) bits</cite>, and ranks are among them. Ranks lie in $\{0,\dots,M-1\}$, so a key has $\lceil\log_2 M\rceil$ bits. Let $V=MR$ be the number of bits in the stream, so $M\le V$. The paper defines <cite data-source="paper">Arect = ⌈log2 (2V )⌉</cite>, hence $\lceil\log_2 M\rceil\le A_{\mathrm{rect}}$. The sort therefore makes at most $A_{\mathrm{rect}}$ passes: <cite data-source="paper">the at most Arect radix passes</cite>.

The sorted keys are the hole ranks in increasing order, so the $j$-th record in sorted order goes into the $j$-th hole in stream order. <cite data-source="paper">One forward full-stream scan reinserts the sorted records.</cite>
