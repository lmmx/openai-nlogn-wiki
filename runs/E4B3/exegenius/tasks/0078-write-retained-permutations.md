## This page

Concept: Resampling with the permutations left in the transform
Gloss: Instead of sorting twice per coordinate, the coordinate permutations of resampling are kept in the transform formula; the chirp absorbs the target permutation and the source permutation cancels across the two forward transforms, the pointwise product and the opposite transform.

## This page in the argument

The step: The two coordinate permutations of Gaussian resampling are kept in the transform formula rather than executed, with the chirp absorbing the target permutation and the source permutation cancelling.
How: write resampling per axis, then express the middle transform through a chirp convolution, and track both permutations algebraically
Why: Sorting twice per coordinate is exactly the movement cost that the paper wants to avoid. (the sources say so: "We retain both permutations in the transform formula and" (paper))

Its sub-steps, in order:

- Gaussian resampling gives $P_sF_s=2^{2\alpha^2}B_0P_tF_tA$ for coprime $s<t$, with $A,B_0$ contractions computable in time $O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$.
- Applied one coordinate line at a time on $d$ axes, the identity becomes $RF_{\boldsymbol s}=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A$, with movement costs from chunk interchanges.
- The chirp absorbs the target permutation, $QF_{\boldsymbol t}u=\overline a\cdot(a*(\overline a\cdot u))/T$, and the source permutation cancels across the two forward transforms, the pointwise product and the opposite transform.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A single deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: reduce multiplication to a convolution computed by Gaussian-resampling, Bluestein and synthetic transforms, accelerate the address movement and butterfly layers by two network-based tape procedures, and add up the costs (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time](main-theorem.md))

**S5.1**: Gaussian resampling gives $P_sF_s=2^{2\alpha^2}B_0P_tF_tA$ for coprime $s<t$, with $A,B_0$ contractions computable in time $O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$.
Source: "P_sF_s=2^{2\alpha^2}B_0P_tF_tA." (paper)
> \begin{lemma}[Resampling without executing the two permutations] \label{lem:no-sort-resampling} Fix $0<\delta<1/8$. Let $p>100$, $2\leq s<t<2^p$ and $2\leq\alpha<\sqrt p$ be integers, with $\gcd(s,t)=1$ and \[ \theta:=t/s-1>p/\alpha^4. \] Define coordinate permutations by \[ (P_su)_j=u_{tj},\qquad (P_tv)_k=v_{-sk}. \] There are linear contractions $A:\mathbb C^s\to\mathbb C^t$ and $B_0:\mathbb C^t\to\mathbb C^s$ satisfying \begin{equation} P_sF_s=2^{2\alpha^2}B_0P_tF_tA. \label{eq:no-sort-one-dimensional} \end{equation} There are fixed multitape algorithms for approximations $\widetilde A:\mathbb D_p^s\to\mathbb D_p^t$ and $\widetilde B_0:\mathbb D_p^t\to\mathbb D_p^s$, each with error less than $p^2$ and time \[ O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha). \] The constant is uniform in $p,s,t,\alpha$. Inputs and outputs use the scalar component format above and occur in increasing coordinate order. \end{lemma}

**S5.2**: Applied one coordinate line at a time on $d$ axes, the identity becomes $RF_{\boldsymbol s}=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A$, with movement costs from chunk interchanges.
Source: "Suppose every pair $(s_i,t_i)$, $1\leq i\leq d$, satisfies" (paper)
> \begin{lemma}[Tensor interface] \label{lem:tensor-resampling} Suppose every pair $(s_i,t_i)$, $1\leq i\leq d$, satisfies Lemma~\ref{lem:no-sort-resampling} with the same $p,\alpha,\delta$. Put $T=\prod_i t_i$, $\gamma=2d\alpha^2$, and \[ R=\bigotimes_iP_{s_i},\quad Q=\bigotimes_iP_{t_i},\quad \mathcal A=\bigotimes_i A_i,\quad \mathcal B_0=\bigotimes_i B_{0,i}. \] Then \begin{equation} RF_{\boldsymbol s} =2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A, \qquad F_{\boldsymbol q}:=\bigotimes_iF_{q_i}. \label{eq:no-sort-tensor} \end{equation} Also write $F_{\boldsymbol q}^+=\bigotimes_iF_{q_i}^+$. Both $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B_0$ are contractions. The linewise implementations of $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B_0$ each have error less than $dp^2$. The total time spent in the one-dimensional algorithms for both maps is $O(dTp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$, with a constant independent of $d$.
