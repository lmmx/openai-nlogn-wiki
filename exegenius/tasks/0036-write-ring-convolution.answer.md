Convolution over $\mathcal R_r=\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ on the remaining axes is computed by transforming both operands, multiplying records pointwise and transforming back.

Arrays are indexed by $\prod_i\mathbb Z/t_i\mathbb Z$ over the $d-1$ remaining axes, with values in $\mathcal R_r$. Let $M=\prod_i t_i$ be the number of records, $T=rM$ the number of complex coefficients, and $*$ cyclic convolution. A disk array has coefficients of modulus below $1$; $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ is the largest coefficient modulus.

For arrays over $\mathcal R_r$ define $\mathcal M_r(f,g)=\frac{f*g}{rM}=\frac{f*g}{T}$. A fixed output coefficient averages $rM$ signed scalar products <cite data-source="paper">$rM$ signed scalar products, and $\mathcal M_r$ is a bilinear</cite>: each of the $M$ first addresses determines one second address, each of the $r$ first polynomial indices determines one second index modulo $r$, and the negacyclic wrap supplies the sign. So $\mathcal M_r$ is a bilinear contraction, $\|\mathcal M_r(f,g)\|_\infty\le\|f\|_\infty\|g\|_\infty$.

Let $F^-$, $F^+$ be the forward and opposite synthetic transforms. Expansion of the finite sums gives $F^-(f*g)=M(F^-f)(F^-g)$. With $F^+F^-=I/M$ ([Discrete Fourier transform](dft.md)), it follows that $MF^+\bigl((F^-f)(F^-g)/r\bigr)=f*g/(rM)$. Write $P(u,v)=uv/r$ for the pointwise product.

The transforms are laid out as in [Tape layout for synthetic transforms](transform-layout.md). Perform both forward transforms and retain their common $LB$ order, with $L$ the layout and $B$ the frequency permutation. Multiply the $M$ pairs of records pointwise there, apply the opposite procedure to that order, and multiply by $M$. Since $LB$ permutes records and leaves polynomial suffixes intact, $P(LBu,LBv)=LBP(u,v)$, so the opposite transform $\mathcal T_+=F^+B^{-1}L^{-1}$ applied to the pointwise product of the stored forward outputs equals $F^+P(F^-f,F^-g)$. The procedure keeps the retained order of [Retained frequency order](frequency-order.md), forming the product there and passing it to the opposite transform. Each record product is formed by [Signed packing](signed-packing.md) in $O(rp\log(rp))$ time, truncated by $Q_p$ to $p$ bits of precision. There are $M=T/r$ pointwise products, so their total cost is $O(Tp\log(rp))$, and the final exact scaling is linear in $Tp$. Each transform costs $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}])$, where $\ell=\log_2r$, $K$ is the layout chunk width, and $\tau,\lambda'$ are exponents from the cost lemma. Put $\delta=\sqrt2\ell\,2^{-p}$ for each forward transform error and $\eta=\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$ for one truncation. Let $u,v$ be the exact stored forward outputs, $\widetilde u,\widetilde v$ the returned ones. By bilinearity of $P$ and the disk bound on both operands, $\|P(\widetilde u,\widetilde v)-P(u,v)\|_\infty<2\delta$. After truncation, $\|\widetilde z-z_*\|_\infty<2\delta+\eta$, where $\widetilde z=Q_p(P(\widetilde u,\widetilde v))$ and $z_*=P(u,v)$. Since the opposite transform $\mathcal T_+$ is a contraction with its own error $\delta$, the output error is less than $3\delta+\eta$. The exact multiplication by $M$ gives total error less than $M(3\delta+\eta)=\sqrt2\,M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$ <cite data-source="paper">$M(3\delta+\eta)=\sqrt2\,M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$</cite>.
