## This page

Concept: Cyclic convolution
Gloss: Convolution of arrays on a product of cyclic groups; the transform turns it into a pointwise product, so convolutions are computed by transform, multiply, inverse transform.

## Where the argument relies on this page

- Convolution over $\mathcal R_r$ on a multi-axis array is computed from two forward synthetic transforms in the same frequency order, a pointwise product and the opposite transform, with error below $\sqrt2 M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$. (on [Convolution from the retained transform layout](synthetic-convolution.md))
- The permuted middle transform $QF_{\boldsymbol t}$ equals a chirp multiplication, a cyclic convolution and another chirp, and the retained source permutation $R$ cancels across the transforms. (on [Chirp identity and permutation cancellation](bluestein-chirp.md))
- Splitting each integer into radix-$2^b$ digits makes the product a convolution modulo $x^S-1$ without wraparound. (on [Multiplication as convolution of radix digits](radix-digit-convolution.md))
- A convolution-preserving map $\Phi$ places the radix-digit list on a product of distinct prime cyclic axes, implemented by controlled shifts and axis moves. (on [Chinese-remainder address map to prime cyclic axes](crt-prime-axes.md))
- Scaling coefficient $k$ by $\zeta^k$ with $\zeta=e^{\pi i/r}$ turns the cyclic convolution in the last coordinate into polynomial multiplication modulo $y^r+1$. (on [Coefficient twist to the negacyclic ring](coefficient-twist.md))

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "let $*$ denote cyclic convolution in the displayed" (paper)

  > For arrays on $\prod_i\mathbb Z/t_i\mathbb Z$ with values in $\mathcal R_r$, let $*$ denote cyclic convolution in the displayed axes and define \[ \mathcal M_r(f,g)=\frac{f*g}{rM}=\frac{f*g}{T}. \] For a fixed output coefficient, each of the $M$ first cyclic addresses determines one second address, and each of the $r$ first polynomial indices determines one second index modulo $r$. The negacyclic wrap supplies the sign. Thus that coefficient averages $rM$ signed scalar products, and $\mathcal M_r$ is a bilinear contraction: \[ \|\mathcal M_r(f,g)\|_\infty \leq\|f\|_\infty\|g\|_\infty. \] If the other operand is a disk array, replacing one operand changes the output by at most the norm distance between its two values.

- "denotes cyclic convolution on the product of the coordinate groups" (paper)

  > The factor $QF_{\boldsymbol t}$ in \eqref{eq:no-sort-tensor} can be computed from a cyclic convolution and two pointwise phase multiplications. The chirp identity below performs the frequency change in $Q$ without applying $Q$ as a separate permutation of the array. The second identity shows how the retained source permutation $R$ cancels when computing a convolution. Here $*$ denotes cyclic convolution on the product of the coordinate groups and a dot denotes coordinatewise multiplication.
