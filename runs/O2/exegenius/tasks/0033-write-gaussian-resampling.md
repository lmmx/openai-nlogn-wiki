## This page

Concept: Gaussian resampling
Gloss: The Harvey–van der Hoeven identity expressing a Fourier transform of prime length s through one of nearby power-of-two length t, sandwiched between Gaussian-kernel contractions A and B₀ and a power-of-two scale.
Also called: Gaussian gridding

## This page in the argument

The step: Gaussian resampling expresses a Fourier transform of prime length $s$ through one of power-of-two length $t$, between Gaussian contractions.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- One deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine computes the exact $2n$-bit product of two $n$-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: build two linear-network tape procedures, fast address interchange and simultaneous butterfly layers, from fixed networks with a strict rank saving; use them in synthetic transforms; place these in the Harvey–van der Hoeven reduction through radix digits, a CRT map, Gaussian resampling with retained permutations, Bluestein chirps and a phase twist; and bound every cost term (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time](main-theorem.md))
- With the source and target coordinate permutations $R,Q$ kept inside the resampling identity, $RF_{\boldsymbol s}=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A$; the factor $QF_{\boldsymbol t}$ is a chirp-wrapped cyclic convolution, and $R$ cancels across forward and inverse source transforms in a convolution.; how: keep both permutations in the Gaussian resampling identity, apply it line by line in growing dimension, absorb $Q$ into the Bluestein chirp, and cancel $R$ in the convolution (on [Resampling with retained permutations](retained-permutations.md))
