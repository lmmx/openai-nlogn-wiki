The tensor product $E\otimes G$ of two finite-dimensional vector spaces $E$ and $G$ over the same field is a space of dimension $\dim E\cdot\dim G$ on which operators and bilinear forms of the two factors act factorwise. It builds the gate label space from three copies of one small space, and lets the dimensions of labels be computed.

Let $(\mathbf e_i)$ be a basis of $E$ and $(\mathbf g_j)$ a basis of $G$. The space $E\otimes G$ has as basis the symbols $\mathbf e_i\otimes\mathbf g_j$, one for each pair, so its dimension is $\dim E\cdot\dim G$. For $\xi\in E$ and $\eta\in G$, the pure tensor $\xi\otimes\eta$ is defined by expanding bilinearly, so it is linear in $\xi$ and in $\eta$ separately. If $E=G=\mathbb Q^2$, an element of $E\otimes G$ is a $2\times2$ array and $\xi\otimes\eta$ is the array with entries $\xi_i\eta_j$. Not every element is a pure tensor: $\mathbf e_1\otimes\mathbf g_1+\mathbf e_2\otimes\mathbf g_2$ is not.

**Factorwise action.** For linear maps $\phi:E\to E$ and $\psi:G\to G$, the map $\phi\otimes\psi$ is the linear map with $(\phi\otimes\psi)(\xi\otimes\eta)=\phi(\xi)\otimes\psi(\eta)$; its matrix is the Kronecker product of the two matrices. Bilinear forms combine the same way, into the form on $E\otimes G$ with
$$\langle \xi\otimes\eta,\ \xi'\otimes\eta'\rangle=\langle \xi,\xi'\rangle_E\,\langle \eta,\eta'\rangle_G .$$
A form is nondegenerate when its Gram matrix (the matrix of pairings of basis vectors) is invertible, which is why dimensions must be finite. The Gram matrix of the product form is the Kronecker product of the two Gram matrices, so a product of nondegenerate forms is nondegenerate.

**Subspaces.** Let $A$ and $C$ be finite-dimensional spaces with nondegenerate forms, $P\subseteq A$ a subspace, and $Q\subseteq C$ a subspace. Extend bases of $P$ and $Q$ to bases of $A$ and $C$. Then the pure tensors of basis vectors of $P$ with basis vectors of $Q$ are among the basis vectors of $A\otimes C$, so $P\otimes Q$ is a subspace of $A\otimes C$ of dimension $\dim P\cdot\dim Q$.

Now let $P$ be nondegenerate and $B=P^\perp$ its orthogonal complement in $A$, so $A=B\perp P$. Take a basis of $A$ made of a basis of $B$ followed by one of $P$. The basis vectors $\mathbf b\otimes\mathbf g_j$ of $A\otimes C$ span $B\otimes C$, and the vectors $\mathbf p\otimes\mathbf g_j$ span $P\otimes C$, so these two subspaces together span $A\otimes C$ with no overlap. They are orthogonal, because $\langle\mathbf b\otimes\mathbf g_j,\mathbf p\otimes\mathbf g_{j'}\rangle=\langle\mathbf b,\mathbf p\rangle\langle\mathbf g_j,\mathbf g_{j'}\rangle=0$ and pure tensors span. Hence
$$A\otimes C=(B\otimes C)\perp(P\otimes C),\qquad \dim(A\otimes C)=\dim B\cdot\dim C+\dim P\cdot\dim C.$$
Tensoring distributes over the orthogonal sum, which is how each gate label's dimension is computed in the nested-label construction.

**In the argument.** Take a space $F$ of dimension $h$ with a nondegenerate form. The label space is $F^{\otimes3}=F\otimes F\otimes F$, of dimension $h^3=m$, and it is nondegenerate <cite data-source="paper">It has dimension $m$ and is nondegenerate.</cite> An empty tensor product counts as the ground field, of dimension one <cite data-source="paper">An empty tensor product is the one-dimensional ground field</cite>, so a stage with no factors on one side contributes a factor $1$. Each input wire's label is the line spanned by a pure tensor $t_{a_1}\otimes t_{a_2}\otimes t_{a_3}$ of vectors spanning nondegenerate lines in $F$.
