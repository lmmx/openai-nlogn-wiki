The tape layout arranges the address bits of an array so that each round of a [synthetic polynomial transform](synthetic-transform.md) runs as one [simultaneous butterfly layer](simultaneous-layer.md) instead of one pass per axis.

Write $\ell=\log_2 r$ and $D=d-1$, the number of transformed axes (not the layer's chunk count). Axis $i$ has length $t_i\in\{2^{\ell-1},2^\ell\}$, so it has $\ell$ address bits (long) or $\ell-1$ (short). With $\boldsymbol t=(t_1,\dots,t_D)$, $F_{\boldsymbol t}^-$ is the forward synthetic transform along all $D$ axes and $F_{\boldsymbol t}^+$ its opposite. Records are [fixed-point Gaussian dyadic records](fixed-point.md) with $\Theta(p)$ bits per coefficient and $d\ell=O(p)$, so a pass over the array costs $O(Tp)$. Outputs keep the bit-reversed order of [retained frequency order](frequency-order.md), written $B$ along every axis.

A round butterflies one address bit of each axis, and a simultaneous layer needs those bits at a common offset in consecutive $K$-bit chunks <cite data-source="paper">we must place the selected bits of the participating axes at a common</cite>. The layout $L$ below provides this.

Put $b=(\ell-1)\bmod K$ and $q=\lfloor(\ell-1)/K\rfloor$, so $\ell-1=qK+b$ (new meanings: $b$ counts an axis's low bits left over after whole chunks, $q$ its whole chunks). Move to a prefix, one slot at a time, the top bit of each long axis and the $b$ lowest bits of each axis. At most $D(b+1)=O(dK)$ slots move, each by one pass, so the moves cost $O(TpdK)$ <cite data-source="paper">listed slots, so the moves cost $O(TpdK)$.</cite>

Each axis now keeps $q$ consecutive chunks. To order them as chunk 1 of every axis, then chunk 2 of every axis, and so on, scan target positions left to right and exchange the chunk there with the required one if they differ, except at the last position. This needs at most $Dq-1$ exchanges, even for nonadjacent chunks <cite data-source="paper">exchanges of width-$K$ address chunks, even when the chunks are</cite>. Each uses the [fast address-chunk interchange](address-interchange.md), costing $O(TpK^\tau)$. Since $DqK\le d\ell=O(p)$, the exchanges cost $O(Tp\,DqK^\tau)=O(Tp\,(DqK)K^{\tau-1})=O(Tp\,pK^{\tau-1})$. The prefix moves and exchanges define the record permutation $L$; undoing them in reverse gives $L^{-1}$.

There is one round per bit position of a long axis: $b+1$ on prefix slots and $qK$ on chunks, $\ell$ in all. In a chunk round the selected bit has the same offset in $D$ consecutive chunks, so one layer does it, costing $O(Tpd^{\lambda'})$; the chunk rounds cost $O(Tp\ell d^{\lambda'})$ in total <cite data-source="paper">$O(Tpd^{\lambda'})$ per round and $O(Tp\ell d^{\lambda'})$ in total.</cite> A prefix round handles its at most $D$ bits individually, each in $O(Tp)$; at most $b+1\le K$ such rounds cost $O(TpdK)$.

Each round is the contraction $\mathcal H$ (a linear map that does not increase norms), the signed coefficient permutation $J_E$, and one truncation $Q_p$. Truncation toward zero is odd and acts separately on each real and imaginary part, so for the array $z$ entering a round $J_EQ_p(\mathcal Hz)=Q_p(J_E\mathcal Hz)$: one truncation follows the exact round. The exact round does not increase the norm of the difference between computed and exact arrays, and the truncation moves each entry by less than $\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$. By the triangle inequality each round raises the error by less than $\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$, so from zero error the $\ell$ rounds leave error below $\sqrt2\,\ell\,2^{-p}$ <cite data-source="paper">zero error, at most $\ell$ rounds therefore</cite>. The permutations in $L$ are isometries and add none.

Arithmetic on named slots after $L$ is conjugated by $L$. The forward procedure moves records by $L$ and then runs $BF_{\boldsymbol t}^-$, giving $(LBF_{\boldsymbol t}^-L^{-1})L=LBF_{\boldsymbol t}^-$. The opposite procedure runs its arithmetic and restores by $L^{-1}$, giving $L^{-1}(LF_{\boldsymbol t}^+B^{-1}L^{-1})=F_{\boldsymbol t}^+B^{-1}L^{-1}$. Adding the costs, both cost $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}])$.
