## This page

Concept: Synthetic polynomial transform
Gloss: A transform over the ring modulo y^r + 1 whose roots of unity are powers of y, so every twiddle multiplication is a signed shift of a coefficient list.
Also called: polynomial transform

## Where the argument relies on this page

- Synthetic transforms over $\mathcal R_r$ on $d-1$ axes of lengths $t_i\in\{2^{\ell-1},2^\ell\}$ with outputs kept in a retained layout $LB$ cost $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}])$ with error less than $\sqrt2\ell\,2^{-p}$. (on [Tape layout for synthetic transforms](transform-layout.md))
- The synthetic transform $F_t^-$ over $\mathcal R_r=\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$, with roots of unity $\omega_t=y^{2r/t}$, is computed by decimation in frequency with outputs written in bit-reversed order, $B_tF_t^-$; the pointwise product keeps this order and the reverse procedure computes $F_t^+B_t^{-1}$, so no reordering is executed. (on [Retained frequency order](frequency-order.md))
- Normalized cyclic convolution over the polynomial ring $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ on the remaining $d-1$ axes is computed from two forward synthetic transforms in retained order, $M$ pointwise polynomial products and the opposite transform, in $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}+\log(rp)])$ time with error less than $\sqrt2\,M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$. (on [Convolution from the retained transform layout](ring-convolution.md))

## Pages that build on this one

- Retained frequency order: The forward synthetic transform writes its outputs in bit-reversed order, the pointwise product keeps that order, and the opposite transform consumes it, so no reordering is executed.

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "Powers of $y$ then" (paper)

  > Splitting the inputs into radix digits reduces multiplication to convolution of short integer coefficients. Our starting point is the Gaussian-resampling reduction of Harvey and van der Hoeven \cite{HarveyHoeven2021}: it replaces the Fourier transforms needed for this convolution by multidimensional transforms whose axis lengths are powers of two. After a further conversion to convolution, one coordinate is stored as the coefficient list of a polynomial modulo $y^r+1$, with $r$ a power of two. Powers of $y$ then serve as roots of unity in the remaining axes, and multiplication by such a root is a signed shift of the coefficient list. These are the synthetic polynomial transforms of Nussbaumer and Quandalle and Nussbaumer~\cite{NussbaumerQuandalle1978,Nussbaumer1980}.

- "For a power of two $t$ dividing $2r$, define $\omega_t=y^{2r/t}$." (paper)

  > For a power of two $t$ dividing $2r$, define $\omega_t=y^{2r/t}$. For $z\in\mathcal R_r^t$ and $0\leq j<t$, set \[ (F_t^-z)_j=\frac1t\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\omega_t^{-jk}z_k, \qquad (F_t^+z)_j=\frac1t\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\omega_t^{jk}z_k. \] Every power of $\omega_t$ is an isometry, and each transform entry is an average of $t$ such images. Thus both transforms are contractions. The ring has zero divisors, so we verify character cancellation directly instead of dividing by $\omega_t^j-1$.
