## This page

Concept: Chinese-remainder address map
Gloss: A ring isomorphism from cyclic convolution of length S = s_1⋯s_d (pairwise coprime) to d-dimensional cyclic convolution on prime axes, implemented on tape by triangular prefix-controlled cyclic shifts.

## This page in the argument

The step: The map $\Phi:x\mapsto\prod_ix_i^{\mu_i}$ is a ring isomorphism from cyclic convolution of length $S=s_1\cdots s_d$ to $d$-dimensional cyclic convolution on the prime axes, performed on tape by triangular prefix-controlled cyclic shifts in $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$.
How: Use the Chinese remainder theorem with coordinate powers $\mu_i=P_i^{-1}$ chosen so that axis $i$ depends only on lower-index digits, which makes the change of order a sequence of controlled shifts.
Why: With these powers the new coordinate $b_i$ is $a_i$ plus a multiple of the lower digits modulo $s_i$, a shift of a later field controlled by earlier fields. (the sources leave this for the reader to work out: spell it out)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- The assignment $\Phi$ sends $x$ to an element of order $S$, so it is an isomorphism and preserves cyclic convolution.
- Each triangular update $b_i=a_i+\mu_i\sum_{j<i}a_jP_j$ is a prefix-controlled cyclic shift of the valid interval, preserving zero padding.
- Exchanging axes of widths $\ell$ or $\ell-1$ costs $O(d^2)$ chunk swaps, so $\Phi$ and its inverse cost $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- There is one deterministic fixed-alphabet, fixed-tape Turing machine multiplying $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: Reduce the product to a digit convolution computed with Fourier-type transforms on power-of-two axes, and accelerate the transforms' address rearrangements and butterfly layers by a power of the logarithm. (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time](main-theorem.md))
- With $p=6b$, $d=\lfloor b^\epsilon\rfloor$, $K$, $r$, $T$ and the tiny exponents fixed, the multiplication algorithm is correct and runs in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time.; how: Fix compatible parameters, present the algorithm built from the transform machinery, prove the recovered coefficients round correctly, and add up the cost rows. (on [Parameter choice and cost balance](parameter-balance.md))
- The algorithm splits both inputs into radix-$2^b$ digits, applies $\Phi$, computes the source transform of each, multiplies pointwise, applies the opposite transform with scalings, rounds and propagates carries to output exactly $2n$ bits.; how: Reduce the product to a cyclic convolution of digit lists on coprime axes and evaluate it by the transform, pointwise product and inverse transform with the retained permutation cancelling. (on [The multiplication algorithm](mult-algorithm.md))

**PB.3.2.1**: The assignment $\Phi$ sends $x$ to an element of order $S$, so it is an isomorphism and preserves cyclic convolution.
Source: "$\Phi$ is an isomorphism and preserves cyclic convolution." (paper)
> Let $s_1,\ldots,s_d$ be the pairwise coprime source lengths, let $S=\prod_i s_i$, and put \[ P_i=\prod_{j<i}s_j,\qquad \mu_i=P_i^{-1}\pmod{s_i}. \] Here $P_i$ denotes an integer prefix product, not a Fourier-coordinate permutation. The assignment \[ \Phi:\mathbb C[x]/(x^S-1)\longrightarrow \mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_d]/(x_1^{s_1}-1,\ldots,x_d^{s_d}-1), \qquad x\longmapsto\prod_i x_i^{\mu_i} \] respects $x^S=1$, so it defines an algebra map. The target monomial group has size $S$, and the image of $x$ has order $S$, because each $\mu_i$ is a unit and the $s_i$ are pairwise coprime. Its powers therefore run through all target basis monomials exactly once. Hence $\Phi$ is an isomorphism and preserves cyclic convolution.

**PB.3.2.2**: Each triangular update $b_i=a_i+\mu_i\sum_{j<i}a_jP_j$ is a prefix-controlled cyclic shift of the valid interval, preserving zero padding.
Source: "Each triangular update is a prefix-controlled cyclic shift." (paper)
> Each triangular update is a prefix-controlled cyclic shift. The controls precede its target in the current physical order. For each prefix, prepare the offset, split the valid target interval into its two consecutive pieces, and merge those pieces in the opposite order. Every target position carries its full spectator suffix as a block; the invalid interval is copied unchanged. There are at most $T/t_i$ control prefixes for target $i$. For any fixed setup degree $C$, all offsets therefore cost $O(dTp^C/r)=o(Tp)$ to prepare, since $t_i\ge r/2$ and $r$ exceeds every fixed power of $p$.

**PB.3.2.3**: Exchanging axes of widths $\ell$ or $\ell-1$ costs $O(d^2)$ chunk swaps, so $\Phi$ and its inverse cost $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$.
Source: "their padding operations cost" (paper)
> The axis widths are $\ell$ or $\ell-1$. To exchange two adjacent axes of unequal width, move the longer axis's leading bit outside their equal-width pair, swap that pair by the proved chunk-swap procedure, and put the leading bit at its new position. This adds a fixed number of linear passes to the equal-width swap cost. Reversing all axes uses $O(d^2)$ such moves. Therefore the map $\Phi$, its inverse, and their padding operations cost \[ O\bigl(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau)\bigr). \] This construction describes payload movement from address $a$ to its new address. It is distinct from the operator convention $(R_cz)_j=z_{cj}$ used for Fourier dilations, which moves a payload from address $j$ to $c^{-1}j$.
