Convolution over the polynomial ring is computed along the [remaining axes](transform-axis.md) as a normalized cyclic convolution of two arrays of records. It is the step that reduces the exact product in [Integer multiplication below n log n](main-theorem.md) to integer products: it transforms both arrays, multiplies the transforms entry by entry, and transforms the product back.

Convolution over the polynomial ring takes its products in the [polynomial ring modulo $y^r + 1$](polynomial-ring-mod-yr-plus-1.md), $R_r$, where $y^r=-1$. So $y^e=-y^{e-r}$ for an integer exponent $e\ge r$. Let $\varphi$ and $\psi$ be the two input records, polynomials in $y$ of degree less than $r$ with complex coefficients. Their normalized product is $\varphi\psi/r$, the ordinary product reduced in $R_r$ and divided by $r$.

Convolution over the polynomial ring computes its transforms by [synthetic polynomial transforms](synthetic-transforms.md), whose roots of unity are powers of $y$. Let $\Theta$ be the forward transform along the remaining axes, normalized by $1/t$ on each axis of length $t$, and let $\Theta^{+}$ be its opposite, which uses the inverse roots of unity. Let $\nu$ be the number of records in the array, the product of the remaining axis lengths. Write $\ast$ for cyclic convolution over the remaining axes, $\varphi\star\psi=\varphi\ast\psi/(r\nu)$ for the normalized convolution, and $\odot$ for the product $uv/r$ taken entry by entry.

**Inverse on one axis.** On an axis of length $t$, $\Theta^{+}\Theta=I/t$. The sum $\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\omega_t^{jk}$, for a frequency address $j$ (an integer index of a transform entry), is $t$ when $t$ divides $j$. Otherwise write $j=2^v j_0$ with $j_0$ odd. <cite data-source="paper">Replacing k by k + t/2v+1 modulo t permutes the summands and multiplies their sum by y rj0 = −1.</cite> So the sum equals its own negative, and since no nonzero element $s$ of $R_r$ satisfies $2s=0$, the sum is zero. <cite data-source="paper">Expanding the composite transforms and applying this identity proves the final formula.</cite> Over all remaining axes the factors $1/t$ multiply to $1/\nu$, so $\Theta^{+}\Theta=I/\nu$.

**Convolution as a product.** <cite data-source="paper">To check normalization, expansion of the finite sums gives</cite>
$$\Theta(\varphi\ast\psi)=\nu\,(\Theta\varphi)(\Theta\psi),$$
Each term $\varphi_a\psi_b$ carries the factor $\omega^{-j(a+b)}$, which depends only on $a+b$ modulo the axis lengths since $\omega_t^t=1$, so the terms regroup. The left side carries $1/\nu$ and the right side $1/\nu^2$, which gives the factor $\nu$. Hence $\Theta(\varphi\star\psi)=\Theta\varphi\odot\Theta\psi$, and applying $\Theta^{+}$ gives
$$\varphi\star\psi=\nu\,\Theta^{+}(\Theta\varphi\odot\Theta\psi).$$

**Matching frequencies.** The entrywise product must pair equal frequencies. <cite data-source="paper">Perform both forward transforms and retain their common LB order.</cite> Both store frequency $j$ at the position $\mathcal P(j)$ of one fixed map $\mathcal P$. <cite data-source="paper">the forward entry with frequency address j is stored at L(Bj)</cite>. <cite data-source="paper">Pointwise multiplication commutes with applying the same record permutation LB to both</cite> operands, so the product is formed in stored order, which the opposite transform <cite data-source="paper">consumes that same order after a pointwise product</cite>.

**Packing.** Convolution over the polynomial ring computes its record products by [signed coefficient packing](signed-packing.md), which turns each polynomial product into an integer product. It packs coefficients into integers, multiplies them, and unpacks. It works because evaluation at a base $B$ respects products, $(uv)(B)=u(B)\,v(B)$, so one integer product holds every coefficient as $B$-adic blocks, provided the blocks do not overlap.

Input coefficients lie in the unit disk, since <cite data-source="paper">A disk array has norm at most one.</cite> Hence $\varphi=2^{-p}(\varphi_1+i\varphi_2)$ and $\psi=2^{-p}(\psi_1+i\psi_2)$, where $\varphi_1,\varphi_2,\psi_1,\psi_2$ are the real and imaginary integer polynomials, of degree less than $r$, with coefficients bounded by $2^p$. With $i^2=-1$, the product is $2^{-2p}$ times $(\varphi_1\psi_1-\varphi_2\psi_2)+i(\varphi_1\psi_2+\varphi_2\psi_1)$. (Source letters: $a,b,c,e$.) <cite data-source="paper">The real and imaginary numerator streams are respectively ac − be and ae + bc.</cite>

Let $B=2^{4p}$. <cite data-source="paper">Set B0 = 24p and, for u ∈ {a, b, c, e}, define the signed evaluation integer</cite> $u(B)$. The four products $\varphi_1(B)\psi_1(B)$, $\varphi_2(B)\psi_2(B)$, $\varphi_1(B)\psi_2(B)$ and $\varphi_2(B)\psi_1(B)$ are integer products. <cite data-source="paper">Multiply the four pairs of absolute values using the established multiplier and restore each product’s sign.</cite> It uses the [established O(n log n) multiplier](established-multiplier.md) as a subroutine.

Each ordinary product has coefficients that are sums of at most $r$ products of integers bounded by $2^p$, so each has absolute value at most $r2^{2p}$. <cite data-source="paper">Let r = 2ℓ < 2p with integers</cite> gives $r<2^p$, so $r2^{2p}<2^{3p}<2^{4p-1}=B/2$. Convolution over the polynomial ring reads each coefficient as the [centered residue](centered-residue.md) modulo $B$ of the value left after the lower blocks are removed. Convolution over the polynomial ring extracts its blocks by a [carry scan](carry-propagation.md): it takes each centered residue, subtracts it, and divides by $B$.

The numerators have degree at most $2r-2$ and are reduced modulo $y^r+1$. Since $y^r=-1$, a coefficient at $j+r$ wraps to position $j$ with a minus sign. So <cite data-source="paper">subtract the coefficient at j + r from the one at j</cite> for $0\le j<r$, taking the coefficient at $j+r$ as zero when $j+r>2r-2$. Dividing by $r2^{2p}$ and truncating by $Q_p$ gives $\varphi\psi/r$ on the $2^{-p}$ grid.

Worked example with $r=2$, $\varphi_1=1+2y$, $\psi_1=3+y$, and the toy base $B=100$, not a power of two. Then $\varphi_1(100)=201$, $\psi_1(100)=103$, and $201\cdot103=20703$, whose base-100 blocks $03$, $07$, $2$ give $\varphi_1\psi_1=3+7y+2y^2$, each coefficient below $B/2=50$. Modulo $y^2+1$, $3-2=1$ and $7$, so $1+7y$.
