The simultaneous butterfly layer applies $H_0$ coefficientwise to one bit of each of $D\le d$ address chunks of $K$ bits at once, in time $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ for fixed $\lambda'<1$ rather than $D$ whole-array passes.

The [two-point kernel $C$](phase-kernel.md), $C=aI+bX$, with $\mathsf S=\operatorname{diag}(1,i)$ satisfies $H_0=b\,\mathsf SC\mathsf S$, $C^2=X$, $C^4=I$ <cite data-source="paper">H_0=b\mathsf SC\mathsf S,\qquad C^2=X,\qquad C^4=I,\qquad</cite>. Let $z$ be the array of records at addresses $x$, with selected bits $x_1,\dots,x_D$. Then $H_0^{\otimes D}=b^D\mathcal S_DC^{\otimes D}\mathcal S_D$ with $(\mathcal S_Dz)(x)=i^{x_1+\dots+x_D}z(x)$, an exact Gaussian dyadic operation: $b^D=(-i/2)^{\lfloor D/2\rfloor}b^{D\bmod2}$ is a shift, a fourth-root phase and at most one factor $b$.

A group of $e=mf$ axes ($e$ counts them) forms $m$ slots of $f$ axes; column $j$ is the vector of bit $j$ of each slot, in $\mathbb F_2^m$. The network of [Binary phase frames](phase-frames.md) computes $C^{\otimes e}$: each edge changes frame by $\prod_hC_{v_h}^{\eta_h}$ on every column, over a basis $v_h$ of its residual space, $\eta_h=\pm1$, $C_v=aI+bX_v$, $X_v$ shifting a column by $v$. For invertible $G$ with first columns $v_h$ and $\mathcal P_G:x\mapsto Gx$, $\mathcal P_GX_v\mathcal P_G^{-1}=X_{Gv}$ <cite data-source="paper">\mathcal P_MX_v\mathcal P_M^{-1}=X_{Mv}.</cite>, so the edge change is $\mathcal P_G\bigl(\prod_hC_{e_h}^{\eta_h}\bigr)\mathcal P_G^{-1}$, $e_h$ standard basis vectors. Apply $\mathcal P_G^{-1}$ per column, then $(C^{\eta_h})^{\otimes f}$ to the $f$ bits of slot $h$, then $\mathcal P_G$: one child on $f=e/m$ axes per residual vector. As $(C^{-1})^{\otimes f}=(-i)^fZ^{\otimes f}C^{\otimes f}Z^{\otimes f}$, $Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, inverse children need only signs and phases. Hence exactly $s$ children.

Gaussian elimination writes $G$ as at most $m(m-1)+3m$ row additions, each XORing selected bits of two slots, performed by [Packed selected-bit addition](packed-bit-addition.md). So the basis change and its inverse cost $O(V((eK)^\tau+1))$ <cite data-source="paper">O(V((eK)^\tau+1))</cite> for fixed $\tau<1$.

Row $Wg+w$ (quotient $g$, remainder $0\le w<W$) goes to wire role $w$, so children have volume $V/W$. Rows are the bits of the first $q_0=\lceil\log_2W\rceil\lceil\log_m(2d)\rceil$ chunks, $q_0$ counting chunks, done individually at $O(V\log d)$; if $D\le q_0$ all are. Their range $2^{q_0K}\ge W^{\lceil\log_md\rceil}$, zero-padded to a multiple, at most doubles the volume and keeps the row count divisible by $W$ per level. With $\beta=\frac12$ and $F(e)$ the time divided by volume,
$$F(e)\le \tfrac sW F(e/m)+O((eK)^\tau+1)\ (e\ge d^\beta),\qquad F(e)=O(e)\ (e<d^\beta).$$
The layer gets its power saving from [Recursive power saving](rank-recurrence.md), whose recurrence this is.

Take $\sigma<1$ with $\gamma=s/W\le m^\sigma$, $K\le d^c$ for fixed $c>0$, and $\lambda$ with $\max\{\tau,\sigma\}<\lambda<1$, $\tau(1+c/\beta)<\lambda$. As $K\le e^{c/\beta}$ for $e\ge d^\beta$, overhead is $O(e^\lambda)$ and internal levels sum to $O(e^\lambda)\sum_{j\ge0}(\gamma/m^\lambda)^j=O(e^\lambda)$. After $j$ levels a leaf has $u=e/m^j<d^\beta$ chunks and $\gamma^j\le(e/u)^\sigma$, so leaves add $O(\gamma^ju)\le O(e^\sigma d^{\beta(1-\sigma)})$. Writing $D-q_0=\sum_ja_jm^j$, $0\le a_j<m$, gives $a_j$ pieces of $m^j$ chunks, at most $(m-1)(1+\lfloor\log_md\rfloor)$, hence a $\log(2d)$ factor:
$$O(\log(2d)[d^\lambda+d^{\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)}+1])=O(d^{\lambda'})$$
for $\max\{\lambda,\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)\}<\lambda'<1$. The paper's $\tau=\sigma=1-2^{-50}$, $c=2^{-56}$, $\lambda=1-2^{-52}$ allow $\lambda'=1-2^{-54}$ <cite data-source="paper">\lambda'=1-2^{-54},</cite>, barely below $D$ passes since $s/W$ falls short of $m$ only by $m^{2^{-50}}$.

The layer is evaluated exactly in [fixed-point Gaussian dyadic records](fixed-point.md). Let $A(e)$ bound the operations on a chain from an input coefficient to an intermediate one. An invocation adds fewer than $E=64(W+m+1)^3$ (gates $24W^3$, corrections $4s+4W+4$, $s<Wm$), and chains concatenate the $s$ children: $A(e)\le sA(e/m)+E$, $A(e)\le8e$ at leaves. For $x\ge1$, $sx+E\le Bx$, $B=s+E$; with $\nu=\min\{j\ge1:m^j\ge B\}$ and at most $\lceil\log_md\rceil$ levels, $A(e)\le8B^2d^{\nu+1}$. Adding pieces, diagonals and $b^D$ stays below $\Delta=\lceil C_0d^{C_1}\rceil$, $C_0=128mB^2$, $C_1=\max\{20,\nu+3\}=20$ here.

Inputs lie on the grid $2^{-p}\mathbb Z[i]$ in the unit disk. By induction on chain depth $\ell$, values lie in $2^{-(p+\ell)}\mathbb Z[i]$ with modulus at most $2^\ell$: addition doubles modulus, halving adds a denominator bit. So $p+\Delta$ fractional and $\Delta+3$ integer and sign bits per real component represent every intermediate coefficient exactly <cite data-source="paper">represents every intermediate coefficient exactly and prevents overflow</cite>. For $d=\Theta(p^\epsilon)$ and $\epsilon C_1<1$, $\Delta=o(p)$: width $O(p)$.

Each row of $H_0$ has absolute row sum one, and absolute row sums multiply under tensor products, so every row of $\mathcal H=H_0^{\otimes D}$ has absolute row sum one <cite data-source="paper">absolute row sum one.</cite> Hence $\mathcal H$ maps disk arrays to disk arrays. Truncation toward zero cannot increase either component's absolute value, so $Q_p$ keeps the disk and changes each component by less than $2^{-p}$: $\|Q_p(\mathcal Hz)-\mathcal Hz\|_\infty<\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$ <cite data-source="paper">truncation cannot increase the absolute value of either component</cite>.
