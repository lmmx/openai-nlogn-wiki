Schönhage–Strassen multiplication computes [Exact integer multiplication](exact-integer-multiplication.md) of two $n$-bit integers in $O(n\log n\log\log n)$ time, and it is the bound that the paper's [main theorem](main-theorem.md) sharpens. <cite data-source="paper">Schönhage and Strassen proved in 1971 that two n-bit integers can be multiplied in O(n log n log log n)</cite> time on a multitape Turing machine.

Let $\vartheta$ be the number of bits in a residue. Schönhage–Strassen multiplication is based on [Congruence](congruence-modulo.md) modulo $2^\vartheta+1$: two integers are congruent when $2^\vartheta+1$ divides their difference. The integers modulo $2^\vartheta+1$ form a ring, a set with addition and multiplication obeying the usual axioms. The method <cite data-source="paper">uses roots of unity in Fermat-type residue rings, where certain multiplications</cite> become shifts.

In this ring $2^\vartheta\equiv-1$. For $\vartheta=4$ and modulus $17$, $2^4=16\equiv-1$, so $2^8\equiv1$. Schönhage–Strassen multiplication counts its roots of unity by the [Order of an element](order-of-element.md) of $2$, which is $8$ here.

Let $\varsigma$ be the number of places a multiplication shifts, with $0\le\varsigma<\vartheta$. Let $\Xi$ be a residue, with $0\le\Xi<2^\vartheta$, and write $\Xi=\sum_{\varpi=0}^{\vartheta-1}\Xi_\varpi 2^\varpi$, where $\varpi$ is a bit position from $0$ to $\vartheta-1$ and $\Xi_\varpi\in\{0,1\}$ is the bit of $\Xi$ at position $\varpi$. Then $2^\varsigma\Xi\equiv\sum_\varpi\Xi_\varpi 2^{\varpi+\varsigma}$. Each term with $\varpi+\varsigma\ge\vartheta$ is congruent to $-\Xi_\varpi 2^{\varpi+\varsigma-\vartheta}$, because $2^\vartheta\equiv-1$. So multiplying by $2^\varsigma$ moves each bit up $\varsigma$ places, and a bit passing the top returns at the bottom negated.

Take $\vartheta=4$, $\Xi=3$ and $\varsigma=3$. Then $2^3\cdot3=24\equiv7\pmod{17}$. Bit $\Xi_0=1$ moves to position $3$, giving $8$; bit $\Xi_1=1$ moves to position $4$, which wraps to $-2^0=-1$. The sum is $8-1=7$.
