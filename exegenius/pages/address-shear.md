An address shear is the map $(H,D)\mapsto(H+D,D)$, which adds one address field to another and leaves the second unchanged. Interchanging address chunks reduces to shears, so their cost enters the main bound: <cite data-source="paper">A compatible choice of rational parameters makes all terms in its cost table fit Theorem 1.</cite> Theorem 1 of [Integer multiplication below n log n](main-theorem.md) gives the time $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$, where $\lg$ is the base-2 logarithm.

Let $m$ be the number of fields in each vector, so $H=(H_1,\dots,H_m)$ and $D=(D_1,\dots,D_m)$. Each field is $b$ digits in radix $q$, a fixed odd prime; $b$ and $q$ change meaning here. <cite data-source="paper">every field ranging over [q b ]</cite>, so its values form the residue ring $\mathbb{Z}/q^b\mathbb{Z}$.

For a rational $m\times m$ matrix $M$, let $\Phi_M$ move the entry at address $(H,D)$ to $(H+MD,D)$. Applying $\Phi_M$ and then $\Phi_{M'}$, for another rational matrix $M'$, adds $M'D$ to $H+MD$, so $\Phi_{M'}\Phi_M=\Phi_{M+M'}$. The shear is $\Phi_I$, with $I$ the identity matrix.

The shear is counted by the [rational rank](matrix-rank.md) $a$ of $M$, the number of linearly independent rows of $M$ over $\mathbb{Q}$. Every such $M$ factors as $M=E_1\Pi E_2$, where $E_1$ and $E_2$ are invertible lower-triangular matrices and $\Pi$ is a $0/1$ matrix with at most one 1 in each row and column. <cite data-source="paper">Each step isolates a rank-one block and leaves the remaining rank in the active submatrix.</cite> Since $E_1$ and $E_2$ are invertible, $\Pi$ has rank $a$, and a partial permutation matrix with $k$ ones has rank $k$, so $\Pi$ has exactly $a$ ones.

The shear is computed with [modular inverses](modular-inverse.md) modulo $q$ of the denominators. <cite data-source="paper">Choose an odd prime avoiding every denominator</cite>, so that <cite data-source="paper">the triangular factors remain invertible there</cite>.

With $M=E_1\Pi E_2$, the shear runs in three stages: replace $D$ by $E_2D$ and $H$ by $E_1^{-1}H$; for each 1 at position $(i,j)$ of $\Pi$, update $H_i\leftarrow H_i+D_j$; replace $H$ by $E_1H$ and $D$ by $E_2^{-1}D$. The result is $H+MD$, with $D$ unchanged. The outer stages are lower-triangular, each updating a field from earlier ones: <cite data-source="paper">updates a later field using earlier fields in linear time</cite>. With $m$ fixed, each costs $O(V)$.

Each update $H_i\leftarrow H_i+D_j$ is one interchange: set $D_j\leftarrow D_j+H_i$, swap $H_i$ and $D_j$, then set $D_j\leftarrow H_i-D_j$. <cite data-source="paper">On an initial pair (h, d) this sequence yields (h + d, d).</cite> For example, modulo $9$, $(h,d)=(3,5)$ becomes $(8,5)$.

A shear of rank $a$ is implemented by $a$ calls of the [address-field interchange](address-interchange.md) procedure. <cite data-source="paper">Its cost is O(V uτ ) for a fixed τ < 1.</cite> Here $u$ is the field width in bits, a constant multiple of $b$ because each digit takes $\log_2 q$ bits. The shear costs $O(aVb^\tau)+O(V)$.

The shear is used by the [bit procedure](bit-procedure.md), which interchanges two fields. It is computed by the [Linear networks and frames](linear-network-frames.md) of the bit procedure. <cite data-source="paper">Thus the intermediate frames cancel</cite>, and <cite data-source="paper">Msink(ρ(w)) − Msource(w) = Im for every input role w.</cite> On every role, the shear is realised as the [endpoint operator](endpoint-operator.md) of the bit network. <cite data-source="paper">For the bit procedure, that endpoint operator is an address shear</cite>. Composition follows the rules of [linear algebra basics](linear-algebra-basics.md).

Ranks govern the recursion. Let $s$ be the total number of smaller calls and $W$ the number of wires, with <cite data-source="paper">that the total number s of smaller calls satisfies s < W m.</cite> A call on volume $V$ splits it into $W$ streams of volume $V/W$, each handled by one smaller call. Triangular and splitting work costs $O(V)$ per call <cite data-source="paper">because their number is fixed.</cite> With $F_k$ the time per unit volume at depth $k$, this gives $F_k\le (s/W)F_{k-1}+O(1)$.

Since $s<Wm$, $s/W<m$, so some $\tau<1$ has $s/W<m^\tau$, and $\sum_{j=0}^{k}(s/W)^j=O(m^{k\tau})$. With $e=m^k$ the number of digits in a chunk at depth $k$, $F_k=O(e^\tau)$, so a chunk of $e$ digits is interchanged in $O(Ve^\tau)$ time.
