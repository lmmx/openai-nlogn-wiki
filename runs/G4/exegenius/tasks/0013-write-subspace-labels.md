## This page

Concept: Subspace labels and the rank budget
Gloss: Nested nondegenerate subspaces attached to gates so each edge's dimension change counts the smaller operations needed, with total dimension lost small enough to give a strict deficit.

## This page in the argument

The step: Attaching nested nondegenerate subspaces to the gates makes each edge's dimension change count the smaller operations it needs, and the total dimension lost on decreasing edges is $L<N/2$, so the sum of absolute changes stays below $Wm$ by a strict margin.
How: Give each stage a label built from tensor factors $A$, $B$, $P$, $Q$ of triple indicator vectors, tabulate the orthogonal residual of every edge, and sum the dimension changes gate by gate.
Why: The labels turn the cost of a change of representation into a dimension count, and only the central wires between times 3 and 4 ever lose dimension. (the sources say so: "The saving will come from keeping the total dimension lost on decreasing" (paper))
Striking feature: Only the central-wire edges from time 3 to time 4 ever decrease dimension, each by exactly $h$, so the total loss is a fixed fraction $100/539$ (bit) or $101/539$ (complex) of $N$, below one half in both networks. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- Every edge joins comparable nondegenerate labels, so its orthogonal residual is defined.
- The only decreasing edges are the central-wire edges from time 3 to time 4, each losing dimension $h$.
- Signed changes telescope at each gate, so the sum of absolute changes is $Wm-2N+2L$.
- The loss ratios $L_{\rm b}/N=100/539$ and $L_{\rm c}/N=101/539$ are both below $1/2$.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, disproving the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture in that model.; how: Reduce the product to a cyclic convolution of radix digits evaluated by Fourier-type transforms, make the two costly ingredients of those transforms (address rearrangement and butterfly arithmetic) cost a power of the logarithm less than a full scan per level by two rank-saving tape procedures, then assemble the algorithm, prove exactness and total the cost. (on [Sub-n log n integer multiplication](sub-nlogn-multiplication.md))
- Two fixed linear networks, one over bits and one over complex numbers, exchange two banks of values, restore every scratch value, and have total edge rank $s$ below $Wm$, where $W$ is the number of wires and $m$ the label dimension.; how: Build a scalar bank-exchange network from three-element subset intersections, lift it to arrays through frames, attach nested subspace labels to its gates, and read off a rational interface for bits and a phase interface for complex values.

**S1.2.1**: Every edge joins comparable nondegenerate labels, so its orthogonal residual is defined.
Source: "Every edge joins comparable nondegenerate labels." (paper)
> \begin{lemma}\label{lem:motif-residuals} Every edge joins comparable nondegenerate labels. The only decreasing edges are the central-wire edges from time 3 to time 4; their dimension loss is $h$. Consequently, if $c$ is the number of central wires per invocation and $W$ the total number of wires, then \begin{equation} L=Ich,\qquad \sum_{e}\bigl|\dim U_{\mathrm{head}(e)}- \dim U_{\mathrm{tail}(e)}\bigr| =Wm-2N+2L. \label{eq:rank-budget-labels} \end{equation} For the binary label space, every nonzero orthogonal residual of an edge has an orthonormal basis. \end{lemma}

**S1.2.3**: Signed changes telescope at each gate, so the sum of absolute changes is $Wm-2N+2L$.
Source: "The signed sum of dimension changes telescopes at every gate: each" (paper)
> The signed sum of dimension changes telescopes at every gate: each touched wire has one incoming and one outgoing incidence with the same gate label. The sum of source dimensions is $N$, while the sum of sink dimensions is $Wm-N$. The signed increase is therefore $Wm-2N$. There are $Ic$ decreasing edges, each of loss $h$; replacing signed changes by absolute changes adds $2L$.

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "A label is a nondegenerate subspace of a fixed bilinear space." (paper)

  > The scalar network already has the required signed exchange. We now choose its intermediate labels to make the changes of frame inexpensive. A label is a nondegenerate subspace of a fixed bilinear space. On every edge one label will contain the other. For such an edge, the \emph{orthogonal residual} is the orthogonal complement of the smaller label inside the larger one; its dimension is the absolute change in label dimension. The saving will come from keeping the total dimension lost on decreasing edges small relative to $N$.
