## This page

Concept: Fast address-chunk interchange
Gloss: The first tape procedure: swapping two disjoint contiguous address fields of u bits, (p,h,g,d,z) ↦ (p,d,g,h,z), in O(V u^τ) for a fixed τ<1 by building the swap from address shears executed through the bit network, with XOR combinations of intermediate data.
Also called: chunk swap, tape interchange

## This page in the argument

The step: Two disjoint contiguous address fields of $u$ bits can be interchanged, $(p,h,g,d,z)\mapsto(p,d,g,h,z)$, in $O(V u^{\tau})$ time for a fixed $\tau<1$.
How: use the bit network, whose edges are address shears, to swap power-of-$m$ widths recursively, then remove the width and row-count restrictions
Why: Direct movement costs a scan per bit of the field; computing XOR combinations of intermediate data lets the swap be built from shears executed through a network whose total rank is below $W m$, which by the recursion saves a power of the width. (the sources do not say this: give the reader the reason)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- A fixed linear network whose total number $s$ of smaller calls is less than $W m$ gives a cost-per-volume recurrence with ratio $s/W<m$, hence a power-saving exponent $\tau<1$. (on [Recursive rank saving](rank-saving-recurrence.md))
- Arbitrary widths and row counts are handled by padding rows from the chunks' own high digits and splitting the remaining width in base $m$, giving $O(Vu^{\tau})$ for an interchange of two $u$-bit fields.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A single deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, disproving the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture.; how: reduce integer multiplication to convolution over a polynomial ring, and speed up the data movement and the butterfly layers of its transforms with two new tape procedures that each save a power of the logarithm (on [Integer multiplication below n log n](main-theorem.md))

**R2.2**: Arbitrary widths and row counts are handled by padding rows from the chunks' own high digits and splitting the remaining width in base $m$, giving $O(Vu^{\tau})$ for an interchange of two $u$-bit fields.
Source: "in $O(Vu^\tau)$ steps, where $V=PG B\,2^{2u}$." (paper)
> \begin{lemma}[Arbitrary-width interchange] \label{lem:chunk-swap} There exist fixed $0<\tau<1$ and one fixed finite-alphabet multitape procedure with the following property. For positive integers $P,G,B$ and $u\ge1$, it transforms an arbitrary bit array on \[ [P]\times[2^u]\times[G]\times[2^u]\times[B] \] by the address permutation \[ (p,h,g,d,z)\longmapsto(p,d,g,h,z) \] in $O(Vu^\tau)$ steps, where $V=PG B\,2^{2u}$. The integers specifying the shape are part of the input. The time includes their processing, all padding and unpadding, and fixed-tape workspace cleanup. In particular, $B=1$ is allowed. \end{lemma}
