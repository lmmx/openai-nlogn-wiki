The complex phase network is built on the [subset-intersection network](subset-intersection-network.md). Each wire carries an array of complex values in place of a scalar, <cite data-source="paper">replace the scalar on each wire w by an array</cite>. Its edges change the frames of these arrays by products of two-point kernels, and this identity is what the [simultaneous butterfly layer](simultaneous-butterfly-layer.md) uses to apply a butterfly on several address bits at once, one step toward the [main theorem](main-theorem.md).

The network takes its addresses from the [binary field](binary-field.md). Let $m$ be the number of coordinates of an address, so addresses are the vectors $x\in\mathbb F_2^m$ and each array has $2^m$ entries. For a binary vector $z$, $\mathrm{supp}(z)$ is the set of coordinates at which $z$ is $1$, and $\mathrm{wt}(z)=|\mathrm{supp}(z)|$ is its Hamming weight, read as an integer. For binary vectors $u,v$, the dot product $u\cdot v$ is the parity of $|\mathrm{supp}(u)\cap\mathrm{supp}(v)|$. For $b\in\mathbb F_2$, $[b]\in\{0,1\}$ is its integer representative.

The complex phase network is indexed by [subspace labels](subspace-labels.md): each vertex carries one label $U$, a subspace with $U\cap U^\perp=0$. <cite data-source="paper">Use the complex scalar network and binary labels.</cite> For a nondegenerate subspace $W$, $P_W$ is the projection onto $W$ along $W^\perp$. For a function $f$ on addresses, $\mathrm{diag}_x(f(x))$ is the diagonal operator that multiplies the value at each address $x$ by $f(x)$. The phase exponent of an address is $q_U(x)=\mathrm{wt}(P_Ux)\bmod 4$, and the frame at a vertex with label $U$ is
$$C_U = H^{\otimes m}\,\mathrm{diag}_x\big(i^{q_U(x)}\big)\,H^{\otimes m},$$
where $H=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$ is the normalized Hadamard matrix.

The complex phase network computes its frames by the [tensor power](tensor-product.md) $H^{\otimes m}$ of $H$, where $\otimes$ is the Kronecker product. The complex phase network has coefficients in [Gaussian dyadics](gaussian-dyadics.md): each entry of $C_U$ and of $C_U^{-1}$ is a Gaussian integer divided by $2^m$. This holds because each entry of $H^{\otimes m}$ is $\pm 2^{-m/2}$, so each entry of $C_U$ is $2^{-m}$ times a sum of terms $\pm i^{q_U(x)}$, and each such sum is a Gaussian integer.

Set $\tau_0=\frac{1+i}{2}$ and $\tau_1=\frac{1-i}{2}$. Then $\tau_0^2+\tau_1^2=0$ and $2\tau_0\tau_1=1$. For a binary vector $v$, $X_v$ is the translation by $v$ on functions of addresses: <cite data-source="paper">Here (Xv f )(x) = f (x + v).</cite> Since $X_v^2=I$, the operator $\Theta_v=\tau_0 I+\tau_1 X_v$ has inverse $\tau_1 I+\tau_0 X_v$, because $(\tau_0 I+\tau_1X_v)(\tau_1 I+\tau_0X_v)=2\tau_0\tau_1 I+(\tau_0^2+\tau_1^2)X_v=I$.

An edge runs from a tail label $U$ to a head label $V$, and its frame change is $C_VC_U^{-1}$. Write $A\perp B$ when every vector of $A$ is orthogonal to every vector of $B$ and $A\cap B=0$, so $A+B$ is their orthogonal sum. The orthogonal residual $\Delta$ of an edge is the orthogonal complement of the smaller label inside the larger one. An increasing edge has $V=U\perp\Delta$, and a decreasing edge has $U=V\perp\Delta$. Let $\mathcal{O}$ be an orthonormal basis of $\Delta$: a set of vectors $v$ with $v\cdot v=1$ and $v\cdot v'=0$ for distinct $v,v'\in\mathcal O$. <cite data-source="paper">For the binary label space, every nonzero orthogonal residual of an edge has an orthonormal basis.</cite>

Take an increasing edge and an address $x$. First, $P_\Delta x=\sum_{v\in\mathcal O}(v\cdot x)\,v$: the sum lies in $\Delta$, and $x$ minus the sum is orthogonal to every $v\in\mathcal O$, hence to $\Delta$. Second, $P_Vx=P_Ux+P_\Delta x$. The right side lies in $V$. The vector $x-P_Ux-P_\Delta x$ is orthogonal to $U$, because $x-P_Ux\in U^\perp$ and $P_\Delta x\in\Delta\perp U$. It is also orthogonal to $\Delta$, because $x-P_\Delta x\in\Delta^\perp$ and $P_Ux\in U\perp\Delta$. So it lies in $(U+\Delta)^\perp=V^\perp$.

For binary vectors $z,w$ (letters local to this argument), a coordinate in both supports is counted twice in $\mathrm{wt}(z)+\mathrm{wt}(w)$ and not at all in $\mathrm{wt}(z+w)$, so $\mathrm{wt}(z+w)=\mathrm{wt}(z)+\mathrm{wt}(w)-2|\mathrm{supp}(z)\cap\mathrm{supp}(w)|$. <cite data-source="paper">If z · w = 0, the intersection cardinality is even, so weight is additive modulo four.</cite> Hence $\mathrm{wt}(z+w)\equiv\mathrm{wt}(z)+\mathrm{wt}(w)\pmod 4$ whenever $z\cdot w=0$.

Now $P_Ux\cdot P_\Delta x=0$, since $P_\Delta x\in\Delta\perp U$. So $\mathrm{wt}(P_Vx)\equiv\mathrm{wt}(P_Ux)+\mathrm{wt}(P_\Delta x)$ modulo 4. The summands $(v\cdot x)\,v$ are pairwise orthogonal. Any partial sum of the earlier summands is a combination of earlier vectors $v$, so it is orthogonal to the next summand. Adding the summands one at a time gives $\mathrm{wt}(P_\Delta x)\equiv\sum_{v\in\mathcal O}[v\cdot x]\,\mathrm{wt}(v)$ modulo 4. Since $q_V(x)$ and $q_U(x)$ are these weights modulo 4,
$$q_V(x)-q_U(x)\equiv\sum_{v\in\mathcal O}\mathrm{wt}(v)\,[v\cdot x]\pmod 4.$$
On a decreasing edge the roles of $U$ and $V$ swap, so the right side is negated.

<cite data-source="paper">Each v has norm one, so wt(v) is odd.</cite> Let $\varepsilon_v\in\{1,-1\}$ with $\varepsilon_v\equiv\mathrm{wt}(v)\pmod 4$ on an increasing edge and $\varepsilon_v\equiv-\mathrm{wt}(v)\pmod 4$ on a decreasing edge. Then $i^{q_V(x)-q_U(x)}=\prod_{v\in\mathcal O}i^{\varepsilon_v[v\cdot x]}$.

For $b\in\{0,1\}$ and $\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}$, $i^{\varepsilon b}=\frac{1+i\varepsilon}{2}+\frac{1-i\varepsilon}{2}(-1)^b$, since both sides equal $1$ at $b=0$ and $i\varepsilon$ at $b=1$. With $b=[v\cdot x]$, $\mathrm{diag}_x\big(i^{\varepsilon_v[v\cdot x]}\big)=\frac{1+i\varepsilon_v}{2}I+\frac{1-i\varepsilon_v}{2}\,\mathrm{diag}_x\big((-1)^{[v\cdot x]}\big)$. The matrix $H\,\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)\,H$ is the bit flip $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, and <cite data-source="paper">tensored over the coordinates in supp(v)</cite> it turns the sign $(-1)^{[v\cdot x]}$, a product of one-coordinate signs over $\mathrm{supp}(v)$, into $X_v$. So conjugating by $H^{\otimes m}$ gives $\frac{1+i\varepsilon_v}{2}I+\frac{1-i\varepsilon_v}{2}X_v$. For $\varepsilon_v=1$ this is $\Theta_v$, and for $\varepsilon_v=-1$ it is $\tau_1 I+\tau_0X_v=\Theta_v^{-1}$. Thus $H^{\otimes m}\,\mathrm{diag}_x\big(i^{\varepsilon_v[v\cdot x]}\big)\,H^{\otimes m}=\Theta_v^{\varepsilon_v}$.

Since $(H^{\otimes m})^2=I$ and diagonal operators multiply pointwise, $C_VC_U^{-1}=H^{\otimes m}\,\mathrm{diag}_x\big(i^{q_V(x)-q_U(x)}\big)\,H^{\otimes m}$. Therefore $C_VC_U^{-1}=\prod_{v\in\mathcal O}\Theta_v^{\varepsilon_v}$: the edge change is one two-point kernel per basis vector, so it costs $\dim\Delta$ kernels. The intermediate labels are chosen <cite data-source="paper">to make the changes of frame inexpensive</cite>.

For $m=1$, take the labels $\{0\}$ and $\mathbb F_2$. Here $C_{\{0\}}=I$, since $q_{\{0\}}=0$, and $P_{\mathbb F_2}$ is the identity, so $C_{\mathbb F_2}=H\,\mathrm{diag}(1,i)\,H=\tau_0I+\tau_1X_1$, the matrix $\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1+i&1-i\\1-i&1+i\end{pmatrix}$. The edge is increasing with $\Delta=\mathbb F_2$, $\mathcal O=\{1\}$, and $\mathrm{wt}(1)=1$ gives $\varepsilon_1=1$, so the formula gives the same kernel $\Theta_1=C_{\mathbb F_2}$.

For general $m$, take the route $U_0=\{0\}$ and $U_\delta=\mathrm{span}(e_1,\dots,e_\delta)$ for $1\le\delta\le m$, where $e_1,\dots,e_m$ is the standard basis and $e_\delta$ has a single $1$ at position $\delta$. Each $U_\delta$ is nondegenerate, since the Gram matrix of $e_1,\dots,e_\delta$ is the identity. The edge from $U_{\delta-1}$ to $U_\delta$ is increasing with $\Delta=\mathrm{span}(e_\delta)$, $\mathcal O=\{e_\delta\}$ and $\varepsilon_{e_\delta}=1$. So its exponent difference is $[e_\delta\cdot x]=x_\delta$, the $\delta$-th coordinate of $x$. The intermediate frames cancel in the product, so $(C_{U_1}C_{U_0}^{-1})\cdots(C_{U_m}C_{U_{m-1}}^{-1})=C_{U_m}C_{U_0}^{-1}$. The exponents telescope to $q_{\mathbb F_2^m}(x)-q_{\{0\}}(x)\equiv\sum_\delta x_\delta=\mathrm{wt}(x)\pmod 4$, since $P_{\mathbb F_2^m}$ is the identity. Hence $C_{U_m}C_{U_0}^{-1}=H^{\otimes m}\,\mathrm{diag}_x\big(i^{\mathrm{wt}(x)}\big)\,H^{\otimes m}$. The diagonal entry at $x$ is a product of single-coordinate factors $i^{x_\delta}$, so $\mathrm{diag}_x(i^{\mathrm{wt}(x)})$ is the $m$-fold tensor power of $\mathrm{diag}(1,i)$. Writing $C=C_{\mathbb F_2}$, the mixed-product rule gives $C_{U_m}C_{U_0}^{-1}=C^{\otimes m}$. This is the frame that the paper's telescoping gives: <cite data-source="paper">phase frames gives C ⊗m on every role</cite>.
