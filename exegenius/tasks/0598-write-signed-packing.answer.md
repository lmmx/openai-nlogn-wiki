Signed coefficient packing turns a product of two integer polynomials, some of whose coefficients are negative, into one integer product. It is the step that lets [convolution over the polynomial ring](polynomial-ring-convolution.md) compute its packed products with a single integer multiplication.

<cite data-source="paper">Signed coefficient packing is a form of Kronecker substitution</cite>. In Kronecker substitution, the coefficients of a polynomial are written as fixed-width blocks of bits in one integer, and the polynomial is evaluated at the base of that integer.

Signed coefficient packing is based on the [polynomial ring](polynomial-ring.md): $f$ and $g$ are integer polynomials, and their product is taken there. Write $f = \sum_{j=0}^{r-1} f_j y^j$ and $g = \sum_{j=0}^{r-1} g_j y^j$, where $f_j$ and $g_j$ are the integer coefficients of $y^j$ in $f$ and $g$, for $j$ from $0$ to $r-1$. Let $p$ be the number of bits in a bound on the coefficient magnitudes, so that $|f_j| \le 2^p$ and $|g_j| \le 2^p$ for all $j$. Here $p$ is not the working precision of earlier pages, and the letters $f$ and $g$ mean what this page states, not their earlier meanings.

Packing fixes the block base $B = 2^{4p}$ and replaces $y$ by $B$. Write $f(B) = \sum_{j=0}^{r-1} f_j B^j$, so block $j$ holds the coefficient $f_j$. <cite data-source="paper">Every block has 4p bits</cite>, one block per coefficient. A negative $f_j$ needs no separate sign: $f(B)$ is simply a signed integer.

Substituting $B$ for $y$ respects products. The ordinary product of $f$ and $g$ is a polynomial $h = fg$ with at most $2r-1$ coefficients, where $h_j$ is the integer coefficient of $y^j$ in $h$, for $j$ from $0$ to $2r-2$. Then $f(B)\,g(B) = h(B)$, so one integer multiplication computes the whole product.

Reading the blocks back needs every coefficient to satisfy $|h_j| < B/2$. Each $h_j$ is a sum of at most $r$ products $f_i g_{j-i}$, with $i$ from $0$ to $r-1$ and $j-i$ also in that range. Each product has magnitude at most $2^{2p}$, so $|h_j| \le r\,2^{2p}$. If $r \le 2^p$ and $p \ge 2$, then $|h_j| \le 2^{3p}$, and $2^{3p} < 2^{4p-1} = B/2$ because $3p < 4p-1$ exactly when $p > 1$. So $|h_j| < B/2$ for every $j$.

Signed coefficient packing recovers its coefficients from centered residues under [congruence modulo $B$](congruence-modulo.md). Let $\sigma$ be the unique integer in $[-B/2, B/2)$ with $f(B)\,g(B) \equiv \sigma \pmod B$, the centered residue of $f(B)\,g(B)$ modulo $B$. Since $h(B) = h_0 + B\sum_{j\ge 1} h_j B^{j-1}$ and $|h_0| < B/2$, the residue $\sigma$ equals $h_0$. Subtract $\sigma$, divide by $B$, and repeat to read $h_1$, then $h_2$, and so on. The paper calls the same block base $B_0$ and reads each coefficient the same way: <cite data-source="paper">next coefficient by its centered residue modulo B0</cite>. Negative coefficients are recovered this way, with no extra sign bit.

As a small example, take $B = 100$ in place of $2^{4p}$, with $f = 3 - 2y$ and $g = 1 + 4y$. Then $f(100) = -197$ and $g(100) = 401$, so $f(100)\,g(100) = -78997$. The product is $h = 3 + 10y - 8y^2$, and $h(100) = 3 + 1000 - 80000 = -78997$. The centered residue of $-78997$ modulo $100$ is $3$; subtracting it and dividing by $100$ gives $-790$. The centered residue of $-790$ is $10$; subtracting it and dividing by $100$ gives $-8$. The coefficients $3$, $10$, $-8$ are recovered, the negative one with no special treatment.

Signed coefficient packing computes its integer products with the [Established O(n log n) multiplier](established-multiplier.md), used as an ordinary subroutine. The paper uses the same subroutine: <cite data-source="paper">To multiply the polynomial records, we use the fixed-tape integer multiplier of Harvey and van</cite> der Hoeven. The packing does not depend on how that multiplier works inside.
