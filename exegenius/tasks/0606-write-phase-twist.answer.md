A phase twist acts on the last [cyclic coordinate](cyclic-coordinate.md), one axis of a multidimensional cyclic convolution, by multiplying each coefficient by a root of unity. It is the step of [Integer multiplication below n log n](main-theorem.md) that lets the remaining axes be convolved over the [polynomial ring modulo $y^r + 1$](polynomial-ring-mod-yr-plus-1.md).

The twist is applied to the convolution from [Bluestein's chirp identity](bluestein-chirp-identity.md), which rewrites the [middle transform](middle-transform.md), the transform on the target lengths between the two Gaussian resampling maps, as a normalized cyclic convolution. The twist acts on the convolution form of the [middle transform](middle-transform.md), not on the transform itself.

The twist turns the last cyclic coordinate into a coefficient list of the [polynomial ring modulo $y^r + 1$](polynomial-ring-mod-yr-plus-1.md). The paper states the effect in these words: <cite data-source="paper">A phase twist changes the last cyclic coordinate into a polynomial</cite> modulo $y^r + 1$. It adds that <cite data-source="paper">its negative wrap gives precisely the original cyclic wrap after the twist is removed.</cite>

A record is a list of $r$ complex coefficients, where $r$ is the record length. Write $\zeta = e^{\pi i/r}$ for a root of unity of order $2r$, where $e$ is Euler's number, the base of the natural exponential. Since $e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta$ equals $1$ exactly when $\theta$ is a whole multiple of $2\pi$, $\zeta^k = e^{\pi i k/r}$ equals $1$ exactly when $k$ is a multiple of $2r$. So $\zeta^{2r} = 1$, no smaller positive power of $\zeta$ equals $1$, and $\zeta^r = e^{\pi i} = -1$.

Here $j$ is the position of a coefficient in a record, counted from $0$; on this page $j$ changes meaning and is not the convolution's input index. The phase twist multiplies the coefficient in position $j$ by $\zeta^j$, which the paper describes as <cite data-source="paper">multiplying coefficient k by the nonzero number</cite> $\zeta^k$. The inverse, called the untwist, multiplies it by $\zeta^{-j}$.

Now the wrap. Let $j_1$ and $j_2$ be the positions of the two coefficients being multiplied, counted from $0$. Their product carries the factor $\zeta^{j_1}\zeta^{j_2} = \zeta^{j_1+j_2}$. If $j_1+j_2 < r$, the term stays at position $j_1+j_2$, and untwisting it gives $\zeta^{j_1+j_2}\zeta^{-(j_1+j_2)} = 1$, so it is unchanged. If $j_1+j_2 \ge r$, a plain cyclic convolution of length $r$ sends the term to position $j_1+j_2-r$ with a plus sign. The polynomial ring instead has $y^{j_1+j_2} = y^r\,y^{j_1+j_2-r} = -y^{j_1+j_2-r}$, because $y^r = -1$, so this wrap carries a minus sign. Untwisting the output at position $j_1+j_2-r$ multiplies it by $\zeta^{-(j_1+j_2-r)}$, so the total factor is $\zeta^{j_1+j_2}\zeta^{-(j_1+j_2-r)} = \zeta^r = -1$. The twist therefore makes the wrap-around of the cyclic convolution match the negative wrap of the polynomial ring. <cite data-source="paper">Untwisting therefore recovers the cyclic coefficient with the correct sign.</cite>

Take $r = 2$. Then $\zeta = i$ and $\zeta^{-1} = -i$. The records $(1, 2)$ and $(3, 4)$ stand for $1 + 2y$ and $3 + 4y$ in $\mathbb{C}[y]/(y^2+1)$. Their twists are $(1, 2i)$ and $(3, 4i)$. The length-$2$ cyclic convolution of the twisted records has position $0$ equal to $1 \cdot 3 + (2i)(4i) = -5$ and position $1$ equal to $1 \cdot 4i + 2i \cdot 3 = 10i$. The untwist multiplies position $1$ by $-i$, giving $10$, so the result is $(-5, 10)$. Computing in the ring directly, $(1+2y)(3+4y) = 3 + 10y + 8y^2 = -5 + 10y$, the same record.
