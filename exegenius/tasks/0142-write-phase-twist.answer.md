A phase twist multiplies each coefficient of a record by a root of unity. It acts on the last [cyclic coordinate](cyclic-coordinate.md) of a convolution, changing that coordinate so that its wrap-around carries the sign of a polynomial ring. It is the step of [Integer multiplication below n log n](main-theorem.md) that lets the remaining convolution be computed over that ring.

A record is a list of $r$ complex coefficients, where $r$ is the record length. The phase twist is applied after [Bluestein's chirp identity](bluestein-chirp-identity.md) has rewritten the middle transform as a cyclic convolution. The source states the twist's effect in one sentence: <cite data-source="paper">A phase twist changes the last cyclic coordinate into a polynomial</cite> modulo $y^r + 1$, and <cite data-source="paper">its negative wrap gives precisely the original cyclic wrap after the twist is removed.</cite>

Write $\zeta = e^{\pi i/r}$ for a root of unity of order $2r$: $\zeta^{2r} = 1$, no smaller positive power of $\zeta$ equals $1$, and $\zeta^r = -1$. Here $j$ is the position of a coefficient in the record, counted from $0$. The phase twist multiplies the coefficient in position $j$ by $\zeta^j$, for every $j$ from $0$ to $r-1$. The inverse twist, called the untwist, multiplies it by $\zeta^{-j}$ instead.

The reason for the twist is that a plain cyclic convolution wraps a term back with a plus sign, while the polynomial ring wraps it with a minus sign. The twist supplies the missing sign. Let $k$ denote the position of the coefficient from the first record, and $l$ the position of the coefficient from the second. Their product carries the twist factor $\zeta^k \zeta^l = \zeta^{k+l}$. A cyclic convolution of length $r$ sends a term with $k+l \ge r$ back to position $k+l-r$ with a plus sign. In $\mathbb{C}[y]/(y^r+1)$ the same term is $y^{k+l} = -y^{k+l-r}$, because $y^r = -1$, so it arrives with a minus sign. The untwist at position $k+l-r$ multiplies the wrapped term by $\zeta^{-(k+l-r)}$, so the total factor is $\zeta^{k+l}\zeta^{-(k+l-r)} = \zeta^r = -1$. That is the minus sign of the ring, so the wrap is negative. A term with $k+l < r$ gets the factor $\zeta^{k+l}\zeta^{-(k+l)} = 1$ and is unchanged. <cite data-source="paper">Untwisting therefore recovers the cyclic coefficient with the correct sign.</cite>

The phase twist turns the last cyclic coordinate into a coefficient list of the [polynomial ring modulo $y^r + 1$](polynomial-ring-mod-yr-plus-1.md).

Take $r = 2$. Then $\zeta = e^{\pi i/2} = i$ and $\zeta^{-1} = -i$. Consider the records $(1, 2)$ and $(3, 4)$, which stand for $1 + 2y$ and $3 + 4y$ in $\mathbb{C}[y]/(y^2+1)$. Their twists are $(1, 2i)$ and $(3, 4i)$. The cyclic convolution of length $2$ of the twisted records has position $0$ equal to $1 \cdot 3 + (2i)(4i) = -5$ and position $1$ equal to $1 \cdot 4i + 2i \cdot 3 = 10i$. The untwist multiplies position $1$ by $-i$, giving $10$, so the result is $(-5, 10)$. Computing directly in the ring, $(1+2y)(3+4y) = 3 + 10y + 8y^2 = -5 + 10y$, the same record.
