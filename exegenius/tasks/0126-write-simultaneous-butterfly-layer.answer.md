A simultaneous butterfly layer applies the two-point map of a [butterfly](butterfly.md) to several selected address bits at once, and computes the result exactly in a single pass over the stored records. It is the step that lets a transform round apply its butterflies on all participating axes together; the transform layout and packed selected-bit addition pages build on it.

Let $\mu$ and $\nu$ be the two values of a pair. Let $H_0(\mu,\nu) = ((\mu+\nu)/2, (\mu-\nu)/2)$ be the two-point map. As a matrix, $H_0 = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$.

Let $D$ be the number of axes on which the layer acts. The layer acts on these $D$ [transform axes](transform-axis.md), one per chunk, where a chunk is a run of consecutive address bits. Let $K$ be the number of bits in each chunk. Let $\rho$ be the offset of the selected bit within each chunk, counted from the least significant end, with $0 \le \rho < K$. The sources describe this step as <cite data-source="paper">a common butterfly layer on several axes</cite>.

The layer applies the length-two case of the [discrete Fourier transform](discrete-fourier-transform.md), $H_0$, to each of the $D$ selected bits independently. This is the [tensor product](tensor-product.md) of $H_0$ over those bits. It acts on each stored coefficient of each record and leaves every other address bit and field unchanged.

Take $D=2$, $K=1$ and $\rho=0$. The two selected bits are then the two bits of a 2-bit address, the first belonging to the first axis. Let the values $1,2,3,4$ sit at addresses $00,01,10,11$. The layer returns $2.5,-0.5,-1,0$ at those addresses. Each output is one quarter of a signed sum of all four inputs; for instance the value at $00$ is $(1+2+3+4)/4 = 2.5$, and the value at $10$ is $(1+2-3-4)/4 = -1$.

Applied one axis at a time, the same numbers come out. Pairing along the first bit, the values at $00$ and $10$ (holding $1$ and $3$) become $2$ and $-1$, and those at $01$ and $11$ (holding $2$ and $4$) become $3$ and $-1$. Pairing along the second bit then replaces the values at $00$ and $01$ by $2.5$ and $-0.5$, and those at $10$ and $11$ by $-1$ and $0$.

The sources give the reason for doing all axes together: <cite data-source="paper">separate butterfly factors would require D whole-array passes</cite>. A whole-array pass is one scan that reads and rewrites every stored record. Applying the one-axis factors in turn needs $D$ such scans. The sources instead compute the layer by one tape procedure, a fixed sequence of reads and writes on the machine's tapes, which handles all $D$ axes together.

The procedure rests on three facts. First, the layer expresses its phase frames through the [complex phase network](complex-phase-network.md), as products of two-point kernels $C$ on the selected bits. Here $I$ is the $2\times 2$ identity matrix. Here $H$ is the normalized Hadamard matrix $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$, and $C = H\,\mathrm{diag}(1,i)\,H = \frac12\begin{pmatrix}1+i&1-i\\1-i&1+i\end{pmatrix} = aI+bX$, so $a=(1+i)/2$ and $b=(1-i)/2$. Each of $a$ and $b$ is a Gaussian dyadic, with numerator $1\pm i$ over $2^1$. <cite data-source="paper">These identities use only Gaussian dyadic coefficients.</cite> The layer rests on [Gaussian dyadics](gaussian-dyadics.md) for this reason.

Second, the layer splits bits into smaller groups by [binary changes of basis](binary-change-of-basis.md). Its phase frames express the $C$ tensor product <cite data-source="paper">using smaller groups of selected bits</cite>. The layer moves its address chunks by [address-field interchange](address-interchange.md). <cite data-source="paper">We implement those changes by moving whole address chunks.</cite>

Third, the procedure evaluates the whole layer exactly and ends with one final [truncation](truncation.md) of its values. <cite data-source="paper">The procedure evaluates the entire layer exactly before this final truncation.</cite> The sources bound the intermediate dyadic values so that this single truncation is enough, and then <cite data-source="paper">convert the completed C layer to the normalized H0 layer</cite>.
