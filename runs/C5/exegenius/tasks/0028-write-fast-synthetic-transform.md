## This page

Concept: Synthetic transforms with retained frequency order
Gloss: Forward synthetic transforms keep the same frequency order through their pointwise product and the opposite transform consumes it, using fast butterfly layers and interchanges.

## This page in the argument

The step: Forward synthetic transforms keep the same frequency order through the pointwise product and the opposite transform consumes it, at the layer-based cost.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- One fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies n-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: Beat the $n\log n$ cost of transform-based multiplication by making the two tape operations (address rearrangement and butterfly layers) cost a power of their width less than a full scan, then plug them into the Harvey-van der Hoeven resampling reduction (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time](main-theorem.md))
- Synthetic transforms over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ are computed with shifts, rearrangements and butterflies at reduced cost.; how: Use powers of y as roots of unity and run the transform by layers, using the fast layers and interchanges (on [Synthetic polynomial transforms](synthetic-transforms.md))
