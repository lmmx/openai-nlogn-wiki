## Page to audit (`guard-width-bound`)

````markdown
# Guard-width bound

A guard-width bound is an explicit number of extra bits that bounds the intermediate coefficients of a [simultaneous butterfly layer](simultaneous-butterfly-layer.md) and makes them exact, so that one truncation at the end suffices.

Inside a layer, each value comes from elementary operations: complex addition and subtraction, halving, sign change, and multiplication by $i$. The *depth* of an intermediate coefficient is the largest number of these operations on a chain from an input coefficient to it, where a chain follows an operand into any result that uses it. Write $\ell$ for the depth of a value, the number of operations on its longest chain. Let $C_0$ and $C_1$ be constants fixed by the network construction, and let $\Delta = \lceil C_0 d^{C_1} \rceil$ be the guard count. The guard count bounds the depth of every intermediate coefficient (cited) The paper sets $C_1 = 20$ (cited).

The guard-width bound limits the denominators of the [Gaussian dyadics](gaussian-dyadics.md) that appear as intermediate coefficients. Inputs lie in $2^{-p}\mathbb{Z}[i]$ with modulus at most 1, where $p$ is the number of fractional bits in each input coefficient. Induction on depth gives a stronger statement: a value of depth $\ell$ lies in $2^{-(p+\ell)}\mathbb{Z}[i]$ and has modulus at most $2^{\ell}$. Addition or subtraction at most doubles the larger modulus of its operands. Halving adds one denominator bit and halves the modulus. Sign changes and multiplication by $i$ change neither bound.

Let $u$ and $v$ be two input coefficients, with $u = 1$ and $v = i$, both of depth 0. Then $u+v = 1+i$ has depth 1, and $(u+v)/2 = (1+i)/2$ has depth 2. Its modulus $1/\sqrt{2}$ is at most $2^2$, and it lies in $2^{-(p+2)}\mathbb{Z}[i]$, as the bound says.

The bound makes a fixed register width enough. Since every depth is at most $\Delta$, each intermediate coefficient has at most $p+\Delta$ fractional bits, and each real component has absolute value at most $2^{\Delta}$. That largest value, $2^{\Delta}$, takes $\Delta+1$ bits, and a sign bit makes $\Delta+2$. The paper reserves $\Delta+3$ integer and sign bits per real component, one more than this count. (cited) Halving shifts the numerator into reserved zero bits, so no bit is lost. The whole layer is evaluated exactly, with one truncation toward zero at the end (cited)
````
