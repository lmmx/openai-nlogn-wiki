## This page

Concept: Signed packing of polynomial products
Gloss: Kronecker substitution: evaluating ring polynomials at 2^{4p} to multiply them with the integer multiplier, then reading coefficients back by centered residues.

## This page in the argument

The step: Two disk-grid ring polynomials $f,g$ modulo $y^r+1$ are multiplied exactly and returned as $Q_p(fg/r)$ in $O(rp\log(rp))$ time, by evaluating at $B_0=2^{4p}$, multiplying four pairs of integers with the established multiplier, and reading coefficients back by centered residues.
How: Signed Kronecker substitution: evaluate each real or imaginary part polynomial at $B_0$, multiply the resulting integers, extract the product coefficients one block at a time using centered residues, then reduce modulo $y^r+1$ and truncate.
Why: A polynomial product becomes one integer product, which the established $O(N\log N)$ multiplier does on records of only $O(rp)$ bits, so no recursion into the new algorithm is needed. (the sources say so: "To multiply the polynomial records, we use the fixed-tape integer" (paper))

Its sub-steps, in order:

- Multiply the four pairs of absolute values with the established multiplier and restore the signs.
- Each product coefficient is below $B_0/2$ in magnitude, so after removing lower coefficients its centered residue modulo $B_0$ identifies it.
- Reduce modulo $y^r+1$ by subtracting the coefficient at $j+r$ from the one at $j$.
- Shift the magnitude right by $p+\ell$ bits to obtain the $p$-bit grid numerator of the normalized product.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, disproving the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture in that model.; how: Reduce the product to a cyclic convolution of radix digits evaluated by Fourier-type transforms, make the two costly ingredients of those transforms (address rearrangement and butterfly arithmetic) cost a power of the logarithm less than a full scan per level by two rank-saving tape procedures, then assemble the algorithm, prove exactness and total the cost. (on [Sub-n log n integer multiplication](sub-nlogn-multiplication.md))
- Normalized convolution $\mathcal M_r(f,g)=(f*g)/T$ of arrays of $M$ ring records over $\mathcal R_r=\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ has error below $\sqrt2\,M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$ and costs $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}+\log(rp)])$, using two forward synthetic transforms, pointwise ring products and the opposite transform.; how: Apply the convolution theorem in the ring: transform both operands, multiply pointwise with signed packing, and transform back, keeping the same frequency order throughout so that no permutation is executed. (on [Convolution from the retained transform layout](synthetic-convolution.md))
