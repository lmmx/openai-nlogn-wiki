Gaussian resampling is a reduction that replaces a Fourier transform of arbitrary length by transforms whose axis lengths are powers of two, which is what makes the transforms of the multiplier cheap. Gaussian resampling expresses each source [Discrete Fourier transform](discrete-fourier-transform.md) through a power-of-two tensor transform, meaning a transform over several axes at once in which each axis length is a power of two.

Here $q$ is the number of values in one source transform. A transform of length $q$ is not directly served by the fast radix-2 method, which needs power-of-two lengths, so the reduction moves the work to lengths that are powers of two. The move between the two sets of frequencies uses Gaussian weights, which are factors whose exponent is quadratic in the frequency index. The construction is the one of Harvey and van der Hoeven; this page states its effect and does not re-derive the identity behind it.

Gaussian resampling is applied to the [Radix-digit convolution](radix-digit-convolution.md) of the two integers' digit coefficients, so the Fourier transforms it replaces are those of that convolution.
