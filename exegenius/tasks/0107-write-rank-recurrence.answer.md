Recursive power saving is the recurrence that makes both tape procedures, [Fast address-chunk interchange](address-interchange.md) and the [Simultaneous butterfly layer](simultaneous-layer.md), cost the data volume times a power below one of their parameter.

A call has a parameter $f$, the number of digits per interchanged chunk or of selected axes in the layer, and a volume $V$, the number of stored data symbols. A call at parameter $mf$, with $m=10^6$ the label-space dimension, here also the number of size-$f$ slots in its parameter, deals its rows into $W$ streams of volume $V/W$, one per wire of a fixed [Linear exchange network with restored scratch wires](exchange-network.md) (bit-valued for interchange, complex for the layer). Each stream is stored in a frame that varies along its wire; only the endpoint frames survive, giving the required operation ([Frames on wire arrays](array-frames.md)). Changing frame across an edge (a wire segment between consecutive gates) takes one call at parameter $f$ on that stream per unit of rank of the change on the $m$ slots. So the call makes $s$ calls at parameter $f$, each on volume $V/W$, where $s$ is the total rank over all edges. With $F(f)$ the time per unit volume of a call at parameter $f$, these children cost $s(V/W)F(f)$; dividing by $V$, each factor $m$ in the parameter multiplies the recursive cost per unit volume by $s/W$, less than $m$ when $s<Wm$ <cite data-source="paper">This strict inequality is the source of the power saving.</cite>

For interchange, all other work at a node (splitting, gates, triangular shears, parking idle streams on a stack tape) takes $O(V)$, and one digit is interchanged in linear time, so

$$F(mf)\le\frac{s}{W}F(f)+O(1),\qquad F(1)=O(1).$$

For $f$ a power of $m$ (other widths are split into such pieces), unrolling through $\log_m f$ levels gives $F(f)=O\big(\sum_{j=0}^{\log_m f}(s/W)^j\big)$. If $s/W<m^\tau$ for a fixed $\tau<1$, this is $O(f^\tau)$: when $s/W\le1$ there are $1+\log_m f$ terms of size at most $1$; otherwise the geometric sum is dominated by its last term, below $m^{\tau\log_m f}=f^\tau$. So an interchange costs $O(Vf^\tau)$ <cite data-source="paper">The chunks can be interchanged in $O(Ve^\tau)$ time</cite>; with $s=Wm$ it would cost $O(Vf)$.

For the layer the node overhead is not constant. Its frame changes are row additions on slots of $(f/m)K$ address bits, $K$ being the number of bits per address chunk, and [Packed selected-bit addition](packed-bit-addition.md) performs each in $O(V((fK)^\tau+1))$ time by moving whole chunks with the interchange above. Recursion stops below $d^\beta$ selected axes, with $\beta=1/2$, where the $f$ two-point kernels are applied one at a time:

$$F(f)\le\frac{s}{W}F(f/m)+O((fK)^\tau+1),\qquad F(f)=O(f)\quad(f<d^\beta).$$

Fix $\sigma<1$ with $s/W\le m^\sigma$, and $\lambda<1$ above $\sigma$ and $\tau(1+c/\beta)$, where $K\le d^c$ for a small fixed exponent $c$. At an internal node $f\ge d^\beta$, so $K\le f^{c/\beta}$ and the overhead is $O(f^\lambda)$. At depth $j$ (here $j$ counts levels below the call) there are $(s/W)^j$ normalized overheads of $O((f/m^j)^\lambda)$, a geometric sum of ratio $(s/W)/m^\lambda<1$, hence $O(f^\lambda)$. Leaves at depth $j$ contribute $(s/W)^jf/m^j\le f^\sigma(f/m^j)^{1-\sigma}<f^\sigma d^{\beta(1-\sigma)}$. With $f\le d$, and a factor $\log d$ for splitting a general axis count into powers of $m$, the layer costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ for any fixed $\lambda'$ between $\max\{\lambda,\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)\}$ and $1$ <cite data-source="paper">\max\{\lambda,\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)\}<\lambda'<1.</cite>

The recursion gets its strict inequality $s<Wm$ from [Nested subspace labels and rank deficit](subspace-labels.md): an edge's rank is the dimension change between the nested subspaces labelling its ends, so a wire climbing from $0$ to all $m$ dimensions costs $m$. The $N$ wires of one data bank start on a line and the $N$ of the other stop one dimension short, saving $2N$; the bit network spends $N$ of it on one extra rank per source, and the shrinking edges cost back only $2L<N$.

Write $s/W=m(1-\eta)$, where $\eta=(Wm-s)/(Wm)$ is the relative deficit (a new meaning of $\eta$ here), equal to $339/22587335000000$ for the bit network and $73/19906842167500$ for the complex one <cite data-source="paper">=\frac{339}{22587335000000}</cite>. Both, near $1.5\cdot10^{-11}$ and $3.7\cdot10^{-12}$, exceed $20\cdot2^{-50}\approx1.8\cdot10^{-14}$ <cite data-source="paper">Both exact deficits displayed above are greater than $20\cdot2^{-50}$.</cite> Since $m<2^{20}$, $\ln m<20\ln 2<20$; as the exponential lies above its tangent line at $0$, $m^{1-2^{-50}}=m\exp(-2^{-50}\ln m)>m(1-2^{-50}\ln m)>m(1-20\cdot2^{-50})>m(1-\eta)=s/W$ for either network. Hence $\tau=\sigma=1-2^{-50}$ satisfies $s/W<m^\tau$ for the bit network and $s/W<m^\sigma$ for the complex one: both recurrences save a power, barely.
