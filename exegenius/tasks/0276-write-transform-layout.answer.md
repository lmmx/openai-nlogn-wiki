The tape layout arranges the address bits of a [synthetic polynomial transform](synthetic-transform.md) so that every round applies a single [simultaneous butterfly layer](simultaneous-layer.md) instead of one pass per axis, which brings the transform below $\log t$ passes.

Axis $i$ has length $t_i\in\{2^{\ell-1},2^\ell\}$, where $\ell=\log_2 r$ is the number of address bits of a full-width axis (not a depth). Call an axis long if it has $\ell$ address bits and short if $\ell-1$. Let $D=d-1$ be the number of transformed axes (a new meaning of $D$), and $\boldsymbol t=(t_1,\dots,t_D)$. Then $F_{\boldsymbol t}^-$ is the forward synthetic transform along all $D$ axes and $F_{\boldsymbol t}^+$ its opposite. $K$ is the chunk width in bits (not a tensor line or segment spacing), and $\tau,\lambda'<1$ are the exponents of chunk interchange and of the layer. Records are [fixed-point Gaussian dyadic records](fixed-point.md) with $\Theta(p)$ bits per coefficient and $d\ell=O(p)$, so a pass over the $T$ coefficients costs $O(Tp)$. The layout keeps the bit-reversed output order of [retained frequency order](frequency-order.md). Write $B$ for bit reversal along every axis (not a row length), so the exact transforms are $BF_{\boldsymbol t}^-$ and $F_{\boldsymbol t}^+B^{-1}$.

The layer acts on one bit position of every participating axis at once only if the selected bits of the participating axes sit at a common offset in consecutive $K$-bit chunks <cite data-source="paper">we must place the selected bits of the participating axes at a common</cite>.

Put $b=(\ell-1)\bmod K$, the bits of an axis left over after whole chunks (not a lowest bit), and $q=\lfloor(\ell-1)/K\rfloor$, the number of whole chunks per axis (not a sequence length), so $\ell-1=qK+b$. Move to a prefix, one slot at a time, the top bit of each long axis and the $b$ lowest bits of every axis. At most $D(b+1)=O(dK)$ slots move so the moves cost $O(TpdK)$ <cite data-source="paper">listed slots, so the moves cost $O(TpdK)$.</cite> The rest of each axis is $q$ consecutive $K$-bit chunks.

Next reorder the chunks so that the first chunks of all axes are adjacent, then the second chunks, and so on. Scan the target positions left to right, exchanging the chunk there with the required one if they differ: at most $Dq-1$ exchanges. The layout exchanges chunks by the [fast address-chunk interchange](address-interchange.md), at cost $O(TpK^\tau)$ each, even for nonadjacent chunks <cite data-source="paper">exchanges of width-$K$ address chunks, even when the chunks are</cite>. Since $DqK\le d\ell=O(p)$, all exchanges cost $O(Tp\,DqK^\tau)=O(Tp\,(DqK)K^{\tau-1})=O(Tp\,pK^{\tau-1})$. The moves and exchanges define the record permutation $L$ (not a linear map or a dimension loss); undoing them in reverse order gives $L^{-1}$.

There are $\ell$ rounds: $b+1$ on prefix bits and $qK$ on chunk bits. In a chunk round the selected bit has the same offset in $D$ consecutive chunks, so one layer performs it, at cost $O(Tpd^{\lambda'})$ per round and $O(Tp\ell d^{\lambda'})$ in total <cite data-source="paper">$O(Tpd^{\lambda'})$ per round and $O(Tp\ell d^{\lambda'})$ in total.</cite> A prefix round processes its at most $D$ bits individually, each at $O(Tp)$, so $O(Tpd)$. At most $b+1\le K$ such rounds give $O(TpdK)$ <cite data-source="paper">most $b+1\leq K$ rounds on prefix slots, giving cost $O(TpdK)$.</cite>

For the array $z$ entering a round (not a suffix), the layer returns $Q_p(\mathcal Hz)$ and the next operation is the signed coefficient permutation $J_E$. Truncation toward zero is odd and acts separately on each real and imaginary part, so $J_EQ_p(\mathcal Hz)=Q_p(J_E\mathcal Hz)$: each round truncates once, after the exact contraction. The contraction does not increase the norm of the difference between computed and exact arrays, and truncation moves each entry by less than $\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$; the permutations in $L$ are isometries and add no error. From zero error the $\ell$ rounds leave error below $\sqrt2\,\ell\,2^{-p}$ <cite data-source="paper">zero error, at most $\ell$ rounds therefore</cite>.

Arithmetic on named slots after $L$ is conjugated by $L$. The forward procedure moves records by $L$ first, so its exact operator is $(LBF_{\boldsymbol t}^-L^{-1})L=LBF_{\boldsymbol t}^-$. The opposite procedure restores by $L^{-1}$ after its arithmetic, giving $L^{-1}(LF_{\boldsymbol t}^+B^{-1}L^{-1})=F_{\boldsymbol t}^+B^{-1}L^{-1}$. Both cost
