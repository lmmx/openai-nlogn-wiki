An integer is a whole number, positive, negative or zero. The target result multiplies integers, and its argument first rewrites each integer as a list of digits so that the multiplication can be carried out on short coefficients.

In a base (also called a radix), an integer is a list of digits, each between $0$ and one less than the base, with the leftmost digit most significant. In base 4, the integer 13 is the digit list $3, 1$, because $13 = 3 \cdot 4 + 1$; in base 2 the same integer is $1101$. The paper reads a binary string as an integer in this way: <cite data-source="paper">let val(x) be its nonnegative integer value</cite>, with the leftmost bit most significant.

A digit list also determines a polynomial. Read in increasing position order, the digits $(1, 3)$ of 13 are the coefficients of the polynomial $1 + 3 \cdot t$, and evaluating it at the base gives $1 + 3 \cdot 4 = 13$. Here the convolution of two coefficient lists is the coefficient list of the product of the two polynomials. Exact multiplication of two integers reduces to a [convolution of short integer coefficients](radix-digit-convolution.md), obtained by splitting each input into radix digits. This is the step the paper states as: <cite data-source="paper">Splitting the inputs into radix digits reduces multiplication to convolution of short integer coefficients.</cite>

For example, take $13 \cdot 11$. The digit lists are $(1, 3)$ for 13 and $(3, 2)$ for 11, since $11 = 2 \cdot 4 + 3$. The product of the polynomials $1 + 3t$ and $3 + 2t$ is $3 + 11t + 6t^2$, so the convolution is the coefficient list $(3, 11, 6)$. Evaluating at the base gives $3 + 11 \cdot 4 + 6 \cdot 16 = 143 = 13 \cdot 11$, but $(3, 11, 6)$ is not yet a digit list, because $11$ is larger than the base.

A carry turns it into one. A carry is the part of a position's value that exceeds the base; it is added to the next position. Write $11 = 2 \cdot 4 + 3$, keep the digit $3$, and pass $2$ to the next position, where $6 + 2 = 8 = 2 \cdot 4 + 0$. Keep the digit $0$ and pass $2$ on again, giving the position value $2$. The digits, most significant first, are $2, 0, 3, 3$, which is $2 \cdot 64 + 0 \cdot 16 + 3 \cdot 4 + 3 = 143$ in base 4. The integer coefficient list is converted to the exact binary product by [carry propagation](carry-propagation.md) across the radix digits.
