The simultaneous butterfly layer is the second tape primitive of the multiplier. It applies $H_0=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$ coefficientwise, to records of $r$ complex $p$-bit coefficients, on one bit of each of $D\le d$ address chunks of $K$ bits (selected at a common offset), then truncates once with $Q_p$. Its time is $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ for a fixed $\lambda'<1$, where $V$ is the array volume, whereas separate butterfly factors would need $D$ whole-array passes. Three letters change meaning from earlier pages. Here $D$ is the number of selected address chunks, not the second chunk value of [Fast address-chunk interchange](address-interchange.md) nor the source group of [Matrix shear by triangular factorization](matrix-shear.md). Here $e$ is a number of selected axes, not a chunk's digit count. Here $z$ is an array, not an address suffix or a binary vector.

## Reduction to the kernel

Let $a=\frac{1+i}2$, $b=\frac{1-i}2$, $X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $\mathsf S=\operatorname{diag}(1,i)$. The [Two-point kernel C](phase-kernel.md) is $C=aI+bX$. Since $ib=a$ and $ab=\frac12$, $\mathsf SC\mathsf S=a\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$, so $H_0=b\,\mathsf SC\mathsf S$. Also $a^2=\frac i2=-b^2$, so $C^2=(a^2+b^2)I+2abX=X$ and $C^4=I$ <cite data-source="paper">H_0=b\mathsf SC\mathsf S,\qquad C^2=X,\qquad C^4=I,\qquad</cite>.

Let $x_1,\dots,x_D$ be the selected bits of an address $x$, and let $\mathcal S_D$ multiply the value at $x$ by $i^{x_1+\cdots+x_D}$: $(\mathcal S_Dz)(x)=i^{x_1+\cdots+x_D}z(x)$. Applying the identity on every selected bit gives
$$H_0^{\otimes D}=b^D\mathcal S_DC^{\otimes D}\mathcal S_D.$$
Since $b^2=-\frac i2$, $b^D=(-i/2)^{\lfloor D/2\rfloor}b^{D\bmod2}$ is a shift, a fourth-root phase and at most one factor $b$. All of these are exact Gaussian dyadic operations, so only $C^{\otimes D}$ remains.

## A group of axes by the network

A group of $e=mf$ selected axes is split into $m$ slots of $f$ axes; one bit from each slot forms a column in $\mathbb F_2^m$. The layer is based on the finite network of [Binary phase frames](phase-frames.md), with $W$ wires and $s$ smaller calls (its $W_{\rm c}$ and $s_{\rm c}$). Each edge changes the frame by $\prod_\xi C_{v_\xi}^{\pm1}$, where the $v_\xi$ form an orthonormal residual basis, $C_v=aI+bX_v$, and $X_v$ translates a column by $v$.

The $v_\xi$ are arbitrary, so let $\Gamma$ be an invertible $\mathbb F_2$-matrix whose first columns are the $v_\xi$, and $\mathcal P_\Gamma$ the address permutation moving the value at $x$ to $\Gamma x$. Then $\mathcal P_\Gamma X_v\mathcal P_\Gamma^{-1}=X_{\Gamma v}$ <cite data-source="paper">\mathcal P_MX_v\mathcal P_M^{-1}=X_{Mv}.</cite>, using only that $\Gamma$ is invertible. The edge change therefore equals $\mathcal P_\Gamma\bigl(\prod_\xi C_{\mathbf 1_\xi}^{\pm1}\bigr)\mathcal P_\Gamma^{-1}$, with $\mathbf 1_\xi$ the $\xi$-th standard basis vector, which acts on one bit of each column. So apply $\mathcal P_\Gamma^{-1}$ to each column, then $(C^{\pm1})^{\otimes f}$ to the $f$ bits of slot $\xi$, then $\mathcal P_\Gamma$: one child on $f=e/m$ axes per residual vector. A child with exponent $-1$ costs no extra call, since $(C^{-1})^{\otimes f}=(-i)^fZ^{\otimes f}C^{\otimes f}Z^{\otimes f}$ with $Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, whose wrappers are address signs and fourth-root phases. Summing the residual dimensions gives exactly $s$ children.

Gaussian elimination writes $\Gamma$ as at most $m(m-1)+3m$ row additions (a swap is three), each XORing the selected bits of one slot into another. The layer performs its row additions by [Packed selected-bit addition](packed-bit-addition.md), which XORs selected bits of two chunks, with a third chunk as restored scratch, using eight cyclic shifts of whole chunk ranges plus a repair of an explicitly bounded set of exceptional addresses. Since the number of additions is fixed, the complete change of basis and its inverse on $e$ axis chunks cost $O(V((eK)^\tau+1))$ <cite data-source="paper">O(V((eK)^\tau+1))</cite> for a fixed $\tau<1$ on a stream of volume $V$.

## The recursion

Rows go to the $W$ wire roles by row number modulo $W$, so each child works on a stream of volume exactly $V/W$. This must hold at every level, so the row count must stay divisible by $W$. Let $q_0=\lceil\log_2W\rceil\lceil\log_m(2d)\rceil$ be the number of leading chunks whose selected bits get $C$ individually, at $O(V)$ each, $O(V\log d)$ in all. Their $q_0K$ address bits form a row index of range at least $W^{\lceil\log_md\rceil}$; padding with zero rows to a multiple of that range at most doubles the volume, and at depth $j$ the row count is divisible by $W^{\lceil\log_md\rceil-j}$.

Let $F(e)$ be the time on $e$ axes divided by the stream volume, and $\beta=\frac12$. A node costs $O((eK)^\tau+1)$ per unit volume for basis changes, scalar gates and reassembly; its $s$ children cost $s(V/W)F(e/m)$; below $d^\beta$ axes the kernels are applied individually. Hence
$$F(e)\le\tfrac sWF(e/m)+O((eK)^\tau+1)\ (e\ge d^\beta),\qquad F(e)=O(e)\ (e<d^\beta).$$
The time bound follows from [Recursive power saving](rank-recurrence.md). Fix rational $\sigma<1$ with $s/W\le m^\sigma$, and $\lambda$ with $\max\{\tau,\sigma\}<\lambda<1$ and $\tau(1+c/\beta)<\lambda$, where $K\le d^c$. At an internal node $e\ge d^\beta$, so $K\le e^{c/\beta}$ and the overhead is $O(e^\lambda)$. As $s/W<m^\lambda$, the internal levels sum to $O(e^\lambda)\sum_{j\ge0}(s/(Wm^\lambda))^j=O(e^\lambda)$. The leaves, with fewer than $d^\beta$ chunks each, contribute $O(e^\sigma d^{\beta(1-\sigma)})$, because $(s/W)^j\le m^{j\sigma}$. Writing $D-q_0$ in base $m$ cuts the remaining chunks into at most $(m-1)(1+\lfloor\log_md\rfloor)$ pieces of $m^j$ chunks; summing them with the row preprocessing gives $O(\log(2d)[d^\lambda+d^{\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)}+1])=O(d^{\lambda'})$ for any fixed $\max\{\lambda,\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)\}<\lambda'<1$.

The saving is barely there. The paper takes $\tau=\sigma=1-2^{-50}$ and $\lambda'=1-2^{-54}$ <cite data-source="paper">\lambda'=1-2^{-54},</cite>, because $s/W$ is only just below $m$: each factor $m$ in $e$ multiplies the cost per volume by almost $m$. The gain is still a power of $d$, and $d=\Theta(p^\epsilon)$ is a power of $p$.

## Exactness

The whole layer is evaluated exactly in [Fixed-point Gaussian dyadic records](fixed-point.md), with one truncation at the end. Let $\chi(e)$ bound the arithmetic operations on any chain from an input coefficient to an intermediate one. One invocation of the network takes fewer than $\Xi=64(W+m+1)^3$ operations: its gates, child corrections and bank corrections total $24W^3+4s+4W+4$ <cite data-source="paper">\(24W^3+4s+4W+4\) is less than</cite>, using $s<Wm$. A leaf pair takes eight operations. The chain bound concatenates the $s$ children, so it uses $s$, not $s/W$:
$$\chi(e)\le s\chi(e/m)+\Xi,\qquad\chi(e)\le8e\ \text{at a leaf}.$$
With $\Psi=s+\Xi$, $sx+\Xi\le\Psi x$ for $x\ge1$, so each of the at most $\lceil\log_md\rceil$ internal levels multiplies the bound by at most $\Psi$, and $\chi(e)\le8d\Psi^{\lceil\log_md\rceil+1}$. With $\vartheta$ the least $j\ge1$ with $m^j\ge\Psi$, $\Psi^{\log_md+2}=\Psi^2d^{\log_m\Psi}\le\Psi^2d^\vartheta$, so $\chi(e)\le8\Psi^2d^{\vartheta+1}$. Concatenating the at most $md$ pieces adds at most $8m\Psi^2d^{\vartheta+2}$, and individual kernels at most $8d$. The whole layer's depth stays below $\Delta=\lceil C_0d^{C_1}\rceil$, with $C_0=128m\Psi^2$ and $C_1=\max\{20,\vartheta+3\}$, which is $20$ for the paper's parameters.

A value at depth $\ell$ lies in $2^{-(p+\ell)}\mathbb Z[i]$ and has modulus at most $2^\ell$: addition at most doubles the modulus, halving adds a denominator bit, and signs and multiplication by $i$ preserve both. So reserving $p+\Delta$ fractional bits and $\Delta+3$ integer and sign bits per real component represents every intermediate coefficient exactly <cite data-source="paper">represents every intermediate coefficient exactly</cite>. Since $d=\Theta(p^\epsilon)$ and $\epsilon C_1<1$, $\Delta=o(p)$, so the width is $O(p)$ and each coefficient operation takes $O(p)$ bit steps.

## The final truncation

Let $\mathcal H=H_0^{\otimes D}$. Each row of $H_0$ has absolute row sum $\frac12+\frac12=1$, and rows of a tensor product consist of products of entries, so every row of $\mathcal H$ has absolute row sum one <cite data-source="paper">absolute row sum one.</cite> Each output coefficient is thus a combination of input coefficients with weights of total absolute value one, and by the triangle inequality $\mathcal H$ maps disk arrays (all coefficients of modulus at most $1$) to disk arrays. Truncating the real and imaginary parts toward zero cannot increase either absolute value, so $Q_p$ keeps the disk, and it changes each part by less than $2^{-p}$. Hence $\|Q_p(\mathcal Hz)-\mathcal Hz\|_\infty<\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$ <cite data-source="paper">truncation cannot increase the absolute value of either component</cite>.
