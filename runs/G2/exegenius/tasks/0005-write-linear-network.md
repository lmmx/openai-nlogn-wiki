## This page

Concept: Linear network with restored scratch wires
Gloss: A fixed circuit of linear gates on wires that exchanges two banks of values with a sign while arbitrary initial values on auxiliary scratch wires are cancelled and restored.
Also called: motif network, restoration schedule

## This page in the argument

The step: A fixed circuit of linear gates on the wires, with arbitrary initial values on its scratch wires, sends the data banks $(X_a,Y_a)$ to $(-Y_a,X_a)$ and restores every scratch value.
How: Build one invocation that adds a control bank into a target bank while the unknown scratch values cancel, using a schedule that first subtracts the effect of the old scratch values, then adds the effect of the source values, then restores the scratch; compose three such invocations (add, subtract, add) in three stages to obtain the signed exchange.
Why: A schedule that first removes, then uses, then restores the scratch values makes the net map independent of what the scratch wires held. (the sources say so: "the following schedule remove the effect of the old scratch values;" (paper))
Striking feature: The net effect on the data banks does not depend on the initial values of the scratch wires, even though the scratch wires take part in the computation and are restored afterwards. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- One forward invocation changes the target value $y_S$ to $y_S+x_S$ and leaves every other value, scratch wires included, unchanged, whatever the initial scratch values.
- A forward invocation from $X$ to $Y$, the inverse invocation from $Y$ to $X$, and another forward invocation from $X$ to $Y$ send $(x,y)$ through $(x,y+x)$ and $(-y,y+x)$ to $(-y,x)$, restoring all scratch values.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, refuting the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture in this model.; how: Reduce the product to a convolution over a polynomial ring computed by Fourier-type transforms; supply the two fast tape procedures that remove the logarithmic factor from address movement and from butterfly arithmetic, both obtained from linear networks that save a power; then assemble the algorithm and tally every cost. (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time](main-theorem.md))
- There are two fixed linear networks of $W$ wires, one over bits and one over Gaussian dyadic numbers, each exchanging two banks of values with a sign while restoring every scratch wire, whose frames can be chosen so that the edge changes have total rank $s<W m$.; how: Construct the scalar exchange network, lift it to arrays with frames, then attach nested subspace labels to its gates and terminals and count the dimension changes, once for bits and once for phases.

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "A gate is a fixed linear map on a specified" (paper)

  > Put \[ h=100,\qquad v=\binom h3=161700,\qquad N=v^3=4227952113000000,\qquad m=h^3=1000000. \] Let $\mathcal T$ be the set of three-element subsets of $[h]$. There are two data banks $X_a,Y_a$, indexed by $a=(a_1,a_2,a_3)\in\mathcal T^3$. A wire carries one scalar. A gate is a fixed linear map on a specified set of wires; untouched wires retain their values. The vertices on a wire are its source, the gates that touch it in time order, and its sink; its edges join consecutive vertices. We call the name of a wire its \emph{role}, so that we can distinguish a fixed place in the network from the value currently carried there.

- "For arbitrary initial values of its auxiliary wires, a forward" (paper)

  > \begin{lemma}\label{lem:scalar-motifs} For arbitrary initial values of its auxiliary wires, a forward invocation changes $y_S$ to $y_S+x_S$ and leaves every other value unchanged. Performing a forward invocation from $X$ to $Y$ at stage 1, the inverse forward schedule from $Y$ to $X$ at stage 2, and a forward invocation from $X$ to $Y$ at stage 3 sends \[ (X_a,Y_a)\longmapsto(-Y_a,X_a) \] and restores every auxiliary value. In the bit construction this is the permutation exchanging $X_a$ and $Y_a$. \end{lemma}

- "Each procedure arises from its own fixed linear network that exchanges two" (paper)

  > Each procedure arises from its own fixed linear network that exchanges two banks of values, with prescribed signs, and restores every auxiliary value. A wire's fixed place in the network is called its role. To use the network on an array, regard a row as the complete ordered suffix, including all active address fields, at a fixed prefix and row index. Distribute rows cyclically among the $W$ roles, padding the row count for each prefix to a multiple of $W$. Let $V$ now denote the node volume after padding. All role streams have the same local address set and each contains $V/W$ bits. A scalar gate is then applied pointwise to matching addresses on the roles that it touches.
