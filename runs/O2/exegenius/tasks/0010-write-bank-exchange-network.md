## This page

Concept: Bank-exchange linear network
Gloss: A fixed linear network of wires (roles) and pointwise gates—copy, gather, scatter, side injection on a schedule of eight rows over three stages—that maps two data banks (X, Y) to (−Y, X) while restoring arbitrary initial scratch values.
Also called: finite network, role, scratch wires, transparent computation

## This page in the argument

The step: For arbitrary initial scratch values, a forward invocation at stage 1, an inverse invocation at stage 2 and a forward invocation at stage 3 send $(X_a,Y_a)\mapsto(-Y_a,X_a)$ and restore every auxiliary value.
How: run an eight-row schedule per invocation that first subtracts the effect of the old scratch values, then copies, gathers, scatters and injects to add $x$ to $y$, then restores the scratch; compose three such invocations
Why: Subtracting the scratch contribution before adding the source contribution, and undoing copy and gather afterwards, makes the net change independent of whatever the scratch wires held. (the sources say so: "The first two rows of
the following schedule remove the effect of the old scratch values;
the next four introduce the source values and add their effect to the
target; the last two restore the scratch values." (paper))
Striking feature: The exchange works for arbitrary, unknown initial scratch values, which all come back unchanged. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- Within one invocation the net change of the target bank is $(\mathcal J\mathcal V+\mathcal R\mathcal G)x$, independent of the initial side and central values.
- Gather and scatter through central wires give the coefficient $|S\cap T|\bmod 2$ (bit network) or $(|S\cap T|-1)/2$ (complex network) from $x_T$ to $y_S$, and side wires cancel the neighbor terms, so each invocation performs $y\gets y+x$. (on [Three-element subset intersection construction](triple-intersection-construction.md))
- The three stages take $(x,y)$ to $(x,y+x)$, then $(-y,y+x)$, then $(-y,x)$.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- One deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine computes the exact $2n$-bit product of two $n$-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: build two linear-network tape procedures, fast address interchange and simultaneous butterfly layers, from fixed networks with a strict rank saving; use them in synthetic transforms; place these in the Harvey–van der Hoeven reduction through radix digits, a CRT map, Gaussian resampling with retained permutations, Bluestein chirps and a phase twist; and bound every cost term (on [Integer multiplication in O(n (lg n)^(1−κ)) time](main-theorem.md))
- Two fixed linear networks, a bit network over $\mathbb F_2$ and a complex network over $\mathbb Z[i,1/2]$, exchange two data banks while restoring every scratch value, and the total number $s$ of smaller operations their frame changes need is strictly below $Wm$, with $s/W<m^{1-2^{-50}}$.; how: build a three-stage bank-exchange network on three-element subsets of a 100-element set, carry arrays through it in frames that commute with the gates, label vertices by nested nondegenerate subspaces whose residual dimensions count the smaller calls, and check both interfaces and the explicit deficits (on [Rank saving s < Wm](rank-saving.md))

**S2.1.1**: Within one invocation the net change of the target bank is $(\mathcal J\mathcal V+\mathcal R\mathcal G)x$, independent of the initial side and central values.
Source: "The net target change is
$(\mathcal J\mathcal V+\mathcal R\mathcal G)x$, independently of
$a,c$." (paper)
> \begin{proof} Write $\mathcal V$ for copy, $\mathcal G$ for gather, $\mathcal J$ for side injection and $\mathcal R$ for scatter. If the initial side and central vectors are $a,c$, the first two rows subtract $\mathcal J a+\mathcal R c$ from $y$. Rows 2 and 3 change them to $a+\mathcal Vx,c+\mathcal Gx$. Rows 4 and 5 therefore add $\mathcal R(c+\mathcal Gx)+\mathcal J(a+\mathcal Vx)$. Rows 6 and 7 restore $c,a$. The net target change is $(\mathcal J\mathcal V+\mathcal R\mathcal G)x$, independently of $a,c$.

banks of values, with prescribed signs, and restores every auxiliary
value." (paper)

  > Each procedure arises from its own fixed linear network that exchanges two banks of values, with prescribed signs, and restores every auxiliary value. A wire's fixed place in the network is called its role. To use the network on an array, regard a row as the complete ordered suffix, including all active address fields, at a fixed prefix and row index. Distribute rows cyclically among the $W$ roles, padding the row count for each prefix to a multiple of $W$. Let $V$ now denote the node volume after padding. All role streams have the same local address set and each contains $V/W$ bits. A scalar gate is then applied pointwise to matching addresses on the roles that it touches.
