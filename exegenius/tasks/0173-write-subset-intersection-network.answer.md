A subset-intersection network is a fixed linear network on the three-element subsets of a ground set. It is the exchange step that the [address-field interchange](address-interchange.md) applies recursively. It exchanges two banks of scalars while restoring every auxiliary wire, meaning every extra wire used as scratch space ends with the value it started with.

The network is based on [linear networks and frames](linear-network-frames.md), so its wires carry scalars and its gates combine them. Gates are the vertices that combine values, and terminals are the endpoints where the two banks attach. The network attaches [subspace labels](subspace-labels.md) to its gates and terminals. Here $m$ is the dimension of the fixed space from which the labels are drawn.

Take the ground set $\{1,2,3,4\}$. Two distinct three-element subsets of it always meet in exactly two elements. So a gate that depends only on intersection size treats every pair of distinct subsets alike, and it treats a subset paired with itself, which meets in three elements, differently.

Gather and scatter are the two operations that group values by intersection size. Gather combines the values of subsets that share an intersection size, and scatter spreads a value back over such subsets. The construction builds its coefficients this way. <cite data-source="paper">The construction uses three-element subsets: gather and scatter create coefficients depending on intersection size, and auxiliary wires cancel the unwanted coefficients.</cite> Coefficients of this kind, with the rest cancelled, give an exchange whose subspace ranks can be controlled.

Here $s$ is the total number of smaller calls made by one call. Here $t$ is the factor by which the stream volume shrinks, so each smaller call works on a stream of $1/t$ of the volume. The step needs $s<tm$, the inequality the rest of the argument uses.
