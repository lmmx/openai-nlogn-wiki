## This page in the argument

The step: The algorithm of digit extraction, address map to prime axes, two source transforms, truncated pointwise product, opposite-sign transform, rescaling by $S$ and $2^{2b+4}$, rounding, inverse address map and carries produces the exact $2n$-bit product.
How: Turn the product into a radix-digit convolution, place it on prime cyclic axes by a Chinese-remainder map, compute the convolution through the three source transforms, and prove that rounding recovers the integer coefficients.
Why: Integer multiplication is a convolution of digit lists, and the source transforms of the preceding sections compute convolutions on prime-length axes, so the digit list must be placed on such axes and the approximate result rounded. (the sources leave this for the reader to work out: spell it out)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- Fixed parameters $b=\lceil\log_2n\rceil$, $p=6b$, $d=\lfloor b^\epsilon\rfloor$, $K=\lfloor d^c\rfloor$, $T\approx n/b$, $r=2^{\lceil(\log_2T)/d\rceil}$, with axes of length $r/2$ or $r$ and distinct primes $s_i$ just below $t_i$ found by deterministic search. (on [Parameter choices and prime axis lengths](prime-axis-parameters.md))
- Splitting each input into radix-$2^b$ digits gives a digit polynomial product of degree below $S$, so convolution modulo $x^S-1$ has no wraparound and every coefficient is below $2^{3b}$. (on [Integer multiplication as radix-digit convolution](radix-digit-convolution.md))
- The map $x\mapsto\prod_i x_i^{\mu_i}$ is an algebra isomorphism from $\mathbb C[x]/(x^S-1)$ onto the prime-axis cyclic ring and is realized on tape by prefix-controlled cyclic shifts. (on [Chinese-remainder map to prime cyclic axes](crt-map.md))
- Multiplying coefficient $k$ by $\zeta^k$ with $\zeta=e^{\pi i/r}$ turns the last cyclic axis into a negacyclic polynomial ring, so the synthetic convolution computes the normalized complex convolution $(f*g)/T$. (on [Coefficient twist from cyclic to negacyclic](phase-twist.md))
- The source transform $RF_{\boldsymbol s}$ is computed as $2^\gamma\mathcal B_0 Q F_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A$ from Gaussian expansion, a chirp transform and Gaussian compression, with tracked errors. (on [Computed source transform](source-transform.md))
- The algorithm computes $\widetilde{\mathscr F}(u)$ and $\widetilde{\mathscr F}(v)$, multiplies pointwise and truncates, applies the opposite-sign transform, and scales by $S$ and $2^{2b+4}S$, where the retained source permutation cancels and the two scales $S$ undo different normalizations.
- The exact counterpart after the three transforms is $(u*v)/S^2$, so the first scale gives $(u*v)/S$ and the second gives $2^{2b+4}(u*v)$, the integer convolution.
- Each recovered convolution coefficient lies within $1/2$ of its true integer value, which is below $2^{3b}$, so rounding and carry propagation give the exact product. (on [Precision budget and exact recovery](exact-recovery.md))

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- One deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in time $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: Keep the transform-based multiplication of Harvey and van der Hoeven (Gaussian resampling, Bluestein chirp, synthetic polynomial transforms) but replace its two costly tape operations, address rearrangement and butterfly layers, by faster procedures obtained from rank-saving linear networks, then check exactness and total cost. Source: "The source-transform procedures now supply the three transforms needed" (paper)
> The source-transform procedures now supply the three transforms needed for the radix-digit convolution. Read each stored operand backward from its least significant end in groups of $b$ bits. This produces the radix-$2^b$ digits $a_0,\ldots,a_{q-1}$ in increasing coefficient order. Buffer each group on a fixed work tape and read it back most significant bit first, reversing its least-significant-first input order; pad the final short group with leading zeroes. For digit $a_j$, write the exact grid numerator $a_j2^{p-b-2}$ in a signed $w_{\rm c}$-bit real word, followed by a zero $w_{\rm c}$-bit imaginary word. This exponent is nonnegative because $p=6b$, and the represented value is exactly $a_j2^{-b-2}$. Append $S-q$ zero coefficient records. Buffering and ordering the groups costs $O(n)$, including the one partial group's padding, and writing the $S$ records costs $O(Sp)$. Together with the initial count and copies, this input conversion costs $O(n+Sp)=O(Tp)$; all $n$ input bits, including leading zeroes, are retained before the final group is padded.
