## This page

Concept: Cyclic convolution
Gloss: The product rule f*g on a cyclic group (or product of cyclic groups), equal to polynomial multiplication modulo x^S−1.

## Where the argument relies on this page

- Two forward synthetic transforms, a pointwise ring product by signed packing and the opposite transform give the normalized convolution $\mathcal M_r(f,g)=(f*g)/T$ over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ with error about $3\ell M2^{-p}$. (on [Convolution from the retained transform layout](synthetic-convolution.md))
- Perform both forward transforms, multiply pointwise, apply the opposite transform and rescale by $M$; the normalization $F^+F^-=I/M$ gives $\mathcal M_r$ and the error stays below $\sqrt2 M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$.
- The Gaussian-resampling identity, the Bluestein chirp and the cancellation of the source permutation express a convolution on prime axes through the power-of-two synthetic convolution without executing the coordinate permutations. (on [Retaining resampling permutations in the transform](retained-permutations.md))
- Bluestein's chirp identity writes the middle transform $QF_{\boldsymbol t}$, with its target permutation, as a phase multiplication, a cyclic convolution with the chirp array $a$, and another phase multiplication. (on [Bluestein chirp identity](bluestein-chirp.md))
- Because $F^+_{\boldsymbol s}R=R^{-1}F^+_{\boldsymbol s}$, the source permutation $R$ cancels across two forward transforms, a pointwise product and the opposite transform, leaving $(u*v)/S^2$.
- Writing both inputs in radix $2^b$ with $q=\lceil n/b\rceil$ digits, the product coefficients are a convolution modulo $x^S-1$ with capacity below $2^{3b}$ and no wraparound. (on [Multiplication as radix-digit convolution](digit-convolution.md))
- The map $x\mapsto\prod_ix_i^{\mu_i}$ is an isomorphism from cyclic convolution of length $S$ onto convolution on a product of prime cyclic axes, realized by triangular controlled shifts. (on [Chinese-remainder map to prime cyclic axes](crt-cyclic-axes.md))
- Multiplying coefficient $k$ by $\zeta^k$ with $\zeta=e^{\pi i/r}$ maps $\mathbb C[x]/(x^r-1)$ isomorphically onto $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$, so negative wrap becomes cyclic wrap. (on [Coefficient twist from cyclic to negacyclic](phase-twist.md))

## Pages that build on this one

- Multiplication as radix-digit convolution: Writing both inputs in radix 2^b with q=⌈n/b⌉ digits, the product coefficients are a convolution with capacity below 2^{3b}, and padding to length S avoids wraparound.
- Chinese-remainder map to prime cyclic axes: Using pairwise coprime lengths s_i, a length-S cyclic convolution becomes convolution on a product of cyclic axes through the map x↦∏x_i^{μ_i}, realized by triangular controlled shifts.
- Coefficient twist from cyclic to negacyclic: Multiplying coefficient k by ζ^k with ζ=e^{πi/r} maps C[x]/(x^r−1) isomorphically onto C[y]/(y^r+1) so negative wrap becomes cyclic wrap.
- Normalized discrete Fourier transform F_q: Length-q transform with entries averaged over roots of unity; it turns convolution into pointwise multiplication (F(u*v)=S·Fu·Fv) and has inverse F⁺ up to normalization.
- Bluestein chirp identity: A Fourier transform as a pointwise phase multiplication, a cyclic convolution with a chirp array, and another phase multiplication.

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "let $*$ denote cyclic convolution in the displayed" (paper)

  > For arrays on $\prod_i\mathbb Z/t_i\mathbb Z$ with values in $\mathcal R_r$, let $*$ denote cyclic convolution in the displayed axes and define \[ \mathcal M_r(f,g)=\frac{f*g}{rM}=\frac{f*g}{T}. \] For a fixed output coefficient, each of the $M$ first cyclic addresses determines one second address, and each of the $r$ first polynomial indices determines one second index modulo $r$. The negacyclic wrap supplies the sign. Thus that coefficient averages $rM$ signed scalar products, and $\mathcal M_r$ is a bilinear contraction: \[ \|\mathcal M_r(f,g)\|_\infty \leq\|f\|_\infty\|g\|_\infty. \] If the other operand is a disk array, replacing one operand changes the output by at most the norm distance between its two values.
