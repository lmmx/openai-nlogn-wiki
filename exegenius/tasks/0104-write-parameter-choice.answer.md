Parameter and prime-length choice fixes, for $n$-bit inputs, every exponent and size so that the hypotheses of the butterfly-layer and resampling lemmas hold at once.

The exponents are fixed rationals, so inequalities among them are integer comparisons. In the [simultaneous butterfly layer](simultaneous-layer.md), $\tau$ and $\sigma$ bound chunk-interchange cost and recursion growth, $\beta$ sets the threshold $d^\beta$ of active chunks handled singly, $\lambda$ is the per-node overhead exponent, $\lambda'$ the final cost exponent in $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ on bit volume $V$, $c$ and $\epsilon$ the chunk-width and axis-count exponents, $C_1$ the guard-bit exponent, and $\delta$ the resampling-cost exponent.

Take $\tau=\sigma=1-2^{-50}$, $\beta=1/2$ and $\delta=1/16$. Take $\lambda=1-2^{-52}$ and $c=2^{-56}$. Take $\lambda'=1-2^{-54}$. Take $C_1=20$, $\epsilon=2^{-75}$ and $\kappa=2^{-182}$. These are deliberately conservative<cite data-source="paper">These constants are deliberately conservative; no optimization is claimed.</cite>

The layer needs $\max\{\tau,\sigma\}<\lambda<1$, $\tau(1+c/\beta)<\lambda$, $\max\{\lambda,\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)\}<\lambda'<1$ and $\epsilon C_1<1$. The layer conditions hold with room to spare: $\tau(1+c/\beta)<1-31\cdot2^{-55}<\lambda$, since $\tau(1+c/\beta)=(1-2^{-50})(1+2^{-55})=1-31\cdot2^{-55}-2^{-105}$ and $\lambda=1-8\cdot2^{-55}$. Also $\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)=1-2^{-51}<\lambda<\lambda'<1$, and $\epsilon C_1=20\cdot2^{-75}<1$.

For large $n$ put $b=\lceil\log_2n\rceil$, $p=6b$, $d=\lfloor b^\epsilon\rfloor$. Put $K=\lfloor d^c\rfloor$, the address-chunk width in bits. So $p$ sets the precision of the [Fixed-point Gaussian dyadic records](fixed-point.md), and $d$ counts the axes. Take the power of two $T$ in $[4n/b,8n/b)$.

Let $r=2^{\lceil(\log_2T)/d\rceil}$ and $\ell=\log_2r$; choose $d\ell-\log_2T\in[0,d)$ of the axis lengths to be $r/2$ and the others $r$, with the last axis of length $r$. The count lies in $[0,d)$ as $\ell$ rounds $(\log_2T)/d$ up, so some axis has length $r$; the product is $r^d/2^{d\ell-\log_2T}=T$. Thus $t_i\in\{r/2,r\}$, $\prod_it_i=T$ and $t_d=r$.

The lemmas assume $a_dp^\epsilon\le d\le b_dp^\epsilon$, $2^{a_rp/d}\le r\le2^{b_rp/d}$ and $d\ell\le C_\ell p$, with constants $a_d,b_d,a_r,b_r,C_\ell$. Since $2^{b-1}<n\le2^b$, the range of $T$ gives $b/2\le\log_2T\le b$ for large $b$. With $b^\epsilon/2\le d\le b^\epsilon\le b$ this yields $\frac1{12}p^\epsilon\le d\le p^\epsilon$ and $\frac p{12d}\le\ell\le\frac p{3d}$. These choices give $Tp=\Theta(n)$, $d=\Theta(p^\epsilon)$, $K=\Theta(p^{\epsilon c})$, $\ell=\Theta(p^{1-\epsilon})$, $r=2^{\Theta(p^{1-\epsilon})}$, so the comparison constants $a_d=a_r=1/12$, $b_d=1$, $b_r=1/3$, $C_\ell=1$ can be fixed for all lengths at once.

Put $\alpha=\lceil(12d^2b)^{1/4}\rceil$. Put $\gamma=2d\alpha^2$ and $\eta=1/(4d)$. Here $\alpha$ sets the Gaussian width of [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md), $\gamma$ is the source scale exponent, and $\eta$ the relative gap of $s_i$ below $t_i$. As $\lceil x\rceil\le2x$ for real $x\ge1$ and $8\sqrt{12}<28$, then $\gamma<28d^2\sqrt b\le28b^{1/2+2\epsilon}\le28b^{2/3}$, so $2\le\alpha<\sqrt p$ eventually and $\gamma=o(b)$.

By the prime-interval lemma, for $0<\eta<1/4$ and $x>e^{2/\eta}$ the interval $(1-2\eta)x<q\le(1-\eta)x$ contains at least $\eta x/(2\log x)$ primes<cite data-source="paper">contains at least $\eta x/(2\log x)$ primes</cite>; both $x=r/2$ and $x=r$ qualify since $\log x>8d=2/\eta$, and the count $x/(8d\log x)$ exceeds $d$. Here $x$ is real and $q$ an integer; indeed $\log(r/2)/d=\Theta(p^{1-2\epsilon})\to\infty$, and $x>8d^2\log x$ because $\log(8d^2\log x)=O(\log p)=o(\log x)$.

The intervals are disjoint since $(1-\eta)r/2<(1-2\eta)r$. Choose distinct odd primes with $(1-2\eta)t_i<s_i\le(1-\eta)t_i$; $\gcd(s_i,t_i)=1$ as $t_i$ is a power of two. Put $S=\prod_is_i$; the $s_i$ supply the prime axis lengths of the [Chinese-remainder address map](crt-map.md). Multiplying the lower bounds and applying strict Bernoulli, $(1-x)^d>1-dx$, gives $S/T>(1-1/(2d))^d>1/2$ for $d\ge2$. With $s_i<t_i$ this gives $T/2<S<T$.

With the relative gap $\theta_i=t_i/s_i-1\ge\eta/(1-\eta)>\eta$ we get $\alpha^4\theta_i>12d^2b/(4d)=3db\ge6b=p$, the separation hypothesis of the resampling lemma; also $t_i\le r\le T<2^p$.

Trial division of $O(r)$ candidates by $O(\sqrt r)$ divisors costs $O(dr^{3/2}\operatorname{poly}(p))$, with logarithm $O(\log p+p/d)=o(p)$.
