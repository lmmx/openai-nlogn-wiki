## This page

Concept: Signed coefficient packing
Gloss: Encoding polynomial coefficients, including negative ones, as one large integer by fixed-width blocks, so that polynomial multiplication becomes integer multiplication.
Also called: Kronecker substitution

## This page in the argument

The step: The polynomial products use signed packing and the established $O(n\log n)$ multiplier as an ordinary subroutine.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A deterministic fixed-tape machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time, with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, below the $n\lg n$ conjecture in this model.; how: bound the cost of an exact multiplication whose transform layers are made cheap by two tape procedures, then fix parameters so every cost term fits (on [Integer multiplication below n log n](main-theorem.md))
- The exact product of two $n$-bit integers is obtained from the convolution of their radix-digit coefficients, computed by transforms on a fixed tape layout and then recovered and carried.; how: reduce the convolution by a Chinese-remainder map, Gaussian resampling, Bluestein's identity and a phase twist to a convolution over a polynomial ring, then recover coefficients and propagate carries (on [Radix-digit convolution](radix-digit-convolution.md))
- Convolution on the remaining axes over $\mathbb{C}[y]/(y^r+1)$ is computed by two forward synthetic transforms, a pointwise product, and an opposite transform.; how: forward synthetic transforms that keep frequency order, with polynomial products by signed packing and an established multiplier (on [Convolution over the polynomial ring](polynomial-ring-convolution.md))

  > on in [15, Section 4.4.3]. The quantitative resampling and bit-cost estimates used here are those of the latter paper. More specifically, the proof adapts Harvey and van der Hoeven’s Gaussian-resampling identity, contraction bounds, and short-convolution procedures [15, Theorem 4.2, Proposition 4.7 and Lemmas 4.8–4.12]. Their framework already permits arbitrary dimension. Here the source and target permutations remain in the transform identity, while the faster tape procedures perform the required axis movements. Bluestein’s conversion to convolution [4] and the synthetic-root convolution algorithm are retained; the latter follows the polynomial-transform construction in [15, Section 2.4 and Section 3]. Signed coefficient packing is a form of Kronecker substitution, with the fixed-tape version of [15, Lemma 2.5] as a direct antecedent.
