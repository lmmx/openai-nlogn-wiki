## Page to audit (`butterfly`)

````markdown
# Butterfly

Here $\xi$ and $\eta$ are the two input values; the sources write them as $u$ and $v$, but this page renames them because $u$ already denotes a field width. A butterfly is the two-point map $H_0(\xi,\eta)=\left(\frac{\xi+\eta}{2},\frac{\xi-\eta}{2}\right)$, which replaces a pair of values by their half-sum and half-difference. The butterfly is an elementary step of the [discrete Fourier transform](discrete-fourier-transform.md): a transform on $q$ entries is assembled from such two-point steps, each acting on a pair of entries whose positions differ in one index bit, that is, in one binary digit of the position.

For example, $H_0(3,1)=(2,1)$. Applying $H_0$ to $(2,1)$ gives $(3/2,1/2)$, which is $(3,1)/2$. Two applications of the butterfly therefore halve the input, which the sources record as.

The butterfly is useful because the transform calculation is built from cheap operations. In the sources' representation, sit alongside address rearrangements and signed shifts. When the entries are polynomial records rather than numbers, the butterfly acts on each complex coefficient of the record separately, so $H_0$.

The [simultaneous butterfly layer](simultaneous-butterfly-layer.md) extends this single two-point step to several selected address bits at once:.
````
