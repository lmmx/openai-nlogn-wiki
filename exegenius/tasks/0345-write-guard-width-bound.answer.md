The guard-width bound makes every intermediate coefficient of one [simultaneous butterfly layer](simultaneous-butterfly-layer.md) exact, by reserving an explicit count $\Delta$ of extra fractional and integer bits per real component, so that a single final truncation suffices.

The two-point map sends two complex input values $u,v$ (these letters change meaning here) to $((u+v)/2,\ (u-v)/2)$. The layer applies it on $D$ selected axes, and $d\ge D$ bounds $D$. The bound applies to the output of the [tensor product](tensor-product.md) of $D$ copies of the map. Its entries are $\pm\tfrac12$, so each output is $2^{-D}$ times a signed sum of inputs.

The bound must allow for operators that are not [linear contractions](linear-contraction.md), meaning linear maps that can increase the largest modulus of a vector. <cite data-source="paper">Its intermediate operators need not be contractions.</cite> Rounding after each stage would be unsafe, since errors need not shrink. <cite data-source="paper">The procedure evaluates the entire layer exactly before this final truncation.</cite>

Intermediate values are exact [Gaussian dyadics](gaussian-dyadics.md), so the bound must limit the denominators of the values. <cite data-source="paper">Intermediate values are exact Gaussian dyadics</cite> Inputs lie in $2^{-p}\mathbb{Z}[i]$ with modulus at most $1$, where $p$ is the fractional precision. <cite data-source="paper">start with disk-valued coefficients in 2−p Z[i]</cite>

Let $\ell$ be the depth of a value, the number of operations on a chain that follows an operand from an input into it. A value at depth $\ell$ lies in $2^{-(p+\ell)}\mathbb{Z}[i]$ with modulus at most $2^\ell$. <cite data-source="paper">lies in 2−(p+ℓ) Z[i] and has modulus at most 2ℓ</cite> By induction on $\ell$, operands of depth $\ell-1$ lie in $2^{-(p+\ell)}\mathbb{Z}[i]$ with modulus at most $2^{\ell-1}$, so a sum has modulus at most $2^\ell$. A halving adds one denominator bit.

The recursion is that of the [complex phase network](complex-phase-network.md): a group of $e$ selected axes splits into $s$ calls on groups of $e/m$ axes, where $W$ is the wire count, $s$ the smaller-call count, and $m$ the factor by which a call divides the axis count (the letter $m$ changes meaning here). Let $A(e)$ bound the operations on any chain within such a group. A chain is charged for all $s$ calls, plus at most $\eta=64(W+m+1)^3$ operations for the gates at the node (<cite data-source="paper">E = 64(W + m + 1)3</cite>, the paper's $E$). Hence <cite data-source="paper">A(e) ≤ sA(e/m) + E</cite> at an internal node, and $A(e)\le8e$ at a leaf.

Each level divides the axis count by $m$, so there are at most $\lceil\log_m d\rceil$ internal levels. <cite data-source="paper">There are at most k ≤ ⌈logm d⌉ internal levels.</cite> Put $\beta=s+\eta$. For $x\ge1$, $sx+\eta\le\beta x$, so each level multiplies a bound of at least $1$ by $\beta$. From the leaf bound $8d\le8d\beta$, we get $A(e)\le8d\beta^{\lceil\log_m d\rceil+1}$. Let $\nu$ be the least $j\ge1$ with $m^j\ge\beta$, so $\log_m\beta\le\nu$ (the letters $\beta,\nu$ change meaning here). Then $\beta^{\lceil\log_m d\rceil+1}\le\beta^2d^{\log_m\beta}\le\beta^2d^\nu$, so $A(e)\le8\beta^2d^{\nu+1}$.

Preprocessing adds at most $md$ earlier pieces of depth $8\beta^2d^{\nu+1}$ each, and at most $d$ kernels add $8d$. The shift by $\lfloor D/2\rfloor$ is charged as that many halvings, and phases add at most $9$. Since $\lfloor D/2\rfloor+9\le10d$, the depth is at most $26m\beta^2d^{\nu+2}$, which is below $128m\beta^2d^{\nu+3}$, where $128$ rounds $26$ up. <cite data-source="paper">Here d ≥ 1, mB 2 ≥ 1, and C1 ≥ ν + 3.</cite> Let $C_0=128m\beta^2$ and $C_1=\max\{20,\nu+3\}$, so every value has $\ell\le C_0d^{C_1}$.

Let $\Delta=\lceil C_0d^{C_1}\rceil$. Every value lies in $2^{-(p+\Delta)}\mathbb{Z}[i]$ with modulus at most $2^\Delta$, so $p+\Delta$ fractional bits and $\Delta+3$ integer and sign bits suffice. <cite data-source="paper">represents every intermediate coefficient exactly</cite> <cite data-source="paper">Shifts consume the reserved fractional zeros and discard no bits.</cite>

The growth of $d$ is stated with [Big-Theta notation](big-theta-notation.md): $d=\Theta(p^{\varepsilon})$ means $d$ lies between two constant multiples of $p^{\varepsilon}$ for large $p$. Since $W$, $m$ and $s$ are fixed, so are $\beta$, $\nu$, $C_0$ and $C_1$, and $\Delta=O(p^{\varepsilon C_1})$. <cite data-source="paper">Since d = Θ(pϵ ), ∆ = o(p)</cite> The bound states growth in the sense of [little-o notation](little-o-notation.md): $\Delta=o(p)$ means $\Delta/p\to0$, which holds since $\varepsilon C_1<1$.

Truncation $Q_p$ moves a real component $x$ to the last multiple of $2^{-p}$ before $x$ towards $0$. <cite data-source="paper">truncation cannot increase the absolute value of either component, and changes each by less than</cite> $2^{-p}$, because the next multiple of $2^{-p}$ away from $0$ lies beyond $x$.
