## This page

Concept: Fast address-chunk interchange
Gloss: Interchanging two disjoint contiguous address fields of u bits in an array costs O(V·u^τ) with τ = 1 − 2⁻⁵⁰, realized by recursively applying the bit network and computing XOR combinations of data.
Also called: chunk swap, address interchange

## This page in the argument

The step: Two disjoint contiguous $u$-bit address fields of an array of volume $V$ can be interchanged in $O(Vu^\tau)$ time.
How: implement each edge's matrix shear by rank-many smaller interchanges via a triangular factorization, run the bit network on row-split role streams, recurse on power widths, then pad to arbitrary widths
Why: An edge matrix of rank a costs a smaller interchanges, and the ranks sum to less than W m, so the recursion saves a power of the width. (the sources say so: "Because the sum of these ranks is less than the number of wires times $m$, the resulting recurrence saves a power of the chunk width." (paper))
Striking feature: The final effect is an exact permutation of data, yet it is computed via XOR combinations of data and works for arbitrary scratch contents. (explain why it holds, from the method)

Its sub-steps, in order:

- Every rational $m\times m$ matrix of rank $a$ factors as $E_1\Pi E_2$ with $E_1,E_2$ invertible lower triangular and $\Pi$ a partial permutation with $a$ ones. (on [Lower triangular factorization](triangular-factorization.md))
- The address shear $(H,D)\mapsto(H+AD,D)$ over $\mathbb Z/q^b\mathbb Z$ with $A$ of rank $a$ takes exactly $a$ smaller field interchanges plus $O(V)$ work. (on [Address shear](address-shear.md))
- The bit network with projection frames gives the shear $H\gets H+D$ on every role, with endpoint matrices differing by $I_m$ and edge ranks summing to $s_{\rm b}<W_{\rm b}m$. (on [Bit network interface](bit-network-interface.md))
- For widths $e=m^k$ with enough rows, the normalized cost satisfies $F_k\le(s/W)F_{k-1}+O(1)$, hence the interchange costs $O(Ve^\tau)$.
- Supplying rows from high digits, decomposing the width in base $m$ and padding binary chunks to radix $q$ extends the bound to $O(Vu^\tau)$ for every width $u$.

It serves these steps, from the main result down:

- A single deterministic fixed-alphabet multitape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.; how: speed up address movement and butterfly arithmetic by recursive linear-network procedures, plug them into Harvey and van der Hoeven's reduction of multiplication to synthetic polynomial transforms, and bound the total cost (on [Integer multiplication below n log n](main-theorem.md))
- Address-field interchange costs $O(Vu^\tau)$ and a simultaneous butterfly layer costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$, with fixed $\tau,\lambda'<1$, where $V$ is the stored bit volume.; how: turn a strict rank deficit of two finite linear networks into recursive power savings for both tape procedures

**S4.4**: For widths $e=m^k$ with enough rows, the normalized cost satisfies $F_k\le(s/W)F_{k-1}+O(1)$, hence the interchange costs $O(Ve^\tau)$.
Source: "Hence $F_k=O(e^\tau)$." (paper)
> The number of recursive calls is the sum of the edge ranks, exactly $s$. Each acts on one role stream of volume $V/W$, retaining all other fields as spectators. Splitting, merging, gates, and triangular operations have total cost $O(V)$ because their number is fixed. Below we verify that scheduling the recursive calls on fixed tapes also costs $O(V)$ at this node. Thus if $F_k$ is a uniform upper bound for time divided by logical volume, then \begin{equation} \label{eq:swap-recurrence} F_0=O(1),\qquad F_k\le(s/W)F_{k-1}+O(1). \end{equation} For $a=s/W<m^\tau$ the geometric sum $\sum_{j=0}^k a^j$ is $O(m^{k\tau})$. If $a\le1$, the sum is at most $k+1=O(m^{k\tau})$; if $a>1$, it is $O(a^k)$. Hence $F_k=O(e^\tau)$.

**S4.5**: Supplying rows from high digits, decomposing the width in base $m$ and padding binary chunks to radix $q$ extends the bound to $O(Vu^\tau)$ for every width $u$.
Source: "Since $e=O(u)$ for fixed $q$, the bound is $O(Vu^\tau)$." (paper)
> Finally, for binary chunks let $e$ be the least integer with $q^e\ge2^u$. Pad each of the two chunk ranges numerically from $[2^u]$ to $[q^e]$, putting zero in an entry if either chunk coordinate is invalid. The increase in volume is less than $q^2$, a fixed factor. The padding is a nested scan through consecutive valid and invalid intervals, with their lengths prepared from the shape. It costs $O(V)$ with a constant depending on $q$. Lexicographic radix-$q$ order of $[q^e]$ is its numerical order, just as binary order of $[2^u]$ is its numerical order. Padding therefore preserves the order of the existing entries without converting the digits of every address. The completed interchange preserves the set on which both chunk coordinates lie below $2^u$. Scan away the other entries to recover the desired binary array. Since $e=O(u)$ for fixed $q$, the bound is $O(Vu^\tau)$. \end{proof}
