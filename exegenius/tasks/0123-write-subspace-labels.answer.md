A subspace label is a nondegenerate subspace of a fixed bilinear space $E$. Labels are attached to each [gate and terminal of a network](network-gate-terminal.md) and fix how much work each change of representation costs, as in the subset-intersection network (a linear network on three-element subsets of a ground set) and the complex phase network (the same kind of network with complex frames).

Let $\langle\xi,\eta\rangle$ be the [bilinear form](bilinear-form.md) of $E$; vectors $\xi,\eta$ are orthogonal when $\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=0$. A subspace $U$ of $E$ is nondegenerate under the [bilinear form](bilinear-form.md) when no nonzero vector of $U$ is orthogonal to every vector of $U$. Its orthogonal complement $U^\perp$ is the set of vectors orthogonal to all of $U$.

On every edge the two labels are nested: <cite data-source="paper">On every edge one label will contain the other.</cite> Write $V\subseteq U$ when every vector of $V$ lies in $U$, and $U\supseteq V$ for the same fact. The residual of the edge is $U\cap V^\perp$, the vectors lying in both $U$ and $V^\perp$: <cite data-source="paper">the orthogonal complement of the smaller label inside the larger one</cite>.

Here $\oplus$ is the direct sum, not XOR: $E=V\oplus V^\perp$ means each vector of $E$ is uniquely a vector of $V$ plus one of $V^\perp$. This holds for nondegenerate $V$, since $V\cap V^\perp=\{0\}$, and the map $p\mapsto\langle p,\cdot\rangle$ from $V$ to functions on $V$ is injective, hence onto. For $V\subseteq U$, each $u\in U$ splits as $p+w$ with $p\in V$, $w\in V^\perp$, and then $w=u-p\in U\cap V^\perp$. So $U=V\oplus(U\cap V^\perp)$ and $\dim(U\cap V^\perp)=\dim U-\dim V$.

The frame at a vertex with label $U$ is $C_U$, and the change across an edge from $U$ to $V$ is $C_VC_U^{-1}$ (<cite data-source="paper">The frame in (5) is Dv = CU at a vertex with label U</cite>). This change <cite data-source="paper">is a product of one translation kernel aI + bXv , or its inverse, per</cite> <cite data-source="paper">vector in an orthonormal basis of the edge residual.</cite> Here each kernel is $\lambda_0I+\lambda_1X_v$, with Gaussian dyadic coefficients $\lambda_0,\lambda_1$, $I$ the identity, $v$ a basis vector, and $(X_vf)(x)=f(x+v)$, where $x$ is an address and $f$ a function of addresses; these letters change meaning here. <cite data-source="paper">(Xv f )(x) = f (x + v)</cite>. An orthonormal basis $v_1,\dots,v_\rho$ has $\langle v_i,v_j\rangle$ equal to 1 if $i=j$ and 0 otherwise, and <cite data-source="paper">For the binary label space, every nonzero orthogonal residual of an edge has an orthonormal basis.</cite> So an edge costs $\dim U-\dim V$ kernels, and <cite data-source="paper">The saving will come from keeping the total dimension lost on decreasing</cite> edges small.

In the complex network, for a binary label $U$ let $P_U$ project onto $U$ along $U^\perp$, and let $q_U(x)=\mathrm{wt}(P_Ux)\bmod 4$, where $\mathrm{wt}$ counts ones. With $H=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}$, the frame is $C_U=H^{\otimes m}\,\mathrm{diag}(i^{q_U(x)})\,H^{\otimes m}$ on addresses $x\in\mathbb{F}_2^m$. Each entry of $H^{\otimes m}$ is $\pm2^{-m/2}$, so each entry of $C_U$ is $2^{-m}$ times a sum of powers of $i$; since $H^2=I$, the inverse has the same form. Each label therefore has a frame whose entries are a [Gaussian integer](gaussian-integer.md) divided by $2^m$ (<cite data-source="paper">Each entry of CU and its inverse is a Gaussian integer divided by 2m</cite>).

The labels are drawn from a [ground set](ground-set.md) of $h$ elements, where $h$ counts the elements of the ground set. For the bit network take $E=\mathbb{Q}^h$, with $\mathbb{Q}$ the rationals, and $\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=\xi^\top(I-J/9)\eta$, where $\top$ is transpose, $I$ the $h\times h$ identity matrix, and $J$ the $h\times h$ matrix of ones. A triple $T$ is a three-element subset of it. Each triple label is built from the [indicator vector](indicator-vector.md) $t_T$ of its triple, which has entry 1 on the triple and 0 elsewhere. Each triple label is a [line spanned by a vector](line-spanned-by-vector.md), the line through $t_T$.

For a triple $T$, $t_T^\top t_T=3$ and $t_T^\top Jt_T=9$ (the square of its entry sum), so $\langle t_T,t_T\rangle=3-9/9=2$. For another triple $S$ with indicator $t_S$, $\langle t_S,t_T\rangle=|S\cap T|-1$, where $|A|$ counts the elements of $A$. Two triples with <cite data-source="paper">S, T are neighbors when |S ∩ T | = 1</cite> therefore have orthogonal lines. <cite data-source="paper">Thus every triple line is nondegenerate, and neighboring triple lines are orthogonal, in the label</cite>.
