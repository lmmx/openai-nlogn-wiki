Cost accounting totals the running time of every step of [The multiplication algorithm](multiplication-algorithm.md) and shows that it is $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$; the saved exponent $\kappa=2^{-182}$ is fixed here, by the smallest of the margins computed below.

Let $V=Tp=\Theta(n)$, the number of stored bits when $T$ coefficients carry $\Theta(p)$ bits each ($V$ is a bit count here, not a label). With the fixed exponents of [Parameter and prime-length choice](parameter-choice.md), namely $\epsilon=2^{-75}$, $c=2^{-56}$, $\delta=1/16$, $1-\tau=2^{-50}$ and $1-\lambda'=2^{-54}$, the sizes are $d=\Theta(p^\epsilon)$ axes, address-chunk width $K=\Theta(p^{\epsilon c})$ bits, $\ell=\Theta(p^{1-\epsilon})$ address bits per axis and Gaussian width $\alpha=\Theta(p^{1/4+\epsilon/2})$. All are powers of $p$, so each cost divided by $V$ is a power of $p$ as well.

Dividing by $V$, seven operations dominate, each a power $p^{1-g_i}$ with margin $g_i>0$ below one.

| Operation | Cost$/V$ | Power of $p$ | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prefix moves | $dK$ | $\epsilon(1+c)$ | $g_1=1-\epsilon(1+c)$ |
| Chunk exchanges | $pK^{\tau-1}$ | $1-\epsilon c(1-\tau)$ | $g_2=\epsilon c(1-\tau)$ |
| Layer rounds | $\ell d^{\lambda'}$ | $1-\epsilon(1-\lambda')$ | $g_3=\epsilon(1-\lambda')$ |
| Axis layouts | $d^2(1+\ell^\tau)$ | $\tau+\epsilon(2-\tau)$ | $g_4=1-\tau-\epsilon(2-\tau)$ |
| Gaussian lines | $dp^{1/2+\delta}\alpha$ | $3/4+\delta+3\epsilon/2$ | $g_5=1/4-\delta-3\epsilon/2$ |
| Chirps | $dp^\delta$ | $\epsilon+\delta$ | $g_6=1-\delta-\epsilon$ |
| Products | $\log(rp)$ | $1-\epsilon$ | $g_7=\epsilon$ |

Each row follows from the cost of its lemma.

- Prefix moves and single butterfly rounds are $O(dK)$ linear scans, each $O(V)$, in [Tape layout for synthetic transforms](transform-layout.md).
- The layout rearranges the $d\ell=O(p)$ address bits of the records by exchanging width-$K$ chunks, so it needs $O(p/K)$ exchanges <cite data-source="paper">Its positional layout uses $O(p/K)$ exchanges of width-$K$ chunks, because $d\ell=O(p)$.</cite> Each chunk exchange costs $O(VK^\tau)$ by [Fast address-chunk interchange](address-interchange.md), and the normalized total is $O(p/K)K^\tau=O(pK^{\tau-1})$. The source and target permutations of [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md) are never executed, since they cancel (see [Retained permutation cancellation](retained-permutations.md)), so no row charges them.
- Each of at most $\ell$ simultaneous rounds costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ by [Simultaneous butterfly layer](simultaneous-layer.md).
- The [Chinese-remainder address map](crt-map.md) needs $O(d^2)$ adjacent axis moves. Each peels one excess bit, exchanges the equal-width parts and restores the bit, costing $O(V(1+\ell^\tau))$ <cite data-source="paper">Each costs $O(V(1+\ell^\tau))$: peel a single excess bit when widths</cite>. Here $d^2\ell^\tau=p^{2\epsilon}p^{(1-\epsilon)\tau}$.
- In [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md), axis $i$ has at most $T/t_i$ lines, where $t_i$ is its padded length, each costing $O(t_ip^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$. The total is $O(dTp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$.
- A chirp numerator of the [Bluestein chirp identity](chirp.md) has $O(p)$ bits and sums $d$ terms. The [Established O(n log n) multiplier](hvdh-multiplier.md), which multiplies $N$-bit integers in $O(N\log 2N)$, forms its squares and products in $O(dp^{1+\delta})$ per entry.
- There are $T/r$ ring-product records, each needing four products on $O(rp)$ bits after [Signed packing](signed-packing.md). They use the established multiplier at length $o(n)$, not the algorithm being built. The total is $O(Tp\log(rp))$, and $\log(rp)=O(p^{1-\epsilon})$ because $r$ is at most $2^{O(p/d)}$.

Substitution gives $g_1>1/2$, $g_2=2^{-75}2^{-56}2^{-50}=2^{-181}$, $g_3=2^{-75}2^{-54}=2^{-129}$, $g_4>2^{-51}$, $g_5>1/8$, $g_6>1/2$ and $g_7=2^{-75}$, so the smallest margin is $2^{-181}=2\kappa$ <cite data-source="paper">Thus $\min_i g_i=2^{-181}=2\kappa$.</cite>

The chunk-exchange row is the smallest because of what it measures. Exchanging chunks by plain scans would be $\tau=1$, giving cost $p$ per unit volume and margin $0$; likewise $\lambda'=1$ would give the layer row margin $0$. Address movement therefore saves a power of $p$ only through the two new tape procedures, and each saving is tiny. In $g_3$ the layer saving $1-\lambda'$ is scaled by $\epsilon$, because only $d=\Theta(p^\epsilon)$ axes share a layer. In $g_2$ there is a further factor $c$: an exchange acts on chunks of only $K=\Theta(p^{\epsilon c})$ bits and saves a factor $K^{-(1-\tau)}=p^{-\epsilon c(1-\tau)}$ against a full pass. The other rows have margins of at least $2^{-75}$.

Setup must also be negligible. A visit is a node of the butterfly-layer or address-layout recursions, or one round or group invocation inside them. These recursions have constant branching, depth $O(\log p)$ and polynomially many rounds and groups. Fix an exponent $A$ with at most $p^A$ visits in total, and an exponent $C$ with descriptor setup of at most $p^C$ steps per visit. The setup costs at most $p^{A+C}=o(Tp)$ <cite data-source="paper">costs at most $p^{A+C}=o(Tp)$.</cite> Per-record and per-line setup is $O(Tp^{A+C}/r)$ or $O(dTp^C/r)$, which is $o(Tp)$ because $r$ exceeds every fixed power of $p$.

Exceptional addresses, where the packed shifts of [Packed selected-bit addition](packed-bit-addition.md) fail, are bounded to a fraction $O(d2^{-K})$ of addresses. Let $V_{\rm call}$ be the volume of one packed change of basis. A record has $\Theta(rp)$ payload bits and an exceptional one adds an $O(p)$-bit key, so the compact stream of exceptional records has length $O\left(\frac{V_{\rm call}}{rp}d2^{-K}(rp+p)\right)=O(V_{\rm call}d2^{-K})$. Stable bit sorting makes $O(p)$ scans of it. Over at most $p^A$ visits of volume $O(V)$, the repair costs $O(Vp^{O(1)}2^{-K})=o(V)$, because $K=\Theta(p^{\epsilon c})$ and $2^{-K}$ beats every power of $p$. Global setup and the prime search for the axis lengths also cost $o(V)$.

The table writes each bound as a pure power of $p$, rounding up factors such as $\log p$ (a chirp product costs $O(p\log p)\le O(p^{1+\delta})$). The lemmas may still leave a fixed number of $\log p$ factors, for example from the $O(\log p)$ recursion depth, and the sources do not itemize them. They are covered by the slack: any fixed power of $\log p$ is at most $p^\kappa$ for large $p$, since a power of a logarithm grows more slowly than any positive power of $p$. Each of the seven powers is at most $1-2\kappa$, so the powers times these logarithms are at most $p^{1-2\kappa}p^{\kappa}=p^{1-\kappa}$. Half of the smallest margin is spent on logarithms and half remains as the saving. Any positive $\kappa$ below $2^{-181}$ would pass the same test, so the choice $\kappa=2^{-182}$ is a convenient one and the size of $\kappa$ is set by $g_2$. The total is $O(Tp\,p^{1-\kappa})=O(n(\log n)^{1-\kappa})$ <cite data-source="paper">O(Tp\,p^{1-\kappa})=O(n(\log n)^{1-\kappa}).</cite>

This bound holds only for large inputs, beyond a cutoff chosen past all the size, precision, margin and layout conditions. Below the cutoff the machine uses schoolbook multiplication. Only finitely many lengths lie below it, so their running times are bounded by one constant <cite data-source="paper">many smaller lengths are included by enlarging the implicit constant.</cite> With $\lg n=\max\{\lceil\log_2n\rceil,1\}$, which is within a constant factor of $\log n$ and at least $1$, the single machine therefore runs in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ for every $n\ge1$.
