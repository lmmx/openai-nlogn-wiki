Scratch storage is auxiliary storage that a procedure may overwrite during its work but must restore to its starting contents, whatever those contents were. The side and central wires of the [Subset-intersection network](subset-intersection-network.md) are scratch storage that is restored by that network, which lets it swap two data banks without changing its scratch values. The [Address-field interchange](address-interchange.md) and the [Packed selected-bit addition](packed-bit-addition.md) build on this restoration.

Scratch wires may start with any values, as the paper allows: <cite data-source="paper">We call all these auxiliary wires scratch wires; their initial values may be arbitrary.</cite>

Write $S$ and $T$ for triples, which are three-element subsets of the ground set. The paper's bit construction uses scalars in $\mathbb{F}_2$ and calls $S$ and $T$ neighbours when they share one element: <cite data-source="paper">scalars lie in F2 , and S, T are neighbors when |S ∩ T | = 1.</cite>

Write $\phi$ for the control vector, with entries $\phi_T$ at triples $T$, and $\mathcal{Y}$ for the target vector, with entries $\mathcal{Y}_S$. Write $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{M}$ for the vectors of starting values of the side wires and of the central wires. Each neighbouring pair $(S,T)$ has a side wire $A_{ST}$, and each element $\ell$ of the ground set has a central wire $C_\ell$.

Four maps act on these wires. $\mathcal{K}$ is copy, which adds $\phi_T$ to $A_{ST}$ for each neighbouring pair. $\mathcal{O}$ is side injection, which adds to $\mathcal{Y}_S$ the side wires $A_{ST}$ over the neighbours $T$ of $S$. $\mathcal{Z}$ is gather, which adds $\phi_T$ to $C_\ell$ for each $\ell\in T$. $\mathcal{R}$ is scatter, which adds to $\mathcal{Y}_S$ the central wires $C_\ell$ with $\ell\in S$.

The forward schedule has eight rows, and one run of it is a forward invocation. Rows 0 and 1 subtract $\mathcal{O}\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{R}\mathcal{M}$ from $\mathcal{Y}$, which cancels the effect of the starting values. Rows 2 and 3 copy and gather, so the side wires hold $\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{K}\phi$ and the central wires hold $\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{Z}\phi$. Rows 4 and 5 scatter and side-inject these into $\mathcal{Y}$, adding $\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{Z}\phi)+\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{K}\phi)$. Rows 6 and 7 undo gather and copy, so they change only wires and restore the starting values: <cite data-source="paper">Rows 6 and 7 restore c, a.</cite>

Rows 0 to 5 therefore change $\mathcal{Y}$ by $-\mathcal{O}\mathcal{L}-\mathcal{R}\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{M}+\mathcal{Z}\phi)+\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{K}\phi)$. The terms in $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{M}$ cancel, leaving $(\mathcal{O}\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{R}\mathcal{Z})\phi$.

Consider one control value $\phi_T$ and one target entry $\mathcal{Y}_S$. The path through gather and scatter carries $\phi_T$ into $\mathcal{Y}_S$ once for each element of $S\cap T$, so its coefficient is $|S\cap T| \bmod 2$. The path through copy and side injection has coefficient 1 exactly when $T$ is a neighbour of $S$, that is, when $|S\cap T|=1$. Over $\mathbb{F}_2$ the two coefficients add to 1 when $T=S$, since $|S\cap S|=3$ is odd while $S$ is not its own neighbour. For every other $T$ the two coefficients agree, so their sum is 0. So $\mathcal{O}\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{R}\mathcal{Z}$ is the identity. The net change is that $\mathcal{Y}_S$ becomes $\mathcal{Y}_S+\phi_S$ and no other entry changes. This is the paper's Lemma 5, which states the result for arbitrary starting values: <cite data-source="paper">For arbitrary initial values of its auxiliary wires, a forward invocation changes yS to</cite> $\mathcal{Y}_S+\phi_S$ and <cite data-source="paper">leaves every other value unchanged.</cite>

Worked example. Take a toy network with no side wires, in which $S$ and $T$ both contain $\ell$. Scatter reads $C_\ell$ into $\mathcal{Y}_S$, and gather adds $\phi_T$ to $C_\ell$. Let $\phi_T=3$ and $C_\ell$ start at $7$. Row 1 subtracts $7$ from $\mathcal{Y}_S$, row 3 makes $C_\ell=10$, row 4 adds $10$ to $\mathcal{Y}_S$, and row 6 restores $C_\ell$ to $7$. The net change is $-7+10=3=\phi_T$.

On arrays, the gates act <cite data-source="paper">pointwise at every address</cite>, so the same rows run at every address at once. Scratch storage restores values at every address of the [Address field](address-field.md) it occupies.
