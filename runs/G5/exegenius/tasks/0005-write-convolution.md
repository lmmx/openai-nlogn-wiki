## This page

Concept: Cyclic convolution
Gloss: The operation combining two coefficient arrays so that coefficient j of the result sums products of entries whose indices add to j modulo the length; it is polynomial multiplication with wraparound.

## Where the argument relies on this page

- The algorithm, with digits placed on prime axes by the Chinese-remainder map, transformed by Gaussian resampling, chirp convolution and polynomial-ring convolution, multiplied pointwise, transformed back, rescaled, rounded and carry-propagated, outputs the exact $2n$-bit product. (on [The multiplication algorithm](multiplication-algorithm.md))
- The normalized cyclic convolution $\mathcal M_r(f,g)=(f*g)/T$ of disk-grid arrays over $\mathcal R_r$ is computed with error below $\sqrt2\,M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$ in $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda\prime}+\log(rp)])$ time. (on [Convolution from the retained transform layout](ring-convolution.md))
- The source transform $RF_{\boldsymbol s}$ on pairwise coprime axis lengths $s_i$ equals $2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A$ on power-of-two lengths $t_i$ with both permutations $R,Q$ left inside the formula, the middle transform $QF_{\boldsymbol t}$ is a chirp convolution, and $R$ cancels in a convolution. (on [Resampling with retained permutations](retained-permutations.md))
- For even $t_i$, the chirp array $a_{\boldsymbol j}=\exp(-\pi i\sum_i s_ij_i^2/t_i)$ gives $QF_{\boldsymbol t}u=\overline a\cdot(a*(\overline a\cdot u))/T$, a cyclic convolution between two pointwise phase products. (on [Bluestein chirp identity](chirp-identity.md))
- Writing each integer as a polynomial in $2^b$ with $q=\lceil n/b\rceil$ digits turns the product into a convolution of digit lists; since the degree is below $S$, convolution modulo $x^S-1$ has no wraparound and each coefficient is below $2^{3b}$. (on [Reducing integer multiplication to digit convolution](radix-digit-reduction.md))
- Multiplying coefficient $k$ by $\zeta^k$ with $\zeta=e^{\pi i/r}$ turns convolution modulo $x^r-1$ into convolution modulo $y^r+1$ on the last axis, so the complex cyclic convolution is the synthetic one conjugated by phase twists at cost $O(Tp^{1+\delta})$. (on [Phase twist to a negacyclic wrap](coefficient-twist.md))

## Pages that build on this one

- Phase twist to a negacyclic wrap: Multiplying coefficient k by ζ^k with ζ = e^{πi/r} turns convolution modulo x^r − 1 into convolution modulo y^r + 1 on the last axis, and untwisting restores the cyclic wrap.
- Discrete Fourier transform and convolution theorem: The normalized transform (F_q u)_k = (1/q) Σ u_j e^{−2πi jk/q}, which turns cyclic convolution into pointwise multiplication.
- Bluestein chirp identity: A phase-weighted identity that rewrites a Fourier transform, including its frequency permutation, as a cyclic convolution with a chirp array a_j = exp(−πi Σ s_i j_i²/t_i) between pointwise phase multiplications.
- Convolution from the retained transform layout: Two forward synthetic transforms, pointwise ring products by signed packing, and the opposite transform, all in one retained order, give the normalized convolution (f*g)/(rM) over the polynomial ring with explicit error.
- Reducing integer multiplication to digit convolution: Writing each integer as a polynomial in 2^b with b-bit digits turns the product into a convolution of digit lists; padding avoids wraparound, and rounding plus carry propagation recover the bits.

## Evidence from the sources, with the paragraph around each quote

- "Splitting the inputs into radix digits reduces multiplication to convolution of short integer coefficients." (paper)

  > Splitting the inputs into radix digits reduces multiplication to convolution of short integer coefficients. Our starting point is the Gaussian-resampling reduction of Harvey and van der Hoeven \cite{HarveyHoeven2021}: it replaces the Fourier transforms needed for this convolution by multidimensional transforms whose axis lengths are powers of two. After a further conversion to convolution, one coordinate is stored as the coefficient list of a polynomial modulo $y^r+1$, with $r$ a power of two. Powers of $y$ then serve as roots of unity in the remaining axes, and multiplication by such a root is a signed shift of the coefficient list. These are the synthetic polynomial transforms of Nussbaumer and Quandalle and Nussbaumer~\cite{NussbaumerQuandalle1978,Nussbaumer1980}.

- "let $*$ denote cyclic convolution in the displayed" (paper)

  > For arrays on $\prod_i\mathbb Z/t_i\mathbb Z$ with values in $\mathcal R_r$, let $*$ denote cyclic convolution in the displayed axes and define \[ \mathcal M_r(f,g)=\frac{f*g}{rM}=\frac{f*g}{T}. \] For a fixed output coefficient, each of the $M$ first cyclic addresses determines one second address, and each of the $r$ first polynomial indices determines one second index modulo $r$. The negacyclic wrap supplies the sign. Thus that coefficient averages $rM$ signed scalar products, and $\mathcal M_r$ is a bilinear contraction: \[ \|\mathcal M_r(f,g)\|_\infty \leq\|f\|_\infty\|g\|_\infty. \] If the other operand is a disk array, replacing one operand changes the output by at most the norm distance between its two values.
