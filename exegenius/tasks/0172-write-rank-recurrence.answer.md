Recursive power saving is the recurrence that makes both tape procedures, the interchange of two address chunks and the simultaneous butterfly layer, cost the volume times a power below one of their parameter.

A call has a parameter $f$ (here a number, not an array or polynomial): the number of digits in each interchanged chunk, or the number of selected axes of a layer. It acts on a stream of volume $V$. A call at parameter $mf$, with $m=10^6$ the label-space dimension, splits each chunk into $m$ slots of parameter $f$. It deals its rows into $W$ streams of volume $V/W$, one per wire of a fixed bit or complex [exchange network](exchange-network.md), and applies the network's gates pointwise. Each vertex carries a frame ([Frames on wire arrays](array-frames.md)); crossing an edge changes the stream's frame, and each unit of rank of that change costs one call at parameter $f$ on that stream: one interchange of a pair of slots in the bit network ([Matrix shear by triangular factorization](matrix-shear.md)), one layer of two-point kernels on a slot in the complex network. So the call makes $s$ calls at parameter $f$, each on volume $V/W$, where $s$ is the total rank of the edge changes.

The recursion gets its strict inequality $s<Wm$ from [Nested subspace labels and rank deficit](subspace-labels.md): an edge costs the dimension change of nested subspace labels, and each route's endpoint frames differ by all $m$ dimensions, so a wire climbing from $0$ to $m$ dimensions costs $m$. One data bank's $N$ wires start on a line and the other's stop one dimension short, saving $2N$; the bit network spends $N$ on extra source ranks, and shrinking edges, losing $L$ dimensions in all, cost back $2L<N$.

Let $F(f)$ be the time per unit volume at parameter $f$ ($F$ is not the label space here). The children cost $s(V/W)F(f)$, so multiplying the parameter by $m$ multiplies the recursive cost per unit volume by $s/W$, less than $m$ <cite data-source="paper">This strict inequality is the source of the power saving.</cite>

For interchange, the rest of the node's work is $O(V)$. Splitting into streams and merging back are linear passes ([Elementary stream operations](stream-ops.md)); the fixed number of gates each make one pointwise pass, and each edge's triangular factors are linear-time field updates. All streams share a fixed set of tapes, reused at every depth: before each child call, the $W-1$ idle streams are pushed onto a stack tape and the active one is copied to the child's input area; afterwards the result is copied back and the idle streams are popped. Each push, pop or copy scans $O(V)$ cells and never an ancestor's parked data, because the stack head stays at the top; with $s$ fixed, this totals $O(V)$. A single digit swaps in linear time:

$$F(mf)\le\frac{s}{W}F(f)+O(1),\qquad F(1)=O(1).$$

Unrolling through $\log_m f$ levels ($f$ a power of $m$), with depth $j$ (here counting levels) contributing $(s/W)^j$, gives $F(f)=O\big(\sum_{j=0}^{\log_m f}(s/W)^j\big)$. If $s/W<m^\tau$ with fixed $\tau<1$, this is $O(f^\tau)$: if $s/W\le1$ there are $1+\log_m f$ terms of at most $1$; otherwise the last term, below $m^{\tau\log_m f}=f^\tau$, dominates the geometric sum. So an interchange costs $O(Vf^\tau)$ <cite data-source="paper">The chunks can be interchanged in $O(Ve^\tau)$ time</cite>.

For the layer (complex network), node overhead grows: frame changes are row additions on slots of $(f/m)K$ address bits ($K$ bits per address chunk), each costing $O(V((fK)^\tau+1))$ through chunk interchanges ([Packed selected-bit addition](packed-bit-addition.md)); parking is $O(V)$ as above. Recursion stops below $d^\beta$ axes, $\beta=1/2$, applying the $f$ two-point kernels singly:

$$F(f)\le\frac{s}{W}F(f/m)+O((fK)^\tau+1),\qquad F(f)=O(f)\quad(f<d^\beta).$$

Fix $\sigma<1$ with $s/W\le m^\sigma$, and $\lambda<1$ above $\sigma$ and $\tau(1+c/\beta)$, where $K\le d^c$, $c$ fixed. Internal nodes have $f\ge d^\beta$, so $K\le f^{c/\beta}$ and overhead $O(f^\lambda)$. At depth $j$, the $(s/W)^j$ normalized overheads $O((f/m^j)^\lambda)$ sum geometrically, with ratio $(s/W)/m^\lambda<1$, to $O(f^\lambda)$; leaves contribute $(s/W)^jf/m^j\le f^\sigma(f/m^j)^{1-\sigma}<f^\sigma d^{\beta(1-\sigma)}$. With $f\le d$, and a $\log d$ factor from splitting into powers of $m$, the layer costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ for fixed $\lambda'$ between $\max\{\lambda,\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)\}$ and $1$ <cite data-source="paper">\max\{\lambda,\sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)\}<\lambda'<1.</cite>

Write $s/W=m(1-\eta)$, with $\eta$ now the relative deficit $(Wm-s)/(Wm)$. By the rank counts it equals $(N-2L)/(Wm)$ for the bit network and $(2N-2L)/(Wm)$ for the complex one, which evaluate to $339/22587335000000$ and $73/19906842167500$ <cite data-source="paper">=\frac{339}{22587335000000}</cite>. Both ($\approx1.5\cdot10^{-11}$, $3.7\cdot10^{-12}$) exceed $20\cdot2^{-50}\approx1.8\cdot10^{-14}$ <cite data-source="paper">Both exact deficits displayed above are greater than $20\cdot2^{-50}$.</cite> Since $m<2^{20}$, $\ln m<20\ln 2<20$; as the exponential lies above its tangent at $0$, $m^{1-2^{-50}}=m\exp(-2^{-50}\ln m)>m(1-2^{-50}\ln m)>m(1-20\cdot2^{-50})>m(1-\eta)=s/W$ for either network. Hence $\tau=\sigma=1-2^{-50}$ gives $s/W<m^\tau$ (bit) and $s/W<m^\sigma$ (complex), exponents barely below $1$.
